# Evidence Dossier — The Growth Playbook

**Module:** `growth-mindset-protocol` (`components/GrowthMindsetModule.tsx`)
**Review date:** 2026-06-23
**Reviewer:** Pre-accreditation literature review (DCU / Brian MacCraith)
**Governing rule:** Only state/advise what peer-reviewed literature supports; every DOI
verified via CrossRef. References surface via inline `Cite` markers + the module-wide
**References** button; data in `data/references/growthMindset.ts`.

**Reviewer note (the headline growth-mindset module — read this).** This is the most
replication-sensitive module in the set, so it warrants the clearest statement of where
the evidence stands. The construct (fixed vs growth / entity vs incremental theories of
intelligence) is well established (Dweck & Leggett 1988), and a longitudinal study links
incremental beliefs to maths achievement across the secondary transition (Blackwell et
al. 2007). **However**, two large meta-analyses (Sisk et al. 2018) found the average
mindset–achievement correlation is weak (r ≈ 0.10) and that broad mindset interventions
produce small, inconsistent effects. **The module is written to stay on the safe side of
that debate**: it does *not* claim "a growth mindset will raise your grades by X." Its
load-bearing claims are (a) the brain physically changes with practice — neuroplasticity,
which is robust (Draganski et al. 2004); (b) effortful, targeted practice builds skill —
deliberate practice (Ericsson et al. 1993); (c) feedback is information that shows where
to focus (Hattie & Timperley 2007); and (d) reframing self-talk ("yet") keeps you
engaged. Those are all defensible independent of the mindset-intervention effect-size
question. **No content was cut or reframed.** Blackwell 2007 and Sisk 2018 are recorded
here as context but are not cited inline, because the module makes the construct/self-talk
case, not the contested "intervention raises achievement" case.

## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 Two Brains: Fixed vs Growth** — The fixed/growth (entity/incremental) distinction
  (**dweckleggett1988**). Verified. The module frames it as an "operating system" /
  self-belief, not a grade guarantee. Alex's story is testimony; diagnostic is interactive.
- **§2 Your Brain is Plastic** — Practice physically reshapes the brain; training induces
  measurable structural change (**draganski2004**). Verified. The neuron simulator is
  illustrative.
- **§3 The Most Powerful Word ("yet")** — Reframing "I can't" → "I can't yet" to keep
  effort open. Linguistic/self-talk reframing; no specific neural or numerical claim is
  made here, so no inline citation is attached.
- **§4 Effort is the Real Talent** — Deliberate (focused, targeted) practice builds skill
  (**ericsson1993**). Verified.
- **§5 Feedback is Fuel** — Feedback is information showing where to focus, best separated
  from identity (**hattie2007**). Verified.
- **§6 Installing Your Growth OS** — Daily-habit framing of the above; no new claim.
