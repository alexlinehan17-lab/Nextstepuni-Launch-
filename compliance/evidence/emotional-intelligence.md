# Evidence Dossier — Building Emotional Intelligence

**Module:** `emotional-intelligence-protocol` (`components/EmotionalIntelligenceModule.tsx`)
**Review date:** 2026-06-23
**Reviewer:** Pre-accreditation literature review (DCU / Brian MacCraith)
**Governing rule:** Only state/advise what peer-reviewed literature supports; every DOI
verified via CrossRef. References surface via inline `Cite` markers + the module-wide
**References** button; data in `data/references/emotionalIntelligence.ts`.

**Reviewer note.** A stress-management toolkit module with seven verified sources, each
mapping cleanly to its construct: chronic stress / cortisol impairing prefrontal function
(Lupien et al. 2009); the ability model of emotional intelligence (Salovey & Mayer 1990 —
the academic construct, distinct from popular "EQ"); somatic markers — bodily signals that
flag emotional states (Damasio 1996); arousal reappraisal ("I'm excited" beats "calm
down", improving performance; Brooks 2014); cognitive restructuring as a CBT technique
(Hofmann et al. 2012); brief structured breathing reducing physiological arousal (Balban
et al. 2023); and sleep-dependent memory consolidation (Diekelmann & Born 2010).
**No content cut.** One loose phrase removed — "clears out the junk that causes brain fog"
(a glymphatic-clearance gloss not cited here) — leaving the cited consolidation claim. The
Neural Tug-of-War / PFC-Shutdown / Arousal-Reappraisal / Box-Breathing widgets are
illustrative or practical tools (the tug-of-war chart curves are stylised, not study data).

## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 Why Stress Messes With Your Brain** — Sustained stress/cortisol takes the prefrontal
  cortex offline ("going blank") (**lupien2009**). Verified.
- **§2 What is Emotional Intelligence** — EI as the ability to perceive, understand and
  regulate emotion (**saloveyMayer1990**). Verified (academic ability model).
- **§3 Reading Your Own Body** — Somatic markers: bodily signals that flag emotional states
  before conscious awareness (**damasio1996**). Verified.
- **§4 Using Your Mind** — Arousal reappraisal ("I'm excited") improves performance over
  trying to calm down (**brooks2014**); cognitive restructuring (**hofmann2012**). Verified.
- **§5 Using Your Body** — Slow/box breathing reduces physiological arousal (**balban2023**).
  Verified.
- **§6 Sleep, Food, Water** — Sleep consolidates learning (**diekelmann2010**). Verified;
  loose "brain fog/clearance" phrase removed.
- **§7 Putting It All Together** — Timeline/application; no new empirical claim.
