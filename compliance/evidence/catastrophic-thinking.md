# Evidence Dossier — Reframing Catastrophic Thoughts

**Module:** `reframing-catastrophic-thoughts-protocol` (`components/CatastrophicThinkingModule.tsx`)
**Review date:** 2026-06-23
**Reviewer:** Pre-accreditation literature review (DCU / Brian MacCraith)
**Governing rule:** Only state/advise what peer-reviewed literature supports; every DOI
verified via CrossRef. References surface via inline `Cite` markers + the module-wide
**References** button; data in `data/references/catastrophicThinking.ts`.

**Reviewer note.** A clinical-psychology toolkit module built on three solid evidence
bases: acute stress impairing hippocampal memory retrieval (the exam "blank"; Lupien et
al. 2009); cognitive behavioural therapy and its techniques — thought records / cognitive
restructuring, decatastrophizing, and graded exposure — whose efficacy is established in a
review of meta-analyses (Hofmann et al. 2012); and Acceptance and Commitment Therapy with
its cognitive-defusion process, the "Passengers on the Bus" exercise (Hayes et al. 2006).
**No content cut.** The student-facing claims are appropriately scoped (these are
self-help framings of clinical techniques, not therapy); "the only way to break this" for
exposure was softened to "a well-evidenced way." The Thought Record, Downward Arrow,
Exposure Hierarchy and Passengers-on-the-Bus widgets are interactive practice tools.

## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 The Catastrophe Machine** — Catastrophizing (rumination, magnification,
  helplessness) as a cognitive pattern. Descriptive framing of a CBT target; the treatment
  evidence is cited at §3–§6.
- **§2 Why You Go "Blank"** — Acute stress floods cortisol, impairing hippocampal memory
  retrieval and prefrontal function ("you've lost the password, not the file")
  (**lupien2009**). Verified.
- **§3 Challenging Your Thoughts (CBT)** — CBT is among the most effective, best-evidenced
  approaches for anxiety (**hofmann2012**). Verified.
- **§4 The Thought Record** — Cognitive restructuring via a structured thought record is a
  core CBT technique (**hofmann2012**). Verified.
- **§5 Decatastrophizing** — The Downward Arrow / "and then what?" technique (CBT)
  (**hofmann2012**). Verified.
- **§6 Facing the Fear** — Graded exposure breaks the fear–avoidance cycle (**hofmann2012**).
  Verified; "the only way" softened to "a well-evidenced way."
- **§7 Stepping Back (ACT)** — Cognitive defusion in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy
  (**hayes2006**); the Passengers-on-the-Bus metaphor is a classic ACT exercise. Verified.
- **§8 The Action Plan** — Synthesis/application; no new empirical claim.
