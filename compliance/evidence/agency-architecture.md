# Evidence Dossier — Controlling the Controllables

**Module:** `agency-architecture-protocol` (`components/AgencyArchitectureModule.tsx`)
**Review date:** 2026-06-23
**Reviewer:** Pre-accreditation literature review (DCU / Brian MacCraith)
**Governing rule:** Only state/advise what peer-reviewed literature supports; every DOI
verified via CrossRef. References surface via inline `Cite` markers + the module-wide
**References** button; data in `data/references/agencyArchitecture.ts`.

**Reviewer note.** A clean attribution-theory module. The three dimensions
(locus / stability / controllability) are Weiner's attributional theory of achievement
motivation (Weiner 1985); the "Internal-Stable-Uncontrollable" pessimistic pattern and
learned helplessness are the reformulated helplessness model (Abramson, Seligman &
Teasdale 1978); and attributional retraining — teaching students to re-attribute setbacks
to changeable causes — has a classic grade-improvement evidence base (Wilson & Linville
1982). **No content cut.** Two phrasings tightened to match the evidence: §1 "the research
is clear: students who focus on what they control … are more resilient and academically
successful" → "those who attribute outcomes to causes they can act on tend to be more
resilient and motivated"; §4 "students who practise this … do better in their exams" →
"in studies, students taught to re-attribute setbacks to changeable causes improved their
grades." The "Dichotomy of Control" is a Stoic principle (Epictetus), framed as such, and
carries no peer-reviewed citation. The Attribution Mapper / Sorter / Reframe widgets are
interactive exercises.

## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 The Story of Failure** — Causal attributions for outcomes shape motivation and
  resilience (**weiner1985**); the "dichotomy of control" framing is Stoic (no journal
  citation). Verified; the achievement claim tightened.
- **§2 The Three Dimensions** — Locus, stability, controllability — Weiner's three causal
  dimensions (**weiner1985**). Verified.
- **§3 The Pessimist's Script** — Attributing failure to internal, stable, uncontrollable
  causes drives learned helplessness (**abramson1978**). Verified.
- **§4 The Agency Re-Write** — Attributional retraining toward internal/unstable/
  controllable causes improves resilience and grades (**wilsonLinville1982**). Verified;
  the outcome claim tightened to "in studies … improved their grades."
- **§5 Your New OS** — Synthesis/habit; no new empirical claim.
