## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 The Great Deception** — We mistake *recognition* for *recall*, producing
  overconfident, miscalibrated judgements of learning. **koriat2005.** Verified.
- **§2 The Passive Traps** — Highlighting/re-reading/passive review are low-utility and
  feel effective without building durable memory (**dunlosky2013**); memory of new
  material fades quickly without active effort (**murre2015**). Verified.
- **§3 The Antidote** — Desirable difficulties: harder, effortful study aids long-term
  retention (**sb2015**); active recall / retrieval practice strengthens memory
  (**rk2006**). Verified.
- **§4 The Strategic Toolkit** — Spacing (**cepeda2006**) and interleaving
  (**rohrer2015**) as the partners to retrieval practice. The Retrospective Revision
  Timetable is a study-management heuristic operationalising these. Verified.
- **§5 The Explain-It Test** — Self-explanation / explaining simply surfaces knowledge
  gaps (**dunlosky2013**, self-explanation); "blind practice" is practice testing
  (**rk2006**). Verified.
- **§6 Bulletproof Knowledge** — Retrieval/effortful practice produces durable, flexible
  knowledge that transfers to novel problems (**butler2010**); genuine mastery via
  low-stakes retrieval reduces test anxiety (**agarwal2014**). Verified.
