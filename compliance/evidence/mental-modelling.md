## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 The Mind's Eye** — Spatial ability decomposes into distinct sub-skills (spatial
  visualisation, mental rotation, spatial orientation/perception). **linn1985** (factor
  categories from meta-analysis); **shepard1971** (the classic mental-rotation paradigm).
  Verified.
- **§2 The Modelling Cycle** — A decompose → construct → mentally test → draw → check
  loop; presented as a practical process. Aligns with the visualisation literature; no
  separate empirical claim beyond §1.
- **§3 The "Glass Box" (DCG)** — Orthographic-projection mental model + "slicing" for
  interpenetration. Domain (DCG) technique grounded in the spatial-visualisation skills
  cited in §1.
- **§4 The "Mental Movie" (Eng)** — Dynamic visualisation of mechanisms and crystal
  structures (FCC/BCC). Domain application of mental simulation; subject content (SEC).
- **§5 The Procedural Trap** — Procedural skill without conceptual understanding is
  fragile and fails to transfer to novel problems. **rittlejohnson2001** (conceptual vs
  procedural knowledge). Verified. (The "examiners report conceptual errors every year"
  claim references SEC Chief Examiner reports — exam-specific, not a psych claim.)
- **§6 The Training Plan** — Spatial skill is malleable and improves with training, not a
  fixed talent (**uttal2013**, meta-analysis of training studies); the hands-on →
  predict-and-check → pure-imagination progression follows engineering spatial-training
  research (**sorby2009**). Verified.
