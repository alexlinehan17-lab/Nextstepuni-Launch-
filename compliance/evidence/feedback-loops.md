## Claim-by-claim record

- **§1 Not All Study Is Equal** — Skill comes from focused/deliberate practice, not
  passive repetition (**ericsson1993**); the essential ingredient is a feedback loop
  comparing work against a correct standard (**hattie2007**). Verified.
- **§2 Two Rules** — (a) Attending to and correcting errors drives learning
  (**metcalfe2017**); (b) doing and checking simultaneously overloads limited working
  memory, so separate them (**sweller1988**, cognitive load). Verified.
- **§3 The Maths Loop** — Split-page try-then-check with written "why it differed" is
  self-explanation, which improves understanding of worked examples (**chi1989**).
  Verified. (The Split-Page Simulator is an illustrative exercise.)
- **§4 The Language Loop** — Back-translation is a practical self-feedback technique
  (translate, delay, back-translate, compare). It operationalises the feedback principle
  (**hattie2007**); no separate empirical claim is made, so no additional citation.
- **§5 The Writing Loop** — Self-assessing an essay against explicit criteria (the SEC
  PCLM marking criteria) is criterion-referenced self-feedback (**hattie2007**). The
  PCLM criteria themselves are exam-specific (SEC), not a psychological claim. Verified.
- **§6 Build Your Engine** — Practical call to action; no claim to verify.
