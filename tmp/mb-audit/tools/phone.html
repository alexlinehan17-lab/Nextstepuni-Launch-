Section 3Desktop versus phone

Nothing is broken on a phone: none of the 517 cards overflowed sideways and no picture failed to load. The phone problems are about size and length, and they are the same few patterns in every subject, so they are best fixed once in the session screen.

1. A picture of the paper that reads on desktop and not on a phone

The app fits each picture to the width of the screen. On desktop the column is about 500px wide. On a phone it is about 330px, and the pictures are 1,000 to 1,600px wide, so they are drawn at 20% to 35% of their size. Trees of the same age, size and type are growing at the same rate in a forest. The increase in the radius of the trunks each year, in cm, over a 10 year period is modelled by
I(t) = 1·5 + sin(πt/5)
where 1 ≤ t ≤ 10 and t ∈ ℕ is measured in years.
Show that I(6) < I(5)
and explain what this means in the context of the growth of a tree.
See it as printed on the paper

How to fix it. On Maths cards the typed wording already exists in the card's data. On a phone, show the typed question first and put the picture one tap away as “See it as printed”; keep the picture first on desktop, where it works. Where a question cannot be typed (a drawing, a graph, a score), cut the picture so it is no wider than it needs to be, stack a wide picture as two rows, and let it be dragged sideways at a readable size instead of shrinking it to fit. A simple rule for the build: any picture that would be drawn below about 45% of its size on a 390px screen must be re-cut or typed. This affects Maths, Construction Studies, DCG, Music, Computer Science, Technology, Engineering, Chemistry and Japanese most.

2. The attached page is a whole A4 sheet squeezed onto a phone

The page reader fits a full exam page to the screen, so on a phone the student sees a tiny page, most of which is other questions. Cropping the page to the question fixes the “other questions” problem and the size problem at the same time. 3. Cards that run to many screens

A typical card is about two and a half phone screens after the reveal. The longest sampled cards in Home Economics, Japanese and Russian run to about ten, Politics and Society to eight, Irish and German to about six. The cause is almost always repetition: the same pool of answers or the same band descriptions printed under every point, or a whole case study pasted above a one-line question. How to fix it. Print a shared pool or rubric once and let each point refer to it; fold case studies and long notes behind “Show the full text”; keep the answer box within one scroll of the question.

4. Tables and lists typed as a single line

A table that was typed out as text reads badly on desktop and worse on a phone, where it wraps mid-row. The same goes for multiple-choice options and lettered parts run into the sentence.

6. Where the official scheme is clearer as a picture

You asked whether some schemes should be a screen grab of the marking scheme instead of text. Yes, for four kinds of answer, and the bank already does it well in Maths: worked calculations with fractions and powers; tables of figures (Accounting, Business break-even, LCVP); drawn answers (DCG, Construction Studies sketches, Economics and Geography diagrams, Chemistry structures); and grade tables (Irish, Politics, Religious Education). The rule to hold to is the same as for questions: the picture must be cut to this part only, must sit inside the scheme panel and not under the question, and must pass the phone-size rule above.
