01 · What we found

The app is running two design languages at once.

Login, onboarding, the sidebar and the newer screens use the Kobra kit: hairlines, soft depth, an ink button. Most modules, older tools and pop-ups still use the earlier chunky style: 2px black borders, hard offset shadows, orange pill buttons. Every screen picked one, or mixed them. That is why it feels like different apps.

The good news: the right language already exists in the product. Login and onboarding are the standard you named, and they are built from tokens and components the rest of the app can share. This is a consolidation, not a reinvention.

02 · Three directions

How much Kobra, and how much character?

The same three screens (Home, a Future Finder question, My Progress) drawn three ways. The layouts are identical on purpose, so what you are comparing is only the language. Use Light / Dark at the top to switch every phone.

Direction 1

Instrument

Kobra taken literally. One sans face, no serif, sentence-case labels, grey canvas with white panels, tight 8–10px corners, orange almost absent. Reads as a precise professional tool.

Brand languageTechnical and neutral. Could be a finance or developer product.
ColourBlack, white, greys. Orange only on a progress line.
WarmthLowest. Characters would feel out of place.
RiskClinical for 16–18 year olds; loses what is already distinctive about the serif and the crew.
Direction 2 · Recommended

Paper & Ink

Kobra's structure with our voice. White paper, hairline cards, a mono eyebrow over a serif title, the ink button with the orange arrow, large plain numerals, and one orange signal per screen. It is the language of your login and onboarding, extended to everything.

Brand languageConsidered, calm, a little editorial. A well-made notebook with instruments in it.
ColourInk and paper, plus one orange used as a signal: the selected thing, the progress, the arrow.
WarmthFrom the serif, warm greys, characters at intros and empty states, and full-colour moment screens.
RiskLowest. About half the app is already here; the rest converges on it.
Direction 3

Block

The earlier house style, systematised instead of retired. 2px ink borders, hard offset shadows, orange pill buttons, bold serif. Loud, playful, unmistakably a student product.

Brand languageEnergetic and chunky. Closer to a game or a zine than to Kobra.
ColourOrange everywhere it can be: buttons, numerals, active tab.
WarmthHighest energy, but every element shouts, so nothing stands out.
RiskMoves away from Kobra, and would mean rebuilding login, onboarding and the sidebar backwards.

Why Paper & Ink

It is the one you already chose. You said login and onboarding are the bar. Both are Paper & Ink. Direction 1 would strip their serif and warmth; Direction 3 would undo them.

It keeps Kobra where Kobra is strongest (structure, restraint, motion) without importing the part that does not suit students, which is the complete absence of colour and voice.

It is recognisable from a single cropped corner. Mono eyebrow, serif title, hairline, ink button with an orange arrow. No other study app has that combination.

It is the cheapest route to consistency. The tokens, the button, the back button and the app bar already exist as shared components. The work is moving every screen onto them and deleting the alternatives.

The same system at three sizes

Tokens and components never change between devices. The arrangement does: the tab bar becomes the sidebar, the bottom button moves into the content, sheets become popovers, one column becomes list and detail.

03 · The system

The rules behind Paper & Ink.

This is now written into the repo as a reusable skill (nsu-design) that I load before any UI work. These are its main rules.

What makes it ours

Paper and ink, one orangeOrange is a signal, never decoration. At most one orange fill in view.
The ink button, orange arrowOne primary action per screen, and it always looks like this.
Mono eyebrow, serif title"START WITH YOU" then "Future Finder". That pair is the identity of every page. Nothing sits above it.
Hairlines, not boxes1px warm-grey lines carry structure. Shadows only for things that float.
Big honest numeralsProgress is a large plain number, as in onboarding.
Moment surfacesFull-bleed orange or ink screens, kept for welcomes, milestones and session complete.
Characters are guestsSmall and slightly tilted at intros, empty states and celebrations. Never between the student and the task.
Motion that connectsThings slide, draw and settle. Nothing pops or bounces.

A button never gets bigger to look more important; it moves up a level. That is the answer to the Practice button. And arrows mean exactly two things: a chevron on a row opens the next level, and the arrow inside a primary button advances. No decorative arrows, no arrows in three colours.

Removed everywhere: in-page "← All subjects" links (15 files), "Back to Journey Results" banners, the wordmark acting as a back button, and any screen showing Back and Close together.

Safe areas: three owners, no exceptions

The Admin log-out, the pause-screen sound control and the Island Mode close button all fail for the same reason: a control positioned by hand in the top corner, ignoring the iPhone's safe area. Patching each one would leave the next screen to make the same mistake.

The rule instead: only three components may touch the top edge — the app bar, the sheet frame, and the session frame. Controls live in that frame's header row, which sits below the Dynamic Island by construction. A test will fail the build if a new screen pins anything to the top itself.

This is the pause screen under that rule: Leave and Sound share one row beneath the island, and the primary action is at the bottom where a thumb reaches it.

Motion

Kobra's site and the emilkowalski skills use the same curves, so the motion rules have one source: fast, decelerating, never bouncy. Press feedback 120ms, state changes 150ms, sheets 400ms on the drawer curve, exits faster than entrances, nothing scaling from zero, and no animation at all on things done hundreds of times a day.

Named moves so the same event always looks the same: a slide highlight (tap Week / Month / Year on any phone here), a selection dot (tap an answer in Future Finder below), a sheet rise (tap "All subjects" on My Progress below), and an attention ring (tap Start on the session demo below).

04 · Screen concepts

Where you need to choose.

All drawn in Paper & Ink. Each has a recommendation; tell me if you prefer another.

A · Start Session

The button tells you what is missing, then takes you there.

Try it: tap the button before choosing anything. Then choose a subject and tap again.

Today the button is greyed out and dead, with one line of 12px grey text below the card. Here it is never dead:

1. Its label always names the next missing thing: "Choose a subject to start", then "Choose how you will study", then "Choose how long".
2. Tapping it scrolls to that control, rings it once in orange, and shows a one-line reason beside it. On the phone it also gives a short warning tap.
3. When everything is chosen it becomes the ink button: "Start session →".

The same pattern becomes the rule for every button in the app that is waiting on the student.

F · Mark Bank

From one endless page to three short ones.

Today a single page holds the intro, subject and level pickers, search, notes, an index strip, every topic in the strand and a practice card. For Politics and Society that is about four screens of scrolling; Biology has over 3,000 questions behind it. The fix is structural.

Subject and level move into the app bar's subtitle ("Politics and Society · Higher"), changed from a sheet, so they stop taking a third of the first screen. Saved cards get their own tab. The explanatory notes move behind an info button.

G · My Progress

The spacing rule, and a picker that works.

Why the dropdown does nothing today: the section menu closes itself whenever it loses focus. iPhones do not give a button focus when you tap it, so the menu shuts before your tap registers. That is a code bug with a known fix, and I will fix it regardless of direction.

Layout: the five sections ("Overview 1 / 5") become a row of chips you can see and swipe, instead of a hidden menu. Sections are 32 apart, each with one label, and "The work adds up" is three numerals in a single card.

Progress is a top-level tab, so it has no Back button.

07 · Plan

System first, then screens.

Each phase leaves the app shippable. Phases 1 and 2 change almost nothing you can see, but they are what stop the next twenty fixes becoming twenty new inconsistencies.

1

Foundations

Lock the tokens and build the handful of shared parts every later phase uses.

  • Four dark surfaces; every feature palette points at them
  • Spacing, radius and motion scales in the Tailwind config
  • One app bar, one sheet (bottom sheet on phones), one session frame, one picker
  • Button levels, including the "not ready" state
  • Build checks: nothing new pinned to the top edge, no new dark greys, no second Back
2

Safe areas and navigation, everywhere

Fixes your three Dynamic Island problems and the twelve like them, in one sweep.

  • All 15 top-edge sites moved onto the three frames
  • One Back per screen; text back links and banners removed; Back goes up, not forward
  • Black Launchpad card removed; tool identity only in the app bar
  • All phone pop-ups become sheets
3

The screens you named

  • My Progress (pickers fixed, new spacing)
  • Start Session interaction, study timer, pause screen
  • Future Finder header and selection animation
  • Paper Trails home and top bar
  • Journey bar and Island Mode intro
  • Today's Plan in dark mode
4

Mark Bank

The Today / Browse / Saved structure, with topic screens. The largest single piece of work.

5

Counsellor and Admin on phones

Shared app bar and tab bar, tables as lists, every section reachable, dark mode for the Admin panels.

6

The long tail

  • Dark-mode sweep: the remaining greys and the ~60 white-only components
  • Older tools and ~70 module files moved off the chunky style
  • One icon set
  • iPad pass: list and detail layouts
