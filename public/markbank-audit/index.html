Mark Bank audit:
how every subject asks, attaches and marks

A review of how questions, their attachments and their marking schemes look across all 55 Mark Bank subjects, on desktop and on a phone. Section 1 shows the subjects that work. Section 2 shows the subjects that need work, with the evidence and the fix for each. A final part covers desktop versus phone and the order to fix things in.

The short version

No subject is completely clean, but the core idea works: where a card has a short typed question and one marking point per row with its marks, it looks good on both screens. The problems come from four places, and the first one is the one you raised.

1Cards do show other questions, and it comes from the attached page

61% of cards (48,343) attach pages from the exam paper. Almost all of them attach the whole printed page, not the question. Only Applied Maths and Music crop to the question. Checked against real papers, a different numbered question is printed on the attached page for about 68% of Construction Studies cards, 49% of Economics, 46% of Business, 37% of Accounting, 31% of Home Economics, 20% of Agricultural Science and 18% of Physical Education. These are low estimates. A student opening the page for an Economics question on credit creation sees all of the national income question first. The typed question and the marking scheme themselves were limited to the right question in almost every card; the leak is the attachment.

2Two subjects have attachments that do not open at all

Home Economics (1,541 cards) and Early Modern History (1,170 cards) show “That page did not load” because the app looks in the wrong storage folder. Each is a small, contained fix. Separately, every listening recording failed to play in my test set-up; I could not tell whether that is my set-up or the app, so it needs one check on the live site.

3Questions shown as a picture of the paper cannot be read on a phone

Every Construction Studies and DCG question, 86% of Maths, about 64% of Computer Science and Music, and 57% of Technology questions are a picture of the printed paper. On desktop that is the best-looking format in the bank. On a 390px phone the same picture is shown at a fifth to a third of its size, so the print is about 5 to 7 pixels high and the student has to tap Enlarge to read the question at all.

4Marking schemes lose their shape when they become text

Alternatives are joined with slashes in one tick box (920 Agricultural Science, 537 Biology, 529 Chemistry and 214 Physics cards). Tables are run into one line with pipe characters (253 of 518 LCVP cards). The same long pool of answers is repeated under every point. Notes written for whoever built the card (“Source discrepancy…”, “no models are invented”, “approved on 18 November 2026”) are printed to the student. In the essay subjects the reveal often has no answer content at all, only rows of mark buttons.

5Some questions point at things the card does not show

“Examine the graph above”, “fill in the grid below”, “the underlined term”, “the phrases in the box” appear on cards where the graph, grid, underline or box is missing or sits only behind the attachment button. Geography, German, Applied Maths and the cloze (fill-the-gap) questions in French, Portuguese and Economics are the main cases.

How this was done

All 78,787 cards in the 95 decks were exported from the code and checked by script: what each card attaches, how its text is formed, and how large its pictures are. For the “other questions” check, 16 subjects were compared against about 150 real exam papers downloaded from the app's own storage. Then 519 cards were chosen across all 55 subjects (12 for each larger subject, 5 to 8 for the smaller languages), mixing ordinary cards with ones the scripts flagged. Each was opened in the app's real session screen at desktop size (1280px) and phone size (390px), before and after the reveal, with its attached page; 517 loaded. Every screenshot was read card by card and scored from 1 to 5 for the question, the attachments, the scheme, the phone view and the desktop view. The serious findings were then re-checked by hand, which removed three that were only slow loading.

Limits: 517 cards is a sample, so a subject's score describes what the sample shows, and the counts quoted from the full bank are the firmer numbers. Audio could not be listened to. Scheme correctness was judged by whether the scheme plainly answers the question on the card, not re-marked against the SEC documents line by line.

Section 1Subjects that work

These 22 subjects ask a clear question, show the right marking scheme for it, and attach nothing from another topic in the cards sampled. None is perfect: each has a short list of small fixes, set out under its examples. Scores are out of 5 from a card-by-card look at the sampled cards.

Physics

4,059 cards · HL/OL · 2010–2026

Question 3/5Attachments 3/5Scheme 4/5Phone 4/5Desktop 4/5

Physics is one of the cleaner subjects: questions are typed at full size, and the scheme is a short tick-list of marking points with marks that reads the same on phone and desktop. The main fault is in the question stems: data tables are flattened into a run of numbers ('s (cm) 65 70 75 80 85 90 t (s) 0.36 0.38...'), a whole reading passage is poured into one paragraph with its headings merged in, and stems refer to a diagram or 'your graph' that is not on the card. Several scheme points are too terse to be an answer ('notation', 'force per', 'unit charge', 'one other detail', 'object') and carry app explanations of examiner shorthand instead of plain wording. A few attachments are wrong or unhelpful: one card shows a marking-scheme crop with answers to other parts in the question area, one opens an attached page on a different question, and one prints a junk 'Also on the diagram' box that repeats the stem twice.

1% of cards attach exam pages (whole printed pages, not cropped) · 214 cards have slash-joined answer lists · 188 questions run past 900 characters of text.

How it looks

2023 HL Q14(d)(iv) — Deferred sitting · 7 marks · topic: 3.2 Modelling electric fields
Clear typed question, relevant photo, and a three-line scheme with a clean formula image and marks

2022 HL Q14(a)(iv) · 12 marks · topic: 1.1 Particle motion in a straight line
Complete self-contained question and four scheme points with values and marks; identical on phone

Small fixes still worth making
  • Data tables flattened into a run of numbers. Render the data as a real table (two rows for cards 4 and 5, a label/value list for card 3), or show a crop of the table from the paper.
  • Attached page shows a different question with the same wording. Remove the attachment, or crop it to Q2.
  • Reading passage shown as one wall of text. Keep headings and paragraph breaks, collapse the passage behind 'Read the passage' on the phone, and put the question first when it can be answered without it.
  • Stem refers to something not on the card. Add the apparatus figure or drop the sentence; add a line of context naming the graph from the earlier part; finish the sentence with a full stop.

Biology

6,601 cards · HL/OL · 2010–2026

Question 4/5Attachments 4/5Scheme 3/5Phone 3/5Desktop 4/5

Most biology cards are clean: a short typed question, a correctly chosen and tightly cropped diagram, and a tick-list scheme that plainly answers the part asked. The recurring fault is examiner mark shorthand leaking into a grey note under the scheme (e.g. '(6(3) + 2)', '(7+7+2+4(1))', '(3(4) + 3(3) + 3(2))') which describes the WHOLE question's arithmetic, not this card, and on those cards the tick points carry no marks at all. Whole short-question cards (Q1-style) flatten the (a)/(i)/(ii) hierarchy so sub-parts sit further left than their parents, and the scheme lists every alternative answer as its own tick box. One card (2020 OL Q5(d)) shows the question only as a crop of the full Q5 grid, which is tiny on a phone and whose scheme gives one answer with no hint which box it belongs to. Genetics notation loses its superscripts (XNXn, XnY_).

537 cards have slash-joined answer lists · 96 questions run past 900 characters of text.

How it looks

2025 HL Q5(a) — Deferred sitting · 3 marks · topic: 2.4 Response
Clear typed question, large legible ear diagram, three labelled 1-mark answers A/B/C.

2024 HL Q11(b)(iv) · 12 marks · topic: 2.5 Reproduction
One-line question and a clean 'Any 4 of these — 3 marks each' point list.

Small fixes still worth making
  • Whole-question mark shorthand shown under single-part schemes. Translate the shorthand into plain words scoped to this part ('first 3 correct points 4 marks each, next 3 worth 3, ...') or put a mark chip on each point; never print the raw bracket sum, and never print the whole question's sum on a part card.
  • Question shown only as a tiny crop of the whole Q5 grid. Type the instruction and the word list as text, crop only the cell picture for (d) (or make the card the whole Q5 with a seven-row answer list '(a) Cheek cell ... (g) Neuron'), and fix the mark total to match.
  • Part hierarchy inverted and alternatives listed as separate ticks on whole-question cards. Indent (i)/(ii) under their letter; group the scheme by part with one row per part: '(c) Any one: keratin / myosin / collagen — 3m'.
  • Genotype notation flattened. Render with real superscripts (X^N X^n, X^n Y) or show a crop of the official scheme's cross diagram.

Technology

2,340 cards · HL/OL · 2010–2026

Question 4/5Attachments 4/5Scheme 3/5Phone 4/5Desktop 4/5

Technology is one of the cleaner subjects: stems are typed in a smaller sans face, the asked part in large serif with (i)/(ii)/(iii) set as a proper list, figures are tight crops of the one drawing needed, and nothing from other questions leaks in. The faults are mostly in how the scheme is chopped up: multi-part cards list every acceptable answer as a separate tick row with no (i)/(ii) headings and no marks per part, several 'two reasons / three elements' answers are squeezed into one comma-joined row worth all the marks, and scheme shorthand ('etc.', slashes, '1.' '2.' prefixes) is left raw. Where the answer is a drawing, the tick row just repeats the question plus an internal note ('The scheme answers this part with a drawing its text cannot carry; its own print is shown with the answer') and the drawing sits in the question column. A few stems have flattened bullet lists or wording that contradicts the layout ('URL shown below' when it is above).

57% of questions are shown as a picture of the paper.

How it looks

2021 OL Section C Q3(b)(iii) · 6 marks · topic: O3 Information and communications technology
Stem, question and three plain scheme statements; reads cleanly on phone and desktop.

2026 HL Section A Q3 · 6 marks · topic: C7 Energy, electricity and electronics
Large legible circuit, one-line question, worked calculation with the final 8 V shown.

Small fixes still worth making
  • Multi-part schemes are one flat list with no part headings or marks. Group the rows under '(i) - x marks' / '(ii) - y marks' headings, with 'Any one of these' sub-groups for advantages, disadvantages, risks and reductions, as the Engineering cards do with 'One of these - 4 marks'.
  • Several required answers squeezed into one comma-joined row. One row per element or reason ('https:// - Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure'), under 'Any three - 8 marks'; a two-column term/meaning layout suits the URL card.
  • Drawing answers: tick row repeats the question plus an internal note. Put the scheme drawing inside the scheme panel under 'Model answer', replace the note with tickable features (relay added; mains supply and fan motor on relay contacts / each decision and process box in order), and drop the internal wording.
  • Bullet list flattened into the stem paragraph. Render the bullets as a real list, one step per line.

Engineering

2,269 cards · HL/OL · 2010–2026

Question 4/5Attachments 3/5Scheme 3/5Phone 4/5Desktop 4/5

Engineering cards are far cleaner than the drawing subjects: the asked part is typed in large serif text, figures are tight crops of just the relevant drawing, and short recall questions (cutting fluids, transformer, up-cut/down-cut milling) look deliberate on both phone and desktop. The main faults are in the scheme text: internal authoring notes are printed to the student ('The paper sets two (c)s either side of an OR ... the scheme prices their romans alike ... so this card holds this part of both branches'), option rows glue a heading onto its sentence ('Compression moulding A measured amount...', 'Advantages of photovoltaic (PV) panels Electricity produced...'), and one row is cut off mid-word. Either/or questions are run together in one paragraph with a bare 'OR'. One figure (2022 Q3(b)(iii) stress-strain graph) has lost its axis values and curve labels, so the calculation cannot be done from the card, and where the scheme is a diagram it appears in the question column while the tick row just repeats the question.

39% of questions are shown as a picture of the paper.

How it looks

2014 HL Q7(b)(ii) · 6 marks · topic: P11 Machining and the lathe
One-line question, six short tickable functions; clean on phone.

2019 HL Q7(b)(i) · 6 marks · topic: P11 Machining and the lathe
Typed question, official up-cut/down-cut diagram and two clear explanatory rows.

Small fixes still worth making
  • Internal authoring notes shown to the student in the scheme. Remove these notes from student view. If guidance is needed, one plain line above the routes: 'This question offered a choice. Mark only the option you answered.'
  • Stress-strain graph cropped without axis values or curve labels. Re-crop the figure from the paper to include axis titles, scale values and the A/B labels, plus the stem line that introduces the graph.
  • Heading glued to its sentence in scheme options. Render the label as a bold title on its own line (or a group heading once: 'Advantages - any one, 4 marks') and the explanation beneath it.
  • Either/or questions run together with a bare 'OR'. Mirror the scheme layout in the question: 'Option A' block, an OR divider, 'Option B' block, each with its own picture.

Computer Science

582 cards · HL/OL · 2020–2026

Question 3/5Attachments 4/5Scheme 3/5Phone 3/5Desktop 4/5

Computer Science is one of the cleaner subjects on desktop: stems are typed, code listings and tables are shown as crisp crops of the paper, and tick-list schemes (true/false, data types, wireframe elements) answer the question plainly. The main faults are in the text around them. Program output that the paper prints on separate lines is flattened into one run-on sentence, band-descriptor rubrics are printed as an unpunctuated string ('10 marks Correct response ... 8 marks Almost correct response ... 5 marks ...'), and alternative answers are joined with slashes and middle dots. Official scheme crops (worked answers, model code) are placed in the question column above the answer box rather than in the scheme, and one of them shows the answers to sibling parts. On a phone the code listings shrink badly: a 44-line program is squeezed into the column at about 5px per line.

64% of questions are shown as a picture of the paper · 56 questions run past 900 characters of text.

How it looks

2022 OL Q13(b)(i) · 4 marks · topic: 2.4 Data
Typed stem, clean crop of the true/false grid, four full-sentence answers each ending in True/False with 1 mark.

2022 HL Q13(b)(i) · 4 marks · topic: 3.1 Interactive information systems
Clear wireframe question and four short 1-mark criteria; nothing extraneous.

Small fixes still worth making
  • Expected program output flattened into the sentence. Render sample output in a monospace block with its original line breaks (or crop it from the paper like the code listing).
  • Band-descriptor rubric printed as a run-on string. Lay the bands out as rows ('10 — correct: asterisks in both places', '8 — almost correct: minor syntax error', '5 — some merit') and make each row the tappable score, replacing the bare number buttons.
  • Scheme crops sit in the question column and can show other parts' answers. Move the crop into the scheme panel under 'Official solution'. For multi-part solutions crop or highlight only the lines for this part (card 3: lines 3-5), or type the answer lines as a code block.
  • Code listings too small on a phone. Render code as real monospace text in a horizontally scrollable block, or show only the lines the part refers to (card 7 needs lines 40-44) with the full listing behind Enlarge.

Religious Education

786 cards · HL/OL · 2010–2026

Religious Education has no attachments and the questions are plain essay prompts that read clearly on phone and desktop. The best part of the scheme is the grey 'Points of reference the scheme lists' box, which gives real indicative content that answers the question asked. The weak part is the band grid under it: on newer papers each of six bands repeats the same long dot-joined descriptor in examiner shorthand ('Evidence of MC How the religious moral code... · Relevance: ... · Use of skill(s)...: ... · Factual Accuracy: little major errors(s)'), and where the scheme marks 20 x 2 the whole grid is printed twice, so a phone card runs to about three full columns (roughly 5,000px) of small grey text and up to 41 mark buttons. Band labels are inconsistent between years (three bands all called 'Weak', 'WEAK - VERY WEAK - NO GRADE' twice, capitals on one card, '20 > 17' on some and '17 - 20' on others).

longest sampled card is about 6 phone screens.

Show the criteria once as row labels and give each band only its distinguishing words (e.g. Evidence: substantial / very good / good / adequate / inadequate / very little), as a compact table; or show a crop of the official grid.   • Bands with no descriptor at all. Add the generic one-line descriptor per band used on the 2019/2021 cards (substantial / very good / good / adequate evidence...).

Spanish

Section 3Desktop versus phone

Nothing is broken on a phone: none of the 517 cards overflowed sideways and no picture failed to load. The phone problems are about size and length, and they are the same few patterns in every subject, so they are best fixed once in the session screen.

3. Cards that run to many screens

A typical card is about two and a half phone screens after the reveal. The longest sampled cards in Home Economics, Japanese and Russian run to about ten, Politics and Society to eight, Irish and German to about six. The cause is almost always repetition: the same pool of answers or the same band descriptions printed under every point, or a whole case study pasted above a one-line question. How to fix it. Print a shared pool or rubric once and let each point refer to it; fold case studies and long notes behind “Show the full text”; keep the answer box within one scroll of the question.
