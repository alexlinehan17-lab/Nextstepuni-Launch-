## Marking-grammar structure — the scale system at Foundation

Foundation uses the **same labelled-scale marking system** as Ordinary and Higher
Level, with the **same level descriptors verbatim** and the **same Full Credit −1
rule and annotation palette**. The core grammar is not Foundation-specific. What
differs is (a) the summary table drops the A scale, and (b) the intermediate mark
values on each scale ladder are different from the OL 2023 paper.

### The scale table at Foundation (p.[4])

| Scale label | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| No. of categories | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 5-mark scale | 0, 3, 5 | 0, 2, 3, 5 | 0, 2, 3, 4, 5 |
| 10-mark scale | 0, 4, 10 | 0, 4, 6, 10 | 0, 4, 5, 7, 10 |
| 15-mark scale | — | 0, 5, 9, 15 | 0, 4, 7, 8, 15 |

The prose still defines the A scale — "Scales labelled A divide candidate
responses into two categories (correct and incorrect)" (p.[4]) — but, unlike the
OL 2023 table, the Foundation summary table starts at B. The 10B scale (0, 4, 10)
IS present at Foundation and is used in the body (e.g. Q3(a)(i), Q7(a)) — a
three-category scale where the single partial rung is a low bar ("Writes any one
item", "Some work of merit").

### How the Foundation ladders differ from OL 2023 (p.[4] vs 2023 p.[28])

The scale *labels and descriptors* are identical; the *mark values on the
intermediate rungs* are set per paper and differ:

| Scale | Foundation 2025 | Ordinary 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| 5C | 0, 2, 3, 5 | 0, 3, 4, 5 |
| 10D | 0, 4, 5, 7, 10 | 0, 4, 6, 8, 10 |
| 15C | 0, 5, 9, 15 | 0, 5, 10, 15 |
| 15D | 0, 4, 7, 8, 15 | 0, 5, 8, 12, 15 |

Takeaway: don't assume a "10D" or "15C" carries the same partial-credit marks
across papers — the ladder values are paper-specific even though the scheme
grammar is shared.

### Level descriptors (verbatim, p.[4])

- **B-scales (3 categories):** "response of no substantial merit (no credit)",
  "partially correct response (partial credit)", "correct response (full credit)"
- **C-scales (4 categories)** add: "response with some merit (low partial credit)",
  "almost correct response (high partial credit)"
- **D-scales (5 categories)** add: "response about half-right (mid partial credit)"

### Full Credit −1 (p.[5])

Identical rule to OL/HL:

> "In certain cases, typically involving incorrect rounding, omission of units, a
> misreading that does not oversimplify the work, or an arithmetical error that
> does not oversimplify the work, a mark that is one mark below the full-credit
> mark may also be awarded. Such cases are denoted with a * and this level of
> credit is referred to as Full Credit −1. Thus, for example, in Scale 10C, Full
> Credit −1 of 9 marks may be awarded."

- "The only marks that may be awarded for a question are those on the scale below,
  or Full Credit −1." (p.[5])
- A rounding penalty is applied **each time it occurs**. No penalty for omitted
  units if the question specifies the unit; generally no penalty for an omitted
  euro symbol in money questions. (p.[5])
- "accept a candidate's work in one part of a question for use in subsequent parts
  of the question, unless this oversimplifies the work involved." (p.[5])

### Annotation palette (p.[6])

Same palette as OL/HL: tick = full credit, cross = 0 credit, star (*) =
rounding/unit/arithmetic error or misreading, horizontal wavy = error, **P/L/M/H**
in the right margin = partial / low / mid / high partial credit, **F*** = Full
Credit −1, **WOM** = "Work of Merit within the body of work" (nothing correct but
work of merit). The right-margin resolution rule is stated explicitly:

> "In a C scale where * and [error] and [misreading] appear in the body of the
> work, then L should be placed in the right margin. In the case of a D scale with
> the same annotations, M should be placed in the right margin." (p.[6])

### Mark allocations across the paper (observed)

- Q1–Q8: **30 marks each** (p.[7]–[14])
- Q9, Q10, Q11: **45 marks each** (p.[15]–[20])
- The observed per-question totals sum to more than 300, so the 300-mark paper
  involves question choice — the exact choice structure is **not stated in the
  marking scheme** and is not asserted here.

## Common errors / where Foundation candidates lose marks (inferred from marking notes)

The marking notes repeatedly flag the same failure modes as Full-Credit−1 or
capped-at-High-Partial, which tells you what examiners see going wrong at
Foundation:

- **Coordinate/answer-pair order.** Giving a point in the wrong order is only High
  Partial, not full: Q8(a) "Answer given as (7,4)" [correct is (4,7)] → High
  Partial Credit (p.[14]).
- **Rounding.** "Apply a * for incorrect or no rounding" → Full Credit −1 recurs
  across Q5(b) (p.[11]), Q10(b)(iii) and Q10(c)(iii) (p.[17]–[18]). Rounding is the
  single most common way a fully-worked answer drops a mark.
- **Units.** Q9(a) "Apply a * for no unit in part (iii)" → Full Credit −1 (p.[15]).
  Omitting a required unit costs one mark.
- **Right answer, wrong label.** Q2(c) "Correct figures assigned to the incorrect
  person" → Full Credit −1 (p.[8]).
- **Bar-chart width, not just height.** Q9(b)(i) "Heights all correct but one or
  more bars an incorrect width" → Full Credit −1 (p.[16]) — Foundation candidates
  are marked on bar width as well as height.
- **Failing to state the conclusion.** Q5(c) "Correctly converts one value but
  fails to conclude the shorter distance" is capped at High Partial (p.[11]); the
  final comparative statement is required for full credit.

## Strategic / structural observations

- **"Work of merit" is the universal low-bar rung.** At Foundation the lowest
  non-zero credit is almost always earned by "a relevant formula", "some relevant
  multiplication/addition", or "identifies [a key quantity]" — e.g. Q1(b/c), Q2(b),
  Q4(b), Q10(a). Writing down a correct relevant formula reliably earns Low Partial
  Credit even with no further progress.
- **Two-part items marked on one D scale** — the recurring Foundation pattern
  (Q2(a), Q3(a)(ii)+(iii), Q4(b/c), Q7(c/d), Q8(b), Q10(b), Q11(a/c)): Low = work of
  merit in one part; Mid = one part fully correct OR work of merit in both; High =
  one part correct + work of merit in the other. Getting one whole sub-part right is
  worth more than scattering partial work across both.
- **Answer-without-work is sometimes explicitly credited** — e.g. Q5(c) "Correct
  answer with incorrect or no calculations" earns Low Partial (p.[11]) — but the
  general default (as at OL) is that unsupported answers get the lowest non-zero
  rung.
