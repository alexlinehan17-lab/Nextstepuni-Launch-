## The scale system (structure of the marking scheme, p.[28])

Candidate responses are marked on labelled scales:

| Scale label | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. of categories | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 5-mark scale | — | 0, 3, 5 | 0, 3, 4, 5 | 0, 2, 3, 4, 5 |
| 10-mark scale | — | — | 0, 4, 6, 10 | 0, 4, 6, 8, 10 |
| 15-mark scale | — | — | 0, 5, 10, 15 | 0, 5, 8, 12, 15 |

Credit-level descriptors (verbatim, p.[28]):

- B-scales: "response of no substantial merit (no credit)", "partially correct
  response (partial credit)", "correct response (full credit)"
- C-scales add: "response with some merit (low partial credit)", "almost correct
  response (high partial credit)"
- D-scales add: "response about half-right (mid partial credit)"

## The Full Credit −1 rule (p.[29])

> "In certain cases, typically involving incorrect rounding, omission of units, a
> misreading that does not oversimplify the work, or an arithmetical error that
> does not oversimplify the work, a mark that is one mark below the full-credit
> mark may also be awarded. Such cases are denoted with a * and this level of
> credit is referred to as Full Credit −1."

- A rounding penalty is applied each time it occurs.
- No penalty for omitted units if the question specifies the unit; generally no
  penalty for an omitted euro symbol in money questions.

## Work-of-merit and supporting-work rules (p.[29])

- "a correct relevant formula written is regarded as Work of merit, award the
  lowest non-zero level of credit"
- "Unless otherwise specified, an answer without sufficient supporting work is
  generally awarded the lowest non-zero level of credit" — note that individual
  questions can override this: several marking notes state "Full Credit: correct
  answer without supporting work" (e.g. Q1(a), Q3(a)).
- "accept a candidate's work in one part of a question for use in subsequent
  parts of the question, unless this oversimplifies the work involved."

## Examiner annotation palette (p.[30])

Right-margin letters record the credit level: **P** (partial), **L** (low
partial), **M** (mid partial), **H** (high partial), **F\*** (Full Credit −1);
tick = full credit for the work, cross = 0 credit, * = rounding/unit/arithmetic
error or misreading, "wom" = work of merit.

## How the scales are applied — recurring marking-note patterns

- **"Work of merit" is the universal Low Partial Credit descriptor** — e.g. a
  relevant formula written down, some correct terms multiplied, some
  substitution attempted (Q1(a), Q3(a)).
- **High Partial Credit is typically "correct formula fully substituted" or
  "error(s) in substitution but finishes correctly"** (Q1(a)(i)/(ii), Q3(a)).
- **Multi-step questions enumerate steps and map them to the ladder** — Q2(b)
  (simultaneous line/circle, Scale 15D) lists four solution steps; Low = some
  work of merit, Mid = two steps correct, High = three steps correct.
- **Missing conclusion → Full Credit −1** — Q2(a)(ii) "show the point is on the
  circle": "Full Credit (−1): No conclusion in (ii)". The calculation alone,
  without the "⟹ on the circle" statement, drops a mark.
- **Answer-format slips → Full Credit −1** — Q1(b): line equation "answer not in
  required format" is High Partial; Q2(a)(iii) "answers not given as a pair of
  coordinates" is Full Credit −1.
- **Two-part items marked on one D scale** — (i) & (ii) combined: Low = work of
  merit in one part, Mid = work of merit in both / one part correct, High = one
  part correct + work of merit in the other (Q1(c), Q2(a)).
