**Note to teachers and students on the use of published marking schemes**

Marking schemes published by the State Examinations Commission are not intended to be standalone
documents. They are an essential resource for examiners who receive training in the correct
interpretation and application of the scheme. This training involves, among other things, marking
samples of student work and discussing the marks awarded, so as to clarify the correct application
of the scheme. The work of examiners is subsequently monitored by Advising Examiners to ensure
consistent and accurate application of the marking scheme. This process is overseen by the Chief
Examiner, usually assisted by a Chief Advising Examiner. The Chief Examiner is the final authority
regarding whether or not the marking scheme has been correctly applied to any piece of candidate
work.

Marking schemes are working documents. While a draft marking scheme is prepared in advance of the
examination, the scheme is not finalised until examiners have applied it to candidates' work and
the feedback from all examiners has been collated and considered in light of the full range of
responses of candidates, the overall level of difficulty of the examination and the need to
maintain consistency in standards from year to year. This published document contains the
finalised scheme, as it was applied to all candidates' work.

In the case of marking schemes that include model solutions or answers, it should be noted that
these are not intended to be exhaustive. Variations and alternatives may also be acceptable.
Examiners must consider all answers on their merits, and will have consulted with their Advising
Examiners when in doubt.

**Future Marking Schemes.** Assumptions about future marking schemes on the basis of past schemes
should be avoided. While the underlying assessment principles remain the same, the details of the
marking of a particular type of question may change in the context of the contribution of that
question to the overall examination in a given year. The Chief Examiner in any given year has the
responsibility to determine how best to ensure the fair and accurate assessment of candidates'
work and to ensure consistency in the standard of the assessment from year to year. Accordingly,
aspects of the structure, detail and application of the marking scheme for a particular
examination are subject to change from one year to the next without notice.

## Section C – 150 marks

**Marking the discursive essay:**

1. Read the entire essay without allocating any marks.
2. Mark the essay using the marking criteria and total the marks.
3. Review total mark awarded using the grade band descriptors.
4. To finalise the total mark review again using the criteria.

### DISCURSIVE ESSAY — marking criteria (max 50 marks per essay)

Each criterion graded across four bands: **Very good / Good / Fair / Weak.**

| Criterion | Max | Very good | Good | Fair | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction (I)** | 10 | Directly addresses and contextualises the issue. **9–10** | Issue reasonably addressed with limited contextualisation. **7–8** | Issue vaguely addressed with no contextualisation. **4–6** | Issue vaguely or completely misunderstood. **0–3** |
| **Knowledge (K)** | 10 | Very good logical essay; comprehensive knowledge and understanding; no significant omissions/errors. **9–10** | Reasonable essay; basic knowledge but limited understanding; minor omissions/errors. **7–8** | Confused essay; vague understanding; major omissions/errors. **4–6** | Weak essay; little/no knowledge; information may be incorrect or contradictory. **0–3** |
| **Evidence (E)** | 10 | Identifies and interrogates a broad range of relevant, authoritative and credible evidence. **9–10** | Identifies and interrogates a good range of evidence with over-reliance on unsubstantiated data. **7–8** | Evidence limited, simplistic or confused, only vaguely relevant. **4–6** | Little/no evidence; evidence not relevant. **0–3** |
| **Analysis & Synthesis (A)** | 5 | Very good argument based on critical and perceptive analysis of the evidence. **5** | Good argument based on basic analysis. **4** | Argument flawed; limited analysis/evaluation; superficial with significant inaccuracies. **3** | Argument poorly constructed, confused or illogical; analysis poor. **0–2** |
| **Evaluation (V)** | 5 | Very good integration of comparative/alternative perspectives; draws very good independent conclusions & clearly justifies own position. **5** | Good integration of comparative/alternative viewpoints; draws independent conclusions & clearly justifies own position. **4** | Limited comparative perspectives; limited independent conclusions & justification. **3** | No comparative perspectives; no independent conclusions or justification. **0–2** |
| **Cohesion (C)** | 10 | Organisation and management of views very good; very good focus, clarity and construction. **9–10** | Organisation basic; reasonable construction. **7–8** | Organisation limited and confused; poor construction. **4–6** | Essay lacks structure, organisation, coherence, focus, context and clarity. **0–3** |

Total = **50 marks per essay** (I 10 + K 10 + E 10 + A 5 + V 5 + C 10).

### ESSAY GRADE BANDS (O1–O8) — used to review the analytic total

- **O1 (45–50):** Coherent, well-structured, supported by relevant, accurate and varied evidence
  using comparative evidence. A thorough grasp of the requirements of the task is obvious, as is
  understanding and independent thinking. Engagement in-depth. Judgements/conclusions
  comprehensively justified. Detailed knowledge, critical engagement, deep insight, sharp focus,
  accomplished argument, supported by a range of evidence and sources.
- **O2 (40–44):** Focuses clearly on the question. Arguments very well structured and unified,
  supported by relevant, accurate and varied evidence. Points very well organised; coherent
  argument using comparative viewpoints. Very good engagement. Independent judgements/conclusions
  with very reasonable justification.
- **O3 (35–39):** Focuses clearly on the question posed. Demands of the question well understood.
  Points tend to be well supported by accurate and relevant evidence, well organised; coherent
  argument present. Evidence of engagement; judgements/conclusions with reasonable justification.
- **O4 (30–34):** Some understanding of the demands of the question. Points relevant, supported by
  some valid references and illustration. A sense that some points are left to stand alone and are
  not fully integrated into a unified argument. Some judgements and independent conclusions with
  limited justification.
- **O5 (25–29):** Question understood. Knowledge limited; tentative points and limited evidence
  that is valid and appropriate. Points limited and evidence not developed into a forceful, unified
  argument. Limited engagement resulting in answers lacking an integrated independent conclusion.
- **O6 (20–24):** Question understood at least partially. Response lacks clear focus and tends to
  wander from point to point. Summary and repetition often takes the place of discussion. Knowledge
  very limited with very little evidence. Very limited engagement. Judgements/conclusions vague or
  personal with very limited justification. Re-reading may be necessary to discover meaning.
- **O7 (15–19):** Question partially understood; response poorly focused. A few valid points stated
  but not purposely linked to make an argument; no evidence to support points. No evidence of
  engagement. Candidate has not formed a judgement, drawn independent conclusions or offered
  justification for their own opinion or position.
- **O8 (0–14):** Question very poorly understood or completely misunderstood. A very poor answer
  which at best offers only fragmented pieces of information with little or no relevant points. No
  relevant arguments or evidence. Response very poor and lacks focus; the reader is confused. Some
  points might be totally inaccurate or irrelevant.

## CITIZENSHIP PROJECT REPORT (100 marks) — guidance

**Rationale and research.** Explain the rationale for the action chosen. Give a clear account of
the aims of the citizenship project, the means chosen to achieve those aims, and the action plan.
In a group project, identify both the group's aims and the aims you had as an individual to fulfil
your role. Provide a summary of research undertaken including the key research findings (data)
which informed the action. Comment on the sources of information used (web-based materials,
literature, interviews, other sources). Include full references in the References and Bibliography
section at the end of the report.

**Execution of citizenship project.** Provide a summary of the actions undertaken. In a group
project, distinguish clearly between group actions and your individual actions by using "we" or
"I" as appropriate. Critically analyse the various elements of the action plan, including any
challenges encountered and how these were dealt with. Describe the outcomes of the project.

**Reflections on knowledge gained and skills developed.** Critically reflect on the personal
insights and knowledge gained about this issue since undertaking the project. Identify and describe
the skills developed, with particular reference to working with others and being personally
effective. Briefly explain the role that reflection, feedback from others, and learning from the
course played in helping you to carry out the project.
