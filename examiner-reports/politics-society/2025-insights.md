  tariff and no cumulative-mark unit** anywhere in this subject — every item is graded on a
  quality descriptor band.

## The marking system in one sentence

Every answer, from a 5-mark short question to a 100-mark essay, is scored by matching the
response against a **named quality band** (Fair / Good / Very good, escalating to Weak /
Excellent on bigger items) and reading off the mark range for that band — a **holistic
descriptor-band** system, not a count of discrete points. The higher the mark value of the
question, the more bands the grid carries and the more the top bands demand *insight and
independence* rather than mere relevance.

## Paper structure and mark allocation

The written paper carries **400 marks** across three sections (80% of the subject); the
Citizenship Project Report is the remaining **100 marks / 20%** (pp.16–17).

- **Section A — Question 1, 50 marks (p.4):** "There are 15 questions of which candidates must
  answer 10 … 50 marks - 10 x 5 marks." Short-answer. Mark all attempted, credit the best 10
  (the standard SEC "best-of" convention; the scheme marks every attempt).
- **Section B — Question 2, 150 marks (pp.10–12):** the single **data/documents-based question**,
  built on stimulus Documents A and B. Sub-parts escalate: 10 + 10 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 50.
- **Section C — discursive essays, 200 marks (pp.13–15):** two essays at **100 marks each**,
  chosen from a list. Each essay is marked on the six-criterion grid (below).
- **Citizenship Project Report, 100 marks (pp.16–17):** three grids — Rationale & Research 35,
  Execution 45, Reflections 20.

## Section A — short-answer grammar (3-band descriptor grid, pp.4–9)

Each 5-mark short question is graded on a three-band descriptor scale, **not** a points tally.
The default grid (verbatim, e.g. (b) p.4, (c) p.5):

> "Very good [explanation/description/commentary] 4–5M / Good 2–3M / Fair 0–1M."

Structural features:

- **Split-mark items.** Where a question demands two named things, the 5 marks split explicitly,
  e.g. "(3M+2M)" — "(i) Describe two functions of the President of Ireland. (3M+2M)" (p.7), and
  "(m) Describe two ways language diversity is protected … (3M+2M)" (p.9). Each of the two
  elements is then graded on its own mini-band (Very good 3M / Good 2M / Fair 1M for the first;
  Good 2M / Fair 1M for the second). **Answer only one element and you can score at most 3 of the
  5 marks** — the two-element discipline is baked into the mark split.
- **Name-then-explain items** front-load a fixed naming mark: "(o) Name the Key Thinker associated
  with The Capabilities Approach … Name: Martha Nussbaum 2M" then a 3-mark explanation band (p.9);
  same shape at "(a)" (2M identify + 3M description, p.4) and "(l)" (Name 2M + describe, p.8).
- **Stimulus short-answers** (image/statistic/quote comment) still use the same 3-band grid — e.g.
  (f) comment on an image (p.6), (g) a Katriona O'Sullivan quote (p.6), (h) an Oxfam statistic
  (p.7). The task verb ("Comment on …") signals the top band rewards *contextualised commentary*,
  not description.
- Indicative content is a "Responses may make reference to:" bullet list ending "Other relevant
  responses" — i.e. the bullets are **suggestions, not a checklist** (reinforced by the general
  instruction, p.3, that suggestions "are not exhaustive and alternative valid responses … are
  acceptable").

## Section B — the data/documents question (mark-tiered descriptor escalation, pp.10–12)

The stimulus question does **not** use one uniform rubric; the descriptor grid *grows* with the
mark value of each sub-part. This is the most distinctive structural feature of the subject.

- **10-mark items (a, b):** three bands only — "Very good 8–10M / Good 4–7M / Fair 0–3M" (p.10).
- **20-mark items (c, d, e, f):** four bands, and the descriptors now name the quality gradient
  explicitly (verbatim, c/p.11): "Very good commentary 16–20M **independent and insightful** /
  Good 11–15M **relevant but lacking insight** / Fair 6–10M **limited, lacking clarity** / Weak
  0–5M **contradictory or confused**." So the jump from Good to Very good at 20 marks is precisely
  the jump from *relevant* to *insightful/independent* — relevance alone caps you in the Good band.
- **Skill-specific verbs drive the top band.** (d) "Critique the methodology employed in Document
  B" lists the methodological levers to interrogate — "the online panel / quota controls /
  representative samples / weighting for gender/age/region/social class" (p.11) — i.e. source-
  critique marks reward naming and evaluating the *method*, not summarising the finding. (f)
  "Evaluate the relevance … to EU policy and decision-makers" requires "insightful discussion
  **using both documents**" for the top band (p.12) — a cross-document synthesis requirement.
- **The 50-mark capstone (g) uses a split rubric** (p.12): the mark is built from **two separate
  grids** — "Conclusions (30 marks)" graded Very Good 24–30 / Good 17–23 / Fair 9–16 / Weak 0–8,
  **plus** "Use of documents (20 marks)" graded Very Good 16–20 ("comprehensive use of documents")
  / Good 11–15 ("basic use of documents") / Fair 6–10 / Weak 0–5. So a strong argument that never
  cites the documents forfeits up to 20 marks, and heavy document use with weak conclusions
  forfeits up to 30 — you must do both. This is the subject's structural analogue of History's
  DBQ contextualisation and Geography's "link-to-the-anchor-or-zero" rule.

## Section C — the discursive essay (six-criterion analytic grid + holistic reconciliation)

**The marking process is a defined two-pass procedure** (p.13, verbatim):

> "1. Read the entire essay without allocating any marks. 2. Mark the essay using the marking
> criteria and total the marks. 3. Review total mark awarded using the grade band descriptors.
> 4. To finalise the total mark review again using the criteria."

So the essay is scored **analytically** (six criteria that sum to 100) and then **reconciled
against a holistic H1–H8 grade-band table** — a hybrid, not a pure additive scheme.

**The six marking criteria (p.14), each graded Excellent / Very good / Good / Fair / Weak:**

| Criterion | Max | Excellent band | What the top band demands (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction (I) | 10 | 9–10 | "Directly addresses, clarifies and contextualises the issue." |
| Knowledge (K) | 20 | 18–20 | "Clear and critical understanding. Extensive knowledge of the issue." |
| Evidence (E) | 20 | 18–20 | "Identifies and interrogates convincingly a broad range of relevant, authoritative and credible sources of evidence." |
| Analysis & Synthesis (A) | 15 | 13–15 | "An excellent argument based on a critical and perceptive analysis of the evidence." |
| Evaluation (V) | 15 | 13–15 | "Comprehensively integrates comparative / alternative perspectives. Draws insightful, independent conclusions & confidently justifies own position." |
| Cohesion (C) | 20 | 18–20 | "Organisation and management of views and opinions is excellent. Excellent focus and clarity throughout. Excellent construction." |

Total = **100 marks per essay**. The two heaviest single criteria are **Knowledge, Evidence and
Cohesion at 20 each** — i.e. *what you know*, *how you source it*, and *how you organise it* are
weighted equally, and Cohesion alone is worth as much as Knowledge.

**What moves an essay up a band is a consistent gradient across every criterion:** from *vague →
addresses → contextualises* (I), *little knowledge → basic → comprehensive → critical* (K),
*not relevant → simplistic → limited → sufficient → broad & interrogated* (E), *illogical →
flawed → basic → critical & perceptive* (A), *no perspectives → limited → integrated →
comprehensively integrated* (V). The recurring discriminators are **comparative / alternative
perspectives, interrogation of evidence, and independent justified conclusions** — these appear
in E, A and V and are the marks a purely descriptive essay cannot reach.

**Holistic grade-band descriptors, H1–H8 (p.15)** — used to review the analytic total. Each band
gives a paragraph portrait; the key gradient (verbatim highlights):

- **H1 (90–100):** "detailed knowledge based on critical thinking, deep insight, sharp focus,
  accomplished argument … supported by a range of evidence and sources" using "comparative
  evidence."
- **H3 (70–79):** "Response focuses clearly on the question set … evidence of critical
  engagement … judgements and conclusions offered have reasonable justification."
- **H4 (60–69):** "some points are left to stand alone and are not fully integrated into a
  unified argument … judgements … with limited justification."
- **H6 (40–49):** "Summary and repetition often take the place of discussion … very little
  evidence … Re-reading may be necessary to discover meaning."
- **H8 (0–29):** "offers only fragmented pieces of information … No relevant arguments … the
  reader is confused."

**The length caveat (p.15, verbatim):** "Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to
reward unwarranted length." — long ≠ better; the bands reward quality, not volume.

## Citizenship Project Report — coursework grids (100 marks, pp.16–17)

Marked on three descriptor grids, each row graded Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair:

- **Section A — Rationale and Research, 35 marks (p.17):** Explanation for the rationale (max 7) +
  Aims with an action plan (max 12) + Summary of findings and sources (max 12) + References and
  Bibliography scored on a fixed **(0/2/4)** step ("Check the back of the booklet", 4 marks). The
  action plan and the research summary are the two heaviest rows (12 each).
- **Section B — Execution, 45 marks (p.17):** three equal 15-mark rows — Summary of actions
  undertaken (graded, and note the explicit **"We / I"** instruction — group vs individual
  contribution must be distinguished), Critical analysis of the action plan, and Outcomes.
- **Section C — Reflections, 20 marks (p.17):** Knowledge & insights (max 6) + Skills (max 6) +
  Reflection (max 4) + Feedback and learning (max 4), graded on a shorter Very Good / Good / Fair
  scale.
- Report guidance (p.16) stresses distinguishing group from individual work ("using 'we' or 'I'
  as appropriate"), **critical** analysis of challenges, and full references — "Standard"
  descriptive reporting sits in the lower bands; the top bands demand critical analysis and
  reflection.

## General examiner conventions / annotations (p.3)

- **Online marking annotations** are minimal compared with History/Geography: a mark indicating
  "THE POINT AT WHICH MARKS WERE AWARDED"; an "INFORMATION NOT VALID / … SECTION HAS BEEN SEEN AND
  READ BY THE EXAMINER" stamp; and "PAGE SEEN BY THE EXAMINER." There is **no cumulative-mark
  bracket and no SRP stamp** — consistent with the descriptor-band (not points-tally) design.
- General instructions (p.3): detail required "is determined by the context and the manner in
  which the question is asked and by the number of marks assigned"; "Words, expressions or phrases
  must be correctly used in context and not contradicted, and where there is evidence of incorrect
  use or contradiction, the marks may not be awarded"; if in doubt "examiners must consult their
  advising examiner before awarding marks"; and the scheme's suggestions "are not exhaustive and
  alternative valid responses … are acceptable."
- **Standard SEC caveat on the finalised scheme** (p.2): schemes are "not intended to be
  standalone documents," model solutions "are not intended to be exhaustive," and "Assumptions
  about future marking schemes on the basis of past schemes should be avoided" — the descriptor
  bands are stable, but exact mark splits per question can change year to year.
