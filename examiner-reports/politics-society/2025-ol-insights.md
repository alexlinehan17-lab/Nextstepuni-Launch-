- **Report type:** Marking Scheme (SEC, finalised published scheme)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Level:** Ordinary
- **Original filename:** SEC `Leaving Certificate 2025 Marking Scheme — Politics and Society,
  Ordinary Level` (`2025 LC Politics and Society OL.pdf`; retrieved 2026-07-06 via the
  educateplus.ie mirror; title page carries the Coimisiún na Scrúduithe Stáit / State Examinations
  Commission imprint).
- **Renamed to:** `2025-ol-marking-scheme.pdf` (20 pages; pp.19–20 blank. Page refs below are PDF
  page numbers, which match the printed footer numbers.)
- **Companion file:** the HL 2025 scheme is filed as `2025-marking-scheme.pdf` /
  `2025-insights.md`. This file records **only the OL-specific marking grammar and how it differs
  from HL** — read the HL insights first for the shared descriptor-band system.
- **Note on context:** Like the HL scheme, this is the finalised per-year marking scheme, not a
  Chief Examiner's Report (Politics & Society is a newer LC subject, first examined 2018; no Chief
  Examiner's Report was locatable). The value of the OL scheme is that it exposes where the
  Ordinary-level assessment *lightens the descriptor grammar* relative to HL: fewer bands, no
  "Excellent"/"Weak" top-and-bottom on short answers, model-answer recall items in the data
  section, a halved essay grid, and a re-weighting toward knowledge and structure over higher-order
  analysis/evaluation.

## The one-sentence OL-vs-HL summary

Both levels use the **same holistic descriptor-band system** (match the answer to a named quality
band, read off the mark range — no points-per-fact tariff anywhere), but OL runs it with **fewer
bands and lower higher-order demand**: three bands (Very good / Good / Fair, no Weak) on every
short answer and every Section B item; a four-band essay grid that tops out at **"Very good," not
"Excellent"**; and an essay that weights *knowledge and structure over analysis and evaluation*.
The 100-mark Citizenship Project is marked on an **identical grid at both levels**.

## Marking-grammar structure — OL vs HL, section by section

### Paper-level mark allocation is redistributed (same 400 written + 100 project total)

| Section | HL | OL |
|---|---|---|
| A — short answer | **50** (15 Qs, answer 10, 10 × 5M) | **100** (20 Qs, answer 10, 10 × 10M) — pp.4–10 |
| B — data/documents | **150** (one Q2, escalating sub-parts to a 50M split-rubric capstone) | **150** (three separate 50M questions Q2/Q3/Q4, all on Documents A & B) — pp.11–13 |
| C — discursive essay | **200** (essay grid = 100M each) | **150** (essay grid = **50M** each) — pp.14–16 |
| Citizenship Project | 100 | 100 (**identical grids**) — pp.17–18 |

**Takeaway:** OL **doubles** the short-answer weight (100 vs 50) and **lightens** the essay section
(150 vs 200). The centre of gravity at OL is Section A recall/description; at HL it is the essays.
(The exam paper, not the scheme, fixes how many essays are answered; the scheme shows the essay
grid caps at 50M and Section C totals 150M — i.e. three 50M essays' worth of marks vs HL's two
100M essays. Number of essays chosen is not stated in the scheme itself.)

### Section A — three-band grid, doubled mark value, extra data sub-question (pp.4–10)

- **Default grid is three bands, no Weak band:** "Very good 8–10M / Good 4–7M / Fair 0–3M" on a
  standard 10-mark item (e.g. (b) p.4, (h) p.6). HL's default 5-mark item is the same three bands
  scaled down ("Very good 4–5M / Good 2–3M / Fair 0–1M"). So the *band count is identical* — the
  OL difference here is purely that each question is worth **10 marks not 5**, giving finer
  gradation inside each band.
- **Two-element items** carry the same discipline as HL: "(2 x 5)" splits the 10 marks, each half
  graded on its own mini-band (Very good 4–5M / Good 2–3M / Fair 0–1M). Answer only one of the two
  and you cap at 5/10 — e.g. (a) "Give two reasons why it is important to vote" (p.4), (q) "Give
  two reasons why ICT… globalised world" (p.9).
- **Name/identify-then-explain items** front-load a fixed mark: (e) "Identify a relevant right 2M"
  then a 6–8/3–5/0–2 description band (p.5); (r) description 6–8M **+ Example 2M** (p.10). Same
  shape as HL's "Name … 2M + explain" items.
- **OL-only structural feature — a data sub-question sits *inside* Section A.** Item (d) has three
  data parts: "(i) What conclusion can you draw from the data above about poverty? 4 marks — Very
  good conclusion 3–4M / Good 2M / Fair 1M", plus (ii) identify one difference between 2022 and
  2023 (3M) and (iii) how might this data help the government (3M) (p.5). At HL all data work lives
  in Section B; at OL a compact data-reading task is folded into the short-answer section too.

### Section B — three 50-mark questions with model-answer recall, **no split-rubric capstone** (pp.11–13)

This is the **biggest OL-vs-HL divergence.**

- **HL:** one 150-mark data question whose sub-parts escalate (10+10+20+20+20+20+50), with a
  four-band gradient at 20 marks ("independent and insightful / relevant but lacking insight /
  limited / contradictory or confused") culminating in a **50-mark split-rubric capstone** —
  "Conclusions (30 marks)" **plus** a separate "Use of documents (20 marks)" grid.
- **OL:** the 150 marks are split into **three self-contained 50-mark questions (Q2, Q3, Q4), all
  built on the same Documents A and B**, each made of small sub-parts (5, 10, 15 marks). There is
  **no 50-mark capstone and no separate "Use of documents" mark grid** — the HL device that
  forfeits up to 20 marks for not citing the documents does not exist at OL.
- **OL Section B front-loads literal-comprehension recall with model answers.** Several sub-parts
  are "According to Document A/B, what is…?" and the scheme gives a **specific correct answer**, not
  a band — e.g. Q2(a) "Zero Hunger" (5M), Q2(b) "A global overview of progress on the
  implementation of the 2030 Agenda…" (10M), Q2(c) "Conflict, the climate crisis and economic
  shocks" (15M) (p.11). At HL even the data items were band-graded commentary; at OL the opening of
  each stimulus question tests whether the candidate can *locate and copy the right fact from the
  document.* Mis-read the document and those marks are lost outright.
- **Where OL does grade on bands, it uses three bands only** (Very good / Good / Fair), never the
  four-band "Weak … contradictory or confused" gradient HL applies at 20 marks. Example: Q2(e)
  "Very good explanation **referring to both documents** 8–10M / Good 4–7M / Fair 0–3M" (p.11) —
  the "both documents" cross-reference expectation survives, but as a band descriptor, not a
  separate mark pot.
- **Name-then-analyse split** persists: Q2(d) "Name: 2m / Identify the main cause: 8m" (p.11); the
  fact carries 2 marks, the explanation 8.

### Section C — the essay: halved grid, four bands (tops at "Very good"), re-weighted (pp.14–16)

- **Same two-pass procedure as HL, verbatim** (p.14): "1. Read the entire essay without allocating
  any marks. 2. Mark the essay using the marking criteria and total the marks. 3. Review total mark
  awarded using the grade band descriptors. 4. To finalise the total mark review again using the
  criteria." Analytic grid + holistic reconciliation, identical process to HL.
- **The essay grid is halved (50M vs HL 100M) and the top band is "Very good," not "Excellent."**
  HL grades each criterion across **five** bands (Excellent / Very good / Good / Fair / Weak); OL
  grades across **four** (Very good / Good / Fair / Weak) — the "Excellent" tier is removed (p.15).
- **The re-weighting is the substantive difference.** OL criterion maxes (p.15): Introduction 10,
  Knowledge 10, Evidence 10, Analysis & Synthesis **5**, Evaluation **5**, Cohesion 10 = 50. HL:
  I 10, K 20, E 20, A 15, V 15, C 20 = 100.

  | Criterion | HL max | HL % | OL max | OL % |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Introduction | 10 | 10% | 10 | **20%** |
  | Knowledge | 20 | 20% | 10 | 20% |
  | Evidence | 20 | 20% | 10 | 20% |
  | Analysis & Synthesis | 15 | 15% | **5** | **10%** |
  | Evaluation | 15 | 15% | **5** | **10%** |
  | Cohesion | 20 | 20% | 10 | 20% |

  So at OL the **higher-order criteria (Analysis, Evaluation) are the lightest**, 10% each, while at
  HL they are 15% each; and the **Introduction more than doubles in relative weight** (20% vs 10%).
  An OL essay that is knowledgeable, well-introduced and well-organised but thin on
  analysis/evaluation still reaches the upper bands — the reverse of HL, where comparative analysis
  and independent evaluation carry 30% of the essay.
- **Grade bands are O1–O8 (45–50 down to 0–14), mirroring HL's H1–H8** in phrasing (p.16): the O1
  "comparative evidence … independent thinking" portrait, the O6 "Summary and repetition often
  takes the place of discussion" mid-band trap, and the O8 "the reader is confused" bottom band are
  the OL analogues of the HL H1/H6/H8 descriptors.
- **The HL "skilful brevity / unwarranted length" caveat is absent from the OL scheme.** HL p.15
  warns "Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length"; the OL
  Section C pages (pp.14–16) carry no equivalent line.

### Citizenship Project — identical grids at both levels (pp.17–18)

The 100-mark coursework is marked on the **same three grids as HL, with the same mark bands**:
Section A Rationale & Research 35 (Explanation for the rationale max 7 / Aims with an Action Plan
max 12 / Summary of findings and sources max 12 / References & Bibliography on the fixed **(0-2-4)**
step, 4M), Section B Execution 45 (three equal 12–15M rows — Summary of actions with the explicit
**"We / I"** group-vs-individual instruction, Critical analysis of the action plan, Outcomes),
Section C Reflections 20 (Knowledge & insights max 6 / Skills max 6 / Reflection 4 / Feedback &
learning 4). This is effectively a **common component** across HL and OL.

### General conventions — unchanged from HL (p.3)

Identical general instructions: detail required "is determined by the context and the manner in
which the question is asked and by the number of marks assigned"; the **contradiction-voids-the-mark
rule** ("Words, expressions or phrases must be correctly used in context and not contradicted…");
consult the advising examiner if in doubt; suggestions "are not exhaustive and alternative valid
responses … are acceptable." Online annotations are the same minimal set (point-at-which-marks-
awarded / section-seen / page-seen) — **no cumulative-mark bracket and no SRP stamp**, consistent
with the descriptor-band design.

## Candidate-behaviour findings (OL-specific, source + page)

1. **Two-part short answers must deliver both parts or cap at half.** "(2 x 5)" items — (a) two
   reasons to vote (p.4), (q) two reasons ICT globalised the world (p.9), Q4(a) one similarity +
   one difference (p.13) — grade each element on its own mini-band; a candidate who gives only one
   element cannot exceed 5 of the 10 marks. The two-element discipline is baked into the mark
   split.
2. **OL Section B rewards literal document-reading first.** The opening sub-parts of each stimulus
   question ("According to Document A/B, what is…?") expect the candidate to locate and copy the
   correct fact — the scheme gives model answers ("Zero Hunger"; "Conflict, the climate crisis and
   economic shocks"), so a mis-read of the document forfeits those recall marks outright (Q2 a/b/c,
   p.11). This is a distinctly OL behaviour hook: comprehension accuracy, not commentary, opens the
   section.
3. **At OL the marks reward knowledge and organisation over analysis.** Analysis & Synthesis and
   Evaluation are the two *lightest* essay criteria (5 marks / 10% each), while Introduction,
   Knowledge, Evidence and Cohesion carry 10 each (p.15). A well-structured, knowledgeable essay
   scores well even if under-analysed — the opposite emphasis to HL, where comparative analysis and
   independent evaluation are worth 30% combined.
4. **Summary is the mid-band trap at OL exactly as at HL.** The O6 (20–24) grade band reads
   "Response lacks clear focus and tends to wander … Summary and repetition often takes the place of
   discussion … Re-reading may be necessary to discover meaning" (p.16) — the behaviour that pins an
   essay in the lower-middle band is describing rather than arguing.
5. **Contradiction / misused terminology voids the mark.** The general instruction (p.3) is applied
   at OL identically to HL: "Words, expressions or phrases must be correctly used in context and not
   contradicted, and where there is evidence of incorrect use or contradiction, the marks may not be
   awarded." A confused Key-Thinker attribution (e.g. Kathleen Lynch (p.9), Hobbes/Locke State of
   Nature (p.10)) can cost the marks even if some relevant content is present.
