## Marking-grammar structure — Composing paper (Higher Level)

The Composing paper is marked in two 50-mark sections (100 marks total on this scheme):

### Section A — Melody Composition (50 marks) (p.3–5)
- Marked on a **single holistic band-descriptor grid**, not point-by-point. Seven quality bands run from **Excellent → Very good → Good → Fair → Poor → Very poor → Very little or no attempt**, and each band is split into **three adjacent mark sub-bands** (e.g. Excellent = 49–50 / 46–48 / 43–45, down to 1–2 and 0) (p.3).
- The descriptor set the examiner rates against: *style and imagination; sense of shape and structure; development of opening ideas; sense of direction and climax* (p.3).
- **Two mechanical criteria sit inside every band and pull the band up or down:** "Appropriate performing directions (phrasing and dynamics) inserted" and "Suitable instrument chosen." At the bottom bands these flip to "Inappropriate…"/"Unsuitable…" and then "No performing directions… / No instrument chosen" (p.3, 5). Omitting phrasing/dynamics or an instrument choice measurably costs bands.
- Each melody question adds task-specific descriptors to the same grid: **Q2 (melody to given words)** adds *"marriage of words and music"* (p.4); **Q3 (dance)** adds *rhythmic integrity of the dance, adherence to the given structure,* and *successful modulation* — the grid explicitly grades the modulation as "successful" → "unconfirmed" → "unsuccessful" → "no attempt at modulation" across the bands (p.5).

### Section B — Harmony (50 marks) (p.6–8)
Marks are split across **named sub-components**, each with its own rule, and the totals are built up (not one holistic band):

- **Q4 (add a melody / harmony):** Melody rated on a band descriptor worth 33 (*style/imagination; awareness of key and underlying harmonic structure; development; direction/climax*), **plus** Bass = **"0.5 mark per correct bass note under each chord symbol (0.5 × 20) = 10"**, **plus** a "Quality of bass line" band worth 7 (p.6).
- **Q5 (chords + bass):** **Chords = "1 mark for each chord that is part of a good progression"** in a listed set of chord boxes and **"0.5 mark for each chord…"** in a smaller listed set (total 20); **plus** "Quality of Chord Progressions" band worth 10 (Excellent 9–10 … Poor 0–1); **plus** Bass = 0.5 per correct bass note; **plus** "Quality of bass line" band worth 9 (p.7).
- **Q6 (descant):** **Chords = "1 mark for each chord that fits melody and is part of a good chord progression"** (listed boxes; some boxes 0.5), **plus** a Descant band descriptor worth 33 (*descant line within harmonic framework; adherence to two-part style of given opening*) (p.8).

### Chord / voice-leading rule-set (the "hard-coded" harmony grammar) (p.7)
Marks are awarded for a chord only in context — it must fit "(i) the chord fitting the melody line at that point… (ii) the preceding chord and (iii) the following chord." Explicit blanket rules:
- "Same chord in adjacent boxes not accepted unless bass changed or 7th added."
- "Accidental and suffix, where relevant, must be fully correct for mark to be awarded."
- "Marks for chords not awarded in isolation. Chords must be part of a good progression."
- Progression prohibitions/requirements: **V7–V not accepted; Vb or V7b should generally be followed by I; V–ii generally not accepted; ii–I generally not accepted except as approach to Ic in a cadential 6/4; the cadential 6/4 must be on the stronger of the two beats and at a cadence point; secondary 7ths must be followed by a chord whose root is a 4th higher.**
- Bass: "No doubling of major 3rd under chord symbol."
- A distinct **"Chord quality"** mark exists precisely so that a candidate who chose chords that "worked" but weren't the best still loses marks there: "While the candidate may have chosen chords that 'worked' at any point, there may have been better choices… reflected in the chord quality mark" (p.7).

## Marking-grammar structure — Listening paper (Higher Level, Core)

### General Notes to Examiners (p.9) — three governing rules
1. "Mark the overall quality of statements/descriptions. **NB Full marks can only be awarded for statements/descriptions that are fully correct.**"
2. Choice questions (e.g. Q5B): "if a candidate answers more than one question, mark all answers and award the marks for the best answer."
3. "In questions where the candidate is asked to identify/name/choose a specific number of features/instruments, **each extra incorrect answer cancels a correct one.**"

### How individual listening answers are marked
- **Fractional per-item marks** are pervasive: pitch identification "0.5 × 5 = 2.5" (p.10); "any three correct instruments" scored "0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5" (p.14); features scored "1 + 1" or "1.5 + 1.5" or "2 + 2" per feature (p.11, 14, 17, 18).
- **One-word / naming answers** carry a flat mark (e.g. cadence type = 2; instrument = 1–2; "Recapitulation" = 1) (p.10–13).
- **Description / explanation answers** are marked "up to N marks" for quality, with an explicit **"Partially correct answer = 1"** fallback appearing on almost every extended item (p.11–18), and occasionally an explicit quality ladder, e.g. "Award for quality: 0, 1, 2 or 3" (p.11).
- **The extended essay-style sub-question (Q5B, 10 marks)** uses its own 7-level band descriptor: "Excellent awareness and detailed knowledge… 10" → "Good knowledge… but lacking in detail 6–7" → "Some general points… lacking sufficient detail 4–5" → "No response… 0" (p.16). Detail and specificity, not just correctness, move the band.
- Two-mark description items reward **specific, correct musical observation** and list acceptable exemplar features; a vague or half-right version is capped at the "Partially correct = 1" level (p.10, 12–18).

## Candidate-behaviour findings (for authored scripts)

1. **Precision beats coverage in Listening.** "Full marks can only be awarded for statements/descriptions that are fully correct"; every extended item has a "Partially correct answer = 1" fallback (p.9, 11–18). A half-right or vague description is explicitly capped at 1 mark, however much is written.
2. **Over-answering is actively penalised.** When asked for a set number of features/instruments, "each extra incorrect answer cancels a correct one" (p.9). Listing extra guesses to hedge can wipe out marks already earned.
3. **…except on choice questions, where extra attempts are safe.** For Q5B, examiners "mark all answers and award the marks for the best answer" (p.9) — so a student unsure which option they answered best loses nothing by attempting more. Students should know which regime a question is under.
4. **Composing chords earn nothing in isolation.** "Marks for chords not awarded in isolation. Chords must be part of a good progression," and "accidental and suffix… must be fully correct for the mark" (p.7). A correct-looking chord that breaks a voice-leading rule (V7–V, mis-placed cadential 6/4, undoubled-3rd violation) scores zero, and a separate "chord quality" mark docks even chords that merely "worked" (p.7).
5. **In melody-writing, mechanical additions and holistic musicianship are marked together.** The band grids reward *shape, structure, development, direction/climax* (and *modulation* / *marriage of words and music* where relevant), but "Appropriate performing directions (phrasing and dynamics) inserted" and "Suitable instrument chosen" sit inside every band — forgetting phrasing/dynamics or an instrument choice drops the band even when the notes are good (p.3–5).

## Quotable lines

1. **Full marks require full correctness (Listening, p.9):**
   > "Mark the overall quality of statements/descriptions. NB Full marks can only be awarded for statements/descriptions that are fully correct."
2. **Over-answering cancels marks (Listening, p.9):**
   > "In questions where the candidate is asked to identify/name/choose a specific number of features/instruments, each extra incorrect answer cancels a correct one."
3. **Chords are marked in context, not in isolation (Composing, p.7):**
   > "Marks for chords not awarded in isolation. Chords must be part of a good progression."
4. **Chord quality is graded even when a chord 'works' (Composing, p.7):**
   > "While the candidate may have chosen chords that 'worked' at any point, there may have been better choices. If that is the case, this will be reflected in the chord quality mark."
5. **Deferred-scheme caveat (p.2):**
   > "The marking schemes for the deferred examinations may not necessarily be as detailed as the corresponding marking schemes for the main sitting of an examination, which serve to ensure consistency across a large team of examiners."
