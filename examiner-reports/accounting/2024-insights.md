## Paper structure & mark allocation (verified)

Leaving Certificate Accounting Higher Level is a **400-mark** paper in three sections; the marking scheme's question numbering and marginal cumulative mark totals corroborate the exam-paper rubric:

- **Section 1 — Financial Accounting (120 marks).** Q1 (A **or** B) is a single 120-mark full-accounts question, **OR** answer any **two** of Q2–Q4 at **60 marks each**. In 2024: Q1A = Final Accounts of a company; Q1B = Final Accounts of a manufacturing company; Q2 = Published Accounts; Q3 = Farm Accounts (Q4 not reproduced in the extracted portion).
- **Section 2 — Financial Accounting (200 marks).** Answer **two** of Q5–Q7 at **100 marks each**. In 2024: Q5 = Interpretation of Accounts; Q6 = Cash Flow / Abridged accounts; Q7 = Correction of Errors & Suspense.
- **Section 3 — Management Accounting (80 marks).** Answer **one** of Q8–Q9 at **80 marks each**. In 2024: Q8 = Flexible Budgets / Costing / Stock valuation; Q9 = Budgeting (production, purchases, cash, budgeted trading & P&L).

Marks-to-minutes: 400 marks over the 3-hour paper ≈ 0.45 min/mark, so the 120-mark Q1 warrants ~54 minutes and a 100-mark Section-2 question ~45 minutes.

## The marking grammar — how the big computational questions are marked

### 1. The "workmark" system — figure-by-figure awards, not holistic

Every line of every statement carries its own bracketed mark award. There is no single global mark for "the account"; the account's mark is the **sum of individual figure awards**. Examples from Q1A Trading, Profit & Loss (p.3):

- `Sales 1,080,700 [3]`
- `Purchases N1 697,000 [7]`
- `Less closing stock N2 (51,380) [7]`
- `Discount 3,500 [3]`, `Depreciation Delivery Vans N3 24,890 [4]`
- `Investment Income N10 2,400 [3]`, `Debenture N11 (8,800) [4]`

The award size scales with the difficulty of deriving the figure: a plain given figure is `[1]`–`[3]`; a figure requiring a multi-step adjustment is `[4]`–`[7]`. The heaviest single awards (`[7]`) go to lines that fold several adjustments together (Purchases, Closing Stock).

### 2. Figures are backed by "N-notes" (workings) — the working is part of the mark

Each adjusted figure references a numbered working (N1, N2, …) reproduced after the statement (p.5, p.9). E.g. `N1 Purchases 747,000 − 46,000 − 4,000 = 697,000`; `N16 Provision for bad debts 69,200 × 4% = 2,768`. The scheme presents the working as the justification for the `[n]` award. Practically: the workings *are* where the marks live — a correct final figure with no derivation is exposed if any component is wrong, and the scheme is built to award the intermediate steps.

### 3. Consequential / own-figure marking (error-carried-forward) — encoded via the penalty legend

The scheme does **not** print a prose "error carried forward" rule (unlike Maths). Instead the ECF mechanism is encoded in the annotation legend (p.34). A figure that is wrong because it *carried forward* an earlier error is not zeroed line-by-line; the transfer error itself is a **−1 penalty** ("non-transfer, incorrect transfer") and a **non-transfer earns half marks**, not zero:

> *"Minus one mark - Penalty, Incorrect calculation, misplaced figure, non-transfer, incorrect transfer or repeated work."* (p.34)
> *"Half marks awarded - Non transfer."* (p.34)

So the operative model is: **you are penalised once for the transfer/calculation slip, and downstream figures built on your own (wrong) sub-total are still marked on an own-figure basis** rather than re-penalised. The scheme also explicitly accepts alternative valid methods — Q5 gearing: *"As an alternative Debt to Equity is fully acceptable here and the student should arrive at an answer of 36.89% for full [marks]"* (p.18).

### 4. Presentation / format marks — the "both totals correct" star

Format is marked as a discrete, losable award, denoted with an asterisk:

- Balance Sheet: `Both totals correct *` (p.4, and again on the Q7 Correction-of-Errors balance sheet, p.27, where the closing total line reads `502,025 [3]*`). A balance sheet that does not balance forfeits this mark regardless of how many individual lines are right.
- Statement headings score marks: `Trading Profit and Loss Account of Yeats Ltd for the year ended 31/12/2023 [1]` (p.3), `Manufacturing account for Sexton Ltd for year ended 31/12/2023 [1]` (p.6) — the correctly-titled/dated heading is worth a mark.
- **Published accounts sequence penalty:** Q2 (Published Accounts) opens with *"Penalties are applied where entries are in incorrect sequence"* (p.10) — in the statutory published-accounts format, putting the right figures in the wrong order is penalised even when the arithmetic is right.

### 5. Surplus answers — no double credit, no penalty

The legend defines a "surplus answer" annotation: *"Surplus answer or part of answer. Marks awarded elsewhere."* (p.34). Extra correct material does not lose marks but earns nothing beyond the allotted award — there is no bonus for volume.

### 6. How marks divide within a question

Within a 120-mark full-accounts question the split is roughly: the Trading/P&L account and the Balance Sheet each carry a large block of figure-awards, plus the format marks (heading, both-totals-correct). Marginal cumulative totals in the scheme (e.g. `75` mid-way through Q1B, p.6) show the mark accreting figure-by-figure to the question total. In the management-accounting questions the same granularity applies: Q8 flexible budget `Contribution 414,800 [3]`, `Profit 299,200 [2]`, `Break Even Point … 9,476 units [5]`, `Margin of safety … 24,524 units [4]` (p.29); Q9 budgeting tables award `[1]` per correctly computed cell (pp.31–33).

## The annotation key (p.34) — the examiner's marking vocabulary

The scheme closes with the SEC annotation legend, which is the literal grammar an examiner applies to a script:

| Annotation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| tick + n | Correct element (n marks) |
| (cross) | No marks awarded — answer incorrect or insufficient |
| (arrow) | Refer to notes/workings |
| (mark) | Page seen by examiner / information not valid |
| (bracket) | Surplus answer or part of answer — marks awarded elsewhere |
| −1 | Penalty: incorrect calculation, misplaced figure, non-transfer, incorrect transfer, or repeated work |
| ½ | Half marks awarded — non-transfer |
| underline | Underline |

## Candidate-behaviour findings for authored "scripts" (derived from marking grammar)

These are **inferred from what the 2024 scheme rewards/penalises**, not from a narrated cohort report:

1. **Skipping workings is self-sabotage.** Because the mark lives in the N-note derivation, a student who writes only final figures cannot part-score a wrong line — the scheme is built to award the *steps* (e.g. Purchases `[7]` backed by `N1 747,000 − 46,000 − 4,000`, p.3/p.5). Source: p.3, p.5.
2. **A balance sheet that doesn't balance loses a dedicated mark.** "Both totals correct *" is a discrete award (p.4, p.27) — forcing/plugging a total to make it agree, or leaving it disagreeing, forfeits it independently of the line figures. Source: p.4, p.27.
3. **Wrong order in Published Accounts is penalised even when arithmetic is right.** "Penalties are applied where entries are in incorrect sequence" (p.10) — the statutory format sequence is itself examinable. Source: p.10.
4. **Transfer discipline matters.** Failing to carry a figure to the next statement, or carrying it incorrectly, is a −1 penalty / half-mark event (p.34) — the loss is bounded (you are not zeroed downstream), but every non-transfer is a live deduction. Source: p.34.
5. **Padding earns nothing.** Surplus correct material is annotated "marks awarded elsewhere" (p.34) — writing more than the required number of points/figures does not raise the mark; time is better spent on unattempted parts. Source: p.34.
6. **Valid alternative methods are accepted.** The scheme explicitly allows Debt/Equity as an alternative to Gearing in Q5 (p.18) — students should not fear a legitimate alternative approach, but must still reach the correct target figure. Source: p.18.
