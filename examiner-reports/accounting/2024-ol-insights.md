## The marking grammar — OL vs HL differences

### 1. Same core "workmark" award system — figures marked one line at a time

OL uses the **identical** figure-by-figure bracketed award model as HL: there is no single holistic mark for an account; the account's mark is the sum of individual `[n]` awards on each line. Examples from Q1A Trading/P&L (p.3): `Sales 486,500 [4]`, `Opening stock 29,000 [3]`, `Purchases 195,400 [3]`, `Depreciation Buildings 8,000 [4]`. Same grammar, same annotation legend (p.19). **So yes — OL uses the same workmark system as HL.**

### 2. Awards are COARSER at OL — fewer, bigger chunks per item

The most visible difference. HL awards are finely granular ([1]–[7] per line, with the heaviest [7]s on multi-adjustment lines). At OL the marks are lumped into **larger blocks per item**:

- Q3 Bank Reconciliation: **each** adjusted-bank-account line is worth `[6]` and the bank error `[8]` (p.10).
- Q5 Interpretation: **each** ratio is a flat `[10]` and **each** written theory definition (Debentures, Tangible Fixed Asset, Carriage Inwards, Ordinary Dividend) is `[10]` (pp.12–13).
- Q8 Marginal Costing: `Selling price per unit [15]`, `Variable cost per unit [15]`, `Break-even point [10]`, `Marginal costing statement [15]` (p.17).

So at OL a smaller number of correctly-completed items each carry a heavy award, versus HL's dense per-figure scatter. Practically: getting one OL item fully right is worth proportionally far more, and a single conceptual slip forfeits a bigger chunk.

### 3. No formal "N-note" workings apparatus at OL

HL adjusted figures each reference a numbered working (`N1`, `N2`, … reproduced after the statement) and the scheme awards the derivation steps inside the N-note. The OL scheme has **no N-note system**: adjustments are shown inline within the statement (e.g. purchases-returns netted on the face of the Trading account, p.3) and only occasional one-line supporting sums appear (`Credit sales + cash sales = total sales 188,200 + 340,000 = 528,200 [3]`, p.15; average-stock `57,000 + 60,000 = €58,500`, p.12). The OL adjustments are simpler and do not require a separate, numbered working schedule to earn the mark.

### 4. The HL "Both totals correct *" balance-sheet star does NOT appear at OL

At HL the balance sheet carries a discrete, losable format award — `Both totals correct *` — forfeited if the sheet does not balance. **The OL 2024 scheme contains no such "both totals correct" star**: the OL balance sheets (p.4 Sole Trader, p.7 Company, p.16 Incomplete Records) show only per-line `[n]` awards and a heading `[1]`, with no balance-agreement format mark and no asterisk row in the annotation legend (p.19). Heading/format marks still exist at OL (`... for the year ended 31/12/2023 [1]`, p.3; sub-headings `Prime cost [1]`, `Selling and Distribution [1]`, pp.5–6), but the specific "make it balance" presentation mark is HL-only.

### 5. No "incorrect sequence" penalty at OL

HL's Published Accounts question opens with *"Penalties are applied where entries are in incorrect sequence"* — the statutory format order is itself examinable. **OL has no Published Accounts question and no sequence-order penalty appears anywhere in the OL scheme.** The statutory-sequence penalty is HL-specific.

### 6. Own-figure / transfer marking — IDENTICAL to HL

The OL annotation legend (p.19) is essentially the same as HL: `Minus one mark – Penalty, Incorrect calculation, misplaced figure, non-transfer, incorrect transfer or repeated work` and `Half marks awarded – non-transfer`. So the error-carried-forward / own-figure model is unchanged between levels: a transfer/calculation slip is a bounded **−1** (not a line-by-line zeroing), a **non-transfer earns half marks**, and downstream figures built on a candidate's own (wrong) sub-total are marked on an own-figure basis. `Surplus answer … Marks awarded elsewhere` is also identical (no bonus for padding).

### 7. Alternative valid methods accepted (same as HL)

Q3 prints two complete, fully-marked alternative presentations of the Bank Reconciliation Statement — starting from the adjusted bank balance **or** from the bank-statement balance (both routes labelled and separately mark-tagged, "Or", p.10). Combined with the standard p.2 boilerplate (*"Variations and alternatives may also be acceptable"*), the OL scheme rewards a legitimate alternative route provided it reaches the correct target figure — mirroring HL's Debt/Equity-vs-Gearing allowance.

### 8. Written-theory answers carry heavy lump awards at OL

A distinctive OL feature: short written definitions are worth full `[10]` blocks in Interpretation (p.13) and Q8(g) defines a fixed cost for `[5]` (p.17); Q9(b) two benefits of a cash budget for `[5]` (p.18). At OL, a well-learned one- or two-sentence definition is worth as much as a whole computed ratio — the theory marks are unusually accessible.

## The annotation key (p.19) — the examiner's marking vocabulary (OL)

| Annotation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| tick + n | Correct element (n marks) |
| (cross) | No marks awarded — answer incorrect or insufficient |
| (arrow) | Refer to notes/workings |
| (mark) | Page seen by examiner / information not valid |
| (bracket) | Surplus answer or part of answer — marks awarded elsewhere |
| −1 | Penalty: incorrect calculation, misplaced figure, non-transfer, incorrect transfer, or repeated work |
| ½ | Half marks awarded — non-transfer |
| underline | Underline |

This is the same eight-item legend as HL — **the OL legend has no additional entries and, notably, no `*` format/both-totals row**.

## Candidate-behaviour findings for authored "scripts" (derived from marking grammar)

Inferred from what the 2024 OL scheme rewards/penalises, not from a narrated cohort report:

1. **At OL, one item = a big chunk of marks, so finishing an item matters more.** Each Bank-Rec line `[6]`, each ratio `[10]`, each marginal-costing part `[10]–[15]` (pp.10, 12, 17). A student who gets the method right but stops halfway loses a heavier block than the equivalent HL slip — completing each attempted item to its final figure is disproportionately valuable at OL. Source: pp.10, 12, 17.
2. **Learn the written definitions — they are free-standing 10-mark (or 5-mark) wins.** Interpretation theory definitions are `[10]` each and cost definitions `[5]` (pp.13, 17–18), independent of any computation. A candidate weak on the big accounts can still bank substantial marks on rote theory. Source: pp.13, 17, 18.
3. **Transfer discipline still matters, and the penalty is the same bounded −1 / half-mark as HL.** Failing to carry a figure forward, or carrying it wrong, is `−1` (or half marks for a non-transfer), not a downstream zeroing (p.19). Own-figure marking protects the rest of the script — but every non-transfer is a live deduction. Source: p.19.
4. **A non-balancing balance sheet does NOT cost a dedicated format mark at OL (unlike HL).** There is no "both totals correct" star on the OL balance sheets (pp.4, 7, 16) — OL candidates are not separately penalised for a sheet that fails to agree, so the marks are purely in the individual lines. (This is a genuine OL-vs-HL relaxation; do not import the HL "make it balance" warning into OL content.) Source: pp.4, 7, 16, 19.
5. **Method flexibility is real — the Bank Rec can be presented either direction.** The scheme fully credits both the adjusted-bank-first and bank-statement-first layouts (p.10). Students should use whichever they know best and reach the target figure, rather than fear a "wrong format". Source: p.10.
6. **Padding earns nothing.** Surplus correct material is annotated "marks awarded elsewhere" (p.19) — writing extra points beyond those required does not raise the mark; time is better spent completing unattempted items. Source: p.19.
