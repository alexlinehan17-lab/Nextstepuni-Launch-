## Marking-grammar structure (OL) — and how it differs from HL

### Paper shape (2025 OL, from the mark-allocation grids, p.4–8)

| | **Ordinary Level 2025** | **Higher Level 2025** (from `2025-insights.md`) |
|---|---|---|
| Section 1 | Short-answer, **answer 10 of 15**, each 10m = **100 marks** (p.4) | 8 short questions, **all compulsory**, 80 marks |
| Section 2 | Long questions, **300 marks**. Part 1 = Q1–Q4, Part 2 = Q5–Q9; each question **75 marks** built from five sub-parts (A)–(E) @ **15m each** (p.5–8). Four questions × 75 = 300 answered. | Applied Business Question (ABQ), compulsory, 80 marks (theory + case **link**) |
| Section 3 | *(none — OL has no third section)* | Extended response, 4 of 8 @ 60m = 240 marks |
| **Paper total** | **400 marks** | **400 marks** |

**The single biggest OL-vs-HL difference: OL has NO ABQ.** The entire "Name / Explain / **Link** to the case" grammar that dominates HL Section 2 — and the *"no link awarded without relevant theory"* rule (HL scheme p.6) — does not exist at Ordinary Level. There are no `Link (1)` sub-marks anywhere in the OL scheme. The nearest OL analogue is a few sub-parts cued *"Explain with reference to the text supplied…"* (Q4(C)(i) p.24; Q8(C)(i) p.39), but these are isolated 15-mark parts, not a compulsory 20%-of-paper structure.

### The signature OL grammar: the asymmetric two-point grid `10(7+3) / 5(4+1)`

The dominant template across Section 2 is, for a question asking for **two** points (e.g. "Outline two…", "Explain two…", "Describe two…", "State two…"):

> **1st point: 10 Marks (7m + 3m) — 2nd point: 5 Marks (4m + 1m)** (recurs throughout p.5–8)

Reading of the split:
- **7m / 4m** = state + basically explain the point.
- **3m / 1m** = the *development* mark (elaboration / consequence / example).
- The two points are **asymmetric**: the first is worth double the second (10 vs 5).

Consequences a student must internalise:
- Two points, both fully developed → 10 + 5 = **15/15**.
- Two points, both merely named (no development) → 7 + 4 = **11/15**.
- **One** developed point only → **10/15 (67%)** — this is the ceiling for the classic OL brevity failure.
- The undeveloped-answer penalty is literally the 3m/1m "development" tranche: the OL scheme prices the difference between a *brief* and a *developed* point at 3 marks on the first point, 1 mark on the second.

This is the mechanical opposite of the HL Section 3 norm (`4@5`, four equally-weighted developed points). At OL, marks are front-loaded onto the first point, so a candidate who runs out of ideas after one point is far less punished than at HL — but a candidate who gives *only brief* points forfeits the development tranche on every part.

### Three-point and descending grids

- **"Outline three…" → `@ 5 marks each (4m + 1m)`** — three symmetric developed points (e.g. Q5(C) entrepreneurial skills, p.7; Q9(D) three insurance terms, p.8). Here all three are equal, so omitting one costs a full 5m.
- **Descending "Name three…" → `4m + 2m + 1m`** (Q3(D)(i), p.5) — recall/brevity cue; naming beats developing.
- **Section 1** uses grids like `4+3+3`, `3+3+2+2`, `@2m each`, `5×2m` (p.4) — recall/matching, the most accessible marks on the paper (2015 CER: OL Section 1 averaged ~80%, p.19).

### Cue effects at OL (which cue → which grid)

- **List / Name / State / Complete / Match / Circle / Tick** → brevity grids (descending or flat `@Nm each`); development earns nothing. The 2015 CER (p.20) confirms these cues "do not present problems" at OL.
- **Outline / Explain / Describe** → the `7+3 / 4+1` developed-point grammar; the 3m/1m development marks are exactly what a one-line answer forfeits. The 2015 CER (p.20): these cues "are often not addressed adequately."
- **Explain with reference to the text** → application to the stimulus is a *marked component* (e.g. Q4(C)(i) `7m (4m+2m+1m)` where the reference to the McGrath-family text carries marks, p.24; Q8(C)(i) `7m (4m+2m+1m)`, p.39).

### Diagram / chart marking (labels dominate)

- **Product Life Cycle, Q8(E) (p.7 grid, p.40 expected):** Title **1m**, Sales axis **1m**, Time axis **1m**, Curved line **2m**, **Labels in order 5 × 2m = 10m**. Labels are 10 of the 15 marks; a correctly-shaped unlabelled curve caps at ~4/15.
- **Break-even chart, Section 1 Q4 (p.4, p.8):** place 4 labels (BEP, TR, TC, FC) → `3 + 3 + 2 + 2`.
- Same universal rule as HL/Geography: **no title, no axis labels, no marks for them** — but at OL it is worth quantifying that the *labels are the majority of the diagram's marks.*

### Calculation marking ("show your workings" = per-line ticks)

- **Net Annual Salary, Q7(E) (p.7 grid, p.37 expected):** marked `1m + 2m×7` across the computation lines (PAYE bands, tax credit, PRSI, USC, totals). Each line of the calculation is its own tick; a bare final figure with no workings forfeits the intermediate marks. (2015 CER, p.20, also flags OL candidates arriving without a calculator.)

### Structured-document marking

- **AGM Notice & Agenda, Q6(C) (p.6 grid, p.33 expected):** Notice `4 × 1m`, Agenda `5 × 2m` ("must contain at least five items"), Signed `1m`. The agenda items are 10 of the 15 marks — brevity here (fewer than five agenda items) is directly capped.

## OL-specific candidate-behaviour findings (source + page)

1. **The brevity trap, quantified.** *"A common problem at the Ordinary level is that only one valid point is often given when two or three points are required. In some cases the same point is repeated"* (2015 CER, p.20). The 2025 scheme prices it exactly: a two-point question is `10(7+3) + 5(4+1)`, so one developed point caps you at **10/15 (67%)** and a repeated point earns nothing for the duplicate (2025 OL MS, p.5–8). *(Source: 2015 CER p.20; 2025 OL MS p.5–8.)*

2. **Development cues under-answered.** *"Question cues such as 'outline', 'explain' are often not addressed adequately… due cognisance [should be] taken of question cue word(s) and consideration given to the allocation of marks"* (2015 CER, p.20). At OL the penalty is concrete: the 3m (first point) and 1m (second point) "development" tranches are forfeited by an unelaborated answer (2025 OL MS, p.5). *(Source: 2015 CER p.20; 2025 OL MS p.5.)*

3. **Charts lose the label marks, which are the majority.** *"[Bar chart] not drawn to the proper scale in many cases and the axes and title were omitted"* (2015 CER, p.21, on OL Q6(B)). In 2025 the PLC grid makes the stakes explicit — labels are **10 of 15** marks, title + two axes only 3 (2025 OL MS, p.7 and p.40). *(Source: 2015 CER p.21; 2025 OL MS p.7, p.40.)*

4. **Answers not anchored to the stimulus.** *"Some candidates did not relate their answer on stock control to the stock of new and used cars at Mason Motor Ltd"* (2015 CER, p.21, OL Q6(E)). The 2025 scheme now marks the stimulus link directly in "Explain with reference to the text" parts — e.g. Q4(C)(i) awards marks for citing that Buildings AFD Ltd missed the 1 May completion date (2025 OL MS, p.24; also Q8(C)(i), p.39). *(Source: 2015 CER p.21; 2025 OL MS p.24, p.39.)*

5. **Calculations submitted without workings.** The 2025 net-salary question is marked line-by-line (`1m + 2m×7`), so a bare answer forfeits the per-line ticks even if the final figure is right (2025 OL MS, p.7, p.37). The 2015 CER separately notes some OL candidates *"didn't have a calculator and had some difficulty working out the answer"* (p.20) — a computation-competency gap at OL. *(Source: 2025 OL MS p.7, p.37; 2015 CER p.20.)*

6. **Section 1 is the OL bank of accessible marks.** OL Section 1 (answer 10 of 15, 100 marks) averaged ~80% in 2015 and is recall/matching — TRUE/FALSE, tick-the-box, match-the-term, fill-the-cashflow (2025 OL MS Q3, Q6, Q7, Q10, Q15, p.8–12). The application/recall marks here are *"some of the easiest to attain"* (2015 CER, p.20–21). *(Source: 2025 OL MS p.4, p.8–12; 2015 CER p.19–21.)*

## Quotable lines (with page refs)

1. **On what "developed vs brief" costs at OL** — the mechanical reading of the recurring grid (2025 OL MS, p.5):
   > *"(A) Explain two: 10 Marks (7m + 3m) / 5 Marks (4m + 1m)"* — first point worth 10, second worth 5; the 3m/1m are the development marks a brief answer loses.

2. **On chart marking at OL** — Product Life Cycle grid (2025 OL MS, p.40):
   > *"Title 1m, Sales 1m, Time 1m, Curved line 2m, Labels (in order) 5 × 2m"* — labels are 10 of the 15 marks.

3. **On the brevity trap** (2015 CER, p.20):
   > *"A common problem at the Ordinary level is that only one valid point is often given when two or three points are required. In some cases the same point is repeated."*

4. **On cues at OL** (2015 CER, p.20):
   > *"While question cues such as 'list', 'name', 'state' do not present problems, question cues such as 'outline', 'explain' are often not addressed adequately."*

## Unverified / open items

- **Section 2 choice rubric not in the marking scheme.** The 2025 OL scheme shows Part 1 = Q1–Q4 and Part 2 = Q5–Q9, each worth 75m, with four questions (4 × 75 = 300) answered — but the *exact* choice rule (how many from each Part) is set by the examination-paper rubric, which is not reproduced in the marking scheme. The 2015 CER described the then-structure as "Part 1 (choose 1 of 3) and Part 2 (choose 3 of 5)"; in 2025 Part 1 has **four** questions, so the structure has shifted since 2015. Treat the precise 2025 rubric as unconfirmed until the OL exam paper is filed.
