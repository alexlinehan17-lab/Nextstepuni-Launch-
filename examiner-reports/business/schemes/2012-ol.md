1(A) What is a contract?
This is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties.
e.g. a contract for the sale of a car.
1(B) Explain three of these elements. Illustrate your answer with reference to given text
Offer:
A voluntary proposal/promise with conditions made by one party (offeror) to another
(offeree) which must be accepted by the other party to be legally binding. The offer can
be made orally, in writing or by conduct and is a clear indication of the offeror's
willingness to enter into an agreement under specified terms.
e.g. They agreed the price of €9,000 – Joe offered €9,000 for the car
Acceptance:
Assent / agreement / consent to the terms of the offer. Offer must be accepted / taken up
by other party as it stands, without conditions.
Acceptance can be made orally, in writing or by conduct.
e.g. Michael accepted Joe’s offer of €9,000
Consideration:
Each party in a contract must give something of value to the other party / something of
value must be exchanged.
e.g. John pays Michael €9,000 and receives a car
Consent to Contract:
Both parties in a contract must enter into a contract of their own free will – no undue
pressure or duress
e.g. Michael had advertised the car for the agreed price and he and Joe signed all the
relevant documentation.
1(C) Explain ‘ Performance’ or ‘Frustration’
Performance:
Both parties to the contract fulfil their obligations and the contract is completed
Both parties have done what they agreed to do.
Frustration:
An unforeseen event prevents the contract from being completed. Something happens
which makes it impossible to complete the contract
e.g. the death or bankruptcy or insanity of one of the parties to the contract.

QUESTION 5 MANAGING
5(A) Explain the three underlined terms.
PAYE
Pay As You Earn – this is a direct tax paid by employees on their income /wages
/salaries. It is deducted at source by the employer at a specified rate and forwarded the
Revenue Commissioners. It is a statutory deduction from wages/salaries.
PRSI
Pay Related Social Insurance – this is a compulsory insurance scheme for all
employees and employers to cover unemployment, illness, maternity and disability
benefits. It is calculated as a percentage of gross income and is deducted at source by
the employer who must forward the full amount (employee and employer portion) to
the state. It is a statutory deduction from wages/salaries.
Tax Credit
A tax credit is a reduction in the amount of tax payable by the taxpayer and depends on
the taxpayer’s personal circumstances. Gross Tax less Tax Credits = Tax Payable.
Examples of tax credits include: Blind Credit, Dependent Relative Credit, Guide Dog
Allowance, Home Carer Credit, Incapacitated Child Credit, One Parent Family Credit,
PAYE Credit, Rent Credit, Widow/Widower's Credit.
5(B) Explain two of the following Principles of Insurance and give one example in each
case.
Insurable Interest:
A person seeking insurance must benefit financially from the continued existence of
the item and suffer by its loss/damage.
e.g. people can insure their own house but have no insurable interest in insuring their
neighbour’s house.
Indemnity:
A person cannot make a profit from an insurance claim. The compensation received
from the insurer will be equal to the financial loss suffered. This protects the insurer
from over-insurance.
e.g. car insured for €10,000. Market value of €8,000. Insured will only receive €8,000
compensation in the event of a write-off.
Utmost Good Faith
When completing a proposal form for insurance, the insured must disclose truthfully
all material facts to the insurer. A material fact is anything which affects the level of
risk the insurer is being asked to cover and therefore the premium charged.
e.g. when taking out motor insurance, type of licence, previous claims (if any), age and
gender must all be disclosed.
