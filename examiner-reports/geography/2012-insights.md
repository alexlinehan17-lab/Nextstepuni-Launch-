# Geography 2012 — Examiner Insights (Chief Examiner's Report)

## Source

- **Report type:** Chief Examiner's Report (SEC) — combined Ordinary Level and Higher Level
  report; this summary focuses on the **Higher Level** section (Section 3, pp.20–34)
- **Year:** 2012 — the most recent published SEC Chief Examiner's Report for LC Geography as of
  retrieval (2026-07-06)
- **Levels:** Higher / Ordinary
- **Original filename:** `2012_Chief_Examiner_Report_LC_Geography.pdf` (examinations.ie archive;
  fetched via the Internet Archive mirror of the official URL)
- **Renamed to:** `2012-chief-examiner.pdf` (34 pages; page refs are the printed page numbers,
  which match PDF pages)
- **Context:** Examination structure then as now — coursework Geographical Investigation 100
  marks (20%) + written paper 400 marks (80%): Part One short answers 80, Core Units 1–2 @ 80,
  Elective 80, Options essay 80 (Table 7, p.20). HL cohort 2012: 20,142; ABC rate 74.1%
  (Table 8, p.20). Read alongside `2025-marking-scheme.md` — the marking grammar it references
  (SRPs, Overall Coherence, GI sections) is still the current system.

## Common errors by question type (Higher Level)

### Geographical Investigation (average 84/100 from sample, p.21)

- Marks are awarded "on a section by section basis, and only to material which is relevant to
  that section"; a minority lost marks "due to a failure to associate specific aspects of the
  investigation process with corresponding sections of the reporting booklet" (p.21).
- "a significant number of candidates tended to overdevelop aspects of the investigation and
  included superfluous material in the various sections" (p.22).
- Planning: "marks were lost where candidates tended to be vague and failed to provide specific
  detail regarding the actual websites, textbooks, maps etc. used" (p.22).
- Results vs conclusions: "Almost all candidates were awarded full marks for results", but
  "some candidates simply restated the results without drawing any conclusions" (p.22);
  evaluations "were often repetitive" (p.23).
- Presentation: "some candidates failed to present a second different method of presentation.
  In some instances axes were poorly labelled or labelling was omitted" (p.23).

### Part One — Short answers (average 66/80, p.23)

- Generally strong. Specific slips: confusing arête / pyramidal peak / lateral moraine (Q1,
  p.23); difficulty identifying the asthenosphere (Q5, p.23); brief definitions of regions
  poorly done (Q6(ii), p.23).

### Part Two — Structured questions (averages 36–44 out of 80 per question; Table 10, p.24)

- "a significant number of candidates attempted more than the required number of questions and
  in some instances attempted questions from both the elective units" (p.24).
- **Wrong-process zero**: on the deposition landform question, "a significant number of
  candidates presented very detailed explanations on the formation of waterfalls which are
  landforms of erosion and were therefore awarded no marks" (p.25).
- **Theory without the link**: candidates "over emphasised the description of the theory of
  plate tectonics, and failed to link the theory to the distribution of earthquakes" (p.25);
  "provided an overview of population in the region rather than focusing specifically on
  distribution" (Q4C, p.26); "simply described the tertiary economic activity without examining
  factors that influence its development" (Q6B, p.27); "a mere description of the multinational
  company with no reference to the influence of the corporate strategies" (Q9B, p.28).
- Sketch maps: high standard overall, but "some candidates drew a sketch of the Ordnance Survey
  map instead of the aerial photograph as the question required" (p.25; also p.28); proportion
  problems, especially connecting roads/streets (p.29).
- Small-mark leaks: "Marks were lost when candidates omitted the unit for the Euro" (Q5A, p.26);
  OS "map evidence was plentiful [but] candidates failed to elaborate in sufficient detail in
  their explanations" (Q7B, p.27); mislabelled or unlabelled graph axes (Q8A, p.28; Q12A, p.30).

### Options essays (Q13–24, p.30)

- "The most common areas of weakness continue to be poor structure and coherence in answers."
- "a significant number of candidates continue to ignore this instruction [the three-or-four-
  aspects rubric] and instead provide too little detail on far too many themes/arguments" (p.30).
- "some candidates misread questions and presented extensive descriptive material that related
  to the topic of the question but did not answer the specific question asked" (p.30) — e.g.
  Q18 Geoecology: "comprehensive descriptions of each of the activities listed but failed to
  examine the impact of these activities on biomes" (p.31).
- "a minority of candidates did not attempt this section of the examination paper which had a
  negative impact on overall grade outcomes for those candidates" (p.30; repeated p.32).
- Geoecology was taken by over 80% of candidates; Q16 answers on podzolisation were "poor in
  many cases" and often "did not deal with each of [the] terms" (p.31).

## Strategic / structural observations

- Poor examination technique list (Conclusions, p.32): "not reading questions carefully,
  failure to answer the question asked, attempting more than the required number of questions,
  failing to answer all of the required parts within individual questions, or leaving sections
  unanswered".
- Time misuse cuts both ways: "there was evidence that some candidates did not use the full
  time allocation with the presentation of cryptic and underdeveloped responses" (p.32).
- The coursework buffer: the GI component is "often particularly beneficial to those who do not
  perform well in the written examination" (p.32).
- Recommendations to students (p.34): concentrate on "three or four aspects of theme rather
  than giving a superficial treatment of a large number of points"; "become familiar with all
  the key terms in geography and all the instructional words"; "practice drawing simple
  annotated diagrams"; "read all the examination questions at the beginning of the examination".

## Misconceptions

- Waterfalls treated as landforms of deposition (they are erosional) — detailed answers earned
  no marks (p.25).
- Physical-landscape regions confused with economic/socio-economic criteria for defining
  regions (Q5B, p.27).
- Weathering vs erosion: "A large number of candidates were unable to explain the difference
  between the process of weathering and the process of erosion" (Q3A, p.26).
- Urban problems in the developing world misread as urban sprawl in the developed world
  (Q10B, p.29).
