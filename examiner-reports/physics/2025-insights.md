## Paper structure and mark allocation

- The paper totals **400 marks** (confirmed by the Irish-bonus table on PDF p.5, headed "Tábla 400 @ 10%" — the "400 @ 10%" table, and by instruction 11 stating 75% of the total = 300 marks).
- Candidates answer a subset of questions on a **best-of** basis (Question 1 or 2 is the mandatory experiment-style item; the remaining long questions are chosen). The scheme lists every question's mark ladder inline; the paper (not the scheme) fixes how many must be answered.
- Marks inside a question are recorded as bracketed values next to each markable element, e.g. `[3]`, `[6 x 1]`, `[2 + 2]`, `[3 + 3]`, `[2 + 1]`. The sum across the listed elements is the question total.

## The mark notation (structure of the scheme, PDF p.3, printed "points to be noted")

The scheme is terse and key-word driven. Instruction list (PDF p.3, instructions 1–5):

1. **Key words in context.** "In many instances only key words are given — words that must appear in the correct context in the candidate's answer in order to merit the assigned marks." (i.e. a right word in a wrong statement does not automatically score.)
2. **Single solidus `/` = equally acceptable alternatives.** "Words, expressions or statements separated by a solidus, /, are alternatives which are equally acceptable."
3. **Double solidus `//` = mutually exclusive answers.** "Answers that are separated by a double solidus, //, are answers which are mutually exclusive. A partial answer from one side of the // may not be taken in conjunction with a partial answer from the other side." (This is the Physics-specific twist: `//` fences off two complete alternative methods so a candidate cannot cherry-pick half of each.)
4. **Non-exhaustive scheme.** "The descriptions, methods and definitions in the scheme are not exhaustive and alternative valid answers are acceptable."
5. **Detail scales with the marks.** "The detail required in any answer is determined by the context and manner in which the question is asked, and also by the number of marks assigned to the answer … it may vary from year to year."

**Bracketed-mark shorthand seen in the question bodies:**
- `[3]` — a single markable element worth 3 marks (the default granule; most short points are worth 3).
- `[6 x 1]` — six independently markable sub-elements at 1 mark each (e.g. six correctly drawn force arrows on a diagram, Q1(iii), PDF p.6).
- `[2 + 2]`, `[3 + 3]`, `[2 + 1]` — a compound element split into named parts, each scoring separately (e.g. two required external forces `[3 + 3]`, Q2(vi)(a), PDF p.7).
- `[–1 if no label present on diagram]` — a targeted one-mark deduction attached to a specific element (Q3(i), PDF p.8).

## How numerical problems are marked (formula → substitution → answer → unit)

Physics numerical parts are marked as a **step ladder of equal (usually 3-mark) granules**, one granule per stage of the calculation. Verified worked patterns:

- **Q3(iv) speed of sound from a graph (PDF p.8):** `slope formula [3]` → `calculate slope [3]` → `v = 4 × slope or c = fλ [3]` → `v ≈ 343.7 m s⁻¹ [3]`. Four independent 3-mark granules: **quote the formula, do the substitution/intermediate calculation, apply the relating equation, state the final answer.** A candidate who writes the correct formula but stops still banks the formula granule.
- **Q1(v) net force (PDF p.6):** `forces up = 8.9 (N) [3]` / `forces down = 8.96 (N) [3]` / `net force (= 0.06 N) ≈ 0 [3]` — each intermediate quantity is its own granule; the final "≈ 0" conclusion granule is separate.
- **Q1(vi) moments (PDF p.6):** `M = Fd [3]` → `any one moment calculated [3]` → `clockwise moments calculated [3]` / `anticlockwise moments calculated [3]`. Note the **`any one moment calculated [3]`** granule — partial numerical work is explicitly rewarded before the full sum.
- **Q2(v) conservation of momentum (PDF p.7):** `p = mv [3]` → `pₐ = 0.108 / p_B = 0.105 [3]` → `p_total (= 0.003) ≈ 0 [3]`.

**General rule of thumb:** writing the correct **formula** is worth its own mark independent of getting the number right; the **substitution/calculation** is a separate mark; the **final numerical answer** is a separate mark; and the **unit** is policed by a global deduction (below), not usually by a dedicated granule.

## Fixed deductions and unit / scale / slip rules (PDF p.3–4, instructions 6–9)

These are the load-bearing "how you lose one mark" rules:

- **Units (instruction 6):** "For omission of appropriate units (or for incorrect units) in final answers, **one mark is deducted**, unless otherwise indicated." A missing or wrong unit on a final answer costs exactly one mark — not the whole answer.
- **Graph scale (instruction 7):** "When drawing graphs, **one mark is deducted** for use of an inappropriate scale."
- **Arithmetical slip (instruction 8):** "**Each time** an arithmetical slip occurs in a calculation, **one mark is deducted**." The penalty is per-slip and repeatable, but the method marks around the slip survive — this is the Physics analogue of error-carried-forward: **a slip costs one mark, not the answer, and subsequent steps built on the slipped value are still creditable** (there is no clause voiding downstream work). ECF is applied through per-question marking rather than a single stated rule.
- **NR vs zero (instruction 9):** "A zero should only be recorded when the candidate has attempted the question item but does not merit marks. If a candidate does not attempt a question item, examiners should record **NR**." (Non-attempt is logged distinctly from a scored-zero attempt.)

**No explicit significant-figures rule** appears in the general instructions — the scheme does not impose a blanket sig-fig penalty; accepted final answers are quoted to a sensible precision (e.g. `343.7 m s⁻¹`, `0.54 m s⁻¹`) and the unit rule (instruction 6) is the only precision-adjacent global deduction.

## Examiner annotation palette (PDF p.4, instruction 10)

Scripts are annotated online per marking-conference direction. Symbols:

| Symbol | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ✗ | Cross | Incorrect element |
| ✓ | Tick | Correct element (**0 marks** — i.e. correct but not a scoring granule) |
| ✓ⁿ | Tick n | Correct element (**n marks**) |
| ∿ (horizontal wavy) | Horizontal wavy line | To be noticed |
| ∿ (vertical wavy) | Vertical wavy line | Additional page |
| −1 | −1 | One mark deducted |
| ^ | Caret | Missing element |

The **"Tick n"** symbol is the key one: examiners write the number of marks awarded directly beside a correct element, so a marked script is legible as a running per-granule tally.

## Irish-language bonus (PDF p.5, instruction 11)

"Bonus marks at the rate of **10% of the marks obtained** will be given to a candidate who answers entirely through Irish and who obtains **75% or less** of the total mark available (i.e. **300 marks or less**). In calculating the bonus to be applied **decimals are always rounded down, not up** — e.g. 4.5 becomes 4; 4.9 becomes 4." Above 300 marks a **tapered table** ("Tábla 400 @ 10%") applies, sliding the bonus from 29 marks (301–303) down to 0 (397–400).

## Documented candidate-behaviour hooks (for authored "how marking works" scripts)

Each is grounded in a specific scheme feature (structural, not a model answer):

1. **The formula is worth a mark on its own.** In every numerical part the relating equation is its own `[3]` granule (`M = Fd`, `p = mv`, `slope formula`, `v = 4 × slope or c = fλ`; PDF p.6–8). A student who "shows the formula, then blanks" still banks marks — writing the equation down first is never wasted.
2. **A wrong unit costs one mark, not the answer.** Instruction 6 (PDF p.3): a right number with a missing/incorrect unit loses exactly one mark. The lesson: always append the unit, but never abandon a numerical answer because you're unsure of the unit.
3. **An arithmetical slip is a one-mark, per-occurrence penalty — keep going.** Instruction 8 (PDF p.3): each slip = −1, and downstream steps built on the slipped value are still credited. Panicking and restarting from scratch after a slip forfeits the method marks already earned.
4. **`//` means commit to one method.** Instruction 3 (PDF p.3): where the scheme offers two mutually exclusive routes (e.g. "length of card // distance between dots", Q2(ii), PDF p.7), a candidate who mixes half of one method with half of the other cannot combine the partials — pick a lane.
5. **Right word, wrong context ≠ marks.** Instruction 1 (PDF p.3): key words "must appear in the correct context." Keyword-stuffing a definition without the correct surrounding statement does not score — the same principle the Biology scheme enforces with its xylem/osmosis example.
