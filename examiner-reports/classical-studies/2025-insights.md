## The paper at a glance — two written sections + coursework

The overall LC Classical Studies assessment is a 400-mark written paper (2.5 hrs) plus a
100-mark Research Study Report coursework component. The written paper:

- **Section A — Stimulus Questions, 200 marks (PDF pp.6–9):** Ten questions (Q1–Q10), each
  built on a stimulus (text extract / image / passage). Most split into sub-parts (a)/(b)/(c)
  with small, fixed mark values (2–20 marks). Marked by **banded point tariffs**, not by
  units of development.
- **Section B — Extended Answers, 200 marks (PDF pp.10–12):** Q11(a) is a two-part guided
  question (a banded 25-mark analysis grid + a 15-mark personal response); **Q11(b) through
  Q16 are 80-mark essay-style questions** all marked by one **common scheme** — Development of
  Material (60) + Overall Quality (20).
- **Research Study Report (RSR), 100 marks (PDF pp.4–5):** coursework, marked on descriptor
  grids for Sections A/B/C plus Overall Coherence.

> "General Information: 0 marks given if an answer is not attempted or not valid according to
> marking scheme." (PDF p.6)

## The Section B essay grammar — "units of development" + Overall Quality (PDF pp.11–12)

This is the single most important structural rule of the paper. Every 80-mark essay (Q11b–Q16)
is marked under **two components that always sum to 80**:

> "For each of these essay style questions a common marking scheme is employed and it is divided
> into two components:
> • Development of material required to fully answer the question (60 marks)
> • Overall quality of the answer (20 marks)" (PDF p.11)

### Development of Material — 60 marks, four "units of development" P1–P4 (PDF p.11)

The examiner is instructed to **look for four units of development, worth 15 marks each**, and
grade each unit on how far it is *developed* (not merely stated):

> "• 15 marks per unit of development … • Look for 4 units of development (P1, P2, P3, P4)" (PDF p.11)

A **unit of development** has a strict three-part anatomy (this is the paragraph definition the
whole essay hinges on):

> "Features of a unit of development: makes relevant point, includes relevant evidence to
> support the point (knowledge/understanding), and develops the point
> (analysis/elaboration/discussion)." (PDF p.11)

Per-unit grading band (out of the 15-mark ceiling, PDF p.11):

| Level | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| Highly-developed | 15 | "15 for a highly-developed point" |
| Well-developed | 13–14 | "13-14 for a well-developed point" |
| Developed | 11–12 | "11-12 marks for a developed point" |
| Basic | 3–10 | "3-10 marks for a basic point" |

So a point that states a fact but adds no evidence + development is capped in the "basic"
3–10 band; reaching the 15-mark ceiling per unit requires point + evidence + analysis. The
worked structure diagram on PDF p.12 shows the intended shape: each paragraph either "Makes
new point, includes evidence and makes development" OR "Expands on previous point by including
new evidence and new/further development."

### Overall Quality — 20 marks, three bands (PDF p.11)

A separate holistic mark for engagement with the question and command of the material:

| Band | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim, condensed) |
|---|---|---|
| High Quality | 18–20 | "analysis / evaluation is coherent, well-structured, and supported by relevant, accurate and varied points." |
| Good Quality | 13–17 | "analysis focuses on the question; points are well-structured, and supported by relevant and accurate evidence." |
| Low Quality | 1–12 | "limited engagement with the question; attempt to make points, but there is a lack of evidence/examples to support points made; **relies mostly on narrative**." |

The Low-Quality band names the classic failure mode explicitly — **narrative instead of
argument.** The distinguishing feature of the top essay grade is developed, evidence-backed
analysis, not more facts.

### Overall H-grade descriptor grid for the essays (PDF p.12)

The scheme also gives an overall-mark descriptor grid mapping the 80-mark total onto exam
grades, and it ties each grade to how many *developed* points the essay contains:

| Grade | Marks | Key marker (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | 72–80 | "At least two highly-developed points" |
| H2 | 64–71 | "Mostly well-developed points" |
| H3 | 56–63 | "Some well-developed points" |
| H4 | 48–55 | discussion "is limited"; "some creative/critical thinking is shown" |
| H5 | 40–47 | "weak or irrelevant evidence"; "a basic level of creative/critical thinking" |
| H6–8 | 1–39 | "limited knowledge … no coherent structure … relies … lack purpose" |

### The sub-divided-essay cap (PDF p.11)

Where a Section B essay has two parts (e.g. Q12 a+b), answering only one part is capped hard on
both components:

> "• Answer must address the full question (parts a and b), for more than three units of
> development to be included in the marks for Development of Material
> • Answer must address the full question (parts a and b), for its Overall Quality to be
> considered above the Low Quality range
> • Material included in each part can be awarded to either part, where relevant, but in such a
> way so that it is not counted twice." (PDF p.11)

So a one-sided answer to a two-part essay is capped at **3 units of development (max ~45 of 60)
AND Low Quality (max 12 of 20)** — a structural ceiling of roughly 57/80 regardless of how good
the one side is. This is Classical Studies' equivalent of History's "TWO elements … Max CM = 50"
haircut.

### Q11(a) — the one banded 25-mark analysis grid (PDF p.10)

Q11(a)(i) is marked on a five-band descriptor grid out of 25 (Excellent/Comprehensive 22–25 …
Very Basic 1–6), and the band is pinned to a concrete evidence count — the top two bands both
require **"Two valid phrases/sentences identified"** with the analysis quality separating them,
while the bottom band has "One valid phrase/sentence identified, no analysis." Q11(a)(ii) is a
15-mark personal response graded full/partial/basic with the recurring yes/no floor rule (below).

## The Section A short-answer grammar — banded point tariffs (PDF pp.6–9)

Section A does **not** use units of development. Each sub-question has its own small band, and
the bands almost always run **full → partial → basic**, with the mark for a sub-part fixed by
the quality tier reached, not by counting points. Examples of the tariff shape:

- Simple recall parts are answer-key style: "1 mark each: true, false, false" (Q1a, p.6);
  "1 mark each for four distinct points" (Q9a, p.9), with a list of acceptable answers.
- Developed parts scale by tier, e.g. the 12-mark Q2c: "12-14 marks – full; well-developed with
  evidence / 9-11 – partial; good development, some evidence / 6-8 – incomplete … mostly generic
  points / 1-5 – basic; one valid point" (p.6).
- "Well-developed" is explicitly worth more than a plain point: "One well-developed point or two
  developed points for full marks" (Q3b, p.6; same phrasing at Q5a, Q8b) — depth substitutes for
  breadth.

### Recurring Section A cap/floor rules (the mechanical ones)

- **The yes/no floor** — recurs across the paper: "1 mark – only a yes or no answer, no other
  valid point made." (Q1b p.6; Q6b p.8; Q11aii p.10). Stating a stance with no justification
  scores the absolute minimum.
- **Address both sides / both references or lose the top band:** "Discussion of both the text
  and the image for full marks" (Q1b, p.6); "Experience of audiences at both venues should be
  described for full marks … reference to both (not nec. in equal amount)" (Q5b, p.7); for the
  reliability question, full marks need "specific evidence in extract and another text"; a
  one-text answer is capped: "4 marks – basic, at least one accurate point, only one author/text
  referenced" (Q8b, p.9).
- **The half-question cap on the 20-mark Q7:** three strategies at 7+7+6, "**if military or
  political is not addressed, 14 marks max**" (Q7, p.8) — ignoring one required dimension caps
  a 20-mark question at 14.
- **The confusion cap:** "**max 3 marks if Greek and Roman roles/practices are confused**"
  (Q9c, p.9) — conflating the two cultures collapses the mark.
- **The "top band needs external evidence" rule:** for the discussion parts, the top band
  requires going beyond the printed stimulus: "Must refer to specific points from the extract
  **and other studied evidence** for top band" (Q6a, p.8).

## Research Study Report (RSR) — 100 marks, descriptor grids (PDF pp.4–5)

The 20% coursework is marked on banded descriptor grids, one per element:

- **Section A – Rationale & Approach (10):** Good engagement 6–10 / Engagement lacking 0–5.
  Looks for aim, link to prescribed topic, overview of approach/sources — "**All three points
  not required for full marks**" but "requires specific points related to project" (p.4).
- **Section B – Extended Essay, three separate 20-mark descriptors** (p.4), each banded Very
  Thorough 16–20 / Thorough 10–15 / Basic 5–9 / Very Basic 0–4:
  - *Knowledge/Understanding* — top band = "synthesis of accurate knowledge that is in-depth,
    wide ranging, and highly relevant"; bottom = "only generic or superficial information."
  - *Analysis/Critical thinking* — top = "logically argued with highly developed points";
    bottom = "does not engage with analysis / critical thinking."
  - *Evidence/Research* — top = "active incorporation of sources, research, evidence into the
    essay … sources examined, analysed, used as evidence"; **bottom band = "sources included
    only in bibliography."** Listing sources without using them scores the floor.
- **Section C – Review & Reflection (10):** Good engagement 6–10 / lacking 0–5 (p.5).
- **Overall Coherence – Communication & Structure (20):** Very coherent 16–20 … Incoherent 0–4;
  rewards organised structure, staying on topic, avoiding repetition, and "sources of research
  correctly and consistently acknowledged" (p.5).

Two examiner instructions frame the whole RSR marking (PDF p.5):

> "Read everything in the RSR before marking anything."
> "Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length."

## General examiner conventions / annotations (PDF p.3)

The online-marking annotation table exposes the machinery of the two systems:

- **Square brackets** = "Material counted towards each unit of development (point) for Questions
  11b–16" — the examiner literally brackets each essay unit.
- **P1, P2, P3, P4** = "Each unit of development (point) awarded marks in Questions 11b–16" —
  the four points the scheme tells examiners to "look for".
- **Q** = "The mark for Overall Quality in Questions 11b–16."
- **Tick (blank)** = "A page has been read and any candidate material has been considered
  towards the mark" — recognition, not a mark; **Tick with number** = "The number of marks
  awarded (e.g. 5 marks)" is the one that credits.
- **NR** = a full question not attempted; **X** = incorrect material; **Zero** = zero marks.
- **K / A / E / OC** = the four RSR descriptors (Knowledge, Analysis, Evidence, Overall
  Coherence).
