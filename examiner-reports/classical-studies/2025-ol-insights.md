## Headline: OL uses the SAME two marking grammars as HL, but scaled down and simplified

Ordinary Level keeps both HL marking systems — (1) banded point-tariffs for Section A short answers,
and (2) the **"units of development" + Overall Quality** system for the Section B essays — and the
same online-marking annotations (square brackets, P1–P4, Q, K/A/E/OC on PDF p.3, identical to HL).
The RSR (100 marks) is marked on descriptor grids that are near-identical in shape to HL. The
differences are all in **mark weighting, band structure, and the removal of the top tiers / the
two-part cap**, not in the underlying method.

### The mark-split is inverted vs HL — Section A is the bigger prize at OL

| | Higher | Ordinary |
|---|---|---|
| Section A (short answers) | 200 marks | **300 marks** (PDF p.6) |
| Section B (extended answers) | 200 marks | **100 marks** (PDF p.12) |
| Written total | 400 | 400 |
| RSR coursework | 100 | 100 |

At OL, 300 of the 400 written marks sit in the recall/short-answer Section A (Q1–Q10, ten 30-mark
questions), and only 100 in the essays. HL splits it 200/200. So essay-writing craft is worth far
less at OL, and Section A accuracy is worth far more — the opposite emphasis.

## Marking-grammar STRUCTURE — OL vs HL, point by point

### 1. Yes, OL uses "units of development" + "Overall Quality" (PDF pp.12–13)

The essay marking is explicitly the same two-component split, verbatim on PDF p.12:

> "Each of these questions (11bi, 11bii, 11biii) has the same marking scheme, which is divided
> between two components: • Development of material required to fully answer the question (20 marks) •
> Overall quality of the answer (10 marks)."

And the **unit-of-development definition is the same three-part anatomy** — with ONE softening. HL
requires the candidate to "develop the point"; OL requires only that the candidate **"makes SOME
development of the point"** (PDF pp.12, 13):

> "Features of a unit of development: candidate makes relevant point, includes relevant evidence to
> support the point (knowledge/understanding) and makes **some development** of the point
> (analysis/elaboration/discussion)."

### 2. Essays are smaller and the unit structure is FLEXIBLE, not fixed at four

HL: every essay is 80 marks = 60 Development (fixed **4 units × 15**) + 20 Quality.

OL has **two different essay tariffs**, and the number of units is not fixed at four:
- **Q11bi/bii/biii — 30 marks each** = 20 Development + 10 Quality. Development = **2 units × 10
  marks** (PDF p.12).
- **Q12–Q16 — 50 marks each** = 30 Development + 20 Quality. Development uses an **"indicative
  structure of units: (15 + 15), (10+10+10), (8+8+7+7)"** (PDF p.13) — i.e. the candidate may make
  two, three, or four units and the marks flex to fit. HL never offers this flexibility.

### 3. The per-unit band DROPS the top "highly-developed" tier at OL

| | Higher (per 15-mark unit) | Ordinary Q12–16 (per 15-mark unit) | Ordinary Q11b (per 10-mark unit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top tier | Highly-developed = 15 | — (no highly-developed tier) | — |
| | Well-developed = 13–14 | **Well-developed = 11–15** | — |
| | Developed = 11–12 | **Developed = 8–10** | **Developed = 10** |
| | Basic = 3–10 | **Basic = 1–7** | **Basic = 7** |
| Floor | — | — | **Very basic = 4** |

HL has a four-tier per-unit ladder topped by a "highly-developed = 15" ceiling. OL Q12–16 collapses
this to **three tiers (well-developed / developed / basic)** with no "highly-developed" label (PDF
p.13). OL Q11b uses a three-point ladder of fixed values 10 / 7 / 4 (PDF p.12).

### 4. Overall Quality is BINARY at OL — the "High Quality" band is gone

HL Overall Quality has three bands: High 18–20 / Good 13–17 / Low 1–12.

OL Overall Quality has only **two bands — Good and Low** (PDF pp.12, 13):
- Q11b: **Good 8–10 / Low 1–7** (PDF p.12)
- Q12–16: **Good 15–20 / Low 1–14** (PDF p.13)

There is no top "High Quality" tier at OL. The ceiling of the essay is the "Good Quality" descriptor,
which is worded like HL's *middle* band ("Focuses on the question; points are structured and
supported by relevant and accurate evidence").

### 5. The narrative cap is IDENTICAL — narrative is still the Low-Quality trap

The Low-Quality descriptor is word-for-word the HL one (PDF pp.12, 13):

> "Low Quality: Limited engagement with the question; attempt to make points, but there is a lack of
> evidence/examples to support points made; **relies mostly on narrative**."

So the single most transferable message — *argument beats storytelling* — survives unchanged into OL.
Retelling the myth/plot instead of answering the question caps the essay in the Low band on the
Quality component.

### 6. The HL "two-part rule" (sub-divided-essay cap) is ABSENT at OL

This is the cleanest structural difference. At HL, a two-part essay (parts a+b) carries an explicit
cap: a one-sided answer is limited to "more than three units of development" being disallowed AND
Overall Quality held in the Low range (HL PDF p.11). **The OL scheme contains no equivalent
"answer must address the full question (parts a and b)" cap** — the Q11b sub-questions are marked as
independent 30-mark items, and Q12–16 carry no a/b two-part cap language (PDF pp.12–13). OL simply
does not have the structural haircut HL uses to punish half-answered two-part essays.

### 7. The overall essay descriptor grid is generic, NOT grade-labelled

HL closes with an H1–H8 grid (PDF p.12) that ties each grade to a *count of developed points* ("H1
72–80: at least two highly-developed points").

OL closes with a **four-band generic grid for Q12–16** (PDF p.14) labelled by quality words, NOT exam
grades, and pinned to knowledge-relevance + evidence + question-coverage rather than a point count:

| Marks | Band | Key marker |
|---|---|---|
| 41–50 | Very Good / Full | good knowledge but not all relevant; addresses the question but not always sustained/coherent |
| 31–40 | Good / Partial | some knowledge not relevant; some aspects of the question not addressed |
| 21–30 | Basic | many details irrelevant; many aspects of the question not addressed |
| 1–20 | Very Basic | limited knowledge; most/all aspects of the question not addressed |

The recurring OL discriminator across every band is **relevance and whether the question is
addressed** — not (as at HL) how many points reach "highly-developed."

### 8. Section A — same banded-tariff family, softer floors

OL Section A (Q1–Q10, PDF pp.6–11) uses the same banded point-tariff shape as HL Section A, but the
bands are typically shorter — usually just **partial/full → basic/incomplete → very basic**, or
fixed split-marks like 9+3, 7+3, 5+5, 20+10. Two mechanical differences from HL worth noting:
- **The Greek/Roman confusion penalty is softer.** HL applies a hard "max 3 marks if Greek and Roman
  roles/practices are confused." OL instead lists "confusion between Greek and Roman customs" as a
  feature of the **lower band** (Q3b, 5 of 10 marks — PDF p.7), a demotion rather than a hard cap.
- **No explicit "yes/no = 1 mark" floor.** The HL yes/no floor rule does not appear at OL; OL leans
  on the generic "very basic" bottom tier instead.

## OL-specific candidate-behaviour findings (source + page)

1. **Copying the stimulus in your own words matters — verbatim repetition is capped.** Q7(a) is
   explicitly capped at "2 marks – only repeating text as it appears (not in own words)" out of 6
   (PDF p.9). Paraphrasing/understanding is rewarded; transcribing the passage is not. This own-words
   cap is an OL-specific mechanical rule (HL 2025 has no equivalent verbatim-repetition floor at this
   granularity).

2. **Opinion questions score nothing at the top without stated reasons.** Multiple OL "give your
   opinion" parts add the rider "clear reasons must be included" (Q5d, PDF p.8) / "clear reasons must
   be included for top marks" (Q7c, PDF p.9), and Q10d requires "at least one clear example" (PDF
   p.11). A bare opinion is held in the very-basic band.

3. **Confusing Greek and Roman customs demotes you to the lower band.** Q3(b) funeral-customs answer:
   "5 marks very basic/basic (general information … confusion between Greek and Roman customs)" (PDF
   p.7) — mixing the two cultures caps the part at half marks.

4. **Narrative instead of argument is the essay trap (same as HL).** The Low-Quality descriptor
   "relies mostly on narrative" applies to every Section B essay (PDF pp.12, 13). Retelling the
   story rather than answering caps the 10- or 20-mark Quality component in the Low band.

5. **RSR: sources must be USED, not just listed.** In the Evidence/Research descriptor, "Sources
   included only in bibliography" is the **Very Basic (0–8) floor** (PDF p.4); the Thorough band
   requires "sources included as part of discussion / development of points." Identical trap to HL —
   a bibliography is not evidence.

6. **"Specific" beats "general" everywhere in Section A.** The recurring OL band split is
   partial/full = "specific" vs very basic/basic = "general" (e.g. Q2c, Q7c, Q8b/c, Q10c — PDF
   pp.6–11). Naming concrete detail is the single most repeated route out of the bottom band at OL.
