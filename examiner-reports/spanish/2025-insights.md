## Match / difference vs French (headline)

**Spanish is only partly a "two-axis" system, and this is the key finding.** French marks *all* written production on the two-column Communication/Language grid. Spanish reserves the two-axis grid for **one** task — Section B Q5 (the 50-mark "Linked Question", Content-Communication 25 + Language 25, p.[9]). The **shorter productive tasks in Section C** (dialogue/letter 30 marks; diary/note 20 marks) are **not** marked on the two-axis grid: each "turn" or "point" is marked *individually* on a single descending band scale keyed to "communicative intention fulfilled" + "verbs correct" (p.[10]–[11]). So the two-axis grammar exists in Spanish but is narrower than in French.

Other same/different notes are flagged inline below.

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — the two-axis grid (Section B Q5 ONLY)

Section B Q5 is the "Linked Question" (50 marks, p.[9]) and is the *only* task marked on the two-column grid. Content/Communication and Language are scored separately and equally (25 + 25), then summed — the same principle as French, but applied to just this one task.

### Grid — verbatim descriptors (p.[9])

Each axis has three bands (TOP / MIDDLE / BOTTOM), each worth 0–25, chosen by descriptor fit then a mark picked within the band.

**1. CONTENT / COMMUNICATION — 25 marks**

| Band | Descriptors | Range |
|---|---|---|
| TOP | High level of coherence · Clear argumentation · Communicative intention fulfilled · Little or no irrelevant material | 18–25 |
| MIDDLE | Reasonable level of coherence · **Comprehensible to Spanish speaker** · Communicative intention more or less fulfilled · Some irrelevant material | 9–17 |
| BOTTOM | Lack of coherence · **Spanish speaker would have difficulty understanding** · Communicative intention not fulfilled · A lot of irrelevant material | 0–8 |

**2. LANGUAGE — 25 marks**

| Band | Descriptors | Range |
|---|---|---|
| TOP | Idiomatic Spanish · Good level of vocabulary · Few grammatical/spelling mistakes · Correct usage of tenses | 18–25 |
| MIDDLE | Vocabulary quite good — generally adequate and appropriate · Verbs generally correct · Not too many spelling/grammatical mistakes | 9–17 |
| BOTTOM | Problems with vocabulary — limited range · Most verbs incorrect · Many spelling mistakes | 0–8 |

**Two structural rules attached to this grid (p.[9]) — both are teaching-critical:**
- **Language is contingent on Content:** *"Where no marks are awarded for Content 1 (i.e. effort totally irrelevant), no marks will be awarded for Language 2."* This is a **difference from French**, where the two axes are fully independent — in Spanish, zero Content forces zero Language (you cannot score on accurate-but-irrelevant Spanish here).
- **Lifting the text scores zero:** *"No marks will be awarded for phrases taken directly from the text."* (French penalises "mere transcription" into the BOTTOM Communication band; Spanish makes copied phrases an outright non-scorer in the production piece.)

The **"Spanish speaker" / "Spanish monoglot" test** is the operational line between MIDDLE and BOTTOM Communication (MIDDLE = "comprehensible to Spanish speaker"; BOTTOM = "Spanish speaker would have difficulty understanding") — the same device French uses with the "French monoglot".

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — the per-point band scale (Section C production)

The shorter productive tasks use a **different grammar** from the grid above. Each unit is marked individually against a single holistic band scale in which "communicative intention" and verb correctness co-determine the mark — content and language are *not* split into two columns.

### General Section C rules (p.[10])
- Candidates attempt **either Q1(a) or 1(b) AND either Q2(a) or 2(b)**. If a candidate answers both parts of a question, "both must be marked and marks will be awarded only for the higher scoring of the two."
- **"Minor inaccuracies (i.e. which do not distort 'message') should be ignored."**
- **"Verbs must be correct for full marks."**
- **"In any part of written production the same mistake will not be penalised twice."** (Matches the French anti-double-penalty principle.)
- For Q1, "the priorities indicated for Content and Language in Section B, Q.5 should be broadly adhered to" — i.e. the grid still informs the holistic judgement even though it is not applied column-by-column.

### Q1 — Dialogue (a) / Letter–Email (b): 30 marks, 5 units × max 6 (p.[10])
Mark **each 'turn' (dialogue) / each point (letter) individually**:

| Mark | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 6 | Communicative intention fulfilled. Verbs must be correct for full marks. |
| 4–5 | Communicative intention more or less fulfilled. Verbs generally correct. |
| 2–3 | Some communicative intention fulfilled. Quite a few grammatical errors and spelling mistakes. Incorrect verb usage / tenses wrong. |
| 1 | Very small part of communicative intention fulfilled. |
| 0 | No communication of message. |

Letter/email notes (p.[10]): five points to make; **"each point should be developed and expanded"**; the task "can be loosely based on the suggestions given" and "candidates can make their own relevant points and need not use the guidelines given."

### Q2 — Diary (a) / Note (b): 20 marks, 4 units × max 5 (p.[11])
Four points to communicate; **"development of points not necessarily essential"** (contrast with the letter, which must be developed). Mark **each point individually**:

| Mark | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Communicative intention fulfilled. Verbs correct. |
| 4 | Communicative intention more or less fulfilled. Verbs generally correct. |
| 3 | Communicative intention more or less fulfilled. There may be a few grammatical errors & spelling mistakes. |
| 2 | Some communicative intention fulfilled. Quite a few grammatical errors & spelling mistakes. |
| 1 | Very small amount of message conveyed. |
| 0 | Message not communicated. |

**Teaching takeaway:** across all Section C tasks the ceiling (full marks on a unit) is explicitly gated on **correct verbs** — a right message with a wrong verb caps the unit below full marks. This "verbs cost you the top mark on every point" rule is more granular than French's band grid.

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — reading comprehension (Sections A & B)

### Answer-language rules
- The per-section header sets it: "Questions must be answered in the language indicated on the examination paper for full marks" (p.[5], p.[8]).
- The scheme's own answer keys show the **standard comprehension answers are given/expected in English** (e.g. Section A journalistic Q1, p.[6]; Section B Q2/Q4, p.[8]) — Spanish is read, answers written in English.
- **Exception — "exact transcription" questions** require a Spanish lift *from the text* and are all-or-nothing on form: *"No marks awarded if extra words are added / if words are added or omitted. Exact transcription required."* (Section A Q1 literature p.[5]; Section A 1(b) Q2 p.[6]; Section B Q1 and Q3 p.[8]). These are the "find and copy the exact Spanish phrase" items.
- **Exception — the opinion question is answered in Spanish:** Section A 1(b) Q4 (6 marks) is graded *"Any mark from 0 to 6 may be awarded for this question. No marks for answers in English"* (p.[6]). This is a **difference from French**, where the equivalent opinion question (Q6) is answered *in English*; in Spanish the short opinion task must be in Spanish.
- Literature Q5 "discussion" (12 marks, p.[5]) is graded on three relevant points but **"No marks awarded for answers supported by reference to the extract"** — i.e. you must discuss, not quote the passage back.

### Mark-allocation / partial-credit patterns
- **"First correct answer = 4m"** ordering rule on the 4+3+3 items: where three points are wanted and the split is 4+3+3, the first correct point earns the 4 (Section A literature Q3, Q4, p.[5]).
- **Graded partial credit** is written into individual items, e.g. Section A 1(b) Q1(e): full 4m for the complete idea, "It makes Spain unique/welcoming = 2m", "Spain is unique only = 1m" (p.[6]); Section B padel item (ii) "only 50 courts [2m] and we have 6000 players [1m]" (p.[7]).
- **"Correct tense required for full marks"** on some manipulation-style comprehension items (Section B Q2, p.[8]) — the comprehension analogue of the production "verbs must be correct" rule.

### Annotation palette (p.[4]) — the examiner's shorthand
`/` identifying end of point · `[` beginning of discounted point · `]` end of discounted point · `‐` omitted point · tick/seen mark = material seen by examiner · `⌇` blank page · `MMS` Modified Marking Scheme applied. (This palette is *simpler* than French's — no `EXC` excess annotation, and no printed reading-comprehension "excess = −1/−2" penalty ladder appears in the Spanish scheme.) Reading conventions (p.[3]): a forward slash `/` before an answer = synonym/alternative (not a second answer); a dash `‐` before an answer = a separate independent acceptable answer; rounded brackets `( )` = non-essential material; square brackets `[ ]` = mark breakdown/clarification; underlined words = essential element.

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — Listening / Aural (80 marks)

General rules (p.[12]):
- **"All answers must be in English."**
- **Wrong-language penalty:** *"If the entire test/section is answered in Spanish: mark as per Marking Scheme and award half of the marks gained."* — a 50% penalty. **This matches French's aural penalty** (both languages: answer the aural in English, or lose half).
- Items are split into segments with printed sub-marks (e.g. 3 + (2+1) + 3 = 9; 3+3+3+3 = 12), and "two of / one of" list items carry a **"First correct answer = 2m"** ordering rule on 2+1 splits (e.g. item 1(b), item 3(d), p.[12]).
- Partial-credit fallbacks are printed per item, e.g. Almodóvar item (c): full 3m for the full idea, "younger people/generations are choosing/watching his movies = 2m", "people watch/choose his movies = 1m" (p.[12]).
- The full aural transcript is printed (p.[16]–[18]) and the item marks sum to 80 (`9+12+13+13+13+8+12 = 80`, p.[14]).

## Modified Marking Scheme (Appendix One, p.[15])

Applied to candidates granted a recorded answer, spell-checked computer, scribe, or a spelling-and-grammar waiver. For Spanish HL:
- **Aural:** no special consideration — it is answered in English/Irish and spelling/grammar are not penalised in the standard scheme anyway.
- **Sections A & B (Spanish-language answers), Section B Q5, and Section C:** *"incorrect spelling, such as missing or incorrect accents, and elements of grammar, such as verb endings and agreement of adjectives, will not be penalised"* — content/communication still marked as standard. (Matches French's MMS treatment: accents treated as spelling, spelling/agreement waived.)

## Quotable lines (with page refs)

1. **The two-axis principle, restricted to Q5 (p.[9]):** Section B Q5 is scored `1. CONTENT/COMMUNICATION 25 marks` and `2. LANGUAGE 25 marks` — content and language marked separately and equally, but *only* for this task.
2. **Language depends on Content (p.[9]):**
   > "Where no marks are awarded for Content 1, (i.e. effort totally irrelevant), no marks will be awarded for Language 2."
3. **Copying the text scores nothing (p.[9]):**
   > "No marks will be awarded for phrases taken directly from the text."
4. **The Spanish-speaker test for the middle band (p.[9]):**
   > "Comprehensible to Spanish speaker · Communicative intention more or less fulfilled" (MIDDLE) vs "Spanish speaker would have difficulty understanding" (BOTTOM).
5. **Verbs gate the top mark on every production unit (p.[10]–[11]):**
   > "6 marks: Communicative intention fulfilled. Verbs must be correct for full marks." / "Verbs must be correct for full marks." (Section C general rules, p.[10]).
6. **Aural wrong-language penalty (p.[12]):**
   > "If the entire test/section is answered in Spanish: mark as per Marking Scheme and award half of the marks gained."
7. **Exact transcription is all-or-nothing (p.[5], p.[6], p.[8]):**
   > "No marks awarded if extra words are added. Exact transcription required."
