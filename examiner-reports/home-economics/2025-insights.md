## Paper structure and mark allocation (p.3)

Instructions to candidates (p.3), verbatim allocation:

- **Section A — 60 marks (p.3, p.6):** "Answer ten questions in this section. Each question carries 6
  marks." There are 14 questions printed (Q1–Q14, pp.6–10); candidates choose any ten. Short-response
  / recall questions.
- **Section B — 180 marks (p.3, p.11):** "Answer Question 1 and any other two questions from this
  section. Question 1 is worth 80 marks. Questions 2, 3, 4, and 5 are worth 50 marks each." **Q1 is
  compulsory**; the other two are chosen from Q2–Q5.
- **Section C — 40 or 80 marks (p.3, p.19):** "Answer one elective question or Question 4 (core) to
  include part (a) and either part (b) or (c). If you submitted Textiles, Fashion and Design
  coursework for examination, you may only attempt Question 2 from this section." The four Section C
  options are: **Elective 1 — Home Design and Management (80 marks, p.19)**, **Elective 2 — Textiles,
  Fashion and Design (40 marks, p.22)**, **Elective 3 — Social Studies (80 marks, p.25)**, and
  **Question 4 — Core (80 marks, p.27)**. Every Section C option is answered as "part (a) and either
  part (b) or (c)".
- **Paper total:** 280 or 320 marks depending on the Section C option chosen (Section C is worth 80
  for the three 80-mark options, 40 for Textiles). This is why the grading table (below) has two
  columns.

### The two-column grading table (p.3)

Because the Textiles elective (Elective 2) is worth only 40 marks while the other Section C options
are worth 80, the paper has two different grade-boundary columns:

| Grade | Elective 1, 3 and C4 (max 320) | Elective 2 (max 280) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 288–320 | 252–280 |
| 2 | 256–287 | 224–251 |
| 3 | 224–255 | 196–223 |
| 4 | 192–223 | 168–195 |
| 5 | 160–191 | 140–167 |
| 6 | 128–159 | 112–139 |

Both columns represent the *same* H-grade percentages against the two possible paper totals — the
Textiles candidate is not disadvantaged, but the boundaries are re-based to a 280 total.

## The core marking grammar — "N points @ M marks (graded …)"

Every substantive question in the scheme is scored as **a fixed number of discrete points, each worth
a fixed mark, with a graded partial-credit ladder attached**. The template repeats across all three
sections. Representative instructions:

- Section A Q1: "2 functions described @ 3 marks (graded 3:2:0)" (p.6)
- Section A Q3: "2 functions @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0)" + "2 sources @ 1 mark (graded 1:0)" (p.6)
- Section B Q1(a): "4 points @ 5 marks (graded 5:4:3:2:1:0)", 20 marks (p.11)
- Section B Q1(d): "5 points @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0)", 10 marks (p.13)
- Section B Q1(e): "4 points @ 5 marks (graded 5:3:0)", 20 marks (p.13)

**The colon-separated numbers after "graded" are the only mark values a single point can earn — and
this is the most important structural fact for students.** The ladder defines how partial credit
works, and its granularity varies by question:

- **`(graded 3:2:0)`** — a 3-mark point is worth 3 (full), 2 (partial), or 0. **There is no 1.** A
  weak point that would "feel like" 1 mark scores nothing.
- **`(graded 3:2:1:0)`** — full 1/2/3 ladder; a minimal correct fragment can still earn 1.
- **`(graded 5:3:0)`** — a 5-mark point is worth 5 (fully developed), 3 (partially), or 0. **A
  minimal attempt earns nothing; there is no 4, 2 or 1.** This coarse ladder is used heavily on the
  20-mark "Discuss/Analyse" parts (e.g. Q1(e) p.13, Q3(b) p.16, Q5(a) p.18), and it is where
  under-developed answers lose the most: half-formed points collapse straight to 0 rather than
  scoring 1–2.
- **`(graded 5:4:3:2:1:0)`** — the full ladder, used on Q1(a) (p.11) where fine gradation is allowed.
- **`(graded 6:5:4:3:2:1:0)`** — full ladder on the 6-mark composite point in Q5(b) (p.18).

So two 5-mark points can be marked very differently: one graded `5:4:3:2:1:0` rewards every increment
of quality, while one graded `5:3:0` is effectively pass/partial/fail. Students cannot tell which
ladder applies from the paper — the safe strategy is always full development to the top of the ladder.

## How the long questions distribute points across named headings

Where a long question names sub-headings or bullet cues, the scheme fixes **how many of the required
points must fall under each heading** — you cannot bank all your points under one heading. Verbatim
distribution instructions:

- Q3(b) storage/cooking, "4 points @ 5 marks (graded 5:3:0)" with "**1 point to refer to storage; 1
  point to refer to cooking/reheating and 2 others**" (p.16). Ignoring one heading caps you.
- Q1(b)(i) Elective 1 housing needs, "6 points @ 3 marks" with "**2 points on each and any 2
  others**" (p.20).
- Q4(a) Elective 1 kitchen design, "4 points @ 4 marks" with "**1 point on each heading and 1 other
  point**" (three headings: family size, ergonomics, environmental awareness) (p.19).
- Q3(a)(i) Elective 3 work, "5 points @ 4 marks" with "**2 points to refer to increased educational
  requirements, 2 points to refer to flexibility in working hours and 1 other**" (p.25).
- Q3(a)(ii) Elective 3, "3 points @ 5 marks" with "1 point on each + 1 other point" (p.25).

**Structural consequence:** the "and 1 other / and 2 others" free points can only be claimed *after*
the per-heading minimums are met. A candidate who writes six excellent points all about one bullet
scores only the one point that heading allows, plus nothing for the others.

## "Name + describe/explain + example/develop" splits and their marks

Home Economics has no ABQ-style case-link mechanic, but many questions split a single answer into a
**named element (low marks) + a developed element (high marks) + sometimes an example (lowest
marks)**, each scored separately:

- Section A Q8 heat transfer: "Name of method @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0)" + "Description … 1 point @ 3
  marks (graded 3:2:1:0)" + "Example 1 @ 1 mark (graded 1:0)" (p.8). Naming is cheap (2), developing
  is the bulk (3), the example is 1.
- Section A Q9 additives: "Name @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0) × 2" + "Function @ 1 mark (graded 1:0) × 2"
  (p.8) — naming worth double the function.
- Section B Q1(b) fatty-acid structure: "4 points on structure @ 1 mark (graded 1:0) × 3" + "1 example
  @ 2 marks (graded 2:0) × 3" (p.12) — here structural detail is worth 1 each; the single example is
  worth 2.
- Elective 2 Q2(a)(ii): "Name one design principle 1 point @ 4 marks (graded 4:0)" + "How it is
  demonstrated 2 points @ 3 marks (graded 3:2:0)" (p.22). Note `graded 4:0` — the naming point is
  **all-or-nothing**, no partial credit.
- Elective 3 Q3(a)(iii): "name of childcare option @ 3 marks (graded 3:0)" + "evaluation 4 points @ 3
  marks (graded 3:2:0)" (p.25) — the "name" gate is all-or-nothing (3:0); the evaluation carries the
  weight.
- Section B Q4(b)(ii) mortgage: "Name: 1 @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0)" + "description: 3 points @ 4 marks
  (graded 4:2:0)" (p.29).

**Pattern:** the naming/identifying element is typically 2–4 marks and often all-or-nothing (`x:0`),
while the marks live in the description/evaluation/development. Getting the name right earns little;
the development is where the grade is decided.

## The structured-table question (Q3(a), 18 marks, p.15)

Q3 Section B ("food poisoning bacteria") shows the fully itemised template — one named subject then a
prescribed count of scored attributes: "Name of bacteria 1 @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0)" + "source 1 @ 2
marks" + "high risk foods 2 @ 2 marks" + "factors affecting growth 3 @ 2 marks" + "food poisoning
symptoms 2 @ 2 marks" (p.15). The number required per attribute is fixed (1 source, 2 foods, 3
factors, 2 symptoms) — extra items in one row do not compensate for a missing row.

## The compulsory Question 1 (80 marks, pp.11–13)

Q1 is the one everyone must answer, and it is the largest single question. Its five parts each use the
grammar above with different ladders:

- (a) "comment and elaborate on four sources of energy" — 4 points @ 5 (graded **5:4:3:2:1:0**), 20 marks (p.11)
- (b) structure + example of three fatty-acid types — 4 structure points @ 1 × 3 + 1 example @ 2 × 3, 18 marks (p.12)
- (c) "Discuss the significance of fatty acids" — 3 points @ 4 (graded 4:3:2:1:0), 12 marks (p.13)
- (d) "detailed account of the formation of emulsions" — 5 points @ 2 (graded 2:1:0), 10 marks (p.13)
- (e) "Analyse the factors that impact food choices" — 4 points @ 5 (graded **5:3:0**), 20 marks (p.13)

The 80 marks are heavily front-loaded on food science and nutrition; parts (a) and (e) (40 of the 80)
are the coarse 20-mark analysis parts where developed points are essential.

## Diagram credit

The scheme credits diagrams only as an *alternative or supplement* to prose, and only when labelled:
"Accept labelled diagram" (Q8 heat transfer, p.8), "Accept clear diagrams" (fatty-acid structures,
p.12), "Accept a well labelled diagram" (cold-water system, p.21), "Credit from clearly labelled
diagram" (refrigeration working principle, p.28). Unlike Geography, there is no explicit "diagram
without labelling = 0" line, but every diagram-credit instruction in the scheme carries the word
"labelled/clear" — an unlabelled diagram earns nothing because it satisfies no marking point.

## General examiner instructions (p.3)

Verbatim, from "In developing the marking schemes the following should be noted" (p.3):

- "In many cases only key phrases are given which contain information and ideas that **must appear in
  the candidate's answer in order to merit the assigned marks**."
- "The descriptions, methods and definitions in the scheme are **not exhaustive and alternative valid
  answers are acceptable**."
- "The detail required in any answer is determined by the context and the manner in which the question
  is asked, and by the number of marks assigned."
- "Words, expressions or phrases must be **correctly used in context and not contradicted, and where
  there is evidence of incorrect use or contradiction, the marks may not be awarded**."

## Annotations and the Section C discount rule (pp.4–5)

- The examiner annotation set is a numeric stamp (0–8) placed near the response, plus deduction stamps
  (P1–P10 = deduct 1–10 marks), "Excess point awarded full/partial marks", "Point/work not
  attempted", "Blank page", and "MMS" (Modified Marking Scheme) (pp.4–5).
- **Section C discount mark (p.5):** "Question C2 relates to the Textile, Fashion and Design elective.
  Where a candidate answers C2 **and** another question from C1, C3, or C4, the examiner applies a
  discount mark so that only the greatest mark is counted towards the paper total." The annotation `A`
  flags the discount (e.g. `A, P10, P10, P3` = −23). This enforces the "one elective only" rule for
  candidates who submitted Textiles coursework and then over-answer.

## Strategic / structural observations

- **The graded ladder, not the question wording, decides partial credit.** A 5-mark point graded
  `5:3:0` (used on most 20-mark "Discuss/Analyse" parts) gives *nothing* for a half-developed point —
  the student who lists thin points scores 0s where a `5:4:3:2:1:0` ladder would have paid 2s. Full
  development to the top of the ladder is the only safe play because the ladder is invisible on the
  paper.
- **Point counts are caps, not targets.** "4 points @ 5 marks" means only four points are marked;
  writing a fifth earns nothing (with the specific exception of "Excess point awarded" annotations
  where the scheme explicitly allows credit for a better later point). Time spent past the required
  count is wasted.
- **Per-heading minimums must be satisfied first.** "1 point on each … and 1 other" means the "other"
  free points are unreachable until every named heading has its point. Over-answering one bullet at
  the expense of another is the classic self-inflicted cap (Q3(b) p.16, Q4(a) p.19, Q3(a)(i) p.25).
- **Naming is cheap; development is the grade.** Across Name+Describe splits, the name is 2–4 marks
  and often all-or-nothing; the marks live in the described/evaluated element. "Name and evaluate"
  and "Name and describe" questions reward the second verb far more than the first.
- **Q1 is unavoidable and food-science-heavy.** 80 of the 240 Section-B-plus-A "compulsory-ish" marks
  ride on one nutrition/food-science question; a candidate weak on macronutrient chemistry cannot
  route around it.
- **Section C is a strategic fork.** Choosing Textiles (40 marks) lowers the paper total to 280 and
  shifts the grade boundaries; the three 80-mark options keep it at 320. The discount-mark rule
  punishes answering two Section C questions.
- **Marks-to-minutes:** at Higher Level the written paper is 280 or 320 marks; Section A's 60 marks
  across 10 short questions is ~6 marks each and should be fast; Section B's compulsory Q1 (80 marks)
  and the two 50-mark questions plus the 80-mark Section C option are where the bulk of time belongs.

## Misconceptions (structural, inferred from the scheme)

- **"A weak point is worth a mark or two."** On `x:2:0` and `5:3:0` ladders it is worth **zero** — the
  ladder skips 1 (and skips 1, 2, 4 on the `5:3:0` grid). Under-developed points on the big questions
  score nothing, not a consolation mark.
- **"More points = more marks."** Only the prescribed count is marked; extra points beyond the cap are
  ignored except where the scheme's "Excess point" annotation explicitly re-credits a better one.
- **"I can put all my points under the heading I know best."** The per-heading distribution
  ("2 points on each and any 2 others") caps each heading independently.
- **"A diagram can replace the written account."** Diagrams are credited only when labelled/clear and
  generally as an *accepted alternative* to prose points — an unlabelled diagram maps to no marking
  point and scores 0.
- **"Naming the right term earns most of the marks."** The name is typically 2–4 marks (often
  all-or-nothing); the development/evaluation carries the question.
