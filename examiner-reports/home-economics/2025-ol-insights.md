## What is IDENTICAL to Higher Level (so the HL insights apply)

- **Paper structure (p.3):** Section A 60 marks (answer any 10 of 14 @ 6 each); Section B 180 marks
  (compulsory Q1 @ 80 + any two of Q2–Q5 @ 50 each); Section C 40 or 80 marks (one elective or Q4
  core, part (a) + either (b) or (c); Textiles coursework candidates must take C2). Paper total 320
  (Electives 1, 3, C4) or 280 (Elective 2 Textiles).
- **Grading table (p.3):** byte-for-byte the same two-column table as HL (Grade 1 = 288–320 / 252–280
  etc.). OL is *not* graded on an easier boundary set — the same percentage bands apply to the same
  raw totals.
- **General instructions (p.3):** the four "In developing the marking schemes…" bullets are verbatim
  identical to HL, including "key phrases … must appear in the candidate's answer" and
  "correctly used in context and not contradicted … marks may not be awarded".
- **Section C discount rule (p.5):** identical — answering C2 (Textiles) and another Section C
  question triggers the `A` discount annotation so only the greatest mark counts. Deduction stamps
  P1–P10 = −1…−10, plus MMS (Modified marking scheme), same as HL.
- **The "N points @ M marks (graded …)" template** is the same marking grammar; the point counts are
  caps, the graded ladder decides partial credit, and per-heading minimums must be met before "+ N
  other" points can be claimed. See HL insights for the full explanation.

## Marking-grammar STRUCTURE — where ORDINARY differs from HIGHER

### 1. The point ladders — OL is coarser at the bottom, and NEVER awards a single mark on a 5-mark point

The single most important OL-vs-HL structural fact. At **Higher Level** the biggest nutrition
question (Q1(a)) is graded `5:4:3:2:1:0` and one composite point `6:5:4:3:2:1:0` — a full ladder that
pays a fragment 1 mark. **At Ordinary Level no written-paper 5-mark point uses a full ladder.** The
three ladders OL actually uses on 5-mark points are:

- **`(graded 5:3:0)`** — the coarse pass/partial/fail ladder. **At OL this is the DOMINANT ladder**, used on
  essentially every "Describe / Discuss / Outline / Explain" 5-mark part across Sections B and C:
  Q1(d) p.11; Q3(a),(b),(c) p.13; Q4(a),(c) p.14; all of Q5 p.15; all of Elective 1 pp.16–17;
  Elective 3 pp.20–21. A half-developed point collapses straight to **0** — there is no 1, 2 or 4.
- **`(graded 5:4:3:2:0)`** — the *finest* ladder OL grants on a 5-mark point, and it **skips the 1**.
  It appears only on the nutritive/dietetic-value questions: eggs Q2(a) (p.12) and yoghurt Q4(a) core
  (p.22). Compare the HL equivalent `5:4:3:2:1:0` — **at OL the same near-empty point that would earn
  1 at HL earns 0.**
- **`(graded 5:3:2:0)`** — used once, on the Q1(a) soup-choice reasons (p.10): pays 5, 3 or 2 but
  never 4 or 1.

**Consequence:** the coarse `5:3:0` is even more pervasive at OL than at HL (where the finer ladders
carried the compulsory Q1). Under-developed points are punished at least as hard at OL as at HL —
arguably harder, because the one place HL is generous (the full `…:1:0` bottom rung on its flagship
nutrition question) does not exist anywhere in the OL written paper.

### 2. Section A is recognition-dominated and graded all-or-nothing `2:0`

This is the clearest OL accessibility difference. Each Section A question is still 6 marks, but OL
breaks it into **three 2-mark items**, and the majority are **recognition formats graded `2:0`**
(all-or-nothing, no partial credit):

- Q1 true/false tick table — `3 @ 2 marks (graded 2:0)` (p.6)
- Q3 true/false tick table — `3 @ 2 marks (graded 2:0)` (p.6)
- Q4 fill-in-the-blank from a word bank — `3 @ 2 marks (graded 2:0)` (p.7)
- Q7 match-the-cheese-to-the-dish — `3 @ 2 marks (graded 2:1:0)` (p.7)

The remaining Section A questions are short-recall `2 @ 2 (graded 2:1:0)` / `3 @ 2 (graded 2:1:0)`
lists (Q2, Q6, Q8–Q13, pp.6–9) and two `3:2:0` items (Q5 effects of cooking rice p.7; Q14 fabric-care
symbols p.9). **HL Section A carried heavier partial-credit ladders (`3:2:0`, `3:2:1:0`) on
describe/name-and-describe items; OL leans on tick-box true/false and matching that are pure
win-or-lose at 2 marks each.** A wrong tick scores 0 with no consolation, so on the binary items a
guess is a free coin-flip.

### 3. Name-vs-describe splits — OL name gates are chunky and usually all-or-nothing

Same mechanic as HL (name element scored separately and cheaply; the marks live in the
development), but at OL the "name" gate is frequently a **large all-or-nothing block**:

- Soup recommendation: `Name 4 marks (graded 4:0)` then `Reasons: 4 @ 5 (graded 5:3:2:0)` (p.10) —
  the naming is a flat 4 or nothing.
- Egg meal component: `egg component 1 @ 3 marks (graded 3:0)` (p.12); accessory `1 @ 3 (graded 3:0)`
  then reason `1 @ 4 (graded 4:2:0)` (p.19) — name all-or-nothing, reason carries the weight.
- Preservation method: `Name 1 point @ 5 (graded 5:3:0)` + `Method 4 points @ 5 (graded 5:3:0)`
  (p.13) — here the name is NOT all-or-nothing (partial 3 possible), the heavier weight is the method.
- Education initiative / childcare support / saving scheme: `Name: 5 (graded 5:3:0)` or
  `Name 3 (graded 3:2:0)` then `2 details @ 5` or `@ 3` (pp.20, 21, 23).

**Pattern:** the name is 3–5 marks, often `x:0` all-or-nothing; the developed
account/reasons/details is graded separately and is where the grade is decided — same lesson as HL.

### 4. Per-heading distribution — identical rule, applied to descriptive (not chemistry) headings

OL keeps the HL "1 point on each named heading + N others" cap, verbatim in form:

- Eggs nutritive/dietetic: `4 points @ 5 (graded 5:4:3:2:0)` with **"1 reference to nutritive value, 1
  reference to dietetic value, + 2 others points"** (p.12).
- Yoghurt core equivalent: same wording (p.22).
- Education & child development: `3 points @ 5 (graded 5:3:0)` with **"1 reference to social, 1
  reference to physical, + 1 other point"** (p.20).
- Primary-school description: three named headings (age range / types of school / curriculum) each
  `1 point @ 5` + `1 other @ 5` (p.20).

Banking all points under one heading forfeits the others — the classic self-inflicted cap, same as
HL. The difference is only in the *headings*: OL headings are descriptive/consumer (dietetic value,
social vs physical development) rather than HL's structural-chemistry headings.

### 5. Mark allocations — OL splits the same questions into more, smaller, more-signposted parts

- OL compulsory Q1(a) is **24 marks** (Name 4 + 4 reasons @ 5) vs HL Q1(a) **20 marks** (4 @ 5).
- OL menu question is fully itemised: `3 meals @ 6 = (2 courses @ 2 (graded 2:1:0) + beverage @ 1
  (graded 1:0)) × 3 + egg component 1 @ 3 (graded 3:0)` (p.12) — the beverage is explicitly worth 1.
- OL Section A splits each 6-mark question into three 2-mark items (see §2); HL more often used two
  3-mark items.

### 6. The compulsory Question 1 — OL avoids macronutrient chemistry

**This is the biggest content-level OL-vs-HL difference on the unavoidable 80-mark question.** At HL,
Q1 is food-science/chemistry heavy (fatty-acid structure, emulsion formation, energy sources). **At
OL, Q1 (80 marks, pp.10–11) is applied consumer nutrition + data interpretation:**

- (a) read a nutrition-label table for two convenience soups and recommend one with 4 reasons — 24
  marks (`Name 4:0` + `4 reasons @ 5, 5:3:2:0`) (p.10)
- (b) account of Vitamin C under sources / functions / deficiency — 24 marks (`4:2:0` throughout) (p.11)
- (c) three ways a teenager can include fruit & veg — 12 marks (`3 @ 4, 4:2:0`) (p.11)
- (d) four factors when selecting convenience foods — 20 marks (`4 @ 5, 5:3:0`) (p.11)

A candidate weak on chemistry can still fully answer OL Q1: it rewards reading a table and
descriptive nutrition, not structural biochemistry. The `5:3:0` and `4:2:0` ladders on parts (b)–(d)
still punish thin points.

### 7. Elective 2 (Textiles, 40 marks) — finer ladders because the marks are smaller

Because Textiles is worth 40 not 80, its points are 3–4 marks and use `3:2:0` and `4:2:0`
(pp.18–19), e.g. fabric finish `Name 3 (graded 3:2:0)` + `Description 1 point @ 3 (3:2:0)` +
`Example 3 (3:2:0)` (p.19). No 5-mark points appear in Elective 2.

### 8. Food Studies Coursework — banded A/B/C/D marking, structurally the same at both levels

Coursework (`2025-ol-marking-scheme.md` pp.24–35) is 160 marks (2 assignments × 80 in 2025 after
adjustments; normally 4 assignments), graded on **A/B/C/D competence bands** rather than point
ladders for the Investigation, Procedure and Evaluation blocks:

- Investigation 32 (Investigation banded /24 + Sources `2 @ 2, 2:1:0` + menu `4 marks`) (p.26)
- Preparation & Planning 8; Implementation 28 (Procedure banded /16 + Key factors `2 @ 4, 4:2:0` +
  Safety/Hygiene `2 @ 2, 2:1:0`); Evaluation 12 (`3 @ 4, 4:3:2:0`) (p.27)
- **`SC` "Scenario applied" = a flat −8 deduction** for a non-compliant dish (uncooked where cooked
  specified; dish unsuitable; investigated method not used; over-use of convenience foods), applied
  only after Advising-Examiner consultation (pp.25, 34–35).
- Dish not identified in the investigation → **−1 / −2 / −4** under meals/dishes/products (pp.26, 34).
- Repeated practical activity across assignments → the lower repeated activity's marks are disallowed
  (`REP` annotation) (pp.34–35).

## OL-specific candidate-behaviour findings

1. **Section A binary items are all-or-nothing coin-flips.** Q1, Q3 (true/false ticks) and Q4
   (fill-the-blank word bank) are each `3 @ 2 marks (graded 2:0)` (pp.6–7) — a wrong tick scores 0
   with no partial credit, so a guess on an unknown binary is pure upside. (Source: OL scheme pp.6–7.)
2. **Half-developed points score zero on the big questions.** Because `5:3:0` is the dominant ladder
   across Sections B and C (pp.11–23), a point that is started but not developed collapses to 0, not
   1–2. Even the finest OL 5-mark ladder, `5:4:3:2:0` (eggs p.12, yoghurt p.22), skips the 1 — no
   5-mark point at OL can earn a single mark. (Source: OL scheme pp.11–23.)
3. **Per-heading caps forfeit points if you bank under one heading.** "1 reference to nutritive
   value, 1 to dietetic value, + 2 others" (p.12) / "1 reference to social, 1 to physical, + 1 other"
   (p.20) mean the "+ others" free points are unreachable until every named heading is served.
   (Source: OL scheme pp.12, 20, 22.)
4. **The compulsory Q1 is data-interpretation + descriptive nutrition, not chemistry.** The
   unavoidable 80-mark question hinges on reading a nutrition-label table (soup comparison, p.10) and
   a descriptive Vitamin C account (p.11) — a chemistry-weak candidate is not routed out of the paper
   the way HL's fatty-acid/emulsion Q1 does. (Source: OL scheme pp.10–11 vs HL scheme pp.11–13.)
5. **A non-compliant coursework dish costs a flat −8 (SC), not pro-rata.** Preparing a dessert where a
   main course is specified, an uncooked dish where cooked is required, or over-using convenience
   foods triggers the `SC` "Scenario applied" −8 deduction (pp.25, 34–35); a dish not identified in
   the investigation additionally loses −1/−2/−4 (p.26). (Source: OL scheme pp.25, 26, 34–35.)
