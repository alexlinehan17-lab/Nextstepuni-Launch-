## Paper structure and the "answer 5" rule (exam-paper rubric, verified)

The Theory Higher Level paper is **300 marks, 3 hours**. The standing rubric (verbatim from the SEC
HL theory paper):

> "(a) Answer Question 1 and four other questions. (b) All questions carry equal marks.
> (c) Answers must be written in ink. (d) Drawings and sketches are to be made in pencil.
> (e) Write the number of the question distinctly before each answer. (f) Neat freehand sketches
> to illustrate written descriptions should be made. (g) The name, sizes, dimensions and other
> necessary particulars of each material indicated must be noted on the drawings."

So: **Question 1 is compulsory**, then **any four** of Questions 2–10. Every question is marked out
of **60** (5 × 60 = 300). The marking scheme confirms the `TOTAL 60` footer on every question grid
(pp. 37–47). Q1, Q7 and Q9 are the large freehand-drawing questions; the rest mix short technical
notes, sketches, discussion and (Q5) a U-value calculation.

Rubric (g) is the load-bearing one for the marking grammar: **every material shown on a drawing
must be named/annotated**, which is exactly why the drawing grids award 1 of every 4 marks per
element for the annotation (below).

## The drawing grid — "N elements × 4 marks (3 drawing + 1 annotation)" (pp. 37, 43, 45)

The big sectional-drawing questions are marked as a **checklist of named construction elements**,
each element worth a fixed small tariff split between the line-work and its label:

- **Q1(a)** vertical section through strip foundation / solid ground floor / external wall /
  window (p. 37): "Foundation + solid ground floor **5 × 4 marks**; External wall **4 × 4 marks**;
  Window detail **3 × 4 marks** — **(3 for drawing, 1 for annotation)**." Then a separate
  **Scale 4 + Drafting 4** band (below). Q1(b) radon-barrier detail = 4. Total 60.
- **Q7(a)** vertical section through chimney stack and roof (p. 43): "**10 × 4 marks + 6 marks
  (3 for drawing, 1 for annotation)**", plus "Two courses of slate above and below the chimney
  stack **4**", plus the Scale/Drafting band. Q7(b) rainwater-penetration detail = 6.
- **Q9(a)** wiring layout, two light points on one switch (p. 45): "**6 × 5 marks + 4 marks
  (4 for drawing, 1 for annotation)**" — the six credited items are the distribution board, single
  switch, two light points, and the live/neutral/earth cabling runs; a separate 4 marks for
  **cable size (1) and colour (3)**.

Consequences of this grammar:
- **The label is a quarter of each element's marks.** A perfectly drawn but unlabelled element
  loses 1 of its 4 marks (the annotation mark) — and rubric (g) makes labelling mandatory. This is
  the Construction-Studies analogue of Geography's "diagram without labelling 0 marks".
- **Marks are earned per *named feature*, not per neat drawing.** Breadth of correct detailing
  (more credited elements shown and labelled) beats a beautiful drawing of only a few elements.
- **Drawing questions carry a fixed shopping-list of expected features** (the scheme lists them
  explicitly, pp. 5, 24, 27), and "any other relevant points" are creditable — the list is not
  exhaustive.

## Scale & Drafting band — the graded quality mark on drawings (pp. 37, 43)

On top of the per-element marks, the major drawing questions carry a separate **8-mark quality
band**, split **Scale 4 marks + Drafting 4 marks**, graded in three tiers:

> "Scale – 4 marks / Drafting – 4 marks: Excellent **8**, Good **6**, Fair **4**." (Q1, p. 37;
> identically Q7, p. 43.)

So the presentation quality of the whole drawing is worth a fixed 8 marks graded 8/6/4 (never
below 4 if a genuine attempt is made) — separate from, and additional to, the element checklist.

## The "3 for note / 3 for sketch" grammar (pp. 38, 40, 42)

Where a question asks for a technical guideline or feature (not a full section drawing), the
default sub-part tariff is **6 marks split 3 for the written note + 3 for the sketch**. Examples:

- **Q2(c)** best-practice guidelines to reduce injury (p. 38): each guideline "**3 for note,
  3 for sketch**" — six guidelines across three tasks, 36 marks.
- **Q4(a)** functional requirements of an external wall (p. 40): each requirement = **Notes 3 +
  Sketches 3**.
- **Q6(a)** low-environmental-impact features (p. 42): each feature = **Notes 5 + Sketches 5**.
- **Q4(b)** high-performance wall constructions (p. 40): each wall = **Notes 5, Sketches 6,
  Position of insulation 2, Dimensions 2** — note that **stating dimensions and showing where the
  insulation sits are separately tariffed marks**, not folded into the sketch.

The lesson: on a "describe/detail with sketch" question, **the sketch is not decoration — it is
typically half the marks**, and omitting it caps the answer at 50%.

## The "point + discussion" grammar on discuss/benefit questions (pp. 42, 44, 46, 47)

Discussion questions use a **name-then-develop** split: a mark for *stating* the point and further
marks for *developing/justifying* it. This is the Construction-Studies equivalent of the
Business "Name + Explain" template.

- **Q6(b)/(c), Q8(a)** (pp. 42, 44): each item "**3 for point, 3 for discussion**" = 6.
- **Q9(c)** approaches to reduce electrical consumption (p. 46): "**2 for point, 3 for
  discussion**" = 5 — naming the approach is worth less than developing it.
- **Q10 alternative** (historic built environment essay, p. 47): each discussion point / guideline
  "**5 for point, 5 for discussion**" = 10 — on the essay-style question the developed argument is
  weighted equally with, and is worth as much as, identifying the point.

Consequence: **a list of bare points caps at roughly half marks.** The marks live in the
development — the "why", the mechanism, the benefit-to-the-homeowner. Naming without discussion
forfeits the discussion tranche every time.

## Per-step calculation marking — the U-value question (Q5, p. 41)

The U-value / heat-loss question is marked as a **procedure with a mark per line of working**, not
a single mark for the final answer:

- **Q5(a)** U-value (33 marks): **each material layer resistance = 3 marks** (external surface,
  acrylic render, external insulation, sand-cement render, hollow block, insulation, plasterboard,
  skim, internal surface), **Total resistance 3**, **U-value calculation 3** — eleven 3-mark steps.
- **Q5(b)** annual heat-loss cost (15 marks): five steps × 3 — heat-loss formula & calc, heating
  duration, kJ/year, kg of pellets, annual cost.
- **Q5(c)** extra insulation thickness for a 0.11 target (12 marks): four steps × 3 — resistance
  for target U (R=1/U), difference in resistances, apply R=T/k, required thickness.

So **"show your workings" is enforced structurally**: each resistance line and each substitution
is its own 3 marks. A correct final U-value with no layer-by-layer working forfeits the bulk of the
33 marks. The scheme explicitly notes "Alternative calculation methods acceptable" (p. 21) and
gives both a step method and a single-formula method for Q5(b) (p. 20).

## Layout / design questions — notes vs sketch, and requirement ticks (Q3, p. 39)

Q3 (revise a ground-floor layout) shows a third pattern: a big **holistic mark for the revised
drawing (16)** plus **explicit 2-mark ticks for each stated design requirement met** — "Include a
downstairs bathroom **2**", "Create an open-plan kitchen/dining and living area **2**", "Optimising
daylight **2**". The three reasons (b) are 6 each; the external redesign (c) is **Notes 10 +
Sketch 10**. Takeaway: where a question lists explicit requirements, **each requirement is a
discrete mark you must visibly satisfy** — the examiner is ticking a checklist against the brief.

## Illustrative-not-exhaustive / "any other relevant points" (pp. 3, throughout)

The scheme's standing instruction (p. 3):

> "Note: Notes and graphics are for illustration and are not exclusive or exhaustive, other
> relevant notes and graphics are acceptable as responses and will be credited accordingly."

Every model list closes with "**Any other relevant points**" (or "Any other relevant detail /
sketches"). So candidates are **not** matching a fixed answer key — a valid alternative detail
earns the element mark. The essay question extends this to "Any other relevant, **cogent,
well-argued** points" (pp. 33, 35), signalling that on the discursive question quality of argument,
not just quantity of points, is credited.

## Practical Test & Coursework marking (pp. 48–63) — for completeness

- **Practical Test (Common Level, 150 marks, pp. 48–62):** marked as **Overall Assembly 14 +
  Marking Out 51 + Processing (per joint) + Shaping/Drilling 17**, with each joint broken into
  per-cut tariffs (e.g. box dovetail: "tails 2 × 4 marks; pins — sawing with the grain 4 × 1 mark,
  paring across the grain 2 × 2 marks", p. 51). **Machinery penalty rule** (p. 49): "The artefact
  is to be hand produced… Where there is evidence of the use of machinery for a particular
  procedure a penalty applies. Component is marked out of **50% of the marks** available for that
  procedure."
- **Practical Coursework (Form A, 150 marks, p. 63):** four headings — **A Planning 40, B Report
  35, C Manipulative Skills 40, D Completion 35**. Note: "While the general headings and marks
  above will largely remain the same, breakdowns may vary for any given year."
