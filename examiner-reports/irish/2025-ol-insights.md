## Overall mark architecture (written papers) — how OL differs from HL

Verified from the section banners and front summary tables (pp.3, 8, 12, 14, 20, 23, 25, 28):

| Paper | Section | OL marks | HL marks (for contrast) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Páipéar 1 (160)** | Cuid I — An Chluastuiscint (Aural) | 60 | 60 |
| | Cuid II — An Cheapadóireacht (Composition) | 100 | 100 |
| **Páipéar 2 (200)** | Ceist 1 — Léamhthuiscint (Reading comp, A + B) | 100 | 100 |
| | Ceist 2 — Prós (Ainmnithe **or** Roghnach) | 50 | 30 |
| | Ceist 3 — Filíocht (Ainmnithe **or** Roghnach) | 50 | 30 |
| | Ceist 4 — Litríocht Bhreise | **— (none at OL)** | 40 |

Written total = 360 (same top-line as HL), but the internal splits are different. The
biggest structural divergence: at OL **there is no separate Litríocht Bhreise (additional
literature) question** — instead Prós and Filíocht are each worth 50 and each offers a
choice of a **prescribed (Ainmnithe)** answer *or* a **general-study (Roghnach)** answer.

## Marking grammar — STRUCTURE (OL-specific mark splits)

### The composition (An Cheapadóireacht, 100 marks) — p.10, band tables pp.12–13

**This is the single biggest OL-vs-HL difference.** At HL the composition is ONE long
piece (500–600 words) marked Stíl 5 / Ábhar 15 / Cumas Gaeilge 80. At OL it is **TWO
short pieces**, and the candidate answers **two of A, B, C, D** (p.10):

- A / B / C / D options are the four genres: **Giota Leanúnach/Blag, Scéal, Litir/Ríomhphost,
  Comhrá** (p.10).
- Each piece is marked out of **50**, split (p.10):

  | Component (Irish) | English | Marks per piece |
  |---|---|---|
  | An Tasc | Completing the task / writing the correct genre | **2** |
  | An tÁbhar | Handling of material / content | **8** |
  | An Cumas Gaeilge | Command of Irish (language) | **40** |

  `50 × 2 = 100 marks.`

**Is language accuracy still the dominant weighting? YES.** Cumas Gaeilge is **40 of 50 =
80%** of each composition piece — exactly the same 80% share language carries in the HL
composition (80/100). Language mastery remains the engine of the composition mark at OL.
Cumas is split into the same two named gnéithe as HL (p.10):
**(i) Cumas Gaeilge** = *Fairsinge, saibhreas agus oiriúnacht* (range, richness, appropriateness
of vocabulary/idiom) and **(ii) Cruinneas Gaeilge** = *Beachtas* (accuracy of syntax, verb
tenses, séimhiú, urú, prepositions, reported speech, spelling).

**OL Cumas Gaeilge band table (40 marks)** — p.12, expressed as *percentages of 40* (an OL
presentation feature; HL prints raw band ranges instead):

| Category | Ard | Meánach | Íseal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sármhaith | 95% = 38 | 90% = 36 | 85% = 34 |
| An-mhaith | 80% = 32 | 75% = 30 | 70% = 28 |
| Maith | 65% = 26 | 60% = 24 | 55% = 22 |
| **Maith go leor** | 50% = 20 | 45% = 18 | 40% = 16 |
| Lag | 35% = 14 | 30% = 12 | 25% = 10 |
| An-lag | 20% = 8 | 15% = 6 | 10% = 4 |
| Easnamhach | — | — | 5% = 2 |

Note the band *names* differ from HL: OL uses **"Maith go leor"** where HL uses *Measartha*,
and **"Easnamhach"** where HL uses *An-íseal*.

**Genre discipline is scored differently from HL.** At HL, writing the wrong genre for the
chosen title zeroes the 5-mark *Stíl* award. At OL the equivalent lever is the **2-mark Tasc**
(awarded only if the candidate actually writes the required Giota/Scéal/Litir/Comhrá, p.10),
plus the **"M" (Míléamh)** annotation for content with no link to the title (p.11). Simpler
and lower-stakes than the HL Stíl mechanism.

**Deficient-length caps use F1/F2/F3 (not HL's S1/S2/S3).** The OL length threshold is
*"leathleathanach nó breis"* (half a page or more), and the short-attempt penalty grid on
p.13 maps band × F-symbol to a capped mark. The F-symbols are defined by line count (p.11):
F1 = ~8–11 lines (6–8 in a letter/email body), F2 = ~4–7 lines (3–5), F3 = ~1–3 lines (1–2),
all measured on *relevant, on-title* material only. A too-short piece is capped before quality
is fully rewarded, exactly as at HL — but the thresholds are line-counts, not the 500–600-word
HL guideline.

### The aural (An Chluastuiscint, 60 marks) — pp.3, 7–9

- Marked as **Tuiscint = 60**, with a **Cumas Gaeilge deduction of (0–6)** —
  *"Bainfear ó 0 go 6 ar chaighdeán na Gaeilge"* (pp.3, 9). **This is double the HL aural
  language deduction (HL = 0–3).**
- Items are worth **3 marks each** at OL (*3 mharc × 4* per Fógra/Comhrá; *3 mharc × 2* per
  Píosa, p.3), versus **2 marks each** at HL. So both the per-item value and the max language
  penalty are larger at OL.
- **Partial credit of 1 mark (occasionally 2)** is given for vague / incompletely-specified
  answers: "RTÉ" = 1 vs "(RTÉ Raidió) na Gaeltachta / RnaG" = 3 (p.7); "San ionad" = 1 vs
  "San ionad aclaíochta" = 3 (p.8); "Sa chlub" = 1 vs "Sa chlub Gaelach" = 3 (p.9); "Nócha"
  = 2 vs "Nócha euro / €90" = 3 (p.8). Precision of the retrieved detail is what buys the full
  mark — the same principle as HL, scaled to the 3-mark item.

### The reading comprehension (Léamhthuiscint, 100 marks) — pp.12, 14–18

- Two comprehensions **A (50) and B (50), both compulsory** (p.14, banners "A – 50 marc"
  p.15, "B – 50 marc" p.17).
- Each question part is worth **5 marks**. Where a part asks for **two pieces of information**,
  the split is **3 marks for the first correct point + 2 for the second** (*"Má tá dhá phointe
  eolais i gceist, tá 3 mharc ar an gcéad cheann atá ceart agus 2 mharc ar an dara ceann atá
  ceart"*, p.14). Single-point parts are a flat 5.
- **No "own words" requirement, and no grammar-identification question.** This is a clean
  OL-vs-HL divergence. At HL, Q6(a) is a 3-mark grammar-identification item and Q6(b) is a
  12-mark answer that MUST be *"ina bhfocail féin"* (own words). **At OL neither exists** — the
  sample answers are direct lifts of passage sentences, and lifting the passage verbatim is the
  expected, fully-credited behaviour throughout the reading comprehension.
- **Redundancy is penalised.** *"Gearrfar pionós i ngeall ar iomarcaíocht"* (p.14) — writing
  extra, redundant sentences around the answer is penalised (annotation "Iom"). Answer the
  point, don't pad.
- **Faulty Irish deduction: 0–5 per comprehension.** *"[Gaeilge lochtach: 0 – 5 a bhaint den
  iomlán a gnóthaíodh]"* applied to A and to B separately (pp.16, 18). Poor Irish costs up to
  5 marks off each comprehension total — a flat deduction, not an integrated per-answer award.

### The literature (Prós / Filíocht, 50 + 50) — pp.19–29

- **Choice of prescribed OR general study.** Prós = 50 (answer **2A Prós Ainmnithe** — Dís /
  Oisín i dTír na nÓg / Hurlamaboc — **OR 2B Prós Roghnach**, a self-chosen story/folktale/novel;
  pp.20, 23). Filíocht = 50 (**3A Filíocht Ainmnithe** — Mo Ghrá-sa / An tEarrach Thiar /
  Colscaradh — **OR 3B Filíocht Roghnach**; pp.25, 28). Within each you answer **two of (a),(b),(c)**,
  each worth **25 marks**.
- **The OL literature marking unit is "point + development."** A 25-mark prose answer = **5
  points of information × 5 marks**, and each 5 = **2 marks for the bare point (lomphointe) + 3
  marks for its development (forbairt)** (p.20). This bare-point/development split is a distinctive
  OL grammar and does not appear at HL. (Roghnach variants reserve 1–2 of the 25 marks for
  correctly naming the title/author, pp.23–24.)
- **Language is a DEDUCTION at OL, not an added award — the key literature divergence.** At HL
  every literature answer earns a **separate +5 Gaeilge award on top** of the content mark. At OL
  there is **no added language award**; instead faulty Irish is subtracted: *"[Gaeilge lochtach:
  0-4 a bhaint den iomlán a gnóthaíodh]"* on every prose and poetry question (pp.20, 22, 24, 27).
  So OL literature language tops out as a −4 penalty, whereas HL literature language is a +5 bonus.
- **Poetry does carry an "own words" rule, with a halving penalty.** Several Filíocht Ainmnithe
  parts instruct *"i d'fhocail féin"* (in your own words) for image/theme questions worth 8–9
  marks, and each carries the note that lines lifted **directly from the verse with no imprint of
  the candidate** are capped: *"Línte cuí díreach as véarsa … gan rian an iarrthóra ar an bhfreagra
  = 2 mharc + 2 mharc"* (pp.25–27) — i.e. a 4+4 or 5+4 part collapses to 2+2 if the candidate just
  copies the poem. This is the OL analogue of the HL "own words" gate, localised to poetry rather
  than to comprehension.

### Modified Marking Scheme (SMM) — p.30

Same principle as HL, with the OL deduction figures. For candidates with reasonable
accommodations: content assessed normally; Irish **accuracy assessed only on the named grammar
features** — *Comhréir chruinn* (correct syntax), *Cruinnúsáid na Copaile* (copula), *aimsirí na
mbriathra* (verb tenses), *na réamhfhocail* (prepositions); **spelling not counted** (no wavy
spelling underlines anywhere in the script); **urú, séimhiú, and noun–adjective agreement not
counted**. SMM applies to: Cluastuiscint (Gaeilge lochtach 0–6), Ceapadóireacht Cumas Gaeilge
(out of 40), Léamhthuiscint (0–5 for A and 0–5 for B), Prós (0–4), and Filíocht (0–4). p.31
prints the SMM 40-mark accuracy band ladder (Sármhaith 34–40 … Easnamhach 0–3).

## OL candidate-behaviour findings (for authored "scripts")

1. **Two short compositions, not one long one — and language is still 80%.** OL candidates
   write **two** pieces of ~half a page each (chosen from Blag/Giota, Scéal, Litir/Ríomhphost,
   Comhrá), each marked out of 50 with **Cumas Gaeilge worth 40** (p.10). The lever that moves
   the mark is the same as HL: accuracy and range of Irish, at an 80% weighting.
2. **In the reading comprehension, copy the passage — don't reword.** OL has **no "own words"
   question**; every sample answer is a lifted passage sentence (pp.15–18). Rewording risks
   introducing errors for no gain. The trap instead is **over-writing**: redundant extra
   sentences are penalised under *iomarcaíocht* (p.14).
3. **Two-point comprehension answers are worth 3 + 2 — the first point carries more.** Where a
   question asks for two facts, the first correct fact scores 3 and the second 2 (p.14). Getting
   one solid, precise fact banks 3 of the 5 marks before the second is even attempted.
4. **In poetry, put it in your own words or lose half.** The Filíocht *"i d'fhocail féin"* parts
   halve a lifted answer to 2+2 marks (*"gan rian an iarrthóra … = 2 mharc + 2 mharc"*, pp.25–27).
   The candidate must show some rewording/interpretation of the verse, not transcribe it.
5. **Precision buys the third aural mark.** OL aural items are 3 marks; a vague answer ("RTÉ",
   "San ionad", "Sa chlub", "Nócha") is capped at 1–2 marks, while the fully-specified detail
   ("RnaG", "San ionad aclaíochta", "Sa chlub Gaelach", "Nócha euro") earns the full 3 (pp.7–9).
   And poor Irish can strip up to **6** marks off the aural total (double the HL 0–3), p.9.
