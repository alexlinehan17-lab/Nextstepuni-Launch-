## Marking grammar — the composition (An Cheapadóireacht, 100 marks) — p.10, pp.12–15

The single most important structure to teach. Three components, marked **holistically as a
unit** then split onto the panel (p.10, p.12):

| Component (Irish) | English | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Stíl chuí scríbhneoireachta | Appropriate style/genre | **5** |
| Ionramháil Ábhair | Handling of material/content | **15** |
| Cumas Gaeilge | Command of Irish (language) | **80** |

- **Language is 80% of the composition mark.** The scheme states outright (p.13) that
  mastery of the language used to handle the relevant ideas *"an ghné den
  scríbhneoireacht is tábhachtaí agus an cheapadóireacht á marcáil"* — **the most
  important aspect when marking the composition.**
- **Marking is a 3-step judgement, not a tick-count** (p.10): (1) assess the effort as a
  unit — what is it worth by category; (2) fix the class within the category —
  **ard / meánach / íseal** (high / middle / low); (3) choose the mark for that class.
- **Length guideline** = 500–600 words (p.10). Off-title / irrelevant material is
  bracketed out (`[ ]` + a vertical wavy line down the right margin, p.13), and cutting it
  can drop the effort below the length threshold and trigger a length penalty (pp.13–14).

### Cumas Gaeilge band table (80 marks) — p.14

The band is driven jointly by **(i) Fairsinge/Saibhreas** (range & richness of vocabulary)
and **(ii) Beachtas/Cruinneas** (accuracy). Each band splits into Ard/Meánach/Íseal:

| Category | Ard | Meánach | Íseal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sármhaith (excellent) | 76–80 | 72–75 | 68–71 |
| An-mhaith (very good) | 64–67 | 60–63 | 56–59 |
| Maith (good) | 52–55 | 48–51 | 44–47 |
| Measartha (moderate) | 40–43 | 36–39 | 32–35 |
| Lag (weak) | 28–31 | 24–27 | 20–23 |
| An-lag (very weak) | 16–19 | 12–15 | 8–11 |
| An-íseal (very low) | — | — | 0–7 |

### Style (5) and Content (15) sub-scales

- **Stíl = 5 marks, and it is genre-discipline, NOT quality** (p.14). If you choose option
  B(a) *Scéal* (story) you must write a story; write an essay/debate under a story title
  and *"an marc cuí ná Stíl (5) = 0"* — style scores **zero**. Quality of the writing is
  judged separately under Ábhar.
- **Ionramháil Ábhair band table (15 marks)** (p.14): Sármhaith 13–15 · An-mhaith 11–12 ·
  Maith 9–10 · Measartha 6–8 · Lag/bhearnach 3–5 · An-lag 0–2.
- **Deficient-length caps** (S-symbols, p.17): S1 = only ~1½ pages relevant → capped at
  Stíl 4 / Ábhar 13 / Gaeilge 68; S2 = ~1 page → 3 / 11 / 56; S3 = ~½ page → 2 / 9 / 44.
  A too-short composition is capped *before* quality is even considered.

## Marking grammar — the aural (An Chluastuiscint, 60 marks) — pp.3, 7

- Comprehension is marked as **Tuiscint = 60**, with **(ii) Cumas Gaeilge (0–3) marc**
  deducted for the standard of the candidate's Irish: *"Bainfear ó 0 go 3 ar chaighdeán na
  Gaeilge"* (p.3). Even in the aural, up to 3 marks are stripped for poor Irish.
- Most items are worth **2 marks** each (2 mharc × n across Fógraí / Comhráite / Píosaí,
  p.3). **Partial credit of 1 mark** is awarded for vague or incompletely-named answers —
  e.g. "RTÉ" = 1 vs "RTÉ Raidió na Gaeltachta" = 2 (p.7); "Ceol" = 1 vs "Ceol beo" = 2;
  "Agallaimh" = 1 vs "Agallaimh spéisiúla" = 2 (p.5). Precision of the retrieved detail is
  what separates full from half marks.

## Marking grammar — the reading comprehension (Léamhthuiscint, 100 marks) — p.16

- Two comprehensions **A and B, both compulsory** (100 marks total).
- **Questions 1–5 are worth 7 marks each**, split (a) = 3 marks, (b) = 4 marks, where
  (b)(i) = 2 and (b)(ii) = 2 (p.16).
- Answers accepted **in the candidate's own words OR in the words of the passage,
  "de réir mar a éilíonn an cheist"** (as the question requires) (p.16).
- **Q6(a) = grammar identification, 3 marks: 2 for the first correct grammatical item,
  1 for the second** (p.16).
- **Q6(b) = 12 marks and MUST be answered "ina bhfocail féin" — in the candidate's own
  words** (p.16). Lifting the passage verbatim here does not earn the marks.

## Marking grammar — the literature (Prós / Filíocht / Litríocht Bhreise) — pp.27–28, p.30

- Every literature answer carries a **separate 5-mark language (Gaeilge) award on top of
  the content (Eolas) mark** (p.27): Prós = 25 Eolas + 5 Gaeilge = 30; Filíocht = 25 + 5 =
  30; Litríocht Bhreise = 35 + 5 = 40.
- **The 5-mark language scale** (p.27, repeated per question): 5 = Sárchumas · 4 = Cumas
  an-mhaith · 3 = Cumas maith · 2 = Cumas measartha · 1 = Cumas lag/an-lag — judged on the
  *"príomhghnéithe de cheart na teanga (gramadach, comhréir, struchtúr, deilbhíocht)."*
- **Content (Eolas) is band-marked**, e.g. Prós (p.27): Sármhaith 22–25 · An-mhaith 18–21
  · Maith 14–17 · Measartha 10–13 · Lag/An-lag 0–9, with descriptors keyed to depth of
  discussion of the statement and richness of textual reference. Filíocht (p.30) uses the
  same band ladder scaled to its Eolas cap, and every question carries a per-part
  **Treoir** (instruction) — e.g. *"discuss at least two images; if only one image is
  discussed, mark the Eolas out of 10"* rather than the full 13 (p.30).
- The support notes are explicitly **not exhaustive and not model answers** — *"Níl na
  nótaí tacaíochta seo uileghabhálach ná críochnúil… Ní freagraí eiseamláireacha iad"*
  (p.27); valid points beyond the notes are credited on merit.

## General examiner conventions

- **Annotation palette** (composition, p.13; comprehension, pp.6, 16): Tic (✓) = valid /
  correct / relevant information; Crosáil (X) = major errors in structure/syntax (or
  Náid/0 = incorrect); **Líne chorrach chothrománach** (horizontal wavy underline) = an
  error of language / grammar / spelling; Easnamh = word(s) omitted; square brackets `[ ]`
  = start/end of off-topic material; **GF/NR** = the question/part was not answered.
- **Cancelled answers** are only considered where no other answer was given; if a candidate
  answers more than once, do not accept the response where the extra answer invalidates the
  correct one (p.6).
- **Accept equivalents:** phrases, expressions and words whose meaning is close to those on
  the scheme are acceptable (p.6).
- **Major vs minor errors:** the scheme insists examiners *"idirdhealú a dhéanamh idir
  mórbhotúin chomhréire agus mionbhotúin ghramadaí agus litrithe"* — distinguish **major
  syntax errors** (which distort the structure of the language and are penalised) from
  **minor grammar/spelling slips** (p.15, p.46). The named accuracy features are: correct
  syntax (Comhréir), correct use of the Copula, correct verb tenses, and correct use of
  prepositions (p.46).
- **Modified Marking Scheme (SMM)** for candidates with reasonable accommodations (p.46):
  content is assessed normally; Irish **accuracy is assessed only on the named grammar
  features**, and **spelling is not counted** (no wavy spelling underlines anywhere in the
  script). Séimhiú, urú, and noun–adjective agreement are *not* counted under SMM. SMM
  applies to: Cluastuiscint Gaeilge (0–3), Ceapadóireacht Cumas Gaeilge (80), and each
  literature 5-mark Gaeilge award.

## Candidate-behaviour findings (for authored "scripts")

Structural rules that reveal how marks are actually won or lost:

1. **Language, not ideas, is the engine of the composition mark.** 80 of 100 marks are
   Cumas Gaeilge, and the scheme names language mastery as *the most important aspect*
   (p.13). A brilliant argument in shaky Irish cannot reach the top bands.
2. **Genre indiscipline zeroes the style mark.** Answering the wrong composition type for
   the title you chose (essay under a story title, etc.) → Stíl = 0, independent of how
   good the writing is (p.14).
3. **Q6(b) of the comprehension must be reworded, not copied.** 12 marks are gated behind
   *"ina bhfocail féin"* — own words — so lifting the passage verbatim scores nothing there,
   even though earlier parts *do* accept the passage's wording (p.16).
4. **Vague retrieval is half-credit in the aural.** Naming "RTÉ" not "RTÉ Raidió na
   Gaeltachta", or "Ceol" not "Ceol beo", drops a 2-mark item to 1 (pp.5, 7). Precision of
   the specific detail is what the second mark buys.
5. **Under-length caps the mark before quality is judged.** A composition of ~1 page (vs
   the 500–600-word guideline) is capped at Gaeilge 56/80 via the S2 rule, and ~½ page at
   44/80 (S3, p.17) — length failure limits the ceiling regardless of quality.
