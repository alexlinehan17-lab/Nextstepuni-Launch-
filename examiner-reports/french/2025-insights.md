## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — written production (the core of the tool)

Written production is marked on a **two-axis grid**: every answer scores separately for **Communication** (is the message got across, is the stimulus exploited, is it relevant and coherent?) and **Language** (is the French idiomatic, accurate, well-controlled?). The two scores are summed. This is the single most important structural fact: **content and language are marked independently and equally weighted** (30+30 for Q.1; 20+20 for the shorter task).

### Marking grid — verbatim descriptors (p.[16])

Each axis has three bands: TOP, MIDDLE, BOTTOM. Mark ranges differ by task (the 30-mark task vs the 20-mark task). Note the ranges as printed **overlap** (e.g. TOP 20–30, MIDDLE 12–19) — the band is chosen by descriptor fit, then a mark picked within it.

**COMMUNICATION** — mark ranges: Q.1 = 30-mark task / Qs.2–6 = 20-mark task

| Band | Descriptors | Q.1 range | Qs.2–6 range |
|---|---|---|---|
| TOP | Stimulus material well exploited · High level of textual coherence · Clarity in argumentation · Communicative intention fulfilled · Little or no irrelevant material · Few mistakes in register | 20–30 | 13–20 |
| MIDDLE | More or less competent treatment of stimulus material · Reasonable level of textual coherence · Comprehensible for French monoglot · Communicative intention more or less respected · Some irrelevant material · Not too many mistakes in register | 12–19 | 8–12 |
| BOTTOM | Mere transcription or very poor treatment of stimulus material · Lack of textual coherence · French monoglot would have difficulty understanding · Communicative intention stultified · A lot of irrelevant material · Mistakes in register | 0–11 | 0–7 |

**LANGUAGE** — same task ranges

| Band | Descriptors | Q.1 range | Qs.2–6 range |
|---|---|---|---|
| TOP | Idiomatic French · Rich vocabulary · Complex sentences well handled · Few mistakes in verbs, agreement or spelling | 20–30 | 13–20 |
| MIDDLE | Vocabulary adequate · Verbs generally correct · Rule of agreement generally respected · Not too many mistakes in spelling | 12–19 | 8–12 |
| BOTTOM | Problems with vocabulary · Most verbs incorrect · Basic rule of agreement not respected · Many mistakes in spelling | 0–11 | 0–7 |

Teaching takeaways from the grid:
- **"Mere transcription" of the stimulus text scores BOTTOM on Communication** — copying the prompt back is explicitly the failure mode. You must *treat* / exploit the material.
- **The "French monoglot" test** is the operational definition of Communication mid-vs-bottom: could a French speaker with no English understand you? MIDDLE = "comprehensible for French monoglot"; BOTTOM = "would have difficulty understanding".
- **Register** is scored under Communication, not Language.
- **Verbs, agreement, spelling** are the three named Language failure points; "most verbs incorrect" + "basic agreement not respected" = BOTTOM Language.
- Because the axes are independent, a candidate with accurate but empty/irrelevant French can score high Language / low Communication, and vice versa.

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — reading comprehension (Section A)

### General rules (p.[4])
- Sub-divisions [(a), (b)] **must be answered on the separate lines allocated**. Two answers crammed on one line "is regarded as one answer and is marked accordingly" (p.[4]).
- "Any extra sub-division is rewarded only if one of the previous answers to the question is cancelled" (p.[4]) — i.e. you can't hedge with a second attempt unless you strike out the first.

### Penalty ladder (p.[4]) — the comprehension marking grammar
1. **Excess material: −1 or −2 Marks** (annotation `EXC`, p.[3]: "Excess implies a deduction of 1 or 2 Marks (Reading Comprehensions only)"). Also stated per-question as "Minus 1 Mark for each extraneous element" (e.g. p.[6], p.[7], p.[11]).
2. **All other errors: maximum −1 Mark per question/segment.** These include:
   - Inappropriate quotation
   - Manipulation when not required
   - Language/grammar errors in manipulation
   - When manipulation is required and not attempted
   - **Answers given in the wrong language** — "answers in Irish/English when French required as in Compréhension Écrite; q.6 segments answered in French" (p.[4]).
- The excess penalty can stack **in conjunction with** any point-2 penalty (p.[4]).

### Lifting vs manipulation (the central comprehension distinction)
Each question is tagged with which is allowed:
- **"Appropriate direct quotation OR correct manipulation acceptable"** — lifting the exact phrase from the text is fine (e.g. Q.1(a)/(b), Q.5(b), p.[5], p.[8]).
- **"Correct manipulation required"** — a direct lift is penalised; you must transform the text (e.g. change person/tense, rephrase). E.g. Q.4(b) p.[7], Q.4(a)/5(a) p.[13].
- The mark ladders show manipulation graded: correctly manipulated (e.g. changing *je vis* → *il a vu*) scores full 5; leaving it in the original first person is docked (e.g. p.[13] Q.5(a): "il a parlé de sa carrière" = 5 Marks; "j'ai parlé de ma carrière" = 4 Marks; "Sa carrière" = 3; "Ma carrière" = 2; "Carrière" alone = 0).
- **Partial-lift ladders**: comprehension answers are marked on a descending scale (5 / 4 / 3 / 2 / 1 / 0) by how much of the required idea is captured — a bullet `•` = full marks; a dash `–` = partial or 0 (p.[3] explanatory note). Round brackets = "extraneous material which is not penalised" (p.[3]).
- **Multiple-choice / identify-the-answer items:** "If candidate clearly identifies / highlights correct answer, without using a tick = 5 Marks. If more than one answer offered = 0 Marks" (p.[6], p.[12]) — hedging by offering two options scores zero.

### Q.6 opinion question (each comprehension)
- Worth **5+5 Marks** (two points, ~50 words total), answered **in English** (the question is posed in English and asks candidates to "Refer to the text in support of your answer") — and **"q.6 segments answered in French"** is a penalised wrong-language error (p.[4], p.[9], p.[14]). So the opinion question flips the language expectation: French comprehension, but the opinion answer is in English.

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — Listening/Aural (Section, 80 marks)

General observations (p.[17]):
- "Separate points need NOT be on separate lines" (contrast with reading comprehension, which requires it).
- "No penalty for excess material which does not invalidate the answer" (contrast: reading comprehension penalises excess).
- "Accept any formulation which communicates the information sought" — content over form.
- **Wrong-language penalty:** "If whole test / whole section answered in French: it is marked according to the Marking Scheme, then a 50% penalty of marks gained is applied" (p.[17]; annotation `L` = "Penalty for Listening Comprehension answered in French", p.[3]). Listening answers are expected in English.
- **Two-segment items** (A-2, C-2, D-1): "where less than full marks is achieved in either segment, the higher mark is to be awarded to each segment" (annotation `D`, p.[3], [17]).
- Items are mostly 3 Marks each (Sections A–D) / 2 Marks (Section E), with near-miss dashes at 2/1/0.

## General examiner instructions & annotations (p.[3])

The annotation palette (the examiner's shorthand):
- **bullet `•`** = answer worth full marks; **dash `–`** = partial or 0 marks; **round brackets** = extraneous material not penalised.
- `✓` Correct · underline/tilde = Language/Grammar error(s) · `MMS` Modified Marking Scheme applied · `^` Omission · `EXC` Excess (−1/−2, reading only) · `P` Points in email · `−` Question not answered · `-1` Penalty of minus one · `D` two-segment higher-mark rule · `L` Listening answered in French penalty.

### Modified Marking Scheme (Appendix 1, p.[25])
Applied to candidates granted a spelling/grammar waiver, scribe, spell-check, or recorded answer. For French HL: **spelling errors and incorrect/missing accents are NOT penalised** ("We are regarding incorrect accents as spelling errors which are not to be penalised"). Grammar test: "imagine you are hearing the answer as a recording. Only mistakes that would be picked up when listening should be penalised." Example: `Elle a allée` → the wrong auxiliary *avoir* "could be heard, so underline as a mistake and penalise" (p.[25]).

### Bonus for answering through Irish (Appendix 2 / p.[29])
Candidates who answer through Irish and score **>75% of total marks** get a bonus on a sliding scale (Table 300 @ 5%); ≤75% gets the standard 5% rate; bonus rounded down (p.[29], in Irish).

## Quotable lines (with page refs)

1. **The two-axis principle (p.[15]–[16]):** written production is scored `Communication 30 Marks / Language 30 Marks` (Q.1) and `Communication 20 Marks / Language 20 Marks` (Qs.2–6) — content and language marked separately and equally.
2. **Transcription fails Communication (p.[16], BOTTOM band):**
   > "Mere transcription or very poor treatment of stimulus material · Lack of textual coherence · French monoglot would have difficulty understanding · Communicative intention stultified".
3. **The monoglot test for the middle band (p.[16]):**
   > "Comprehensible for French monoglot · Communicative intention more or less respected".
4. **The comprehension penalty cap (p.[4]):**
   > "All other errors to be penalised a maximum of minus 1 Mark per question/segment" — including "Manipulation when not required", "When manipulation is required and not attempted", and answers "given in the wrong language".
5. **Hedging scores zero (p.[6], p.[12]):**
   > "If more than one answer offered = 0 Marks."
6. **Aural wrong-language penalty (p.[17]):**
   > "If whole test / whole section answered in French: it is marked according to the Marking Scheme, then a 50% penalty of marks gained is applied."
