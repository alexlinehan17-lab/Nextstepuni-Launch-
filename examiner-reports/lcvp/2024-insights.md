## Paper structure and how marks divide

The written paper is marked out of **160 marks** across three sections (title banners in the scheme, pp. 2, 3, 6):

| Section | Title | Marks | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Audio Visual | **30** | 8 short questions (Q1–Q8), **all compulsory**, based on a video/audio-visual clip shown in the exam (p. 2) |
| **B** | Case Study | **30** | 3 questions (Q1–Q3) with sub-parts, **all compulsory**, based on a case study issued in advance (p. 3) |
| **C** | General Questions | **100** | 7 questions printed (Q1–Q7); candidates answer **four**, each worth **25 marks** (100 ÷ 4). Themed on LCVP activities/portfolio work (p. 6 onward) |

Each Section C question totals exactly 25 marks across its sub-parts (verified by summing Q1: 4+4+2+2+4+9 = 25). So the four-question choice is a clean 4 × 25.

## The core marking grammar

The whole paper is built from one repeating atom: a **point** plus **expansion(s)**. The notation beside each question tells the examiner (and, once decoded, the candidate) exactly how credit is split. Learning to read this notation is the single most useful thing a candidate can take from the scheme.

### Notation decoded

- **`N @ 1m`** — a **list/recall** grid. Each correct item scores 1 mark; there are N marks available. No expansion is read or rewarded. Examples: Q2 Audio Visual `2 @ 1m`, Q3 `3 @ 1m` (p. 2); Section C "List four…" questions `4 @ 1m` (Q3(a) p. 9, Q5(a) p. 12). **Writing paragraphs on a `@1m` list wins nothing beyond the item itself.**
- **`n @ 2m (1 + 1)`** — the standard **point + expansion** grid. 1 mark for a valid point, 1 mark for developing/explaining it. Partial credit is available: a bare point still scores 1 of 2. This is the most common grid on the paper (used throughout Sections A, B, C).
- **`n @ 2m (0/2)`** — an **all-or-nothing** grid. The 2 marks are awarded 0 **or** 2, with **no partial credit**. Used where the scheme demands a fully-developed answer or a candidate's own named choice. Where it appears on developed points the scheme annotates it **"expansion needed"** (Q6, Q7 Audio Visual, p. 2–3) — i.e. a point with no expansion scores **zero**, not one.
- **`n @ 3m (1 + 1 + 1)`** — a **point + two expansions** grid (1 for the point, 1 each for two developments). Used for the heavier 9-mark "Explain three…" questions that close most Section C parts (e.g. Q1(d) p. 6, Q4/Q6(d) pp. 11, 14).
- **`Option 1m + 3 valid reasons 3@1m`** — the **recommendation** grid: 1 mark for stating which option you pick, then 3 marks for three justifying reasons (Section B Q3(iii), p. 5).
- **Heading marks** — some structured-document questions award a mark for the correct heading/format. Q2(b) agenda: **"Heading AGENDA = 1 mark"** then **"5 valid agenda items 5@1m"** (p. 7).

### The `(0/2)` vs `(1 + 1)` distinction — the key candidate-facing rule

Two questions can both be worth `n @ 2m` yet mark completely differently:

- `(1 + 1)` rewards a half-answer: name the point, get 1 of 2.
- `(0/2)` is a cliff-edge: an undeveloped point scores **0**.

The "expansion needed" annotation on the `(0/2)` Audio Visual questions (p. 2) makes this explicit. A candidate who lists points telegraphically does fine on `(1+1)` and `@1m` grids but is wiped out on `(0/2)` grids. Knowing which is which changes how much you write.

### Recurring examiner instructions (general marking rules)

These phrases recur across the scheme and govern how points are counted:

- **"No repetition of expansions"** / **"No repetition of points"** / **"No repetition of reason allowed"** — on the 9-mark `3@3m` questions, a candidate cannot recycle the same development across the three required points; each must be distinct (Q1(d) p. 6; Q1(d) "Method… No repetition of reason" p. 7; Q6(d) "No repetition of points" p. 14). Padding with restated points earns nothing.
- **"List is not exhaustive, but answer must be relevant to [the case]"** — open lists are accepted beyond the printed model answers, **provided the answer is applied to the specific case/person** (Section B Q1, p. 3; Q4(a) "List is not exhaustive" p. 11). Relevance/application is the gate, not matching the scheme's wording.
- **"*(compulsory point)"** — some grids reserve one of the marks for a specific mandatory item. In the agenda question, **"Election of Officers … *(compulsory point)"** (p. 7); in the work-experience evaluation, **"Career aspiration/future studies … *Compulsory point"** (p. 11). Miss the starred item and that mark is gone regardless of how many other valid items you give.
- On the "name your own activity/business/enterprise" questions, marking is `(0/2)` — e.g. Q2(a) "Name of any business 2 mark (0/2)" (p. 7); Q7(a) "Name of the activity 2 marks (0/2)" (p. 14). Any valid candidate-supplied answer scores full, but there is no half mark.

## Strategic / structural observations

- **The notation is the instruction.** Unlike a prose rubric, LCVP tells you the mark shape in-line. `@1m` = list tersely and move on. `(1+1)` = one point, one sentence of development. `(0/2)` = develop fully or score nothing. `3@3m (1+1+1)` = one point plus two *distinct* developments.
- **Command words map to grids.** "List" / "Name" → `@1m` recall (no development credit). "Outline" / "Describe" / "Explain … two/three" → `(1+1)` or `(1+1+1)` point-plus-expansion. "Recommend / which option" → option mark + reasons. The verb tells you how much to write.
- **Section C is the volume section.** 100 of 160 marks (≈63%) sit in four chosen questions of 25 marks each. Each 25-mark question typically runs (a) small explain, (b)/(c) two `3@2m` parts, (d) one `3@3m` 9-marker — a predictable escalating shape.
- **Marks-to-minutes.** The paper is 2½ hours (150 min) for 160 marks ≈ **0.94 min/mark**. A 25-mark Section C question ≈ 23–24 minutes; a 2-mark Audio Visual recall ≈ under 2 minutes. The Audio Visual section is front-loaded and time-cheap; the four Section C questions dominate the clock.
- **Application beats recall of the model answer.** The scheme repeatedly says the printed answers are not exhaustive and that credit depends on relevance to the specific case/person — so a well-applied point outside the scheme's list still scores.

## Misconceptions (structural failure modes the grammar punishes)

- **Treating every 2-mark point as partial-creditable.** On `(0/2)` questions an undeveloped point scores 0, not 1. The "expansion needed" flag is the tell.
- **Over-writing on `@1m` lists.** "List four aspects/careers" is `4@1m` — four terse items is full marks; paragraphs waste time for no gain.
- **Repeating developments on the 9-markers.** `3@3m` requires three *distinct* points each with *distinct* expansions; "No repetition" means restating the same idea earns nothing.
- **Skipping the compulsory starred item.** In the agenda and work-experience-evaluation grids one mark is ring-fenced for a specific item (Election of Officers; Career aspiration). Other good items don't substitute for it.
- **Ignoring case application in Section B.** "List is not exhaustive, but answer must be relevant to Sandra" — generic enterprise characteristics that aren't tied to the case can fail even if correct in the abstract.
