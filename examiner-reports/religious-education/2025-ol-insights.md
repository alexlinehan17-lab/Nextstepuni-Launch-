## The one-sentence version

At the **exam-question** level, OL and HL are marked by the **identical six-band holistic grid**
(same bands, same four descriptor rows, same mark ranges). The real OL-vs-HL differences are
**(1) OL has NO descriptive/"implicit-reference" mark cap** — the single most important HL rule
is simply absent at OL; **(2) OL command words are lower-order** (Outline / Describe / Explain
how / Give an account) rather than HL's Assess / Compare / Discuss-the-reliability, so there is
nothing to cap; **(3) OL Section A offers a choice of three questions, not two**; and **(4) the
coursework grids collapse to four bands with different cut-points** from the HL five-band grid.

## Marking grammar — structure, and what is SHARED with HL (pp.3–4)

These are identical to HL; do not treat any as an OL difference:

- **Band-not-count.** Every exam question is marked holistically into one of **six bands —
  Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair, Weak, Poor** — against that question's **Marking Criteria
  (MC)**. No adding-up of points; one band per question. (p.3, General Introduction: *"the mark
  for the candidate's answer is awarded within a range from excellent to very good, good, fair,
  weak, very weak or no grade."*)
- **The same four descriptor rows** print under every question: **Evidence of MC / Relevance /
  Use of skill(s) / Factual Accuracy** (e.g. p.5).
- **The same band-to-marks ranges** by question value:

  | Question value | Excellent | Very Good | Good | Fair | Weak | Poor |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | **40 marks** (pp.5, 6, 10, 12, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 24, 25, 26, 30, 31, 32, 34, 36) | 40–34 | 33–28 | 27–22 | 21–16 | 15–10 | 9–0 |
  | **20 marks** (pp.7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 20, 23, 27, 33, 35, 37) | 20–17 | 16–14 | 13–11 | 10–8 | 7–5 | 4–0 |

  (No 10-mark questions appear in the 2025 OL paper — the HL paper had a 10M×2 tier; OL's
  two-item questions are all 20M×2.)
- **MC / MC X annotations** (p.4) are identical: `MC` = first evidence of the criteria; `MC X` =
  no evidence at all; `[ … ]` = duplicate answer, ignored; wavy lines = seen/blank pages.
  Two-item questions are coded **MC × 2** and score two separate banded marks.
- **Contradiction voids marks** (p.3): *"Words, expressions or phrases must be correctly used in
  context and not contradicted and where there is evidence of incorrect use or contradiction, the
  marks may not be awarded."* Factual Accuracy is one of the four rows, so errors pull the whole
  band down rather than being a separate deduction.
- **Same 320-mark paper skeleton:** Unit One / Section A (80) → Unit Two, any two of three
  sections B/C/D (160) → Unit Three, one of four sections F/G/I/J (80). Same Irish-answer bonus
  ("Tábla 320 @ 10%", p.38) with the identical taper table as HL.
- **Same exemplars-are-illustrative caveat** (p.3): *"The descriptions, definitions and points in
  the scheme are not exhaustive and alternative valid answers are acceptable."*

## OL-vs-HL DIFFERENCE 1 — the descriptive / "implicit-reference" cap is GONE (the big one)

This is the single most important structural difference. At **HL**, higher-order questions carry
a **Note** that ceilings a merely descriptive answer at the **FAIR band** — verbatim *"…with only
implicit reference to [the higher-order skill]… mark on its merits – **Max 10 marks**"* (HL pp.5,
7, 9, 11, 15) and *"**Max 21 marks**"* on a 40M question (HL p.18). The HL scheme contains **six**
such `Max` caps.

At **OL the word "Max" appears zero times** in the whole scheme (verified by full-text search).
The OL equivalents of those Notes drop the ceiling entirely and instead simply **permit** implicit
treatment with no penalty:

- Q1(a), 40M: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to the search for meaning."* (p.5)
- Q1(b), 40M: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to a question about the meaning of life."* (p.6)
- Q2(b)(ii), 20M: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to the search for the meaning of life today."* (p.9)
- Q3(b), 20M×2: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to a similarity and difference in the understanding of divine revelation."* (p.11)
- D(b), 20M×2: *"Note: Allow descriptive answers and implicit reference to stages of moral development."* (p.20)
- D(c), 40M: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to a Christian denomination."* (p.21)
- F(b)(ii), 40M: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to how one type of non-violence could be an effective response…"* (p.25)
- I(a)(i), 40M: *"Note: Allow implicit reference to the evidence of Pre-Christian religious belief."* (p.30)

The only escalation instruction anywhere in the OL scheme is a *consult-the-examiner* note, not a
cap: C(c), 40M — *"Note: If salvation/liberation is explained without reference to a difference in
understanding consult your advising examiner."* (p.18).

**Consequence for student advice:** the HL trap — "a brilliant description that never actually
assesses/compares can't climb above Fair" — **does not exist at OL**. An accurate, well-organised
descriptive answer at OL can band all the way to Excellent, because the OL command words only ever
ask for description/outline in the first place (see Difference 2).

## OL-vs-HL DIFFERENCE 2 — the command-word register is lower-order

The OL "Use of skill(s)" rows and question stems ask for **lower-order Bloom skills**: *setting
out accurate information*, *giving an accurate account*, *outline*, *describe*, *explain how*,
*looking closely at*, *accurately identifying* (pp.5–37). There is **no** *Assess*, *Evaluate*,
*Discuss the reliability of*, or *Compare-and-judge* stem — the higher-order verbs that populate
the HL paper and trigger its caps. Where OL does ask for a similarity/difference (e.g. Q3(b) p.11,
C(c) p.18, I(b)(ii) p.33) the skill is framed as *"an accurate account of a similarity and a
difference"* or *"accurately identifying"* — a describe-both task, not a weigh-and-judge task.
This is why the cap can be absent: the demand it polices is never made.

## OL-vs-HL DIFFERENCE 3 — Section A offers THREE questions, not two

**Unit One / Section A (80 marks):** *"Candidates must answer parts (a) and (b) from one of the
following three questions"* — **Q1 The Tradition of the Search, Q2 The Search for Meaning Today,
Q3 Concepts of God** (pp.5–11). The HL paper offers a choice of **two** Section A questions. OL
therefore gives more question choice at the opening unit. (Units Two and Three have the same
choose-two-of-three / one-of-four structure as HL.)

## OL-vs-HL DIFFERENCE 4 — the coursework grids are 4-band with different cut-points

The 20% coursework titles are **common to OL and HL** (Sections E and H, p.39), and the coursework
**annotation codes are identical** (MC, SI, SS, SF, S, I, P, Q, PI, V, MC X, NR — p.40). But the
**descriptor grids are marked on a coarser band structure at OL**, and the mark boundaries differ
from both the OL exam grid and the HL coursework grid:

- **Part A — Summary of the Investigation (p.41):** **four** bands headed
  **EXCELLENT / GOOD / FAIR / WEAK-VERY WEAK**, with marks **40–33 / 32–25 / 24–17 / 16–0**.
  (Note the header skips "Very Good".) Descriptor rows: Evidence of MC, *sources of information*
  (2nd source = **SI**), *steps taken & skills used* (**SS**), *summary of findings* (**SF**),
  *supporting evidence for conclusions*, *personal engagement*.
- **Part B — Personal reflection (p.42):** **four** bands headed
  **EXCELLENT / VERY GOOD / Good / FAIR-WEAK-VERY WEAK**, same marks **40–33 / 32–25 / 24–17 /
  16–0**. Rows: Evidence of MC, *use of skills* (**S**), *why the title was of interest* (**I**),
  *different perspectives — identification **and balancing*** (**P**), *questions that arose*
  (**Q**), *personal insights* (**PI**), *most valuable part* (**V**).

By contrast the **HL** coursework grid is a **five-band** grid with the cut-points
`40>34, 33>28, 27>22, 21>16, 15>0` (HL p.35). So OL coursework is graded on fewer, wider bands and
at a **more generous top boundary** (OL Excellent starts at 33; HL Excellent starts at 34) and a
**more generous pass floor** (OL Fair reaches down to 17; the top mark for a "some evidence" answer
is higher at OL). The P row still explicitly rewards *balancing* perspectives, and the Q row still
rewards *distinguishing fact from opinion* (p.40, p.42) — a reflection that reports only one
viewpoint bands lower, same as HL.

## Candidate-behaviour findings (OL-specific, for student-facing content)

1. **At OL, a strong description is a top-band answer — there is no "you didn't evaluate" penalty.**
   The Max-marks cap that ceilings descriptive HL answers at Fair is entirely absent; OL command
   words ask only for description/outline (Notes pp.5, 6, 9, 20, 21, 25, 30 all say *"Allow
   implicit/descriptive"*; zero `Max` caps in the whole scheme). *(Source: OL marking scheme 2025,
   pp.5–30; contrast HL pp.5, 7, 9, 11, 15, 18.)*

2. **Accuracy is still the silent grade-killer.** Factual Accuracy is one of the four descriptor
   rows on every question, and *"many major errors"* drops an answer to Weak while *"substantial
   errors"* drops it to Poor, regardless of how much is written; contradicting yourself can void
   marks outright. *(Source: OL scheme p.3 General Introduction + the Factual Accuracy row on every
   grid, e.g. pp.5, 12, 21.)*

3. **Answering a two-item question with only one item costs a whole banded mark.** Questions worth
   20M×2 are coded **MC × 2** and scored as two separate bands that sum to the total (e.g. Q2(a)
   "Describe **two** examples" p.7; D(b) "**two** stages of moral development" p.20; B(b) "**two**
   of the following" p.13). Supplying only one item forfeits one of the two bands. *(Source: OL
   scheme pp.7, 11, 13, 16, 20, 23, 33, 35, 37.)*

4. **Choosing a thin question/section is expensive because there is no partial-points accumulation.**
   Whole answers are banded across four descriptor rows; a candidate who picks a Unit-Two section
   (B/C/D) or Unit-Three section (F/G/I/J) they have thin material on bands low on Evidence-of-MC
   and Relevance with no way to scrape points back. OL's extra Section-A choice (three questions,
   p.5) makes the opening unit the easiest place to bank marks. *(Source: OL scheme pp.5, 12, 22.)*

5. **Similarity/difference questions must actually name BOTH — but only descriptively.** OL
   comparison stems (Q3(b) p.11; C(c) p.18; I(b)(ii) p.33) reward *"an accurate account of a
   similarity **and** a difference"* / *"accurately identifying"*. Giving both, even without any
   evaluation of which is more significant, lands in the top bands — the higher-order weighing HL
   demands is not required here. *(Source: OL scheme pp.11, 18, 33.)*
