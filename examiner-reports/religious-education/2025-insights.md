## The marking system in one sentence

Unlike Geography (SRP tariff) or History (cumulative marking by paragraph), **RE is marked
holistically band-by-band**: the examiner reads the whole answer to a question, judges it
against that question's **Marking Criteria (MC)**, and drops it into one of **six grade bands —
Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair, Weak, Poor** — each band being a fixed mark range for that
question's value. There is no adding-up of points; there is one band per question.

> "Where a mark is graded in the examination marking scheme, the mark for the candidate's answer
> is awarded within a range from excellent to very good, good, fair, weak, very weak or no
> grade." (p.2, General Introduction)

Every question in the paper is printed with the **same four-row × six-column descriptor matrix**
(pp.4–31). The four rows describe *what each band looks like*; they are not four separately
scored components. The rows are:

1. **Evidence of MC** — how much accurate evidence of the specific Marking Criteria is present
   (substantial → very good → good → adequate → inadequate → very little/no evidence).
2. **Relevance** — completely & clearly relevant → clearly → generally → limited → little → not
   relevant.
3. **Use of skill(s)** — the named command skill for that question (e.g. "setting out
   information", "making an informed judgment", "identifies a similarity and/or difference",
   "gives an account of a reason"): Excellent → very good → good → some → little → very little/no.
4. **Factual Accuracy** — no major error(s) → very little → little → some → many → substantial
   error(s).

## The band-to-marks ranges (the fixed grade grammar)

The band boundaries are fixed by the question's mark value. Three values recur across the paper:

| Question value | Excellent | Very Good | Good | Fair | Weak | Poor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **40 marks** (p.6, p.11, and every 40M question) | 40–34 | 33–28 | 27–22 | 21–16 | 15–10 | 9–0 |
| **20 marks** (p.4, p.7, p.9, etc.) | 20–17 | 16–14 | 13–11 | 10–8 | 7–5 | 4–0 |
| **10 marks** (p.8, p.20) | 10–8 | 7 | 6–5 | 4 | 3–2 | 1–0 |

So on a 40-mark question the difference between a top-of-Fair (21) and a bottom-of-Excellent (34)
answer is 13 marks decided on a **single holistic band judgement**, not on a countable list of
points. A Poor answer still floors at a non-zero band ("9–0", "4–0", "1–0"), i.e. any relevant
attempt lands in the lowest band rather than scoring zero.

## The "MC / MC X" annotation — the mechanical heart of the scheme (pp.3, 4)

The examiner physically marks where the answer first satisfies the question's Marking Criteria:

> "Code **MC** in the left margin where the Marking Criteria is first evident in the candidate's
> answer." (repeated under every question, first stated p.4)

The online-marking annotation table (p.3) defines the machinery:

- **MC** = "First evidence of question's Marking Criteria in the candidate's answerbook."
- **MC X** = "No evidence of question's Marking Criteria in the candidate's answerbook." — i.e.
  the examiner records explicitly when a script never meets the criteria at all.
- **`[` … `]`** = "Beginning … End of **duplicate** answer to a question for which marks are
  already awarded." — RE's version of the anti-double-credit rule: re-answering a question you
  have already been marked on earns nothing; the bracketed span is flagged and ignored.
- Horizontal wavy line = "Answer page seen by examiner to a question for which annotation/marks
  are entered elsewhere"; vertical wavy line = "Blank page seen by examiner / No evidence of
  candidate's answer."

Where a question asks for **two** items (e.g. "Outline **two** images of God", 10M × 2), the
instruction is "**Code MC × 2**" — the examiner marks the criteria twice and awards two separate
banded scores that sum to the question total (p.8, p.7, p.11, p.15).

## The single most mechanical rule — the "implicit reference" / descriptive cap

Most higher-order questions ("Assess…", "Compare…", "Discuss the reliability…", "Explain how…
could challenge…") carry a **Note** telling the examiner to allow a purely descriptive answer
**but to ceiling it at the FAIR band** if the candidate does the lower-order skill (describe /
outline / give an account) without the higher-order skill (assess / compare / judge / evaluate
reliability) the command word demanded. This is RE's equivalent of History's "TWO elements …
Max CM = 50" haircut. The cap is always **the top of the FAIR band for that mark value**:

- 20-mark "Assess the impact…": *"If a candidate gives an accurate account … with only implicit
  reference to how it had an influence … mark on its merits – **Max 10 marks**."* (p.5) — 10 is
  the FAIR ceiling of a 20-mark question (10–8).
- 20M × 2 "explain how … faith could be challenged": *"… with only implicit reference to how it
  could challenge a person's religious faith, mark on its merits – **Max 10 marks × 2**."* (p.7)
- 20-mark "Compare the ideas about God…": *"… with only implicit reference to a similarity
  and/or a difference … **Max 10 marks**."* (p.9)
- 20M × 2 "Discuss the reliability…": *"… with only implicit reference to an assessment of their
  reliability … **Max 10 marks × 2**."* (p.11)
- 20M × 2 "Compare how the community of believers is structured…": *"… with only implicit
  reference to similarities and/or differences … **Max 10 marks × 2**."* (p.15)
- 40-mark "Compare the understanding of moral failure…": *"… with only implicit reference to a
  similarity and/or a difference between them, mark on its merits – **Max 21 marks**."* (p.18) —
  21 is the FAIR ceiling of a 40-mark question (21–16).

The structural lesson: **a strong description that never actually does the comparing / assessing /
judging the command word asked for cannot climb above FAIR (a D/C-grade band), no matter how
accurate.** Many questions also carry a bare *"Note: Allow descriptive answers."* (pp.6, 9, 11,
13, 14, 15, 18) — descriptive treatment is legitimate, but the cap is what bites when the
higher-order skill is absent.

## The contradiction / misuse rule — accuracy can void a mark (p.2)

> "Words, expressions or phrases must be correctly used in context and not contradicted and where
> there is evidence of incorrect use or contradiction, the marks may not be awarded." (p.2)

The **Factual Accuracy** row of every matrix reinforces this: an answer with "many major error(s)"
sits in the Weak band and "substantial error(s)" in Poor, regardless of how much material is
present. Accuracy is one of the four descriptor dimensions, so errors pull the whole band down —
they are not a separate deduction.

## Paper structure and where the 320 written marks divide

The written paper is **320 marks** (confirmed by the Irish-answer bonus table headed "Tábla 320 @
10%", p.32). It is built from three Units:

- **Unit One — Section A, The Search for Meaning and Values (80 marks, pp.4–9):** candidates
  answer **ONE of two questions** (Q1 or Q2). Each question totals 80 across its parts — e.g. Q1 =
  (a)(i) 20 + (a)(ii) 20 + (b) 40; Q2 = (a) 20×2 + (b)(i) 10×2 + (b)(ii) 20.
- **Unit Two — 160 marks (pp.9–18):** answer **any two parts (a)/(b)/(c) from two of three
  sections** — B Christianity: Origins and Contemporary Expressions; C World Religions; D Moral
  Decision-Making. Each section yields 80 marks from its two answered parts (parts are 20×2, 20×2,
  or 40).
- **Unit Three — 80 marks (pp.18–31):** answer **any two parts from ONE of four sections** — F
  Issues of Justice and Peace; G Worship, Prayer and Ritual; I Religion: The Irish Experience; J
  Religion and Science.

So the written total is 80 + 160 + 80 = **320**. Because whole answers are banded, **choosing a
question/section you have thin material on is expensive** — there is no way to accumulate partial
points; a thin answer simply bands low across all four descriptor rows.

**Answering through Irish (p.32):** normal-rate bonus is 10% of the 320-mark total, applied up to
75% of marks; above 75% the tapering table (p.32) applies (e.g. base 267–270 → +15 bonus; base
317–320 → +0). Bonus marks are rounded down.

## Coursework — its own descriptor grids (Part A + Part B, pp.33–36)

RE coursework (the 20% project, titles common to OL and HL, p.33) is done on **one prescribed
title** chosen from **Section E or Section H** (2025 titles listed p.33). It is marked on two
descriptor grids, each headed by a **40-mark MC band** ("Marks 40 > 34, 33 > 28, 27 > 22, 21 > 16,
15 > 0" — note the coursework grid collapses Weak/Poor into one **WEAK/POOR** band, unlike the
six-band exam grid):

- **Part A — Summary of the Investigation (p.35):** banded on Evidence of the title's Marking
  Criteria (40 marks), plus descriptor rows for *sources of information* (2nd source coded **SI**),
  *steps taken and skills used* (coded **SS**), *summary of findings* (coded **SF**), *supporting
  evidence for conclusions*, and *personal engagement with the title*.
- **Part B — Personal reflection (p.36):** banded on Evidence of the Marking Criteria (40 marks),
  plus *use of skills* (coded **S**), *why the title was of interest* (**I**), *different
  perspectives encountered* (**P** — "identification **and balancing** different perspectives"),
  *questions that arose* (**Q**), *personal insights gained* (**PI**), and *most valuable part*
  (**V**).

Coursework online annotations (p.34) mirror the exam's MC/MC X logic and add **NR** = "No
Response. Not completed by the candidate." The scheme repeatedly rewards *balancing* perspectives
and *distinguishing fact from opinion* in the descriptor language (p.34, p.36), so a reflection
that only reports one viewpoint bands lower on the P row.

## General examiner conventions (p.2)

- The scheme is **not exhaustive**: "In many cases only key phrases are given… The descriptions,
  definitions and points in the scheme are not exhaustive and alternative valid answers are
  acceptable. If you are unsure of the validity of an alternative answer, contact your Advising
  Examiner." (p.2) — i.e. the bulleted "e.g." exemplars under each question are *illustrative*, not
  a checklist to hit.
- **Do not assume future schemes copy this one:** "Assumptions about future marking schemes on the
  basis of past schemes should be avoided… aspects of the structure, detail and application of the
  marking scheme… are subject to change from one year to the next without notice." (p.2)
