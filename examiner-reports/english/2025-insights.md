## The PCLM criteria for assessment (p.3)

Every task on both papers is assessed against four criteria (verbatim, p.3):

| Criterion | Weighting |
|---|---|
| Clarity of Purpose (P) | 30% of the marks available for the task |
| Coherence of Delivery (C) | 30% of the marks available for the task |
| Efficiency of Language Use (L) | 30% of the marks available for the task |
| Accuracy of Mechanics (M) | 10% of the marks available for the task |

Each answer is a response to a specific task requiring candidates (p.3):

- "To display a clear and purposeful engagement with the set task"
- "To sustain the response in an appropriate manner over the entire answer"
- "To manage and control language appropriate to the task"
- "To display levels of accuracy in spelling and grammar appropriate to the
  required/chosen register."

Schematised detail of the criteria is Appendix 1 (p.50); the Grade Grid used to apply
them is Appendix 2 (pp.51–52).

**The primacy-of-P rule (p.3):** "Given the primacy of Clarity of Purpose (P), marks
awarded for either Coherence of Delivery (C) or Efficiency of Language Use (L) cannot
exceed the marks awarded for Clarity of Purpose."

**Appendix 1 schematised descriptors (p.50):**

- **P — Clarity of Purpose:** engagement with the set task, e.g. relevance, focus,
  originality, freshness, evidence of critical literacy (where appropriate), clear aim,
  understanding of genre — 30
- **C — Coherence of Delivery:** ability to sustain the response over the entire
  answer; where appropriate: continuity of argument, sequencing, management of ideas,
  choice of reference, use of examples, engagement with texts, control of register and
  shape, creative modelling — 30
- **L — Efficiency of Language Use:** management and control of language to achieve
  clear communication, e.g. vocabulary, syntax, sentence patterns, punctuation
  appropriate to the register, use of lively interesting phrasing, energy, style,
  fluency appropriate to the task — 30
- **M — Accuracy of Mechanics:** spelling and grammar, e.g. appropriate levels of
  accuracy in spelling, grammatical patterns appropriate to the register — 10

## Discrete vs combined criteria awarding

Two application modes (Appendix 2, pp.51–52):

- **Discrete criteria:** P, C, L, M are awarded separately in that order ("marks will
  be indicated on candidates' scripts in the order in which they are set out above",
  p.3), each sub-mark graded on the 30%/10% rows of the grid. Used for the big
  single-task answers: Paper 1 QB (P15/C15/L15/M5, e.g. p.6), the Composition
  (P30/C30/L30/M10, p.14), the Single Text (P18/C18/L18/M6, p.22), the single-part
  70-mark Comparative question (P21/C21/L21/M7, p.35), and Prescribed Poetry
  (P15/C15/L15/M5, p.49).
- **Combined criteria:** one holistic mark against the criteria, using single-row
  grids for 40/30/20/15/10-mark denominations (p.52). Used for the Comprehending QA
  parts (15/15/20), the two-part Comparative question (30 + 40) and the Unseen Poem.

## Mark allocations by question type

**Paper 1 (200 marks)**

- **Section I — Comprehending (100 marks)** (p.4): Question A (50) on one text +
  Question B (50) on a *different* text — "Candidates may NOT answer a Question A and
  a Question B on the same text." Introductions and accompanying illustrations are
  integral parts of the texts. QA splits 15 + 15 + 20 (combined criteria); QB is a
  functional-writing task marked discretely P15/C15/L15/M5 (p.6).
- **Section II — Composing (100 marks)** (pp.13–14): one composition from seven,
  reflecting "information, argument, persuasion, narration, and the aesthetic use of
  language". The paper is built around a single theme and *all* texts on the paper are
  a resource — candidates "are free to refer to, quote from, or draw ideas from any or
  all of the texts" (p.13). Marked discretely P30/C30/L30/M10 (p.14).

**Paper 2 (200 marks)**

- **Section 1 — The Single Text (60 marks)** (p.21): one question; candidates
  answering on dramatic texts "are free to support the points they make by reference
  to performances of the texts that they have seen". Marked discretely
  P18/C18/L18/M6 (p.22).
- **Section II — The Comparative Study (70 marks)** (p.32): one question from Theme
  or Issue / Cultural Context / General Vision and Viewpoint. May not answer on the
  Single Text text; all texts must be prescribed for comparative study for the year;
  "Candidates may refer to only one film". Two formats: two-part 30(a) + 40(b)
  (combined criteria; part (b) requires two *other* texts, and "Candidates are not
  required to make comparative links with the text discussed in part (a)", p.34), or a
  single 70-mark question marked discretely P21/C21/L21/M7 (p.35). Candidates may
  ground responses in "one/more key moment/s" but illustrative reference may come from
  any part of the text (p.32).
- **Section III — Poetry (70 marks)** (p.42): compulsory A *and* B. A — Unseen Poem
  (20): either Q1 (10 + 10) or Q2 (20), combined criteria. B — Prescribed Poetry (50):
  marked discretely P15/C15/L15/M5 (p.49). Candidates "have freedom of choice in
  relation to the poems studied" and "are not required to refer to any specific
  poem/s" (p.44).

## General instructions to examiners

- **Marking schemes are working documents** (p.2): the draft "is not finalised until
  examiners have applied it to candidates' work"; "The Chief Examiner is the final
  authority regarding whether or not the marking scheme has been correctly applied to
  any piece of candidate work." Model answers "are not intended to be exhaustive."
- **Indicative material is a guide, not a checklist** (p.3): "The indicative material
  is not exhaustive and all appropriate valid answers should be marked according to
  their merits, using the criteria for assessment."
- **Codes and annotation** (p.3): examiners "place an accurate tick at the points
  identified in the answer, underline all errors in candidates' work and use the
  proper shorthand codes". Appendix 5 (p.57) lists the online annotations: tick =
  "Correct, Relevant, Valid, Of Merit"; horizontal wavy line = error underlined;
  vertical wavy line = page seen / inefficient use of language; boxed MMS = modified
  marking scheme applied; boxed INVL = text used in an invalid fashion; the highest
  mark for a question goes in the marking panel, with additional attempts at the same
  question shown in square brackets.
- **Use the full range of marks** (p.3): examiners should "utilise the full range of
  marks as appropriate".
- **Unseen poetry stance** (p.42): "guard against the temptation to assume a 'correct'
  reading of the poem. Reward the candidates' awareness of the patterned nature of the
  language of poetry, its imagery, its sensuous qualities, and its suggestiveness."
- **Recurring per-question directives:** "Mark ex N by reference to the criteria for
  assessment"; "Pay particular attention to the quality of the response"; "Reward a
  sustained focus, development of ideas and clarity of expression" (e.g. p.4);
  candidates "should engage with all aspects of the question, although not necessarily
  equally" (e.g. pp.6, 9).

## Reasonable accommodations — the modified marking scheme (Appendix 3, pp.53–54)

Candidates with an approved spelling/grammar-waiver accommodation (RACE) are assessed
on everything "except spelling and written punctuation elements":
P 30% + C 30% + L (including grammar) 30% assessed; "Spelling and Written Punctuation
10% (not to be assessed)". Effectively marked out of 90%; the paper total is divided
by 9 and added back to produce the grand total, with fractions rounded down (worked
example p.53: 115 ÷ 9 = 12 → 127). Under combined criteria, 40/30/20/15/10-mark
questions are assessed out of 36/27/18/13/9 with their own grid (p.54).

## Question-choice anomalies (Appendix 4, pp.55–56)

- **QA and QB answered on the same text (Paper 1):** "Mark both attempts out of full
  marks. Disallow the lower mark."
- **Two or more QAs and one QB (or vice versa):** mark all; "Allow the Question B to
  stand and the highest Question A from a different text."
- **All QAs and QBs answered:** mark all; "Award the highest combination."
- **No attempt at Shakespeare (Paper 2):** mark the non-Shakespearean Single Text and
  the comparative answer per the scheme, then apply an adjustment: for a two-part
  (30/40) comparative, disallow the 1(a) marks OR 50% of the 1(b) marks (rounded
  down); for a single-part 70-mark question, disallow one third (rounded down).
  "Award... whichever provides the candidate with the best outcome."
- **Invalid text / invalid combination in the Comparative Study:** same adjustment
  machinery — disallow the (a) answer, or 50% of (b), or one third of a 70-mark
  answer; "Award the valid marks that provide the candidate with the best outcome."
