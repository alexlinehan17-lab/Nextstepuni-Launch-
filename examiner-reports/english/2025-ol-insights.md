## marking-grammar STRUCTURE — what is the SAME as HL

OL uses the **identical PCLM criteria-for-assessment system** as HL. All the top-level machinery
is shared verbatim:

- **The four PCLM criteria and weightings (p.3, Appendix 1 p.72):** Clarity of Purpose (P) 30%,
  Coherence of Delivery (C) 30%, Efficiency of Language Use (L) 30%, Accuracy of Mechanics (M) 10%.
  The Appendix 1 schematised descriptors are word-for-word the HL descriptors (p.72).
- **The primacy-of-P rule (p.3, verbatim, identical to HL):** "Given the primacy of Clarity of
  Purpose (P), marks awarded for either Coherence of Delivery (C) or Efficiency of Language Use (L)
  cannot exceed the marks awarded for Clarity of Purpose."
- **Indicative-material-is-not-a-checklist rule (p.3, verbatim, identical to HL).**
- **Discrete-criteria ordering** (P first, then C, L, M on the script) — same rule, p.3.
- **Reasonable-accommodations modified scheme (Appendix 3, p.75):** same mechanism as HL — assess
  out of 90%, divide paper total by 9, add back, round fractions down (worked example p.75: 125 ÷ 9
  = 13 → 138).
- **Online annotation palette (Appendix 4, p.76):** same symbols as HL — tick = "Correct, Relevant,
  Valid, Of Merit"; horizontal wavy line = error underlined; vertical wavy line = page seen /
  inefficient language; boxed MMS = modified scheme; boxed INVL = text used in an invalid fashion.

**So: OL still uses PCLM.** The rulebook is the same; the differences are all in (a) the grade-grid
labels, (b) the *combined-criteria mechanics*, (c) the mark denominations, and (d) the paper
architecture / question structure.

## marking-grammar STRUCTURE — OL-vs-HL DIFFERENCES

### 1. Grade-grid labels are O1–O8, not H1–H8 (Appendix 2, pp.73–74)

The bands are labelled **01–08** (i.e. O1–O8) instead of H1–H8. The numeric band boundaries on any
given denomination are the same ratios (e.g. 100-mark grid O1 = 100–90, O2 = 89–80 … O8 = 29–0,
p.73 — identical numbers to the HL 100-mark grid), but the grade letter is O.

### 2. Combined criteria is a TWO-BLOCK split (P&C / L&M) — the signature OL difference (pp.73–74)

This is the single most important OL-specific mechanic. At **HL, combined criteria = one holistic
mark** against a single-row grid. At **OL, combined criteria is broken into two grouped sub-blocks**:

- **P&C** (Purpose + Coherence together) = **60%** of the question
- **L&M** (Language + Mechanics together) = **40%** of the question

Every combined-criteria question on the OL paper carries this footer, e.g. `Combined Criteria ex 15
P+C = 9  L+M = 6` (p.5), `ex 10  P+C = 6  L+M = 4` (p.6), `ex 20  P+C = 12  L+M = 8` (p.60). The
Appendix 2 combined grids give both a P&C row and an L&M row for the 20/15/10-mark denominations
(pp.73–74):

| Combined denom | O1 | O2 | O3 | O4 | O5 | O6 | O7 | O8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 marks (total) | 20–18 | 16 | 14 | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 5–0 |
| — P&C (of 12) | 12–11 | 10 | 9 | 8 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3–0 |
| — L&M (of 8) | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2–0 |
| 15 marks (total) | 15–14 | 12 | 11 | 9 | 8 | 6 | 5 | 4–0 |
| — P&C (of 9) | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2–0 |
| — L&M (of 6) | 6 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1–0 |
| 10 marks (total) | 10–9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2–0 |
| — P&C (of 6) | 6 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1–0 |
| — L&M (of 4) | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1–0 |

(HL, by contrast, has 40/30/20/15/10 single-row combined grids with no P&C / L&M split.)

### 3. Discrete-criteria denominations differ (Appendix 2, p.73)

OL discrete grids are **100 / 50 / 40 / 30 marks**. HL discrete grids are **100 / 70 / 60 / 50
marks**. This reflects OL's smaller per-question values (30-mark Single Text questions, 40-mark
Comparative (b), 50-mark QB). The discrete 100-mark grid is shared (used for the Composition).

### 4. Paper 1 — Comprehending Question A is split into FOUR sub-parts, not three (pp.5–6)

- **OL QA (50):** (i) 15 + (ii) 15 + (iii)(a) 10 + (iii)(b) 10 — all **combined criteria** with the
  P&C / L&M split. HL QA (50) splits 15 + 15 + 20.
- One OL QA sub-part is typically a short **personal/creative** prompt (2025 (iii)(b): "Describe a
  moment when love was shown to you…", p.6) — a scaffolded personal-writing turn embedded in
  comprehension. HL QA stays analytical.
- **QB (50)** is the same at both levels: a functional-writing task marked **discretely P15/C15/L15/M5**
  (OL 2025: a charity pamphlet, p.7). "Candidates may NOT answer Question A and Question B on the
  same text" (p.7) — shared rule.

### 5. Paper 1 — Composition: SAME as HL (pp.13–20)

One composition from a set, marked **discretely P30/C30/L30/M10, ex 100** (p.14). Same mechanics as
HL. OL prompts are more tightly scaffolded / genre-signposted (e.g. "write a short story… in the
first person", "write a personal essay… reflect on at least two things", pp.14–15) but the marking
grammar is identical.

### 6. Paper 2 — Single Text (60) is answered as TWO 30-mark questions, not one 60-mark question (pp.21–24)

Big architectural difference. **OL:** for the chosen text (A–I) the candidate "answer[s] any two of
the following four questions. Each question carries 30 marks" (p.21). A 30-mark question is either:
- **split into three 10-mark parts** (a)/(b)/(c), each **combined `P+C = 6  L+M = 4`** (p.21), or
- a **single discrete 30-mark task**, `Discrete Criteria ex 30  P = 9  C = 9  L = 9  M = 3` (p.24).

**HL:** the Single Text (60) is ONE question marked discretely P18/C18/L18/M6. OL therefore gives
candidates two shorter, more scaffolded bites and a discrete 30-mark grid that HL does not use.

### 7. Paper 2 — Comparative Study (70): three named categories, and a (a)+(b) structure with a discrete 40-mark (b) (pp.52–58)

- **OL categories:** **A Theme / B Social Setting / C Relationships** (p.52). HL uses **Theme or
  Issue / Cultural Context / General Vision and Viewpoint** — OL renames and simplifies the modes.
- **OL structure:** every comparative question is `(a)(i) 15 + (a)(ii) 15 + (b) 40`. Parts (a)(i)
  and (a)(ii) are **combined** (`P+C = 9  L+M = 6`) and are heavily scaffolded ("Name one of the
  texts… identify a theme…", "Name another text…", pp.52–53). Part (b) is the comparative task,
  marked **discretely `ex 40  P = 12  C = 12  L = 12  M = 4`** (pp.52, 55–58).
- Shared rules: may not use the Single Text text; "may refer to only one film"; apply the question
  to both texts "though not necessarily equally"; "the emphasis is on identifying similarities
  and/or differences" (p.53).

### 8. Paper 2 — Poetry (70): Unseen 20 + Prescribed 50 (pp.59–61)

- **Unseen Poem (20):** two compulsory questions, each **combined `ex 10  P+C = 6  L+M = 4`**
  (p.59). (HL Unseen offers a Q1 10+10 / Q2 20 choice; OL sets two fixed 10-mark questions.)
- **Prescribed Poetry (50):** one poem (A–D at OL), structure `1(a) 15 + 1(b) 15 + 2 [20]`, all
  **combined** (`ex 15  P+C = 9  L+M = 6`; the Q2 option `ex 20  P+C = 12  L+M = 8`, pp.60–61).
  HL Prescribed Poetry (50) is a single question marked **discretely P15/C15/L15/M5**. So OL splits
  poetry into three scaffolded combined parts where HL marks one discrete essay.

### 9. Question-choice anomalies are far simpler at OL — and there is NO Shakespeare rule (p.4)

OL's entire misreading-of-instructions procedure is two lines (p.4): "Mark all attempts out of full
marks. The lower mark/s will automatically be disallowed" (for both Section I and Section 2 over-
answering). **HL** has an elaborate Appendix 4 (QA/QB-on-same-text, the compulsory-Shakespeare
adjustment, invalid-comparative-text machinery). OL has **no compulsory Shakespeare requirement** —
a Shakespeare play is merely one optional Single Text (A–I; Shakespeare appears in the Single Text
options, pp.41–44), so there is no Shakespeare-choice penalty apparatus at all.

## Summary of OL structural allocations (2025)

| Paper / Section | Marks | OL structure & marking | HL equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 §I Comprehending QA | 50 | 15+15+10+10, all combined (P&C/L&M) | 15+15+20 combined |
| P1 §I Comprehending QB | 50 | discrete P15/C15/L15/M5 | same |
| P1 §II Composition | 100 | discrete P30/C30/L30/M10 | same |
| P2 §I Single Text | 60 | 2 × 30 (each: 3×10 combined, or discrete P9/C9/L9/M3) | 1 × 60 discrete P18/C18/L18/M6 |
| P2 §II Comparative | 70 | (a)(i)15 + (a)(ii)15 combined + (b)40 discrete P12/C12/L12/M4 | 30(a)+40(b) combined, or 70 discrete P21/C21/L21/M7 |
| P2 §III Unseen Poem | 20 | 2 × 10 combined | Q1 10+10 or Q2 20 combined |
| P2 §III Prescribed Poetry | 50 | 15+15+20, all combined (P&C/L&M) | 1 × 50 discrete P15/C15/L15/M5 |

The through-line: **OL replaces most of HL's big discrete-essay tasks with shorter, scaffolded,
combined-criteria parts** graded on the two-block P&C / L&M split. Discrete P/C/L/M marking survives
at OL only for QB (50), the Composition (100), an optional single Single-Text question (30), and
Comparative part (b) (40).

## Candidate-behaviour findings for authored scripts (OL-specific)

1. **P&C is 60% of every short OL answer — purpose + staying on task dominates.** Because combined
   OL questions bank 60% on P&C and only 40% on L&M (grids pp.73–74), an OL candidate who answers the
   actual task set, stays focused and develops the point banks the majority of marks even with
   ordinary language. The scheme repeatedly cues this: "Reward focus, development, support, clarity
   and accuracy" (pp.21–23) and "Reward sustained focus, clarity of expression, developed and
   supported ideas" (pp.5–6). *(Source: 2025 OL marking scheme, pp.5–6, 21–23, 73–74.)*

2. **Follow the explicit instruction-counts — the scheme awards marks for doing the mechanical
   requirement.** OL prompts specify quantities and the notes enforce them: "They must reflect on at
   least two things" (p.15), "must discuss at least three reasons" (p.22), "Identify two emotions"
   (p.60), star rating "N.B. – not just one and five" (p.22). Candidates who under-supply the
   required number cap their Clarity-of-Purpose. *(Source: pp.15, 22, 60.)*

3. **OL comprehension embeds a personal-writing turn — treat it as a real composition, in register.**
   QA (iii)(b) "Describe a moment when love was shown to you…" (p.6) and QA (ii)-type "give reasons"
   tasks are marked on PCLM, not on comprehension recall; the scheme says "Award a sense of
   appropriate register" and rewards a genuine, developed personal moment. Thin one-line answers lose
   the P&C block. *(Source: p.6.)*

4. **Single Text and Comparative reward NAMING and grounding in the text over abstract commentary.**
   OL notes demand concrete anchoring: "Support your answer with reference to the text",
   "demonstrate your knowledge of the text" (pp.21–23), and the Comparative scaffolds insist
   candidates "Name one of the texts… identify a theme…" then "Name another text…" (pp.52–53).
   Advice/opinion must be "grounded in the plot of the text" (p.23). Unsupported general assertion is
   the classic OL mark-loss. *(Source: pp.21–23, 52–53.)*

5. **Comparative part (b) is the only big discrete OL task and it must actually COMPARE.** Worth 40
   discrete marks (P12/C12/L12/M4, pp.52, 55–58), the (b) task is where "the emphasis is on
   identifying similarities and/or differences" and "requires the candidate to make comparison(s)
   throughout their response" (p.53). Candidates who treat two texts separately (two mini-essays)
   forfeit the Coherence marks that reward sustained comparison. Apply to both texts "though not
   necessarily equally" (p.53). *(Source: pp.52–58.)*

## Quotable lines

1. **Primacy of Clarity of Purpose (p.3, identical to HL — the rule that anchors the whole scheme):**
   > "Given the primacy of Clarity of Purpose (P), marks awarded for either Coherence of Delivery (C)
   > or Efficiency of Language Use (L) cannot exceed the marks awarded for Clarity of Purpose."
2. **Indicative material is not a checklist (p.3):**
   > "The indicative material is not exhaustive and all appropriate valid answers should be marked
   > according to their merits, using the Criteria for Assessment."
3. **The OL comparative emphasis (p.53):**
   > "Consideration should be given to the quality of the comparative writing – the emphasis is on
   > identifying similarities and/or differences. This requires the candidate to make comparison(s)
   > throughout their response."
4. **Chief Examiner as final authority (p.2, shared with HL):**
   > "The Chief Examiner is the final authority regarding whether or not the marking scheme has been
   > correctly applied to any piece of candidate work."
