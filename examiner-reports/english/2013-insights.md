# English 2013 — Examiner Insights

## Source

- **Report type:** Chief Examiner's Report (SEC)
- **Year:** 2013 (the most recent SEC Chief Examiner's Report for LC English)
- **Levels:** Higher and Ordinary
- **Original filename:** `Chief_Examiner_Report_English_2013.pdf` (examinations.ie;
  our copy retrieved via the Internet Archive snapshot of the official URL)
- **Renamed to:** `2013-chief-examiner.pdf`
- Page refs are PDF page numbers, which match the printed page labels.
- **Context:** the syllabus examined (introduced 1999, first examined 2001) is the
  same PCLM-assessed course as today, but the pre-2017 A1–NG grading scale applies to
  the statistics. Cross-reference `2025-insights.md` for the current marking-scheme
  structure.

## How the criteria map to skills (pp.4, 13, 17)

- The four criteria P/C/L/M are "applied discretely or in combination" (p.4). Recall
  and reproduction skills (e.g. spelling accuracy) sit under Mechanics; higher-order
  composition skills sit under P, C and L (p.4).
- "candidates' language skills are continuously assessed in the marking of answers to
  all questions on both Papers 1 and Paper 2… The criteria for assessment… are applied
  in the case of every answer at both Higher and Ordinary Levels." (p.13)
- "Candidates are rewarded for skills in critical literacy under aspects of the
  assessment criteria 'Clarity of Purpose' and 'Coherence of Delivery' such as: focus,
  relevance, continuity of argument and management of ideas." (p.17)

## Common errors by question type

### Paper 1, Section I — Comprehending

- HL average 34/50 (68%) on both QA and QB (Table 4, p.7).
- Overly literal reading costs marks: "a number of candidates failed to appreciate
  William Trevor's nuanced reference to the word 'fashion'… it impacted negatively on
  the ability of candidates to understand what the writer meant" (pp.14–15).
- Visual literacy weak at both levels: "close 'reading' of visual images was not
  always strongly evident" (p.14).
- OL review-writing: some candidates "did not appear to appreciate that writing a
  review requires more than providing a summary… and an expression of the reviewer's
  verdict in terms of 'stars'" (p.10). Better answers were reflective and evaluative.
- Letter-writing rubrics flagged for future attention: "greater attention will be paid
  to the rubrics appropriate to the task (e.g. return address, date, salutation and
  closing signature). Any standard formatting of these rubrics will be acceptable."
  (p.15)

### Paper 1, Section II — Composing

- HL average 67/100 (p.7). Best work: "fresh, well-organised, confident responses";
  weaker scripts were "extremely brief and lacked development" and "some candidates
  fail to grasp the fundamentals of short story writing" (p.15).
- Genre control is the discriminator: "It is imperative that candidates who choose to
  write in a particular genre be familiar with the conventions of that genre and show
  evidence of this knowledge" (p.16). Strong short stories showed narrative shape,
  characterisation, coherent plot, setting, dialogue and atmosphere (p.16).
- OL: "it is difficult to sustain a response or develop a range of ideas necessary to
  acquire maximum marks if the answer is brief in the extreme" (pp.15–16).

### Paper 2, Section I — The Single Text

- HL average 38/60 (63%) (p.7). *Macbeth* dominated "almost to the exclusion of the
  four other options" (p.8).
- Quotation quality cuts both ways: "many candidates benefitted from the appropriate
  use of apt and accurate quotation. They also observed that the careless use of
  quotation, observed in a significant number of responses, served to undermine
  answers." (p.8)

### Paper 2, Section II — The Comparative Study

- The weakest section at both levels: HL average 42/70 (60%), OL 39/70 (58%)
  (pp.7, 9); "candidates in general scored less well here than in other sections"
  (p.19).
- "a significant minority of candidates were hampered by a rigid and formulaic
  approach" (p.8); "Formulaic approaches… can hinder candidates by inhibiting their
  engagement with the terms of the questions and curtailing the expression of
  independent opinion" (pp.19–20).
- "examples of under-achievement… could often be attributed to a loss of focus in
  terms of the requirements of the task"; "The quality of evidence cited to support an
  opinion was a significant discriminator used by examiners." (p.18)
- Invalid texts (rare but penalised): not permitted are "a text already answered on as
  a Single Text", "a text not on the prescribed list for the current year", "the use
  of two films" (p.8). Candidates "should be prepared to refer to three texts" (p.8).
- OL: many candidates "demonstrated 'banks of knowledge'… However, in order to score
  well… candidates were required to demonstrate an ability to manage this knowledge in
  answer to the specific questions asked" (p.11).

### Paper 2, Section III — Poetry

- HL: Unseen Poetry was the lowest-scoring element on the paper, average 11/20 (55%)
  (p.7), and "a number of candidates did not answer on this compulsory section of the
  examination, resulting in a loss of up to twenty marks" (pp.8–9).
- Studied Poetry (HL average 33/50): "Candidates were most successful when they
  avoided a formulaic approach and demonstrated the ability to link and cross
  reference the work of their chosen poet" (p.9).
- Failure to attend to all parts of the question marred answers (OL example: ignoring
  "the teacher's attitude and behaviour" in the *Anseo* question, p.12); some
  candidates showed "very cursory acquaintance with the poems" (p.12).

## Strategic / structural observations

- **Section averages (HL, Table 4, p.7):** Comprehending QA 68%, QB 68%, Composition
  67%, Single Text 63%, Comparative Study 60%, Unseen Poetry 55%, Studied Poetry 66% —
  "a reasonably consistent performance… across all elements" (p.7), with the unseen
  poem and the comparative as the weak points.
- **Omission and timing:** "A small number of candidates did not complete all
  necessary sections of the examination, inefficient time management may have been a
  factor" (p.20); "Omitting questions or parts of questions inevitably has a
  deleterious effect on outcomes" (p.21).
- **Question choice is constrained:** QA and QB may not be answered on the same text
  (pp.7, 10), so the QA selection shapes the QB options.
- **Level choice:** examiners judged that HL candidates receiving grade E or lower
  (1.7%) "would have been better served by taking the subject at Ordinary level" (p.6).
- **Process writing recommended:** "research, plan, draft, re-draft and edit" (p.20).

## Misconceptions

- That the marker expects agreement with the question's premise: "questions… are
  framed to invite candidates to engage with them but not necessarily to agree…
  challenging the terms of a question, perhaps disagreeing with some part or the
  entire premise outlined, is an acceptable way in which to approach an answer" —
  catered for in the schemes "by the inclusion of points of disputation and also by
  use of the term 'et cetera'" (p.17).
- That a review = summary + star rating (OL, p.10).
- That reproducing prepared "banks of knowledge" scores marks without shaping them to
  the question asked (pp.11, 20).
- That language is to be read literally: "Candidates need to guard against an overly
  literal interpretation of language" (p.14).
