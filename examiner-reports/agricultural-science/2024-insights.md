## Paper / component structure and mark allocation

Agricultural Science is assessed in two components. This marking scheme covers **both**:

| Component | Weight | Marks | Where in scheme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual Investigative Study (IIS) coursework | 25% | 100 | pp.6–11 |
| Written examination | 75% | 300 | pp.12–end |
| **Total** | **100%** | **400** | |

### Written examination — "best-of" answering (pp.12, 18)

| Section | Rule | Per question | Section total |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Answer any **10** questions | **10** marks each | **100** |
| B | Answer any **4** questions | **50** marks each | **200** |

("Section A — Answer any 10 questions, 10 marks for each question, Total for section is 100 marks", p.12; "Section B — Answer any 4 questions, 50 for each question, Total for section is 200 marks", p.18.) Section B questions are internally structured with an **Or** between alternative full-question options (e.g. Q13 (a)–(c) **Or** (d), p.18), so candidates choose one route through each attempted question.

### IIS — one holistic mark per section, from a five-band rubric (pp.6–11)

Each IIS section is awarded a **single mark** on an Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair / Weak band scale, with a mark range for each band and "indicative content" sub-allocations to guide the single mark:

| IIS section | Marks | Band cut-offs (Exc / VG / Good / Fair / Weak) | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction and background research | 20 | 18–20 / 14–17 / 10–13 / 6–9 / 0–5 | p.7 |
| The investigative process | 25 | 23–25 / 18–22 / 13–17 / 8–12 / 0–7 | p.8 |
| Results, analysis, and conclusions | 35 | 32–35 / 25–31 / 18–24 / 11–17 / 0–10 | p.9 |
| Reflection on the study | 10 | 9–10 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 0–2 | p.10 |
| Communication and innovation | 10 | 9–10 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 0–2 | p.11 |
| References | (no discrete marks) | checked within sections; weak referencing depresses the host section's mark | p.10 |

IIS total = **100**. Suggested word ranges are printed per section (e.g. Introduction 300–500 words, p.7; Results 600–1000 words, p.9). "Communication and innovation" is explicitly **"not a distinct section of the report"** (p.11) — it is a cross-cutting quality mark.

## The points-based mark notation (structure of the scheme, pp.3–4)

The scheme is "constructed in order to minimise its word content" and gives "only key phrases … the information and ideas that must appear in a candidate's answer in order to merit the assigned marks" (p.3). Marks are recorded with a compact grammar:

- **The mark appears in bold in the marks column**, e.g. `2` (p.3).
- **`3(2)`** means "there are 3 parts to the answer, each part is allocated 2 marks" (p.3). This `n(m)` form is the workhorse notation throughout (e.g. `3(2)`, `2(2)`, `4(1)`, `2(4)`, `3(4)`).
- **`4 + 2 + 2 + 2`** is the **front-loaded points list**: "the first correct answer encountered is awarded 4 marks and each subsequent correct answer is awarded 2 marks" (p.4). Compound forms combine both, e.g. `6 + 2(2)` (6 for the first point, then two further points at 2 each — seen at Q3(b), p.13) and `2(3) + 2(2)` (Q4, p.14).
- **Award unit marks separately** — "if an answer merits three 2-mark units, write 3 separate '2's … (2, 2, 2)" (p.3).
- **Subsection marks are not always tied to a specific part** — "there may be four parts to a question … and a total of 10 marks allocated" without a fixed per-part split (p.3).

### The four punctuation conventions (p.3)

- **`or`** — alternatives "where only one answer is required" are separated by `or`.
- **Solidus `/`** — words/expressions separated by `/` are "alternatives that are equally acceptable for a particular point"; **and** where *multiple* answers are required, each markable word/term/phrase is separated from the next by `/`. (One symbol, two jobs: alternatives within a point, and the boundary between successive markable points.)
- **Brackets `( )`** — a bracketed term "is not a requirement of the answer, but is used to contextualise the answer or may be an alternative valid answer".
- **Asterisk `*`** — "This happens when the only acceptable answer is a specific word or term. Each such instance is indicated in the scheme by an asterisk `*`." (p.3)

### Synonyms, context, and names vs. formulae (pp.3–4)

- **Synonyms are generally acceptable**: "alternative valid answers are acceptable … This does not preclude synonyms or terms or phrases which convey the same meaning" — **but** "there may be instances where the scheme demands an exact scientific term or unequivocal response and will not accept alternatives" (p.3).
- **The context rule**: "words, expressions or phrases must be correctly used in context and not contradicted and where there is evidence of incorrect use or contradiction, the marks may not be awarded" (p.3). Correct terminology embedded in a wrong or contradicted statement does not score.
- **Detail is scaled to the marks**: "The detail required in any answer is determined by the context, the phrasing of the question, and by the number of marks assigned … This may vary from year to year" (p.3).
- **Names ↔ symbols/formulae**: "names and symbols / formulae of elements / compounds are equally acceptable. However, in some cases where a name is specifically asked for, the symbol / formula may be accepted as an alternative" (p.4).
- **Italics** flag an instruction or qualification to the examiner about the answer (p.4).
- **Binding force**: "Examiners must conform to this scheme" (p.3); where a candidate gives a valid answer with no provision in the scheme, the examiner "must first consult with his / her advising examiner before awarding marks" (p.3).

## Penalty / cancelling regimes

### Cancelled (crossed-out) answers (p.4, quoting S.63O Instructions to Examiners 2023, §5.4, p.18)

- **Only answer cancelled:** "If the only answer offered is cancelled ignore the cancelling and mark as usual" — i.e. "treat the answer as if the candidate had not cancelled it."
- **Cancelled then replaced:** "If an answer is cancelled and a second version of the answer is given, you should accept the cancellation and award marks, where merited, for the un-cancelled version only."
- **Two un-cancelled versions:** "mark both and accept the answer that yields the greater number of marks. You may not, however, combine points from both versions to arrive at a manufactured total."

### Surplus answers (p.4)

- Rule: **"A surplus wrong answer cancels the marks awarded for a correct answer."**
- Worked example (p.4): scheme `A = Suffolk / B = Shorthorn / C = Belgian blue / D = Texel — 4(1)`. Candidate gives `A = Texel, Suffolk / B = Shorthorn / C = Belgian blue / D = Texel`. The surplus answer (Texel at A) is incorrect, so the candidate scores **4 − 1 = 3 marks**. Over-listing to hedge a match is penalised, not free.

### Examiner annotations (pp.5–6)

Scripts are marked **online**. Annotations are **red** for the examiner, **green** where an advising examiner also marked (these are visible on appeal). The palette (p.5): `√` correct response (also "use when all marks awarded to any additional correct answers"); `×` incorrect; `[` **surplus incorrect answer that has cancelled a correct answer**; a "seen" symbol on every blank page/part-page; a "part of significance" symbol; a zero-marks symbol; and digit symbols **1–12** to show the exact marks awarded for an item. Distinctive to Ag Science: **in the IIS, where a single item is worth more than 12 marks, two annotations are combined to show the total** (p.5), because the digit palette only runs to 12.

### Marking the IIS — procedure (pp.6–7)

- "Read the entire Individual Investigative Study (IIS) without allocating any marks. Mark the IIS using the marking criteria and total the marks" (p.6).
- "Each section of the IIS is awarded a single mark, which varies between sections" (p.6); "indicative content" is provided per section to guide that single mark.
- **Brevity / length caution:** "Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length" (p.7).
- Descriptors "should be interpreted in the context of the challenges and demands of the investigation the candidate has chosen" (p.7) — the rubric is applied relative to the candidate's own chosen brief, not an absolute template.
