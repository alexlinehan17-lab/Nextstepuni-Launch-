## Headline finding: the marking grammar is the SAME at OL as at HL

The Introduction, "How to use the marking scheme", the worked examples, the three penalty regimes, the annotation palette and the Irish-bonus table are **word-for-word the same framework** as the HL scheme. There is **no separate, lighter marking grammar for Ordinary Level.** The `n(m)` points notation, the synonym/context rules, the Section-A surplus penalty, and the Sections B&C asterisk-nullification rule all apply at OL exactly as at HL. What changes between OL and HL is the **difficulty and granularity of the questions**, not the rules for awarding marks.

## Marking-grammar STRUCTURE (Ordinary Level)

### Paper structure and mark allocation — IDENTICAL to HL

The paper totals **400 marks** (confirmed by the "400 @ 10%" Irish-bonus table, p.7). Every section is "best of":

| Section | Rule | Marks | Per question | Questions offered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Best 5 | 100 | 20 | **Q1–Q7 (7 offered)** |
| B | Best 2 | 60 | 30 | Q8–Q10 (3 offered) |
| C | Best 4 | 240 (`4(60)`) | 60 | **Q11–Q17 (7 offered)** |

("Section A Best 5 **100**", p.8; "Section B Best 2 **60**", p.14; "Section C Best 4 **4(60)**", p.17.) The mark split — 100 / 60 / 240, and 20 / 30 / 60 per question — is **the same as HL**. In Section C, Q16 and Q17 are each "Any two of (a), (b), (c), (d) **30, 30**" (pp.27, 31) — the same long-question shape as HL.

> **Correction to the existing HL doc.** `2023-insights.md` records Section A as "Best 5 (**of 6**)" and Section C as "Best 4 (**of 6**)". Cross-checking the HL 2023 extraction (`2023-marking-scheme.md`) shows the HL paper actually runs **Q1–Q7 in Section A and Q11–Q17 in Section C — i.e. 7 questions offered in each, not 6.** So the OL and HL papers offer the **same number of questions per section** (7 / 3 / 7, answer 5 / 2 / 4). The "(of 6)" figures in the HL doc appear to be an error and should be read as "(of 7)". (Not edited here — flagged for correction.)

### The points-based mark notation (p.3) — IDENTICAL to HL
- **`or`** separates alternatives where only one answer is required.
- **A solidus `/`** separates each markable word/term/phrase where multiple answers are required.
- **The mark appears in bold** next to the answer, e.g. `3`.
- **`5(4)`** = five parts, each worth **4 marks**.
- **`2(4) + 4(3)`** = the first two correct answers encountered earn **4 marks** each, each subsequent correct answer **3 marks**.
- **A term in brackets `( )`** is not required — it contextualises the answer or is an alternative valid answer.

### The context rule — APPLIES at OL (p.4)
Key words score **only in the correct context**. The OL worked example is habitat-study-flavoured (not the HL xylem example): for "Outline how you quantified a named animal in your habitat study", the answer "I captured hares using a pooter and counted them" is capped at **3(3)** because a pooter is not a valid capture method for hares — the correct-terminology-in-a-wrong-statement principle, identical to HL.

### The three penalty / cancelling regimes — ALL APPLY at OL
1. **Cancelled answers (p.4):** a single crossed-out answer is treated as if not cancelled ("What is pollination?" → cancelled single answer still scored **2(3)**). Cross-out-and-replace → only the un-cancelled version is marked. Two un-cancelled versions → the higher-scoring one is taken; "Points may not … be combined … to arrive at a manufactured total."
2. **Surplus answers — Section A only (pp.4–5):** "A surplus wrong answer cancels the marks awarded for a correct answer." OL worked example (p.5): *Carbon / hydrogen / oxygen / nitrogen* `4(3)`; "Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, calcium, aluminium" → the surplus wrong answer cancels one correct answer → **2(3)**. If the surplus wrong answer is itself cancelled, no penalty → **3(3)**.
3. **Asterisk nullification — Sections B and C (p.5):** "there may be instances where a correct answer is nullified by the addition of an incorrect answer … when the only acceptable answer is a specific word or term. Each such instance is indicated in the scheme by an asterisk `*`." The rule is stated identically to HL. **Note:** the 2025 OL scheme body contains **no actual `*` instances** (the only asterisk in the document is the one defining the rule on p.5) — so the convention exists at OL but was not invoked in the 2025 paper, whereas the HL 2023 scheme did use it (e.g. `*Glycerol`).

### Annotations (p.6) and Irish bonus (p.7) — IDENTICAL to HL
Online marking; examiner annotations in **red**, advising-examiner in **green**; a distinct symbol for "surplus incorrect answer (cancelled a correct answer)". Irish bonus: 10% of marks obtained for candidates scoring 300 (75%) or less, tapered above that; decimals always rounded **down**.

## OL-vs-HL differences (summary)

- **Same:** total (400), section split (100/60/240), per-question marks (20/30/60), number of questions offered per section (7/3/7, answer 5/2/4), points notation, synonym/context rule, Section-A surplus penalty, Sections B&C asterisk rule, annotation palette, Irish bonus.
- **Different:** only the **content and cognitive demand of the questions**. OL Section A is heavier on recognition/one-word recall ("Which one of the following…", complete-the-formula, name a vitamin — p.8), and OL question stems are more scaffolded than the HL equivalents. The marking *machinery* is unchanged.
- **In practice this year:** the asterisk-nullification rule, though defined, produced **zero `*` instances** in the 2025 OL scheme (vs at least one in HL 2023). Suggestive that "only-one-specific-term" nullification bites less often at OL, but this is a single-paper observation, not a rule.

## Candidate-behaviour findings (from worked examples; source + page)

These are illustrative of how OL candidates lose marks under the scheme (the scheme is not a Chief Examiner's Report, so behaviour commentary is limited to its worked examples):

1. **Right term, wrong context, still capped.** "I captured hares using a pooter" earns only **3(3)** — the capture method must actually suit the named animal; naming a technique that doesn't fit the organism forfeits that mark (p.4).
2. **Padding an answer with an extra wrong item costs you a correct one (Section A).** In "name the four elements in protein", adding "aluminium" to three correct elements drops the score from 3(3) to **2(3)** — over-answering is actively penalised in Section A (p.5).
3. **Crossing out and stopping doesn't erase the answer.** A single cancelled answer is marked as if not cancelled (the "pollination" example still scores **2(3)**) — candidates who scribble out a correct answer without replacing it are still credited (p.4).
4. **You cannot stitch a total from two attempts.** Where a candidate gives two un-cancelled versions, only the better-scoring version counts; "Points may not … be combined … to arrive at a manufactured total" (p.4).
5. **Section-C long questions reward breadth, not a fifth part.** Q16/Q17 are "Any two of (a)–(d) 30, 30" (pp.27, 31) — only the best two sub-parts score, so spreading effort thinly across all four sub-parts wastes time that the "best 2" rule will not reward.
