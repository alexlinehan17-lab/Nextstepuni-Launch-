## Paper structure and mark allocation

The paper totals **400 marks** (confirmed by the Irish-bonus table, which is the "400 @ 10%" table, p.7). Every section is answered on a **"best of"** basis — the candidate's strongest answers count:

| Section | Rule | Marks | Per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Best 5 (of 7 — Q1–Q7) | 100 | 20 |
| B | Best 2 (of 3 — Q8–Q10) | 60 | 30 |
| C | Best 4 (of 7 — Q11–Q17) | 240 (`4(60)`) | 60 |

(Section A "Best 5 **100**", p.8; Section B "Best 2 **60**", p.11; Section C "Best 4 **4(60)**", p.13. Question count corrected 2026-07-06 during 2025-HL cross-verification: Sections A and C each present **seven** questions, not six — Q1–Q7 and Q11–Q17 respectively. Confirmed in both the 2023 and 2025 HL schemes.)

**Irish bonus (p.7):** "Bonus marks at the rate of 10% of the marks obtained will be given to a candidate who answers entirely through Irish and who obtains 75% or less of the total mark available" (i.e. 300 marks or less). Above 300 a tapered table applies. "In calculating the bonus to be applied, decimals are always rounded down, not up."

## The points-based mark notation (structure of the scheme, p.3)

The scheme is deliberately terse ("constructed so as to minimise its word content", p.3). Marks are recorded with a compact grammar:

- **`or`** separates alternative answers where **only one answer is required**.
- **A solidus `/`** separates each word/term/phrase for which marks are allocated, where **multiple answers are required**.
- **The mark appears in bold next to the answer**, e.g. `3`.
- **`5(4)`** means "there are five parts to the answer, each part allocated **4 marks**."
- **`2(4) + 4(3)`** means "the first two correct answers encountered are awarded **4 marks** each and each subsequent correct answer is awarded **3 marks**." (This is the canonical points-list pattern: a longer accepted-answer list, marks front-loaded.)
- **A term in brackets `( )`** "is not a requirement of the answer, but is used to contextualise the answer or may be an alternative valid answer."
- Worked example of a points list (p.4): "Outline how water from the soil reaches the leaf" accepts *concentration gradient / osmosis / root hair / … / tension* — **"Any six 6(3)"** (any six of the listed terms, 3 marks each).

**Binding force (p.3):** "Examiners must conform to this scheme and may not allow marks for answering outside this scheme."

## Synonyms and the context rule (p.3–4)

- Synonyms are **generally acceptable**: the scheme "contains key words, terms and phrases … This does not preclude synonyms or terms or phrases which convey the same meaning" — **but** "there may be instances where the scheme demands an exact scientific term or unequivocal response and will not accept alternatives" (p.3).
- **Key terms only score in the correct context (p.4).** Worked example: for "Outline how water … reaches the leaf", the answer "Water is drawn up the xylem by osmosis" contains two key terms (xylem, osmosis) but "the statement is incorrect and the candidate can only be awarded **3 marks** for referring to the movement of water through the xylem." Correct terminology embedded in a wrong statement earns only the marks the *correct* portion supports.

## The three penalty / cancelling regimes

### 1. Cancelled (crossed-out) answers (p.4)
- **Cross out and leave it:** quoting the *S.63o Instructions to Examiners* (§5.4, p.19), "Where a candidate answers a question or part of a question once only and then cancels the answer, you should ignore the cancelling and treat the answer as if the candidate had not cancelled it." (Worked "pollination" example: cancelled single answer still scored `2(3)`, p.4.)
- **Cross out and replace:** "If an answer is cancelled and an alternative version given, the cancellation should be accepted and marks awarded … for the un-cancelled version only."
- **Two un-cancelled versions:** "both … should be marked and the answer accepted that yields the greater … number of marks. Points may not, however, be combined from multiple versions to arrive at a manufactured total."

### 2. Surplus answers — **Section A only** (p.4–5)
- Rule: "A surplus wrong answer cancels the marks awarded for a correct answer."
- Worked (p.4): "The walls of xylem vessels are reinforced with…" — scheme = *Lignin* **4 marks**. Answer "Chitin, lignin" → **4 − 4 = 0 marks**.
- Worked (p.5): "Name the four elements … in protein" (*Carbon / hydrogen / oxygen / nitrogen* `4(3)`). "Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, calcium, aluminium" → the surplus wrong answer "cancels one of the three correct answers, therefore the candidate is awarded `2(3)` marks." (But if the surplus is itself **cancelled**, no penalty — s/he may be awarded `3(3)`.)

### 3. Asterisk nullification — **Sections B and C** (p.5)
- "In the other sections of the paper (Sections B and C), there may be instances where a correct answer is nullified by the addition of an incorrect answer. This happens when the only acceptable answer is a specific word or term. Each such instance is indicated in the scheme by an **asterisk `*`**." (Seen in the scheme, e.g. `*Glycerol` at Q1(c), p.8.)

## Examiner annotations (p.6)

Scripts are marked on an online platform. Annotations are **red** for the examiner, **green** where an advising examiner has also marked. The palette distinguishes: correct response; one mark awarded; two marks awarded; incorrect response; **surplus incorrect answer (cancelled a correct answer)**; blank-page "seen"; a part of significance; and separate correct/incorrect symbols for **diagrams** and for **labels on diagrams** (i.e. diagram content and its labels are marked as distinct markable elements).
