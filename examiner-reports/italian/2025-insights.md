## Match / difference vs French & Spanish (headline)

**Italian matches French's two-axis system and is broader than Spanish's.** Like French, Italian marks **all** of Section C written production on a two-column grid: **Content & Communication** scored separately from **Language**, then summed (Appendix 2, p.[31]–[32]). Spanish reserved that grid for a single task (its Q5); Italian applies it across all three writing tasks (C1 composition, C2 guided composition, C3 formal writing), exactly as French does.

**Italian's distinctive twist is a graduated content-caps-language rule (p.[26]), a middle ground between French and Spanish:**
- **French** — the two axes are fully independent (accurate-but-empty French can still score Language).
- **Spanish** — zero Content forces zero Language (all-or-nothing coupling).
- **Italian** — weak Content *proportionally caps* Language: "In C1 and C2 where the marks awarded for content and communication are **7 or less**, language will be marked **out of 5**. In C3 where the marks awarded for content and communication are **12 or less**, language will be marked **out of 7**." So off-point work can't be rescued by good Italian, but it isn't zeroed either.

Other same/different notes are flagged inline below. In short: manipulation rules and the aural 50% penalty match French; the reading wrong-language penalty is **harsher** than French (50% vs French's −1); and Italian carries a **prescribed-novel literature strand** (Calvino, Sciascia, plus an unseen literary passage) with an essay graded on its own band appendix — absent from the French/Spanish 2025 schemes. Italian's writing descriptors also use "completion of the **communicative task**" and coherence/argumentation rather than the French/Spanish "native-speaker/monoglot" comprehensibility test.

## Paper structure & mark allocations (verified, p.[6])

The HL written+aural paper is marked out of **300** total (the Oral adds a further 100 elsewhere for the full 400; not in this document):

| Section | Marks | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Listening Comprehension Test** (Section A multiple choice; Section B dialogues 1–3) | 80 | p.[5], [7]–[10], [34]–[38] |
| **Reading Comprehension** — Section A (journalistic, 60) + Section B (literary/prescribed, 60) | 120 | p.[11]–[25] |
| **Writing** (Section C: C1 25 + C2 25 + C3 50) | 100 | p.[26]–[28], [31]–[32] |
| Oral (not in this document — separately examined) | 100 | — |

This 120 / 100 / 80 split (+100 Oral) **matches the French HL structure**; Spanish 2025 used a different split.

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — written production (Section C, the two-axis grid)

Section C is worth 100 marks across three tasks, each split into **Content & Communication** and **Language** columns (plus a Format sub-mark on the formal-writing task):

| Task | Total | Content & Communication | Language | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** — Composition linked to the journalistic passage | 25 | 15 | 10 | 3 points × 4m + Introduction (0/2m) + Conclusion (0/1m); intro & conclusion **required** (p.[26]) |
| **C2** — Guided composition | 25 | 15 | 10 | 12m for points (6 points scored 0,1,2) + 3m overall coherence (0,1,2,3) (p.[27]) |
| **C3** — Formal Writing (letter/email) | 50 | 25 | 15 | **Format 10m** on top (p.[27]) |

**The content-caps-language rule (p.[26]) — the single most important structural fact for the tool:** a weak Content & Communication score drags Language down (C1/C2: content ≤7 ⇒ Language out of 5; C3: content ≤12 ⇒ Language out of 7). This is Italian's version of Spanish's "Language depends on Content", but graduated rather than all-or-nothing, and unlike French where the axes are independent.

### Content & Communication descriptors — Appendix 2 (p.[31])

The first figure applies to the essay-type tasks (out of 15), the second to the letter (out of 25):

| Band | Descriptors | Essay | Letter |
|---|---|---|---|
| TOP | Good level of coherence · Clear argumentation · **Full completion of communicative task** · Good range of vocabulary · Appropriate idiomatic expressions · No irrelevant material | 13–15 | 21–25 |
| 2 | Reasonable coherence · Reasonably clear argumentation · Almost full completion of task · Reasonable vocabulary · Little irrelevant material | 11–12 | 18–20 |
| 3 | Adequate coherence · Argumentation not always clear · Elements of task not always fulfilled/developed · Lack of vocabulary interferes at times · A good deal of irrelevant material | 8–10 | 14–17 |
| 4 | Little coherence · Unclear argumentation · Communicative task barely fulfilled · Poor vocabulary · Mostly irrelevant material | 6–7 | 10–13 |
| BOTTOM | Lacks coherence · Very confused argumentation · Communicative task not fulfilled · All irrelevant material | 0–5 | 0–9 |

Note: Italian's operational test is **"completion of the communicative task"** + coherence/argumentation — it does **not** use the French "French monoglot" / Spanish "Spanish speaker" comprehensibility test. That native-speaker device is absent from the Italian scheme.

### Language descriptors — Appendix 2 (p.[32])

| Essay (out of 10) | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 7–10 | Idiomatic Italian, good vocabulary, good grammatical accuracy, few spelling mistakes |
| 4–6 | Adequate vocabulary, some incorrect verbs and agreements, many spelling mistakes |
| 0–3 | Very limited vocabulary, most verbs incorrect, many spelling mistakes |

| Letter (out of 15) | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 11–15 | Correct topic-related vocabulary, most verbs correct, few spelling mistakes |
| 6–10 | Adequate vocabulary, some incorrect verbs and agreements, some spelling mistakes |
| 0–5 | Inappropriate/irrelevant vocabulary, most verbs incorrect, many spelling mistakes |

**Verbs are the named Language failure point** (as in French and Spanish): "most verbs incorrect" is the bottom-band marker.

### Section C task-specific rules (teaching-critical)
- **C2 targets rote answers:** "Candidates must not produce something learnt off by heart and off the point" (p.[27]).
- **C3 formal writing has a Format mark (10m):** Date 1m · Address 2m (3m if candidate chooses email) · Greeting 2m · Closing formula 3m · Final salutation 2m. "Correct register, full and appropriate greeting and endings … spelt correctly are required for full marks." Full name (e.g. Alex Murphy) must be provided; own name not required. Each point "should be expanded and developed" (p.[27]).
- **Section C header rule (p.[26]):** "Quality NOT quantity. Simple correct good Italian. Variety of tenses and phrases, rich vocabulary, linking words and idiomatic expressions should be included."
- **Per-point credit grid (p.[28]):** points out of 2 = (0,1,2); out of 3 = (0,1,2,3); out of 4 = (0,1,2,3,4), with named quality tiers ("1m poor point, 2m good point, 3m very good/excellent point", etc.).

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — reading comprehension (Sections A & B, 120 marks)

### Manipulation / lifting rule (p.[11] journalistic, repeated verbatim p.[13] literary)
- "Evidence is needed that candidates have understood the text. **When manipulation is not necessary, material can be copied from the text.**" — direct lifting is allowed where no transformation is required (contrast Spanish, where copied phrases score zero in production; this is the *comprehension* rule, and matches French's lifting/manipulation split).
- "**When manipulation is required and not done candidates will be penalised and 1 mark will be deducted.**"
- "**When direct speech and narrator voice sentences, verbs and pronouns in the answers have to be altered and it is not done, candidates will be penalised, and 1 mark will be deducted.**" — the person/voice-shift manipulation, explicitly −1.
- "Candidates may be penalised, and 1 mark will be deducted for **excess and redundant material**." (matches French's excess penalty; Spanish had none.)
- "Where candidates provide the **same correct answer to two separate questions, full marks will be awarded only once**." (p.[11], [13]).

### Answer-language rules
- The scheme's answer keys give the standard comprehension answers **in Italian** (Italian read, answers written in Italian — e.g. p.[11]–[19] keys are Italian).
- **The opinion/discussion Q5 must be answered in English:** "In question Q5 responses must be written in English" (Section A, p.[11]); "In question B1 Q5, B2 A Q5 and B2 B Q5, responses must be written in English" (Section B, p.[13]). **This matches French**, where the equivalent opinion question (Q6) is answered in English — and differs from Spanish, whose opinion question is answered in Spanish.
- **Wrong-language penalty (harsher than French):** "If whole point / whole question answered in Italian [where English required]: mark according to the Marking Scheme, then **deduct 50% of marks gained**" (p.[11], [13]). French penalised wrong-language answers by only −1 per segment; Italian applies the full **50%** aural-style penalty to the English-required reading questions.

### The prescribed-literature strand (a genuine difference from French/Spanish 2025)
Section B (60 marks) is choice-based and includes a **literary/prescribed-text** component absent from the French/Spanish 2025 schemes:
- **B1 — Unseen literary passage** (2025: *Scherzetto*, Domenico Starnone), p.[13]–[15].
- **B2 — Comprehension on a prescribed novel** (2025 options: *Marcovaldo*, Italo Calvino / *A ciascuno il suo*, Leonardo Sciascia), p.[16]–[20].
- **B3 — Essay on the prescribed text**, graded on **Appendix 1** (p.[29]–[30]), scored in bands from 6–60. Rules (p.[21]): "Reference to the text is important · Knowledge of the text to be rewarded · **Quality and NOT quantity is important** · Three relevant points, well-argued and supported from the text, are sufficient · **Title and characters' names translated into English will be penalised**." The essay may be written in English **or** Italian (p.[29]). Intro present = 1m/3m; conclusion present = 1m/3m (p.[30]).

## THE MARKING GRAMMAR — Listening / Aural (80 marks, p.[5], [7]–[10])

- **Answers must be written in English** (Section B, p.[5]).
- **Wrong-language penalty:** "If whole test / whole section answered in Italian: mark according to the Marking Scheme, then **deduct 50% of marks gained**" (p.[5]). **Matches French and Spanish** (answer the aural in English or lose half).
- **Multiple-choice rules (p.[5]):** two answers circled and not cancelled = no marks; two circled with one cancelled = accept the non-cancelled answer; one circled and cancelled = accept it.
- **Vocabulary-list rule (p.[5]):** "Where a list of vocabulary is required, **one incorrect answer nullifies a correct answer**" — hedging with extra guesses actively costs marks. "Where candidates provide inaccurate combination of points … full marks will not be awarded."
- Items use printed sub-mark splits (e.g. 5+4, 3+3, 2+2) and graded near-miss fallbacks (e.g. p.[8]–[10]). Full aural transcript printed p.[34]–[38].

## General examiner instructions & annotations (p.[4])

The reading conventions / annotation palette:
- **Forward slash `/`** = synonym or alternative answer (answers separated by `/` are **not** counted as different answers).
- **Rounded brackets `( )`** = material not essential for full marks.
- **Square brackets `[ ]`** = answers marked and discounted as the lowest-scoring excess responses.
- **Underlining** = key elements; **asterisk `*`** = elements required.
- **Linking arrows `↕`** = linking points so maximum value can be awarded.
- **Marking scales** (p.[4]): responses graded on Scale A (3 categories: incorrect / partial / correct) or Scale B/C (4 categories). 5-mark scales: **A = 0,3,5**; **B = 0,1,3,5**; **C = 0,3,5,7**.

### Modified Marking Scheme (Appendix 3, p.[33])
Applied to candidates granted a recording device, word processor with spell/grammar check, voice-activated word processor, scribe, or a spelling-and-grammar waiver:
- **Sections A & B:** standard scheme, but **marks will not be deducted for lack of manipulation**.
- **Section C:** Content & Communication marked as standard, but **spelling and certain elements of grammar (verb endings, agreement of adjectives) will not be penalised**. (Matches the French/Spanish MMS treatment.)

## Quotable lines (with page refs)

1. **The two-axis principle across all writing (Appendix 2, p.[31]–[32]):** every Section C task is scored `Content & Communication` and `Language` separately, then summed — the same principle as French, applied to all three tasks (unlike Spanish's single-task grid).
2. **Content caps Language — Italian's distinctive graduated rule (p.[26]):**
   > "In C1 and C2 where the marks awarded for content and communication are 7 or less, language will be marked out of 5. In C3 where the marks awarded for content and communication are 12 or less, language will be marked out of 7."
3. **Lifting vs manipulation (p.[11], [13]):**
   > "When manipulation is not necessary, material can be copied from the text. When manipulation is required and not done candidates will be penalised and 1 mark will be deducted."
4. **Direct-speech → narrator voice manipulation (p.[11], [13]):**
   > "When direct speech and narrator voice sentences, verbs and pronouns in the answers have to be altered and it is not done, candidates will be penalised, and 1 mark will be deducted."
5. **Wrong-language penalty on English-required reading questions — harsher than French (p.[11], [13]):**
   > "If whole point / whole question answered in Italian: mark according to the Marking Scheme, then deduct 50% of marks gained."
6. **Rote answers explicitly targeted (p.[27]):**
   > "Candidates must not produce something learnt off by heart and off the point."
7. **Literary essay — quality over quantity, no translated names (p.[21]):**
   > "Quality and NOT quantity is important. Three relevant points, well-argued and supported from the text, are sufficient." … "Title and characters' names translated into English will be penalised."
8. **Aural / list hedging backfires (p.[5]):**
   > "Where a list of vocabulary is required, one incorrect answer nullifies a correct answer."
