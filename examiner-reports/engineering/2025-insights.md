## Paper structure and mark allocation (written examination)

- The written paper totals **300 marks** (PDF p.6 header: "Higher Level – 300 marks"). It is one of two assessment components; the practical examination is marked separately (its own grid, p.33).
- **Rubric:** "**Answer any six questions.**" (PDF p.6). The paper prints **nine** questions (Question 1–Question 9), **each worth 50 marks**; 6 × 50 = 300. The scheme does not itself flag any question as compulsory — it lists the mark ladder for all nine and leaves the "answer any six" rubric to govern choice. (In practice Question 1 is the short-answer question and is where most candidates start; the scheme treats it as one of the six.)
- **Question 1 is the short-answer question:** "**Any ten @ 5 marks each**" (PDF p.6). It prints **thirteen** parts (a)–(m); a candidate answers any ten, and only the best ten count toward the 50 marks. Each part is internally worth 5 marks, split either as a single `5` granule or as `3 + 2` (main point 3, development 2). E.g. Q1(a) `3 + 2`, (b) `5`, (c) `3 + 2`, (e) `Any one @ 5`, (g) `5` (PDF pp.7–9).
- **Questions 2–9 are the long "answer all parts" questions:** each is 50 marks broken into lettered/roman sub-parts with individually printed mark values (PDF p.6 gives the full ladder for every question). Marks inside a part are recorded as bracketed/added values next to each markable element, summing to the part total.

## The mark-split notation (structure of the scheme)

Unlike the terse keyword-and-solidus scheme used in the science subjects, the Engineering scheme presents **full model-answer prose** and marks it with an **additive split notation**. The load-bearing patterns (all verified in the question bodies, PDF pp.7–32):

- **`3 + 2`** — a 5-mark point split into a **main statement (3)** + a **development/second element (2)**. The dominant pattern for "explain / give reasons / state the function of" items (e.g. Q1(a), Q1(c), Q1(f), Q1(h), Q1(j); Q3(a) sub-parts). Getting the headline point banks 3; the elaboration banks the remaining 2.
- **`5`** — a single undivided granule for a compact defined item (e.g. Q1(b) function of a heatsink, Q1(g) absolute vs incremental dimensioning, Q1(k) operation of an electromagnet).
- **`4 + 4`, `8 + 8`, `6 + 6`, `2 + 2`, `2 + 2 + 2`** — a compound part split into equally-weighted named components, each scoring separately (e.g. Q5(a)(ii) three crystal structures `2 + 2 + 2`, PDF p.16; Q4(a)(i) `4 + 4`, PDF p.14).
- **`8 + 1 + 1`** — a diagram worth 8 marks **plus two 1-mark labels credited separately** (Q5(b)(i) "Draw the thermal equilibrium diagram **and label**", PDF pp.16–17; the two `+1`s are the `solidus line` and `liquidus lines` labels). Labelling is an explicit, separately-scored granule — an unlabelled diagram forfeits those marks.

### The "Any N @ …" menu cap — the single most important structural rule

Many parts offer a **menu** and credit only the best N answers at stated per-item values:

- **`Any one @ 5`** — Q1(e): three named scientists printed; answer **one**, 5 marks (PDF p.8).
- **`Any two @ 8 + 8`** — Q3(iii)-style comparison; best **two** points at 8 each = 16 (PDF p.16).
- **`Any three @ 6 + 6 + 6`** — the standard part-(b) of most long questions; best **three** points at 6 each = 18 (e.g. Q6(b), Q7(b), Q8(b); PDF pp.20, 22, 30).

The cap is a two-way rule: **writing fewer than N loses the unfilled granules**, and **writing more than N earns nothing extra** — only the best N are read. This is the Engineering analogue of Business's "X required" cap.

### The part-(c) "OR" fork

The final part of several long questions offers a **choice of route**, printed as an `OR` (PDF p.6 grid; e.g. Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9):
- **`(c) 16`** — a single 16-mark extended comparison/description, **OR**
- **`(c) (i) 4 + 4  (ii) 8`** — a split alternative summing to 16.
A candidate commits to one branch; the two branches are mutually exclusive and sum to the same 16 marks.

## How calculation questions are marked

Numerical parts are comparatively few (this is a materials/technology paper, not a mechanics one), and are marked as a **single combined granule** covering formula → substitution → answer, with the **unit shown in the accepted answer**:

- **Q3(b)(iii) Ultimate tensile strength (PDF p.13):** `UTS = Max Load / C.S.A = 181 kN / 113.1 mm² = 1.6 kN/mm²`, worth **4 marks**. The model prints the formula, the substituted values, and the final answer **with its unit** — the whole chain sits under one 4-mark value rather than a step-ladder of separate granules.
- **Q5(b)(ii) ratio of phases (PDF p.17):** `A = 34, B = 60, C = 100; |AB|/|BC| = 26/40`, worth **4 marks** — reading values off the equilibrium diagram and forming the lever-rule ratio.
- **Graph-plotting marks are granular:** Q3(b)(i) "Load-extension graph" = **10 marks** for the plotted curve, and Q3(b)(ii) "On graph" = **`2 + 2 + 2`** for marking three specific features on it (elastic limit, start of necking, fracture point are the labelled features in the model, PDF p.13). So the graph itself and each annotated feature are separately credited.

Unlike Physics, the scheme states **no global unit-deduction, arithmetical-slip or scale-penalty rule** in its general instructions — the general instructions (p.4) are only the four listed below. Precision/units are policed inside each part's model answer, not by a blanket deduction.

## Sketch / diagram and labelling rules

- **Diagrams are marked as their own granule**, separate from the accompanying text. Where a part says "draw … and label", labels carry explicit `+1` (or higher) sub-marks — see `8 + 1 + 1` (Q5(b)(i), PDF pp.16–17).
- Most long-question sub-parts pair a **model sketch with prose**; the extracted scheme shows heavily annotated reference diagrams (heat sink p.7; tensile-test apparatus p.12; induction-hardening coil p.14; optical pyrometer p.15; rotational-moulding cycle p.23; resistance welding electrodes pp.20–21). The scheme's diagrams are "examples only" (general instruction 1) — a valid alternative sketch that conveys the same labelled features is accepted.

## General examiner instructions (PDF p.4)

The written scheme carries only four numbered general instructions (the science-subject solidus/deduction apparatus is absent):

1. **Solutions are examples only.** "The solutions presented are examples only. **All other valid solutions are acceptable** and are marked accordingly." (Reinforced by the standing note repeated at the head of the scheme on p.7.)
2. **Detail scales with marks and context.** "The detail required in any answer is determined by the context and the manner in which the question is asked, and by the number of marks assigned to the answer … may vary from year to year."
3. **Irish-language bonus.** "Bonus marks at the rate of **5%** of the marks obtained will be given to a candidate who answers **entirely through Irish** and who obtains **less than 75%** of the total marks. … decimals are always **rounded down, not up** e.g., 4.5 becomes 4; 4.9 becomes 4." A tapered table ("Tábla 300 @ 5%", PDF p.5) applies above 75%: the bonus slides from 11 marks (base mark 226) down to 0 (base marks 294–300).
4. **Annotation table.** Instruction 4 (p.4) points to a table of the online-marking annotations used throughout; the table itself is an embedded image and does not extract to text.

## Practical Examination grid (PDF p.33) — brief note

The document also contains the **Day 2 Practical Examination** marking scheme, a **concept-mark grid**: the test-piece is broken into Sections 1–6, each worth a block of marks awarded against named machining features ("Marking Out 5", "10 mm × 3 mm Slots 5", "Drill and Tap M5 … 5", etc.), plus a Section 1 quality block scored on **Assembly (5) / Function (10) / Finish (5) = 20** (PDF p.33). This is a discrete feature-by-feature checklist, structurally different from the written scheme's prose-model marking, and is marked out of 100 for the practical component.

## Documented candidate-behaviour hooks (for authored "how marking works" scripts)

Each is grounded in a specific scheme feature (structural, not a model answer):

1. **Answer exactly the number asked in an "Any N @…" part — no more, no fewer.** Q1 is "Any ten @ 5" of thirteen parts; part-(b) of the long questions is "Any three @ 6 + 6 + 6" (PDF pp.6, 20). Writing a fourth point earns nothing; the examiner reads only the best N. Over-answering wastes time; under-answering leaves granules on the table.
2. **On a `3 + 2` point, the headline is worth more than the elaboration.** Every 5-mark "explain" item splits 3 (main statement) + 2 (development) (PDF pp.7–9). Naming the point clearly first banks the 3; a well-developed second sentence banks the 2. A vague opener that never states the point plainly can miss the 3 even if the follow-on is good.
3. **Labels on a diagram are their own marks.** "Draw … and label" prints `8 + 1 + 1`, with the labels separately credited (Q5(b)(i), PDF pp.16–17). An unlabelled or mislabelled diagram forfeits those `+1`s no matter how good the drawing.
4. **In a calculation, show formula → substitution → answer with the unit.** The UTS model prints `Max Load / C.S.A = 181 kN / 113.1 mm² = 1.6 kN/mm²` (Q3(b)(iii), PDF p.13) — the accepted answer carries its unit. The full chain sits under one mark value, so a bare number risks the whole granule.
5. **"All other valid solutions are acceptable" — the model answer is not a required wording.** General instruction 1 (PDF p.4) and the standing note (p.7): the printed solutions "are examples only." A correct alternative that conveys the same technical content scores full marks; candidates should not fear that different phrasing from a revision book will be penalised.
