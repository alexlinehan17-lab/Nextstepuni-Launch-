## The headline finding — OL and HL share ONE marking grammar; the paper differentiates, not the scheme

This is the single most important OL-vs-HL fact and it is verifiable across both extraction files:
**the Visual Studies written-paper descriptors and their mark bands are word-for-word identical at
Ordinary and Higher Level.** Differentiation between the two levels is achieved almost entirely
through the **question paper** (the stimuli chosen and the cognitive demand of each question), which
the scheme states explicitly:

> "These descriptors should be interpreted in the context of the challenges and demands of the
> question the candidate has chosen." (OL PDF p.16 for Section A; repeated OL PDF p.21 for
> Sections B & C — identical sentence appears in the HL scheme)

Consequences for teaching OL specifically:

1. **Section A "short-question" descriptor** (OL PDF p.17): the OL Low/Moderate/High descriptor text
   and the 4/5/6-mark band boundaries are **identical to HL** (Low 0–1 / 0–1 / 0–2; Moderate 2–3 /
   2–3 / 3–4; High 4 / 4–5 / 5–6). Same "Limited → Good → Thorough" quality ladder, same seven bullet
   criteria (understanding the question; application to new/unfamiliar situations; problem-solving in
   Visual Studies; analysis/interpretation/evaluation of the stimulus; evidence-based arguments;
   coherence and focus; visual-art terminology and/or sketches).
2. **Sections B & C four band-descriptor strands** (OL PDF pp.22–25): the four strands, their marks,
   and their band boundaries are **identical to HL**:

   | Strand | Marks | Low | Moderate | High | OL PDF p. |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Coherence and Focus | 10 | 0–3 | 4–6 | 7–10 | p.22 |
   | Subject Knowledge | 20 | 0–7 | 8–13 | 14–20 | p.23 |
   | Relevant Examples | 10 | 0–3 | 4–6 | 7–10 | p.24 |
   | Visual Language (terminology and/or sketches) | 10 | 0–3 | 4–6 | 7–10 | p.25 |

   The descriptor wording within each strand matches HL (limited/good/thorough recall + critical
   thinking for Subject Knowledge; relevant/recognised artists for Relevant Examples; "**If sketches
   are used**" conditional for Visual Language). There is **no separate, gentler OL band table** —
   an OL candidate is measured on the same Low/Moderate/High ceilings as an HL candidate, against an
   easier question.
3. **Practical / Invigilated grade descriptors** (OL PDF pp.12–13, "GRADE DESCRIPTOR – PRACTICAL
   COMPONENTS 2024, Ordinary Level", O1–O8) are **verbatim the same excellent → little-or-no ladder**
   as the Higher-Level H1–H8 descriptors (OL PDF pp.10–11). The differentiation for the *practical*
   components is done numerically, not in words (see next section).

## OL-specific marking machinery that DOES differ from HL — the reference-scale conversion

The practical components (Coursework 250 + Invigilated Practical 100 = 350) are set on a **common
brief** — HL and OL candidates do the same tasks — and marked once on a **reference scale designed to
coincide with the Higher-level scale**. OL marks are then produced by a conversion the HL scheme
does not carry (OL PDF pp.3–5):

- **Grade alignment stated explicitly:** "H5=O1; H6=O2; H7=O3" (OL PDF p.3). An OL O1 aligns to a
  Higher H5.
- **Practical Coursework conversion (OL PDF p.4):** reference ≥150 → final **250**; reference 75–149
  → **add 100**; reference 1–74 → **×2 and add 25**; reference 0 → 0.
- **Practical Examination conversion (OL PDF p.5):** reference ≥60 → final **100**; reference 30–59 →
  **add 40**; reference 1–29 → **×2 and add 10**; reference 0 → 0.

So an OL candidate's practical work is upgraded off the shared reference scale — the generosity is in
the *conversion arithmetic*, invisible on the descriptor page. **The Visual Studies written paper
(150) has NO such conversion** — it is marked directly on the shared descriptors; its differentiation
is the OL question paper alone.

## Section A given-headings and mark allocations at OL (PDF pp.17–20)

Section A "Today's world" is the same shape as HL — **answer any five of Q1–Q7, each 10 marks, split
into part (a) + part (b)** worth 4/5/6 each — but the *content* is pitched at OL. The OL 2024 given
headings and splits (all page refs OL PDF):

| Q | (a) task & given headings | (a) marks | (b) task | (b) marks | p. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Description of the **photograph** — headings: **colour, pattern** | 6 | "Do you think this photograph was set up or was it a snapshot? Reasons" | 4 | p.18 |
| 2 | "List and explain two different ways that visual artists earn a living" | 6 | How a named organisation could support a professional visual artist | 4 | p.18 |
| 3 | Description of **ring** — headings: **shape, colour, materials** | 6 | Target market for this ring; reasons using evidence from the illustration | 4 | p.18 |
| 4 | Description of **artwork** — headings: **materials, colour, location** | 5 | "What do you think this artwork is about? Reasons" | 5 | p.19 |
| 5 | Description of **design** — headings: **lettering, imagery, layout** | 6 | Messages the packaging communicates about the video game; reasons | 4 | p.19 |
| 6 | Description of the **costume** of a film/TV/theatre character | 6 | What a designer should consider when designing a costume; reasons | 4 | p.19 |
| 7 | **Analysis** of structure — headings: **form, scale, pattern** | 6 | Why the architects chose steel; reasons | 4 | p.20 |

**How this contrasts with the HL Section A (2024)** (HL PDF pp.18–20 in the HL extraction):

- **Stimulus register:** HL leans fine-art — *painting* (Q1), *print* and "sense of depth" (Q2), a
  *contemporary living sculptor* (Q3), curatorship and exhibitions (Q5), *poster design* (Q7). OL
  leans everyday visual culture / design-and-craft — *photograph*, *ring* (jewellery), *video-game
  packaging*, *costume*, *architecture in steel*. More concrete, observable objects at OL.
- **Given headings are more technical at HL:** HL Q1(a) = "composition, perspective, colour"; HL Q6
  = "composition, motif, colour"; HL Q7 = "layout, light, contrast". OL substitutes more directly
  observable headings — "colour, pattern"; "shape, colour, materials"; "lettering, imagery, layout"
  — and OL Q1 gives only **two** headings vs HL's three.
- **Part-(b) cognitive verb is scaled down:** HL part (b) uses "interpretation", "discussion",
  "opinion" and repeatedly "**Justification** for the answer given". OL part (b) mostly asks for
  simple "**Reasons for answer given**" and often frames a **binary/closed prompt first** ("set up
  or a snapshot?", "who is the target market?") before asking for reasons — lower inference load.
- **Mark-split pattern is identical across levels:** part (a) is the higher-tariff description
  (usually 6), part (b) the lower-tariff justification (usually 4), and **Q4 is 5/5 at both levels**.

## General examiner instructions — same as HL (all present verbatim in the OL scheme)

These carry across unchanged and are worth teaching OL students identically:

- **Descriptor marking, not itemised points:** "This marking scheme uses descriptor marking to
  allocate marks. The mark to be allocated is dependent on the mark available for that particular
  part of the question." (OL PDF p.16) There is **no "5 for a name" itemisation** anywhere.
- **Skilful brevity protected; length not rewarded:** "Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity,
  nor to reward unwarranted length." (OL PDF pp.16, 21)
- **Open-example marking:** "Candidates may choose from a wide range of relevant examples and content
  to answer a question. Relevant content must be credited." (OL PDF p.21)
- **Best-answer rule for over-answering:** "If candidates answer more than one question etc. per
  section then the question with the highest score is used to award marks." (OL PDF p.21)
- **Sketches folded into the relevant part, not ring-fenced:** "Where sketches are included to answer
  any question, they should be reviewed as part of the material on which the mark is based using the
  mark allocation for the part of the question that the sketch is answering or supporting."
  (OL PDF p.16) In Sections B & C sketches are one of two routes to the 10-mark Visual Language
  strand, gated by "If sketches are used" (OL PDF p.25) — terminology alone can max it.
- **Examiner self-educates on unfamiliar work; correct terminology in context:** OL PDF p.16 (same as
  HL). Sparse two-symbol annotation set (blank Tick / vertical wavy) — OL PDF p.14. The Irish Bonus
  (10% under 75%, scaled table over 75%, decimals rounded down) — OL PDF pp.14–15.

## Candidate-behaviour findings specific to OL (source + page)

1. **The four Section B/C strands are independent ceilings, same as HL.** An OL essay that is
   knowledgeable but drifts off the question loses the 10-mark **Coherence and Focus** strand; one
   that recalls facts without analysis is capped inside **Subject Knowledge** (only "critical thinking
   to analyse and evaluate" lifts a response above Low 0–7); one that cites no recognised artists
   forfeits **Relevant Examples**. A lopsided OL answer is structurally capped exactly as at HL.
   (OL PDF pp.22–24)
2. **Naming recognised artists/artefacts is a discrete OL earner.** The Relevant Examples strand
   rewards "a … choice of artists, and examples of artworks/artefacts that are … relevant/recognised
   … to justify the response" — an OL candidate who describes an artwork but attaches no named,
   recognised example simply cannot reach the strand's Moderate/High band. (OL PDF p.24)
3. **In Section A part (a), covering ALL the given headings is the mark route.** Every OL part (a) is
   scaffolded with named headings (e.g. Q3 "shape, colour, materials"; Q5 "lettering, imagery,
   layout"); the descriptor's "High" band requires "thorough … understanding" across them, so an OL
   answer that addresses only one heading self-limits to Low/Moderate. (OL PDF pp.17–20)
4. **Part (b) requires a reason, not just an opinion.** Six of the seven OL part-(b) prompts end in
   "Reasons for answer given" / "Reasons for your answer"; the descriptor rewards "arguments …
   based on evidence" and penalises answers "based on little or no evidence." An unjustified OL
   opinion caps at the Low band. (OL PDF pp.17–20)
5. **Sketching is optional at OL, not a guaranteed pot of marks.** Every Visual Language band opens
   "If sketches are used", and sketches elsewhere are only credited "using the mark allocation for
   the part … the sketch is … supporting." An OL candidate can score full marks on terminology alone
   — but a prose-only candidate loses nothing by omitting sketches, unlike the old History &
   Appreciation paper where a sketch part was ring-fenced. (OL PDF pp.16, 25)

## Quotable lines (page refs, OL PDF)

1. **Differentiation is by the question, not a different OL scheme (OL PDF p.16 / repeated p.21):**
   > "These descriptors should be interpreted in the context of the challenges and demands of the
   > question the candidate has chosen."
2. **The method is descriptor marking (OL PDF p.16):**
   > "This marking scheme uses descriptor marking to allocate marks. The mark to be allocated is
   > dependent on the mark available for that particular part of the question."
3. **HL/OL grade alignment for the practical components (OL PDF p.3):**
   > "In order to ensure the correct alignment between the standard required to achieve grades at the
   > two levels (H5=O1; H6=O2; H7=O3) the work is all marked initially on a reference scale."
4. **Subject Knowledge is lifted by analysis, not recall — OL wording (OL PDF p.23, High band):**
   > "A thorough level of critical thinking to analyse and evaluate their knowledge of the section
   > of focus."
5. **Sketches are conditional, terminology is the alternative (OL PDF p.25, Visual Language strand):**
   > "If sketches are used: the sketches demonstrate a … ability to support the answer and
   > communicate ideas."
6. **Section A part (a) is anchored to the given headings — OL Q3 (OL PDF p.18):**
   > "Description of ring using the given headings: shape, colour, materials."
