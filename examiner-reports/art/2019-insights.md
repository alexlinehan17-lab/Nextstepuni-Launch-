# Art 2019 — Examiner Insights (Marking Scheme, Higher Level)

## Source

- **Report type:** Marking Scheme (SEC, finalised published scheme)
- **Year:** 2019
- **Level:** Higher (common scheme; the Higher/Ordinary differential is handled by a
  reference-mark conversion for coursework, not by different question schemes)
- **Original filename:** SEC `Leaving Certificate 2019 Marking Scheme — Art (Higher Level)`
  (retrieved 2026-07-06 via the educateplus.ie mirror of the SEC PDF; title page carries the
  Coimisiún na Scrúduithe Stáit / State Examinations Commission imprint)
- **Renamed to:** `2019-marking-scheme.pdf` (32 pages; page refs below are PDF page indices+1,
  matching the printed footer numbers)
- **Spec note (important):** This is the **OLD Leaving Certificate Art syllabus** (pre-2022
  revision). Its written component is the **History and Appreciation of Art** paper (150 marks),
  which is exactly the paper this task targets. The **revised spec** (first examined 2023/2024)
  replaces it with a **"Visual Studies"** written exam (150 marks / 30%, marked differently — see
  the SEC 2024 Art scheme). The 2019 scheme is the correctly-named *History & Appreciation of Art*
  written-paper document; it was chosen over the 2024 new-spec scheme because the target paper
  name only exists on the old spec.
- **Note on context:** This is the finalised per-year marking scheme, not a Chief Examiner's
  Report. Its value for a "how SEC marking works" tool is that it documents the **point-allocation
  ("mark-per-part") marking grammar** the History & Appreciation paper uses — which is a
  fundamentally different family from History's CM/OE bands or Geography's SRPs. There is **no
  descriptor band and no SRP tariff** in this section: each sub-part carries a fixed mark ceiling,
  and the "Notes" column splits that ceiling into a **naming allocation + a description/discussion
  allocation + (per question) a sketch allocation**.

## Paper shape — three components, one written paper (pp.2, 18)

The whole subject is 500 marks across three components (p.2 — text is on the 2024 scheme; the
2019 mark weightings for the written paper are visible on the History & Appreciation cover, p.18):

- **Coursework** (project) — 200 marks in 2019 (pp.4–13).
- **Life Sketching** (invigilated life drawing) — 50 marks (pp.15–17).
- **History and Appreciation of Art** (the written paper) — **150 marks** (pp.18–29).

The written paper is answered as **THREE questions of 50 marks each**, one from each of three
sections (pp.19–29):

- **Section I – Art in Ireland** (Q.1–Q.7, pp.19–22)
- **Section II – European Art (1000 – Present)** (Q.8–Q.15, pp.23–26)
- **Section III – Appreciation of Art** (Q.16–Q.20, pp.27–29)

So the written paper total (150) = 3 × 50. Every question is worth exactly 50 and is
internally decomposed the same way.

## The marking grammar — mark-per-part, split by the "Notes" column

Unlike History (CM/OE bands) or Geography (2-mark SRPs), the History & Appreciation section is a
**pure point-allocation scheme**: each question is broken into lettered parts (A, B, C, D, and
sometimes E), each part has a **fixed mark ceiling**, and the "Notes" column tells the examiner
**how to sub-divide that ceiling**. There are three recurring building blocks:

### 1. Naming is a small, separately-itemised award
Almost every "name X" instruction is worth a **fixed 5 marks** (or 3 marks when it is the "name"
slice of a combined part). Representative:

- "Name one building by the architect you have selected. **5**" (Q.4A, p.20).
- "Name of chosen painting. **5**" (Q.10A, p.24); "Name of chosen work. **5**" (Q.14A, p.26).
- Where a part combines naming + discussion, the Notes split it: "**5 for name, 10 for
  description and discussion**" (recurring — Q.2C, Q.3D, Q.6D, Q.7B/C, Q.8C, Q.9D, Q.10D,
  Q.11C, Q.12D, Q.13D, Q.14C; pp.19–26). Q.15A even triples the split: "**5 for named work, 5 for
  named artist, 10 for description and discussion**" (p.26).

**Behaviour implication:** naming the wrong/anonymous work forfeits only the small naming slice,
but the far larger "description and discussion" slice is where the marks concentrate — a named
work with no development scores 5 out of 15.

### 2. "Description and discussion" is the mark reservoir, steered by *named lenses*
The bulk of each 50-mark question sits in parts that ask for **description and discussion against
explicitly named analytical lenses**. The scheme never awards "discussion" in the abstract — the
question and Notes name the aspects the discussion must cover, e.g.:

- "refer to **form, function and construction techniques**" (Q.1A/B, p.19).
- "with reference to **subject matter and composition**" … "with reference to **style and colour**"
  (Q.5A/B, p.21) — the two 15-mark parts split one artwork across two named lens-pairs.
- "with reference to the **artist, subject matter, style, composition, materials, techniques and
  the period**" for a single 20-mark deep-dive part (Q.14B, p.26).
- Q.1A even itemises inside the part: "name, description and discussion … **15 — 3 for name, 12 for
  description and discussion**" (p.19).

So a candidate is rewarded for **covering every named lens** in the prompt; each lens is a mark
opportunity, and omitting one caps the achievable mark for that part.

### 3. Sketches are a fixed, itemised award in every question (usually 5–10 marks)
**Every** History & Appreciation question carries a dedicated **Sketches** part (the last
lettered part), worth **5 or 10 marks**, and the Notes always specify how the sketch marks split
between the required subjects:

- "Sketches. **10** — 5 for monument one, 5 for monument two." (Q.1D, p.19)
- "Sketches. **5** — 2 for given illustration, 3 for other artefact." (Q.2D, p.19; the "2 for the
  given illustration, 3 for the candidate's own example" split recurs across Q.3E, Q.5D, Q.9E,
  Q.11D, Q.12E, Q.13E, Q.17D, Q.19D, Q.20D — pp.19–29).
- "Sketches relating to part B and part C. **10** — 5/5" (Q.4D, p.20; Q.16D "**7/3**", p.27).

**Behaviour implication:** sketching is not optional colour — it is a ring-fenced ~10–20% of every
question's marks, and candidates who write only prose surrender it. Note the recurring split that
gives **more marks to the candidate's OWN chosen example than to the reproduced given
illustration** (3 vs 2), rewarding independent visual recall.

## Section III is different in emphasis — it rewards the candidate's *own design proposal*

Section III (Appreciation of Art, Q.16–Q.20) keeps the same 50-mark / lettered-part grammar but
its "C" part consistently asks for the candidate's **own visual concept / design proposal with
reasons**, worth a full 15 marks (a quarter of the question):

- "Briefly outline your own **visual concepts** for a public artwork … **give reasons for your
  design decisions**. 15" (Q.17C, p.27); near-identical wording at Q.18C, Q.19C, Q.20C (pp.28–29).
- "Briefly outline your own ideas for how art galleries … can better interact with the public …
  **5 for idea one, 5 for idea two**." (Q.16C, p.27) — again the Notes count *discrete ideas* as
  the mark units.

So in Section III the marks reward a **candidate-generated proposal justified with design
reasoning**, not just analysis of the given stimulus.

## Caps and zero-type rules — where they live

This paper's hard caps sit almost entirely in the **Life Sketching** section (a drawing task),
not the written History & Appreciation section. They are worth knowing as the scheme's model of
"non-compliance penalties" (pp.16–17):

- "**No face / blank face / incorrect pose = 5 marks maximum**" (Long Pose parts, p.17; a 4-mark
  cap on the shorter pose, p.16).
- "**Full back pose = 3 marks maximum**"; "**Page cropped / added to = 3 marks maximum**";
  "Half figure / no feet = 3 marks maximum" (pp.16–17).

Within History & Appreciation itself the "cap" mechanism is softer and structural rather than
stated: because each part has a fixed ceiling split by the Notes, **failing to address a named
lens, or answering only one of a two-work requirement, simply forfeits that slice** — there is no
descriptor band that could pull a whole answer down, and no bonus for exceeding the named lenses.

## General examiner conventions (p.1, standard SEC front matter)

The standard SEC note applies (p.1): the scheme is **"not intended to be standalone documents …
an essential resource for examiners who receive training in the correct interpretation and
application of the scheme"**, with marking monitored by Advising Examiners and overseen by the
Chief Examiner. I.e. the printed mark splits are the skeleton; trained examiners apply
professional judgement within each part's ceiling — there is no published descriptor rubric for
the "description and discussion" slices in this subject.

## Quotable lines

1. **Naming is a small, separable slice; discussion is the reservoir (Q.2C et al., p.19):**
   > "5 for name, 10 for description and discussion."
2. **Discussion is always steered by named lenses, never abstract (Q.1A/B, p.19):**
   > "Name, description and discussion of monument one: refer to form, function and construction techniques."
3. **A single deep-dive part names every lens at once (Q.14B, p.26):**
   > "Detailed discussion of chosen work with reference to the artist, subject matter, style, composition, materials, techniques and the period in which it was produced."
4. **Sketches are ring-fenced marks in every question, and your own example outscores the reproduced one (Q.2D, p.19):**
   > "Sketches. 5 — 2 for given illustration, 3 for other artefact."
5. **Section III rewards a justified original proposal, not just analysis (Q.17C, p.27):**
   > "Briefly outline your own visual concepts for a public artwork which would enhance an outdoor space in your area, give reasons for your design decisions."
6. **The scheme is a trained-examiner skeleton, not a standalone rubric (p.1):**
   > "Marking schemes published by the State Examinations Commission are not intended to be standalone documents. They are an essential resource for examiners who receive training in the correct interpretation and application of the scheme."
