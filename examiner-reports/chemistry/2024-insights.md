## Paper structure and mark allocation

The scheme covers **11 questions** (Q1–Q11). Every question is worth **50 marks**, verified by summing the parenthetical part-marks: Q1 and Q2 each total exactly 50 (page 6, page 7). The candidate answers **8 questions**, giving **400 marks** — confirmed by the "Tábla 400 @ 10%" Irish-bonus table (page 5), which is explicitly the 400-mark table. (The "answer eight of eleven, all questions carry equal marks" rubric lives on the exam paper, not verbatim in the scheme; the 2013 Chief Examiner's Report confirms the expectation: "attempt at least 8 questions", p.25, and notes "5% of candidates did not attempt the required 8 questions", p.14.)

- **Section A** (experimental / mandatory-practical questions) and **Section B** (theory) is the paper's split, but the marking scheme is a flat per-question list; it does not restate the section rubric. Q1–Q3 are the experimental questions in 2024; Q4–Q11 are theory.
- **Irish bonus (page 5):** identical wording to Biology — "Bonus marks at the rate of 10% of the marks obtained will be given to a candidate who answers entirely through Irish and who obtains 75% or less of the total mark available (i.e. 300 marks or less). In calculating the bonus to be applied decimals are always rounded down, not up." Above 300 a tapered table applies (page 5).

## The points-based mark notation (structure of the scheme, page 3–4)

Chemistry uses the same compact points grammar as Biology, with its own solidus conventions. The eleven numbered "points to be noted" on pages 3–4 are the rulebook:

- **The mark for each markable element appears in round brackets** after the answer, e.g. `pipette (3)`, `Mr = 60 (2)`. A fully correct response's part-marks add to the question's contribution: "examiners may award one total mark (e.g. 6 marks) or a number of partial marks (e.g. 2 marks, 2 marks, 2 marks) that add to the same total" (point 11, page 4).
- **Front-loaded points lists.** Where a list of acceptable answers is longer than the number required, marks taper. Canonical 2024 examples:
  - `aldehyde / ketone / carboxylic acid / ester [any three] (3 + 2 + 1)` — first correct 3 marks, second 2, third 1 (page 10).
  - `H2CO3 / HCO3– / CO32– / H+ [any three] (3 + 2 + 1)` (page 10).
  - `[any two] (4 + 2)` — first correct 4 marks, second 2 (page 10).
  - `[any three] (3 × 2)` — three required, 2 marks each (page 19).
  This is Chemistry's equivalent of Biology's `2(4) + 4(3)` engine: a longer accepted-answer list with the marks weighted toward the first answers.
- **Solidus `/` = equally acceptable alternatives** (point 2, page 3): "Words, expressions or statements separated by a solidus, /, are alternatives which are equally acceptable."
- **Double solidus `//` = mutually exclusive alternatives** (point 3, page 3): "A double solidus, //, separates answers which are mutually exclusive. **A partial answer from one side of the // may not be taken in conjunction with a partial answer from the other side.**" (Used where the question admits distinct reagent routes — e.g. the ethanal-oxidation test on page 7: `acidified KMnO4 // Fehling's // Tollen's`, with each route's colour-change and half-equation locked to its own side.)
- **Brackets `( )` are context, not a requirement** (point 5, page 3): "Material that is bracketed is not required in that year." Bracketed words contextualise or offer a valid alternative but are not needed to score.
- **Binding force** (point 4, page 3): "The descriptions, methods and definitions in the scheme are not exhaustive and alternative valid answers are acceptable." The scheme is a floor of accepted answers, not an exhaustive list — examiners "consider all answers on their merits" (Note to teachers, page 2).

## The context rule and exact-term demands (point 1, page 3)

Chemistry has Biology's context rule almost verbatim:

> "In many instances only key words are given – words that must appear in the correct context in the candidate's answer in order to merit the assigned marks. Where incorrect terminology is used or where part of the candidate's answer contradicts another part, the marks may not be awarded." (point 1, page 3)

- **Self-contradiction voids marks:** if one part of the answer contradicts another, the marks may be withheld.
- **Exact-term demands** appear inline as square-bracket adjudications, e.g. `pink [do not accept purple] (3)`, `to colourless [do not accept clear] (3)` (page 6); `spectrometer / spectroscope [do not accept mass spectrometer] (3)` (page 11); `hydrogen chloride [do not accept HCl or hydrochloric acid] (3)` (page 15). Where a specific scientific term is demanded, near-synonyms are explicitly rejected.
- **Substance identity flexibility** (point 6, page 3): "When a candidate is asked to identify a chemical substance, either the name or formula is accepted, unless otherwise indicated." Deionised water = distilled/pure water; organic compounds accept IUPAC or traditional name; inorganic accept oxidation-state or traditional naming (e.g. "potassium manganate(VII) or potassium permanganate for KMnO4").

## Cancellation and surplus (point 1, page 3; annotation table, page 4)

- **Cancellation of over-supplied answers:** "Cancellation may apply when a candidate gives more than the required number of responses, or a list of correct and incorrect answers" (point 1, page 3). This is the Chemistry equivalent of Biology's surplus-answer penalty — a wrong answer added to a correct list can cancel a correct mark. The annotation palette (page 4) carries a dedicated **`C C` Cancellation** symbol and a **`[` Square bracket = Surplus element** symbol, confirming surplus/cancellation is a marked, distinct adjudication.
- The scheme does **not** use Biology's asterisk (`*`) convention for nullification; in Chemistry the "correct answer nullified by adding an incorrect one" mechanism is handled by the general cancellation rule (point 1) plus inline `[do not accept …]` notes, rather than a per-instance asterisk.

## Calculation, units, rounding and structure deductions (points 7–9, pages 3–4)

This is Chemistry's most distinctive and heavily-used marking machinery — the equivalent of Maths's "Full Credit −1":

- **Arithmetic-error / rounding deduction (point 7, page 3):** "Each time an arithmetical error occurs in a calculation, one mark is deducted; this also applies to inappropriate or incorrect rounding of numerical answers." The **−1 applies each time** the error occurs (per-occurrence, not once per question). The annotation table lists a dedicated **`−1`** symbol (page 4).
- **The Mr (relative molecular mass) rule (point 7, page 3):** an arithmetic error in an Mr costs one mark **only if the candidate shows the addition of all the correct atomic masses and the error is clearly arithmetical**. "If the addition of atomic masses is not shown, the candidate loses the marks for an incorrect Mr." — i.e. showing your working converts a full loss into a −1.
- **Units (point 9, page 4):** "For omission of appropriate units (or for incorrect units) in final answers, one mark is deducted, unless otherwise indicated."
- **Organic-structure drawing (point 8, page 4):** "For drawing the molecular structure of an organic compound, one mark is deducted if the H atoms are omitted in a systematic way and one mark is deducted if bonds to H atoms are omitted in a systematic way." (Two separate −1s possible — omitted H atoms and omitted H bonds are independent deductions.)
- **Graph plotting** is capped, not −1'd: `[maximum of 3 marks for points plotted on paper other than graph paper]` (page 8).

## Attempt vs. non-attempt: NR (point 10, page 4)

> "A zero should only be recorded when the candidate has attempted the question item but does not merit marks. If a candidate does not attempt a question item examiners should record NR." (point 10, page 4)

A genuine wrong attempt scores 0; a blank scores NR. There is **no negative marking** (confirmed by the 2013 CER, p.25: "there is no negative marking and any attempts to answer parts of questions may earn marks").

## Examiner annotation palette (point 11, page 4)

Scripts are annotated per element. The palette: **Cross** = incorrect element; **Tick** = correct element (0 marks); **`n` Tick** = correct element worth n marks; **Horizontal wavy line** = "to be noticed"; **Vertical wavy line** = additional page; **Ellipse** = incorrect material; **`−1`** = one-mark deduction; **`0`**; **`^`** = missing element; **`C C`** = cancellation; **`[`** = surplus element. (Note the "Tick = correct element (0 marks)" vs "n Tick = n marks" distinction: a bare tick acknowledges a correct element that carries no marks; the number rides on the tick.)

## Does it match Biology's conventions?

Broadly **yes**, with named differences:

| Convention | Biology (2023 HL) | Chemistry (2024 HL) |
|---|---|---|
| Points list, front-loaded | `2(4) + 4(3)` | `(3 + 2 + 1)`, `(4 + 2)`, `(3 × 2)` — same principle, different notation |
| `/` = equal alternatives | Yes | Yes (point 2) |
| Mutually-exclusive marker | (context-dependent) | Explicit **`//`** with "no cross-combining" rule (point 3) |
| Context rule (key word only in correct context) | Yes (p.3–4) | Yes, near-verbatim (point 1) |
| Brackets = not required | Yes | Yes (point 5) |
| Surplus wrong answer cancels a correct mark | Yes — Section A "surplus" | Yes — general "cancellation" rule (point 1) + `[`/`C C` annotations |
| Asterisk `*` nullification | Yes (Sections B/C) | **No** — handled via cancellation + inline `[do not accept…]` |
| Irish 10% bonus, rounded down | Yes | Yes, identical wording (page 5) |
| Per-occurrence −1 for arithmetic/rounding/units | (less prominent) | **Central** — points 7–9, own `−1` annotation |

The context rule, the front-loaded points list, the bracket-is-optional rule, and the Irish-bonus wording are effectively shared SEC science-marking DNA. Chemistry adds a heavier, per-occurrence **calculation-deduction regime** (arithmetic / rounding / units / organic-structure), and replaces Biology's asterisk with a general cancellation rule.

## Documented candidate behaviour (from 2013 Chief Examiner's Report — for authored "scripts")

These are real, examiner-flagged behaviours, useful for teaching how marks are actually lost:

1. **"Clear" instead of "colourless"; "lower meniscus" instead of "bottom of the meniscus" lose marks** (2013 CER, p.24): "Incorrect terminology, such as the use of 'clear' instead of 'colourless', 'lower meniscus' instead of 'bottom of the meniscus' will cause the loss of marks." (Directly mirrored in the 2024 scheme's `to colourless [do not accept clear]`, page 6.)
2. **"Describe"/"Explain" cues need sentences, not words** (2013 CER, p.24): "Questions containing the cues 'Describe' or 'Explain' generally require an answer in the form of a sentence or sentences. Answers in the form of a phrase or a word are rarely sufficient for full marks."
3. **Rounding is a recurring, per-occurrence mark-drain** (2013 CER, p.23): "Students should learn how to correctly round off decimals in a calculation" — and the 2024 scheme's point 7 deducts a mark *each time* incorrect rounding occurs (page 3).
4. **Show your atomic-mass addition to protect the Mr mark** (2024 scheme, point 7, page 3): an Mr arithmetic slip is only a −1 *if the addition of atomic masses is shown*; otherwise the full Mr marks are lost. A concrete "working saves marks" script.
5. **Common calculation slips examiners see repeatedly** (2013 CER, p.16): "Incorrect multiplying of interim answers by five, use of 16 as the relative atomic mass of oxygen (O2) and multiplying by 100 instead of 1000 or division by 1000 to get ppm were errors that were regularly observed." (The "factor of 5" trap recurs in the 2024 vinegar-dilution Q1, page 6.)
6. **Attempt all 8; partial marks are freely given** (2013 CER, p.14, p.25): "5% of candidates did not attempt the required 8 questions"; "there is no negative marking … partial marks are frequently awarded for partially correct answers … marks are awarded for attempts that show partial knowledge."
7. **Organic reaction mechanisms are chronically under-answered at Higher level** (2013 CER, p.21, p.23): "organic reaction mechanisms continue to be poorly answered and therefore additional focus on mechanisms … is warranted."
