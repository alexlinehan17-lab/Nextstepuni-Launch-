# Chemistry HL — Second-Year Verification (2025 vs cited 2024 rules)

## Source
- **Verifying against:** SEC *Leaving Certificate 2025 Marking Scheme — Chemistry, Higher Level*
  (`2025-marking-scheme.pdf`, 24 pp., mirrored from educateplus.ie `2025 LC Chemistry HL.pdf`;
  extracted to `2025-marking-scheme.md` via PyPDF2 with page markers).
- **Baseline:** SEC 2024 Chemistry HL marking scheme (`2024-marking-scheme.md` / `2024-insights.md`),
  already filed. This document is a *cross-year drift check* of the five cited rules, not a full
  insights write-up.
- **Rejected sources:** none consulted from mock/Pre-Leaving providers; the PDF cover page confirms
  "Coimisiún na Scrúduithe Stáit / State Examinations Commission … Leaving Certificate 2025 …
  Higher Level Chemistry" (official).
- The five rules being verified are structural marking conventions stated in the scheme's
  **"points to be noted" general notes (PDF p. 3, notes 1–10; note 11 continues p. 4)** plus
  their in-body application. These notes are near-verbatim stable text carried year to year.

## Rule-by-rule verification

| # | Cited rule | Status in 2025 | Page ref (2025) | Notes / drift |
|---|-----------|----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Exact-term demands enforced inline with "[do not accept …]" — e.g. "clear" not accepted for "colourless" | **CONFIRMED (mechanism); example NOT PRESENT (question-specific)** | p. 3 note 1 (context principle); p. 16 for an inline bracket example | The inline bracketed exact-term enforcement mechanism is intact: p. 16 marks `dipole-dipole ["Van der Waals" do not cancel]`. However the specific `[do not accept …]` *phrasing* and the "clear vs colourless" example do **not** appear in the 2025 paper — that example is 2024-question-specific (2024 scheme p. of Q on indicators: `to colourless [do not accept clear]`, plus `pink [do not accept purple]`, `[do not accept mass spectrometer]`, `[do not accept HCl or hydrochloric acid]`). 2025 simply had no colour-change-to-colourless item (its colour Qs resolved to blue / blue→red for Fehling's, p. 13–14), so there was no occasion to reject "clear". **No principle drift** — the exact-term/synonym-rejection convention is unchanged; the illustrative example is not reproducible from 2025 alone. Keep the 2024 scheme as the citable source for the "clear/colourless" example. |
| 2 | Mr rule — an Mr arithmetic slip is only −1 IF the atomic-mass addition is shown; otherwise all Mr marks lost | **CONFIRMED — verbatim** | p. 3 note 7 | "Each time an arithmetical error occurs in a calculation, one mark is deducted. This deduction applies to incorrect Mr values, but only if a candidate shows the addition of all the correct atomic masses and the error is clearly an arithmetical one. If the addition of atomic masses is not shown, the candidate loses the marks for an incorrect Mr." Word-for-word identical to the 2024 note 7. No drift. |
| 3 | "//" separates mutually-exclusive methods; partials from opposite sides can't combine | **CONFIRMED — verbatim, and applied in body** | p. 3 note 3; applied p. 6, p. 8 | Note 3: "A double solidus, //, separates answers which are mutually exclusive. A partial answer from one side of the // may not be taken in conjunction with a partial answer from the other side." In-body use e.g. p. 6 (`… // 0.630 …`), p. 7 solvent-extraction list (`non-polar // low solubility in water // …`). Single `/` = equally acceptable alternatives (note 2). No drift. |
| 4 | Context rule — key terms must appear in a correct context to score | **CONFIRMED — verbatim** | p. 3 note 1 (and note 5) | Note 1: "In many instances only key words are given – words that must appear in the correct context in the candidate's answer in order to merit the assigned marks. Where incorrect terminology is used or where part of the candidate's answer contradicts another part, the marks may not be awarded. Cancellation may apply …". Reinforced by note 5: "The detail required in any answer is determined by the context and manner in which the question is asked …". No drift. |
| 5 | Describe/Explain cues need full sentences; a word/phrase rarely earns full marks (Chief Examiner point) — does the scheme's general notes support "correct context" / full-answer expectations? | **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED** | p. 3 notes 1 & 5 | The marking scheme's general notes support the underlying principle — correct context (note 1) and detail-scaled-to-question (note 5) — which is the marking-scheme correlate of the Chief Examiner's "full sentence for Describe/Explain" guidance. The explicit sentence-level statement ("a word/phrase rarely earns full marks") is a **Chief Examiner's Report** point and is *not* restated verbatim in the 2025 marking scheme (marking schemes never carry that phrasing). So: principle CONFIRMED via notes 1 & 5; the exact "full-sentence" wording remains sourced to the Chief Examiner's Report, not the scheme. No drift in the scheme-level support. |

## Anything needing a session edit
**None.** All five cited rules hold in the 2025 scheme. The only nuance worth recording (not a
correction): the **"clear" not accepted for "colourless"** example under rule 1 is
question-specific to 2024 and does not recur in 2025 — any student-facing content that uses that
example should cite the **2024** scheme (where `to colourless [do not accept clear]` literally
appears), while the general "[do not accept …]" convention can be cited from either year.

## Anything unverified
- The literal `[do not accept …]` bracket phrasing could not be re-confirmed from the 2025 paper
  because no 2025 question triggered a synonym rejection; the equivalent inline-bracket mechanism
  was confirmed instead (p. 16, `["Van der Waals" do not cancel]`). The exact-term-rejection
  convention is therefore verified in *substance* across both years, and in *exact phrasing* only
  from 2024.
