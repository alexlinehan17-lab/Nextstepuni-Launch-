## The marking system in one sentence

Every long answer is marked under **two headings that always sum to 100% of the question**:
**Cumulative Mark (CM)** for accurate, relevant historical *content*, and **Overall Evaluation
(OE)** for the *quality of the answer as a whole against the set question*. The ratio is fixed:

> "The proportion of CM to OE remains 60/40 throughout the examination paper." (p.12; repeated p.24)

- In Sections 2 & 3 (the essays): **Max CM = 60, Max OE = 40** (p.13, p.25).
- In the DBQ Q4 (Contextualisation, a shorter essay): **Max CM = 24, Max OE = 16** — same 60/40
  ratio scaled down (p.11, p.23).

## CM — how "marking by paragraph" works (pp.11, 13, 25)

CM is **not** a fixed points-per-fact tariff like Geography's SRPs. Instead the examiner
divides the answer into paragraphs and grades each paragraph on a band. The scheme's own
definition of what counts as a markable unit (verbatim, p.11 / p.13):

> "A paragraph or paragraph equivalent may be one of the following:
> (i) A relevant introduction giving the background situation and/or defining the terms and
> explaining the approach;
> (ii) An episode, phase or stage in a sequence of events;
> (iii) An aspect of a topic/issue, with supporting factual references;
> (iv) A point in an argument or discussion, with supporting factual references;
> (v) An explanation of a concept or term, with supporting factual references;
> (vi) A number of significant, relevant statements of fact, explanation or comment which,
> although not connected or related, can be taken together and assessed as a paragraph
> equivalent;
> (vii) A good concluding paragraph or summation, which is not mere repetition."

Anti-padding rule attached directly to (vii): **"Summation which is mere repetition = max 4
marks"** (p.11, p.13).

**Per-paragraph CM grading band (Sections 2 & 3, out of a 12-mark paragraph ceiling, p.14;
identical band appears at the DBQ Q4 on p.11):**

| Band | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | 11–12 | "Outstanding piece of analysis, exposition or commentary. Clearly expressed, accurate and substantial information." |
| Very good | 8–10 | "Very good material, accurately and clearly expressed." |
| Good | 6–7 | "Worthwhile information, reasonably well expressed." |
| Fair | 3–5 | "Limited information/barely stated." |
| Poor | 0–2 | "Trivial/irrelevant/grave errors." |

So a candidate accumulates CM paragraph-by-paragraph up to the Max CM ceiling (60, or 24 in the
DBQ). Reaching the ceiling takes roughly 5–6 strong paragraphs; more paragraphs than needed
score **notional/excess marks with zero value** (annotation "Excess paragraph marks", p.3).

## OE — the "quality of the whole answer" mark (pp.12, 14, 24)

OE is a single holistic judgement of how well the whole answer addresses the set question.

> "In awarding OE, the examiner will consider how well the candidate deals with the set
> question." (p.12, p.24)

**OE grading table, Sections 2 & 3 (Max 40, p.14):**

| Band | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | 34–40 | "Excellent in its treatment of the set question, particularly if it shows detailed learning, wide reading, analysis or extensive coverage." |
| Very good | 28–33 | "Very good – but not excellent – in its treatment of the set question, ie: accurate and substantial." |
| Good | 22–27 | "Good standard treatment of the set question, without being exceptional in the information or the commentary supplied." |
| Fair | 16–21 | "Fair attempt at the set question, but has identifiable defects, e.g.: incomplete coverage, irrelevant data, factual inaccuracies." |
| Weak | 10–15 | "Poor, in that it fails to answer the question as set, but has some merit." |
| Very weak | 0–9 | "Very poor answer which, at best, offers only scraps of information." |

The three things OE explicitly rewards (p.14):

> "• To what extent has the candidate shown the ability to analyse the issues involved in the
> question asked (i.e.: more than mere narrative)?
> • To what extent has the candidate marshalled the relevant evidence to support his/her
> analysis?
> • To what extent has the candidate shown the ability to argue a case and to reach conclusions
> (i.e.: to answer the question as asked)?"

**DBQ Q4 OE band (Max 16, p.12 / p.24):** Excellent 14–16, Very good 11–13, Good 6–10, Fair
4–5, Weak 2–3, Very weak 0–1. With the instruction: "In awarding OE marks, the examiner should
not expect answers of more than 40% in quantity compared to the answers to the longer topic
questions in Sections 2 and 3." (p.12) — i.e. the DBQ essay is meant to be shorter.

## The "TWO elements" cap — the single most mechanical rule in the essays (pp.15–18)

Almost every Sections 2 & 3 question has a note beneath it telling the examiner how many
*elements* the question demands. If the question names two (e.g. "Davitt + Larkin",
"Metternich + Marx", "Berlin + Cuba", "North + South", "at home + abroad") and the candidate
answers only one, the CM ceiling drops:

> "NOTE: TWO elements (Davitt + Larkin)  If only ONE, Max. CM = 50" (p.15)

This recurs verbatim across dozens of questions (pp.15–18) — the penalty for a one-sided answer
to a two-part question is a **fixed 10-mark CM haircut (60→50)**, never a zero. The OE ceiling
(40) is unaffected by the note, though a one-sided answer will typically band lower on OE
anyway ("incomplete coverage" is an explicit Fair-band defect, p.14). Topic 5 EWW adds a
variant: "Refer to at least two European countries If only ONE, Max. CM = 50" (p.18).

## The Documents-Based Question (DBQ, 100 marks) — its own four-part grammar (pp.8–12)

The DBQ is marked differently from the essays: three of its four parts use **sliding-scale
marks per document/answer, not paragraph CM**. Marking by paragraph only begins at Q4.

1. **Comprehension (20 marks, p.8):** four short factual retrieval items, **5 marks each**,
   answer-key style (accept any of the listed valid phrasings). No CM/OE.
2. **Comparison (20 marks, pp.8–9):** two parts. Each part asks the candidate to compare the two
   documents; **each document is marked on a sliding scale out of 5** — "Excellent = 5, Very
   good = 4, Good = 3, Fair = 2, Weak = 0–1" — so a two-document answer is out of 10 per part.
   Hard cap: **"Answer referring to one document only = 5M max"** (p.8, p.9). This is the DBQ's
   equivalent of the essay two-elements rule: engage both documents or lose half the marks.
3. **Criticism (20 marks, pp.9–10):** two parts, each marked as one **sliding scale out of 10**
   — "Excellent = 9–10, Very good = 7–8, Good = 5–6, Fair = 3–4, Weak = 0–2" (p.9, p.10). Part
   (a) is typically "are the points convincing?" (evaluate the argument), part (b) "assess the
   value of this source" (source-evaluation: primary vs secondary, editor's expertise, bias,
   what the source omits). The scheme rewards defining the source type and weighing
   reliability, not just paraphrase (see the worked indicative points, p.10).
4. **Contextualisation (40 marks, pp.11–12):** a mini-essay. **This is where marking by
   paragraph begins** (p.11). Max CM = 24, Max OE = 16 (the 60/40 ratio again).

RSR "relevance-significance-reference" style acronyms do **not** appear in this scheme; History's
document skills are labelled Comprehension / Comparison / Criticism / Contextualisation, and the
value-of-source ("Criticism (b)") item is the closest analogue to source-reliability marking.

## Research Study Report (RSR) coursework — 100 marks (pp.4–6)

The RSR is the 20% project component, marked out of 100 across three grids:

- **Outline Plan (15 marks, p.4):** Citation of the sources = 6 marks (Accurate citation of each
  appropriate source = 2 marks; "Author + title only = 1 mark"; "Extra item of validating
  information (e.g.: publisher, date) = 1 mark"; **2 × 3 = max 6 for three sources**). Other
  elements = 9 marks: Define and justify / Identify aims / Identify approach, each max 3, graded
  Very good 3 / Good 2 / Fair 1. Explicit warning: **"'Standard school text books will not be
  regarded as suitable sources for the purposes of the research study.' DES/NCCA Guidelines,
  p.14."** (p.4)
- **Evaluation of the Sources (25 marks, p.5):** graded on how many sources the candidate
  actually engages. Referencing ALL THREE sources → Excellent 22–25 … Weak 0–8; only TWO → caps
  at Excellent 15–17; only ONE → caps at Excellent 8–9. So, as in the exam papers, **breadth of
  engagement sets the ceiling**.
- **Extended Essay (60 marks, p.6):** Historical essay = 50 (Historical knowledge 25 / Research
  skills 15 / Presentation 10, each with its own Excellent-to-Weak band) + Review of the
  research process = 10. Research-skills band explicitly rewards "Appropriate depth of
  investigation / Use of sources / Fair and balanced treatment of issues"; Presentation band
  rewards "Literacy / Coherence / Structure".

## General examiner conventions / annotations (p.3)

Online marking annotations (p.3) reveal the machinery:

- **Square brackets** "Indicate beginning and end of paragraph to be awarded Cumulative Marks
  (CMs)." — i.e. the examiner literally brackets each CM paragraph.
- **Blank tick** = "Valid/correct/relevant/of merit (a blank tick does not award any marks)" —
  a tick alone is recognition, not credit; **tick-with-a-number** is the one that awards marks.
- **Excess paragraph marks:** "Where an answer has already scored the maximum CMs allowable, any
  remaining material will be awarded notional marks with zero value attached." — over-writing
  past the ceiling earns nothing.
- **Invalid combination penalty:** if a candidate answers an invalid combination (e.g. three
  questions from one topic), "the lowest scoring invalid answer(s) will be deducted from the
  total mark using minus-mark annotations" via the Adjustment (ADJ) line — **choosing the wrong
  combination of questions can cost marks off the total.**
