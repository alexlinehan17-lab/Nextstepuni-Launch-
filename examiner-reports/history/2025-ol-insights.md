## Headline: OL does NOT use the HL system

The single most important finding: **Ordinary Level History is marked on a completely different
grammar from Higher Level.** Almost every structural rule the HL insights file documents is
replaced at OL:

| Dimension | Higher Level (2025-insights.md) | Ordinary Level (this scheme) |
|---|---|---|
| Markable unit | "Paragraph or paragraph equivalent" (7 defined types), graded on a band | **"Core Statement"** — a flat **5 marks each** tariff (p.10, p.11) |
| How CM is earned | Each paragraph banded Excellent→Poor out of a 12-mark ceiling | Each completed Core Statement = 5 marks; incomplete last one = 1–4 marks (p.10) |
| CM:OE ratio | Fixed **60/40 throughout the paper** | **Not 60/40.** Part B is CM 20 / OE 10 (2:1); Part C is CM 30 / OE 10 (3:1) (p.11, p.21) |
| Long-answer structure | Sections 2 & 3 are full essays (Max CM 60 / OE 40) | Each Topic = three parts **A + B + C** — no full essay (p.11, p.21) |
| DBQ Comprehension | 20 marks (4 items × 5) | **40 marks (5 items × 8)** (p.8, p.18) |
| DBQ Contextualisation | 40-mark mini-essay (CM 24 + OE 16) | **20 marks, pure Core Statement (4 × 5), NO OE** (p.10, p.20) |
| "Two elements / if only ONE Max CM = 50" cap | Present, recurring on essays | **Absent at OL.** Replaced by per-item "one element only = 3m" notes on Part A |
| RSR sources required | THREE | **TWO** (p.4, p.5) |

## OL long-answer structure — Sections 2 & 3 (pp.11, 21)

Each Topic (300 marks across the two chosen topics) is split into three parts, and the candidate
answers **all three** of the chosen topic's parts:

> "Each Topic is divided into three parts:
> A. A stimulus-driven unit with 5 short questions to be attempted (maximum: 30 marks)
> B. A short paragraph to be written in response to one of four headings (maximum: 30 marks)
> C. A longer paragraph to be written in answer to one of four questions (maximum: 40 marks)" (p.11)

- **Part A (30 marks):** "Typically the marks will be awarded as 6 marks per question, with 6
  marks x 5 giving the maximum 30 marks." (p.11) These are short stimulus-comprehension items with
  an answer key. Many carry a split-credit note where a two-part answer is required, e.g.
  "Callan, Co. Kilkenny (one element only = 3m)" (p.12) or "any two, 3m + 3m" (p.13). This is the
  OL analogue of HL's completeness demand — but it is a **3-mark half-credit on a single item**,
  not the 10-mark essay haircut HL uses.
- **Part B (30 marks):** short paragraph → **Max CM = 20, Max OE = 10.**
- **Part C (40 marks):** longer paragraph → **Max CM = 30, Max OE = 10.**

Note the OE ceiling is a **flat 10 marks** in both B and C regardless of the part's total — so OE
is proportionally *smaller* at OL (25% of Part B, 25% of Part C) than the 40% it carries at HL,
and CM dominates even more heavily. There is no essay at OL; the largest single answer is the
40-mark Part C "longer paragraph".

### The Core Statement — OL's fundamental unit (pp.10, 11)

Where HL marks by banded "paragraph or paragraph equivalent", OL marks by **Core Statement**, a
fixed 5-mark tariff much closer to Geography's SRP model:

> "A Core Statement may be defined as one of the following:
> • A significant factual statement which is relevant to the question asked.
> • A valid explanation, opinion or comment which is relevant to the question asked.
> • A significant introductory OR concluding statement which is relevant to the question asked." (p.10)

> "Having read the answer, it should be broken up into Core Statements, with a tick (√) put at the
> end of each completed Core Statement. Each completed Core Statement is awarded 5 marks. An
> incomplete Core Statement at the end of an answer may merit 1-4 marks." (p.10)

So to max Part B a candidate needs **4 Core Statements** (4 × 5 = 20 CM); to max Part C they need
**6 Core Statements** (6 × 5 = 30 CM). The grammar is countable and mechanical: each relevant,
completed point is 5 marks up to the ceiling.

### OE at OL — a flat /10 holistic mark (p.11, p.21)

> "In awarding OE, the examiner will consider how well the answer responds to the heading or
> addresses the set question." (p.11)

OE grading table (out of 10, identical for Part B and Part C):

| Band | Marks |
|---|---|
| Excellent | 9–10 |
| Very good | 7–8 |
| Good | 5–6 |
| Fair | 3–4 |
| Poor | 0–2 |

Difference vs HL: the HL OE band carries the explicit three-part "analyse the issues / marshal the
evidence / argue a case and reach conclusions (more than mere narrative)" reward criteria. **That
analysis-over-narrative language is absent at OL** — OE is simply "how well the answer responds to
the heading or addresses the set question", with the standing instruction that examiners judge
"the range of valid answers which may be expected of a candidate at Ordinary Level" (p.11).

## OL Documents-Based Question — same four skills, different weightings (pp.8–10, 18–20)

The DBQ is still 100 marks and still Comprehension / Comparison / Criticism / Contextualisation,
but the internal mark split is re-weighted heavily toward retrieval and away from the essay:

1. **Comprehension — 40 marks** (HL: 20). Five short retrieval items at **8 marks each**
   (5 × 8 = 40), answer-key style (p.8, p.18). This is the biggest single change: OL puts double
   the DBQ weight on straight comprehension.
2. **Comparison — 20 marks.** Two parts, each **5M + 5M (Max 10 per part)**. Same hard cap as HL:
   "If only one doc, max. 5m" (p.8, p.9). "Mark quality of explanation on its merits. Point with
   quote/explanation can earn 5m." (p.8) The Early Modern paper states it as "Reference to both
   documents required." (p.18)
3. **Criticism — 20 marks.** Two parts, each **5M + 5M**. Simpler than HL: OL asks for a
   source-type identification plus a valid reason — e.g. "Doc A and a valid reason 5M + 5M" (p.9),
   or at Early Modern "Secondary source and a valid explanation 5M + 5M" and "Look for two reasons
   5M + 5M" (p.19). There is **no sliding-scale-out-of-10 per part** as at HL; it is flat
   point-plus-reason credit.
4. **Contextualisation — 20 marks** (HL: 40). **This is NOT a mini-essay at OL and carries NO OE.**
   It is marked purely by Core Statement: "the examiner will be looking to award 5 marks each to
   four Core Statements for the full 20 marks allocation." (p.10, p.20) So the OL DBQ ends with a
   four-point, 20-mark Core-Statement task, not the CM-24/OE-16 essay HL sets.

## Research Study Report (RSR) at OL — two sources, not three (pp.4–6)

Structurally the same 100-mark grid (Outline Plan 15 / Evaluation of Sources 25 / Extended Essay
60), but the source requirement is halved relative to HL:

- **Outline Plan (15):** "Two sources are asked for." (p.4) "Full and accurate citation of one
  source = 3 marks … 3 marks x 2 = 6 marks for two sources" (Title 1 + Author 1 + one further
  validating item 1). Other elements = 9 marks (define/justify + aims + approach, graded Very good
  8–9 / Good 6–7 / Fair 3–5 / Weak 0–2, taken as a whole). Note: OL asks for **two** sources vs
  HL's three, and the "standard school textbooks are not suitable sources" warning that appears in
  the HL Outline Plan is **not reproduced** in the OL Outline Plan text (p.4).
- **Evaluation of the Sources (25):** ceiling set by breadth of engagement, as at HL but scaled to
  two sources: "Reference to BOTH sources → Very good 21–25 … Weak 0–7"; "Reference to only ONE
  source → Very good 14–17 … Weak 0–4" (p.5). Engaging only one of the two roughly halves the top
  band.
- **Extended Essay (60):** Historical essay 50 (Historical knowledge 25 / Research skills 15 /
  Presentation 10) + Review of the research process 10 (Very good 8–10 … Weak 0–3) (pp.5–6). The
  Research-skills band rewards "Study chosen is one of historical significance (not obscure or
  trivial) / Fair and balanced treatment of issues / Shows appropriate depth of investigation"
  (p.5).

## Shared machinery (same as HL)

- **Annotations (p.3):** identical convention — "Tick with number" awards marks; a **blank tick**
  is "Valid/correct/relevant/of merit (a blank tick does not award any marks)"; Cross/X = invalid.
- **Choice adjustments (p.3):** the online system "awards marks to the two highest topics answered
  in Section 2 and Section 3." Extra lines "Extra A", "Third A/B/C" re-insert marks for a valid
  third set; over-attempting Part A's triggers deductions (e.g. "X-6 (minus 6 marks)") — choosing
  the wrong combination of questions can cost marks off the total.
- **Bonus for answering through Irish (p.26):** standard 400-mark @ 10% bonus table for candidates
  scoring above 75% (`Marcanna Breise as ucht freagairt trí Ghaeilge`).
