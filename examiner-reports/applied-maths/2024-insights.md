## Which marking model does it use? (the key finding)

**Applied Mathematics HL uses neither the Maths A/B/C/D scale system nor the Physics formula→substitution→answer granule ladder.** It runs on the **classic Irish "blunders / slips / misreadings" penalty model**, layered with two extra mechanisms:

1. **Numbered-penalty deduction system** — every error is classified and a fixed deduction applied (blunder −3, slip −1, misreading −1). This is the load-bearing model for most written-paper items.
2. **Systemic-error marking scales** — certain higher-value items are marked on an *attempt ladder* (e.g. `[0/8/16/24/27]` for a 30-mark item) keyed to how many "systemic errors" the work contains, not to per-step granules.
3. **Rubric-band grid** — the separate **Mathematical Modelling Project** (100 marks) is marked on descriptive bands ("Very thorough / Thorough / Basic / Very basic") per report section, with no numeric per-step marking at all.

So a "how SEC marking works" tool should present Applied Maths as its **own third model**: penalty-based with an error taxonomy, distinct from the Maths scale-category system and the Physics granule ladder.

## Paper structure and mark allocation

- The **written paper totals 400 marks** (confirmed by the Irish-bonus instruction, PDF p.5: bonus applies to a candidate "who obtains 75% or less of the total mark available (i.e. 300 marks or less)"; the bonus table is headed "Tábla 400 @ 5%").
- Marks are printed **inline beside each markable step**, most commonly as a `5` at the end of a solution line (the written paper is largely built from 5-mark steps), e.g. Q2(a)(ii) three steps each `5` (PDF p.7); Q3(a) integration-by-parts steps each `5` (PDF p.8).
- Higher-value items that are scale-marked carry a **bracketed attempt ladder** next to the mark total, e.g. `10 [0/4/7]` (Q1(a), PDF p.6), `15 [0/6/9/12]` (Q1(b)(i), PDF p.6), `30 [0/8/16/24/27]` (Q8(a)(i), PDF p.13), `20 [0/8/14/17]` (Q10(a)(i), PDF p.15).
- The **Mathematical Modelling Project** is a separate 100-mark component marked on the rubric grid (PDF p.16–18); its section weights are Introduction & Research 20, Modelling Process 50 (split 15/20/15 across Explain-&-Justify / Compute-&-Iterate / Present-&-Analyse), Interpretation 15, Communication & Innovation 15.

## The penalty system (structure of the scheme, PDF p.3, instruction 4)

The core marking grammar. Penalties are applied *to* an otherwise-scoring solution:

- **Mathematical error ("blunder") — −3.** A substantive conceptual/method error.
- **Mathematical/numerical slip — −1.** A minor arithmetic/transcription error.
- **Misreading (if not serious or leading to oversimplification) — −1.**
- **Oversimplification rule (instruction 5):** "A misreading or slip or omission which **oversimplifies** the question may be regarded as **equivalent to a mathematical error** and is marked accordingly." — i.e. a slip that makes the problem easier is upgraded from −1 to −3.

This is a **subtractive** model: examiners start from the full mark for a step and deduct per classified error, in contrast to the Physics *additive* granule model (award per stage reached) and the Maths *scale-category* model (place the whole response on an A/B/C/D scale).

Single solidus `/` in the scheme "indicates different valid attempts" (instruction 3, PDF p.3) — i.e. equally acceptable alternative methods/answers. (No double-solidus mutually-exclusive convention is defined, unlike Physics.)

## Systemic-error marking scales (PDF p.3, instruction 6)

For scale-marked items the scheme prints the ladder **in bold** and awards by counting "systemic errors":

| Item value | One systemic error | Two systemic errors | >2 systemic errors (method evident) | Valid attempt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30-mark | 27 | 24 | 16 | 8 |
| 20-mark | 17 | 14 | — | 8 |
| 15-mark | 12 | — | — | 6 (also covers two errors) |
| 10-mark | 7 | — | — | 4 (also covers two errors) |

- These general ladders map directly onto the bracketed per-item scales seen in the body: `30 [0/8/16/24/27]` and `20 [0/8/14/17]` are the table read right-to-left.
- **Per-item scales can be finer than the general table.** Several 15-mark items show `[0/6/9/12]` (Q1(b)(i) PDF p.6, Q8(b) PDF p.13) — a `9` mid-level the general 15-mark table doesn't list — and Q1(a) is `10 [0/4/7]`. The bracket beside the item is authoritative when it differs.
- **"Valid attempt" is the floor for any credited scale item** — a candidate who presents a recognisable attempt that cannot score higher still banks the bottom rung (8 / 8 / 6 / 4 respectively).

## Slip / units / error-carried-forward behaviour

- **Slips cost −1, not the step.** Instruction 4 (PDF p.3). A numerical slip is a one-mark deduction; the method around it survives.
- **Oversimplifying slips are upgraded to blunders (−3).** Instruction 5 (PDF p.3) — the one place a "small" error becomes expensive: if it removes the difficulty, it is marked as a mathematical error.
- **Error-carried-forward is applied through per-question marking, not a single stated rule** — as in Physics, there is no global ECF clause, but the subtractive model means downstream steps built on a slipped value are still creditable (the slip is penalised once at its source). Instruction 1 (PDF p.3) reinforces that alternative valid routes are accepted: "The descriptions, methods and definitions in the scheme are not exhaustive and alternative valid answers are acceptable."
- **Naming/identification penalties are item-specific.** Q1(b)(i) (PDF p.6): "Deduct 3 marks if the algorithm used is not correctly named" and "Allow 3 marks for the name of a correct algorithm if no other work is presented" — a worked example of a targeted −3 and of a floor-credit for a correct name alone.
- **No blanket units rule and no significant-figures rule** appear in the general instructions (unlike Physics, which has an explicit −1 units deduction). Units are policed per item where relevant; accepted answers are quoted to sensible precision (e.g. `θ = 5.37°`, `T = 31.18 N`, PDF p.7).

## NR vs zero (PDF p.4, instruction 7)

Identical convention to Physics: "A zero should only be recorded when the candidate has attempted the question item but does not merit marks. If a candidate does not attempt a question item examiners should record **NR**." Non-attempt is logged distinctly from a scored-zero attempt.

## Examiner annotation palette (PDF p.4, instruction 8)

Scripts are annotated as directed at the marking conference:

| Symbol | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ✗ | Cross | Incorrect element |
| ✓ | Tick | Correct element |
| S | Slip | Deduct one mark |
| Box "2" | Box 2 | Partially correct element — award 2 marks |
| ^ | Caret | Missing element |
| ∿ (horizontal wavy) | Horizontal wavy line | To be noticed |
| ∿ (vertical wavy) | Vertical wavy line | Additional page |

The **"S" (slip)** annotation is the visible trace of the −1 penalty; a **boxed number** records a partial-credit award on a scale item.

## The Mathematical Modelling Project rubric (PDF p.16–18)

A wholly separate, **qualitative** marking model for the 100-mark project component — no per-step marks, no penalty codes. Each report section is placed on a four-band descriptor scale with a mark range:

- **Bands (per section):** Very thorough → Thorough → Basic → Very basic, each with a mark range (e.g. Introduction & Research /20: 16–20 / 11–15 / 6–10 / 0–5; Explain & Justify /15: 12–15 / 8–11 / 4–7 / 0–3).
- **Sections & weights:** Introduction & Research 20; Modelling Process 50 (Explain & Justify 15, Compute & Iterate 20, Present & Analyse 15); Interpretation of Results 15; Communication & Innovation 15.
- **Examiner instructions (PDF p.16):** "Be careful **not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length**"; "Mark descriptors should be interpreted in the context of the challenges and demands of the specific problem which the candidate has chosen"; and read the whole booklet before marking. Communication & Innovation "is not a distinct section of the report" — it is marked holistically across the whole project.

This is the closest Applied Maths comes to an essay-style banded scale, and it contrasts sharply with the penalty-driven written paper.

## Candidate-behaviour findings (for authored "how marking works" scripts)

Each grounded in a specific scheme feature (structural, not a model answer):

1. **Errors are priced, and the price differs — a blunder is worth three slips.** Instruction 4 (PDF p.3): a method blunder is −3, an arithmetic slip is −1. The lesson: protecting your method (setting up the right equations) is worth 3× guarding against arithmetic slips — a slip only ever costs one mark.
2. **A slip that makes the problem easier is re-priced as a blunder.** Instruction 5 (PDF p.3): a slip/misreading/omission that "oversimplifies the question" jumps from −1 to −3. Students should be wary of "convenient" mis-copies that turn a hard integral/system into an easy one — that is the one slip that costs like a blunder.
3. **On scale-marked items, a recognisable attempt always banks the floor.** Instruction 6 (PDF p.3) + brackets like `30 [0/8/16/24/27]`, `20 [0/8/14/17]` (PDF p.13, p.15): even work with more than two systemic errors scores 8/30 if the method is evident, and any valid attempt scores the bottom rung. Attempting the big items is never zero-return.
4. **Naming the right tool earns marks even with no working.** Q1(b)(i) (PDF p.6): "Allow 3 marks for the name of a correct algorithm if no other work is presented" — and the mirror penalty "Deduct 3 marks if the algorithm used is not correctly named." Writing "Prim's algorithm" / "Kruskal's algorithm" (or the relevant named method) is itself creditable and its omission is a specific −3.
5. **The modelling project rewards judgement, not volume.** Project rubric (PDF p.16): examiners are told "not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length," and Communication & Innovation is marked holistically across the whole report. Padding the project does not raise the band; a concise, well-justified model can hit "Very thorough."
6. **Non-attempt (NR) is logged separately from a scored zero.** Instruction 7 (PDF p.4). Leaving an item blank is recorded as NR, not 0 — a distinction that matters for the SEC's own analysis, and a reminder that there is no downside to putting *something* creditable down.
