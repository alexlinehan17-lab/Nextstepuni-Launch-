## Paper structure and mark allocation (PDF pp.4–13)

The written paper (60% of the subject; the Student Assignment is the other 40%) is marked out of **240** and is split into three sections. The candidate does **3 of 4** short questions in Section A, **2 of 3** long questions in Section B, and **1 of 5** long questions in Section C.

| Section | Content | Questions on paper | Answered | Marks each | Section total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (short) | Core | A-1 … A-4 | 3 | 20 | 60 |
| B (long) | Core | B-1 … B-3 | 2 | 60 | 120 |
| C (long) | Applied Graphics | C-1 … C-5 | 1 | 60 | 60 |

(Verified by summing the itemised marks: e.g. A-1 = 15 + 5 = 20 (PDF p.4); B-1 = 16 + 29 + 15 = 60 (PDF p.6); C-5 assembly components sum to 60 (PDF p.13).)

Each question's marks are printed as a bracketed part-total next to the part heading — e.g. "(a) Complete the dimetric projection of the packaging **(15)**" — with the 15 then itemised across named steps that sum to it (PDF p.4). Applied Graphics (Section C) covers the option topics: Dynamic Mechanisms/cams (C-4), Structures/perspective (B-3), Surface Geometry (C-2, C-3), Geologic Geometry/earthworks (C-1), and Assemblies/sectional views (C-5).

## The marking grammar — how drawing questions are scored (PDF pp.4–13)

The load-bearing structural facts about how DCG marks work:

**1. Every question is decomposed into named, small-value construction steps, and the steps sum to the part total.** A single 15-mark part is never one holistic judgement; it is a list of 3–8 discrete markable actions. Example, A-1(a) "Complete the dimetric projection **(15)**" (PDF p.4):
- (i) Completion of front lower surface — **3**
- (ii) Completion of front upper surface — **5**
- (iii) Determine the top surface — **3**
- (iv) Determine the side surfaces — **4**

Because each granule is scored on its own, a candidate who gets some surfaces right and others wrong still banks the marks for the correct ones. There is no "all-or-nothing" for a drawing part.

**2. Construction/method is marked separately from the finished curve.** The scheme repeatedly splits "locate the points" and "draw the curve" into distinct granules, so the geometric setup scores even if the final freehand curve is imperfect. Example, A-2(a) "Draw the elliptical outline **(18)**" (PDF p.4):
- (i) Location of second vertex — **2**
- (ii) Construction to locate points above the major axis — **8**
- (iii) Location of points below major axis — **4**
- (iv) Draw curve — **4**

The construction to find the points (8 + 4 + 2 = 14 of the 18) dwarfs the mark for drawing the curve itself (4). The same "construction → locate points → draw curve" ladder recurs for the parabola (B-1(a), PDF p.6), the hyperbola (C-2(a), PDF p.10) and the locus (C-4(b), PDF p.12).

**3. There are explicit standalone "use of appropriate method" marks.** The scheme awards marks for *choosing and setting up the correct technique*, independently of the point/answer it produces. Examples (PDF p.4):
- A-4(a)(iv) "Use of appropriate method to determine point on front surface" — **2**
- A-4(b)(vii) "Use of appropriate method to determine the true shape of surface S" — **2**

So even an incomplete answer that shows the right method (auxiliary view, cutting planes, etc.) earns the method granule.

**4. Presentation/convention elements carry their own (small) marks.** Hidden detail, dimension lines and correct outlining are itemised, usually at 1 mark:
- A-3(a)(v) "Hidden detail" — **1** (PDF p.5)
- C-3(a)(vi) "Hidden detail in plan" — **1**; C-3(a)(vii) "Complete the elevation of the main body" — **1** (PDF p.11)

**5. Multi-component assembly/section questions are marked component-by-component, each split into outline + inner detail.** The Section C sectional-elevation question (C-5, 60 marks, PDF p.13) allocates a small budget to every named part, and further splits each into its outline and its internal detail:
- Assembly — relative positioning of main components — **7**
- End Cutter — outline **2** / inner detail **5**
- Right-Hand Cap — outline **5** / inner detail **2**
- Sliding Plunger — main body **5** / top-and-bottom guide arms **4**
- Handle — outline **4** / inner detail **2**  … (and so on for every component)

A candidate who draws only some components of the assembly still scores each one they complete; a component drawn in outline but missing its section detail still banks the outline marks.

**6. Alternative valid solutions are explicitly credited.** The header on the marking-scheme and sample-solution divider pages states: "Other valid solutions are acceptable and are marked accordingly" (PDF p.3 and p.14). Several granules encode this with an "or", e.g. C-1(b)(xi) "Draw line from Sh parallel to RfSf (**or** Sf parallel to RhSh) in elevation" — **2** (PDF p.9); C-2's ellipse question also asks for the *second* focal point/vertex, marking each independently. The sample solutions "are not intended to be exhaustive" (PDF p.2).

**No accuracy-tolerance table, no stated unit/scale deduction, no negative-marking rule** appears anywhere in the scheme's general text. Accuracy is enforced implicitly: a granule is either awarded for a correctly-located point / correctly-drawn line or it is not, and the Chief Examiner is "the final authority regarding whether or not the marking scheme has been correctly applied" (PDF p.2). Some granules note the accuracy purpose in-line, e.g. C-3(c)(xii) "Location of apex on development (to ensure accuracy)" — **1** (PDF p.11), and C-4(a)(iv) requires "min. 7 incl. end points" for the U.A.R. construction (PDF p.12) — the mark depends on doing enough construction points.

## Student Assignment assessment sheet (PDF p.25) — for contrast

The 40% coursework component is marked on a **different grammar**: holistic criterion bands, not step-counting. Seven criteria, each a paragraph of descriptors with a single mark budget:
1. Design Research & Design Feature Comparison — 30
2. Freehand Graphical Representation — 20
3. SolidWorks Parts, Assembly, Drawing & eDrawing — 35
4. Hardcopy outputs from SolidWorks — 15
5. Graphical exploration of design solution — 25
6. Presentation of Modification/Concept Design — 10
7. SolidWorks files and Hardcopy output — 25

Note the explicit **"Marks deducted for pages in excess of maximum"** line at the foot of the sheet (PDF p.25) — the assignment penalises over-length (the 10-page HL limit), the one clear deduction rule in the whole document.

## Documented candidate-behaviour findings (for authored "how marking works" content)

Each is grounded in a specific scheme feature (structural, not a model answer):

1. **Construction lines are worth more than the finished curve — never rub them out.** In every conic/locus question the "construction to locate points" granule is the largest single award (e.g. A-2: 8 marks for the construction vs 4 for drawing the ellipse; PDF p.4). A student who shows the full construction but draws a slightly wonky curve keeps the majority of the marks; leaving the construction lines in place is how you evidence them.
2. **A drawing part is never all-or-nothing — attempt every step.** Each 15- or 20-mark part is a ladder of 3–8 independently scored steps (A-1, A-3, A-4; PDF pp.4–5). Getting three of four surfaces right still banks those three. Abandoning a question because one step defeats you forfeits the steps you *can* do.
3. **The method itself scores — show your approach even if you can't finish.** Standalone "Use of appropriate method to determine…" granules (A-4(iv), A-4(vii); PDF p.4) reward choosing the right technique (auxiliary view, cutting planes) independently of completing it. Setting up the correct auxiliary/true-shape method banks marks before a single final point is located.
4. **In assembly/section questions, draw component-by-component and outline first.** C-5 budgets marks to each named part and splits every part into outline + inner detail (PDF p.13). The optimal strategy under time pressure is to get every component's *outline* down first (banking the larger of the two sub-marks for several parts) rather than fully finishing one component and running out of time.
5. **Presentation conventions (hidden detail, completing an outline) are free marks — don't skip them.** Hidden detail and "complete the elevation" are itemised at 1 mark each (A-3(v), C-3(vi)/(vii); PDF pp.5, 11). They're small, but they're guaranteed marks that time-pressed candidates routinely omit.
6. **There's no single "correct" method — a valid alternative construction is fully credited.** "Other valid solutions are acceptable and are marked accordingly" (PDF pp.3, 14), reinforced by "or" alternatives inside granules (C-1(b)(xi), PDF p.9). A candidate should use the construction they know best rather than second-guessing the "expected" one.
