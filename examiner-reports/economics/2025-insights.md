## Marking-grammar structure

### 1. Two marking modes: "support notes" vs "descriptor marking"

The scheme states plainly that its sample answers are **not model answers** and not exhaustive (p.2):

- Support notes "are neither exclusive nor complete"; further relevant points are "rewarded on their merits."
- BUT "in many cases [they] may contain **key phrases which must appear** in the candidate's answer in order to merit the assigned marks."
- "Words, expressions or phrases must be **correctly used in context and not contradicted**" — incorrect use or self-contradiction can forfeit the marks.

For point-development parts, marks are not awarded per fact but by a **descriptor band** (p.2). Two bands are used, chosen by the mark value of the part:

**For a part worth up to 4 marks:**

| 3 Excellent | 2 Good | 1 Fair | 0 Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-depth, relates to question, concise, logical | Some knowledge, limited development | Vague knowledge | No knowledge / repeats the statement |

**For a part worth up to 5 marks (adds a "Poor" band):**

| 4 Excellent | 3 Good | 2 Fair | 1 Poor | 0 Weak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| In-depth, relates to question, concise, logical | Some knowledge, limited development | Vague knowledge | Confusing / contradictory knowledge | No knowledge / repeats the statement |

The load-bearing distinction: a merely restated point sits at the **bottom** of the band; "relates to the question" and "development" are what move an answer up. "Repetition of statement" is explicitly a zero/weak descriptor — re-writing the question or the stimulus earns nothing.

### 2. The named-point template — "N @ M"

The dominant grammar across both sections is `N @ M` (e.g. `2 @ 3`, `2 @ 6`, `2 @ 7`, `2 @ 5`, `2 @ 4`). It means: name **N** distinct developed points, each marked out of **M** on the descriptor band above. Consequences:

- **The number of points is capped.** "Outline **two** factors" marked `2 @ 6` counts only the first two; a third earns nothing. Fewer than N caps the total.
- **Each point is developed, not listed.** With M = 6 or 7, a bare named point sits low in the band; the marks live in the explanation/development, which the sample answers model as *statement → mechanism → consequence* (a "however…" contrast sentence is the recurring shape).
- The mark value **is** the instruction on depth: the scheme says "the detail required … is determined by … the number of marks assigned" (p.2). A `@3` point wants a sentence; a `@7` point wants a short developed paragraph.

### 3. Annotation grammar (how a script is ticked) — p.3

Marks are applied as numbered ticks: annotations ❶–⓫ each mean "valid information worth that many marks." Two non-numeric annotations carry the negative rules:

- A **surplus** mark ("surplus answer or part of answer", N/A) — material beyond the required number of points; seen and ignored, not penalised, not credited.
- **0** = incorrect answer; and a "page seen / information not valid" mark. Incorrect or contradictory content is actively zeroed, consistent with the p.2 "not contradicted" rule.

### 4. Section A — 75 marks, short questions (p.4 onward)

Section A is headed **"SECTION A (75 marks)"** (p.4). Structure of a short question:

- A stimulus (news extract, CSO table, chart or diagram) followed by 2–3 short parts.
- Parts are marked with either a single descriptor value (e.g. `6`) or the `N @ M` template (e.g. `2 @ 3`).
- Some questions offer an internal choice ("Answer either (b) or (c)", p.6) — only one is marked.
- Definition/term parts ("Outline what you understand by the economic term …") are marked as a single descriptor value against the required meaning, with key phrases expected (e.g. cost–benefit analysis: "weigh up / evaluate whether the benefits outweigh the costs … benefits must equal or exceed its costs", p.4).

### 5. Calculation rules — "Show all your workings"

Numerical parts are marked step-by-step, not answer-only. The recurring grid is **formula (1) + substitution (1) + answer (remaining marks)**:

- Savings-rate calc (p.5): the formula line `savings / disposable income × 100` is annotated ❶, the substitution `€6.59bn × 100 / €43.91bn` is annotated ❶, then the answer `15%`.
- Export-percentage calc (p.42-ish, printed): same shape, formula ❶ + substitution ❶ + answer `31.48% / 32%`.
- **Rounding tolerance is built in:** the scheme lists a range of acceptable answers (`31.48% / 32%`), so a correctly-worked rounded figure is not penalised.
- **A recurring named deduction: "Deduct 1 mark if % omitted" / "Deduct 1 mark for omission of %"** (pp.5, 57, and others). A numerically correct answer written without its unit loses a mark.
- Cost-table completion (Total − Fixed = Variable; Average = Total ÷ Output; profit = Revenue − Total cost) is marked with each labelled cell (A, B) shown with its own workings — the missing cell's working is its own mark.

So the answer alone never scores full marks; skipping the formula or the substitution line forfeits those ticks even when the final number is right.

### 6. Diagrams and graphs carry named, itemised marks

Diagrams are not decoration — they are broken into named marks and **labelling is a distinct, forfeitable component.** Question cues make labelling explicit: "Label your diagram fully", "Clearly label any changes you make", "fully clearly labelled diagram (including the axes)", "Do not use abbreviations."

Verified diagram/graph grids:

- **Supply-and-demand graph on graph paper (printed p.50): total 17 marks = `17 × 1`.** Breakdown given in the margin: complete/fill the two **Axes = 2**, **Demand curve = 6**, **Supply curve = 6**, plus equilibrium point **E**, price **Pₑ**, quantity **Qₑ**. Every plotted point and label is its own 1-mark tick; the instruction "You must complete/fill in the price axis and the quantity axis" is enforced by those 2 axis marks.
- **LRAC construction diagram (printed p.67): 8 marks = `SRAC × 3` (draw three short-run curves) + `Labels × 3` + `LRAC × 1` + `Label × 1`.** Half the marks are for labelling the curves you drew.
- **Monopoly long-run-equilibrium diagram "including the axes" (printed p.25): 12 marks** for the labelled diagram (P, Q, MR, AR, MC, AC, equilibrium E, Q₁, P₁, C₁, SNPs) plus a separate explanation part.
- **Minimum-wage labour-market diagram (printed p.16): 6 marks** — the required labels are the marks: NMW line above Wₑ, the new Q_S and Q_D, and the "excess supply / unemployment gap" region; "Clearly label any changes you make."
- **Kinked-demand / read-the-diagram part (printed p.20):** "Write out **in full** what each numbered item represents. **Do not use abbreviations**" — writing "MC" instead of "Marginal cost" would not satisfy the cue.

Pattern: an unlabelled or partly-labelled diagram loses the label marks item-by-item; abbreviations where "in full" is demanded score nothing for that item.

### 7. Question-cue effects

The cue verb sets the grid:

- **"Calculate … Show all your workings"** → step-marked (formula + substitution + answer), unit required.
- **"Outline / Discuss two …"** → `2 @ M` developed-point template; count is capped at two.
- **"Discuss one argument for and one against"** → the balance is required; both sides must appear (the p.4 sample lays out a "for" set and an "against" set).
- **"Complete the diagram / graph … label fully"** → per-element `× 1` or itemised grid; labels are separately marked.
- **"Write out in full … do not use abbreviations"** → full terms only.
- **"Explain the shape of the curve"** → descriptor-band explanation (the kinked-demand answer is graded on the elastic-before / inelastic-after mechanism, not on naming).

### 8. Section B — long questions (pp.23–70)

Section B (Q11–Q16, six themed long questions) is built from the same `N @ M` developed-point template plus the diagram and calculation grids above, at higher mark values (`2 @ 6`, `2 @ 7` are common). Each long question threads a real-world stimulus (CSO data, news headlines) through several parts mixing calculation, diagram and developed-point marking. (The scheme text does not print a Section-B total in a single header; the paper overall is the 80% written component.)

### 9. Student Research Project (SRP) — descriptor-grid marking (pp.71–74)

The SRP is marked entirely on **five-band qualitative descriptors** (Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair / Weak), not point-counting. Structure of the grid:

- **Introduction — Line of Inquiry (LOI): 10 marks** (bands 9–10 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 0–2). Top band requires the LOI "stated clearly as a question/problem/issue", relevant to the theme, with **SMART aims** linked to the specification's Learning Outcomes.
- **Evidence of Data: 5 marks**, with two flat named deductions: **"Deduct 1m if no quantitative data. Deduct 1m if 2 sources not included."**
- **The Research Process: 40 marks**, split across descriptor rows — Application & Analysis, Interpret & Evaluate, Arguments & Judgements, Data (Application & Analysis) — each banded 9–10 / 7–8 / 5–6 / 3–4 / 0–2. Top bands demand *critical/perceptive* analysis, objective evaluation of arguments, and judgements "supported with relevant data/diagrams/curves."
- General marking instruction (p.71): "**Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length.**" And: read the entire study before marking; interpret descriptors "in the context of the challenges and demands of the investigation the candidate has chosen."

The SRP grammar rewards *analysis, interpretation and evaluation supported by data*, and explicitly does not reward length — the opposite failure mode to point-counting.

## General examiner conventions (verbatim, p.2)

- Marking schemes "are not intended to be standalone documents"; examiners are trained, calibrated on sample scripts and monitored by Advising Examiners; the Chief Examiner is "the final authority."
- Model solutions "are not intended to be exhaustive. Variations and alternatives may also be acceptable. Examiners must consider all answers on their merits."
- **"Assumptions about future marking schemes on the basis of past schemes should be avoided"** — the detail of how a question type is marked "are subject to change from one year to the next without notice." (Relevant caveat for any tool teaching from a single year.)

## Candidate-behaviour findings (structural, from the scheme)

1. **Answer-only calculations self-cap.** Every calculation is step-marked formula + substitution + answer, so a right final number with no workings forfeits the method ticks; and a correct number with the **% / unit omitted is a named 1-mark deduction** (pp.5, 57). *(Source: scheme pp.5–6, 57.)*
2. **Unlabelled or abbreviated diagrams bleed marks item-by-item.** On the S&D graph, labels/axes are 5 of the 17 marks; on the LRAC diagram, labels are 4 of 8; "Do not use abbreviations" makes shorthand score zero for that item. *(Source: scheme pp.16, 20, 25, 50, 67.)*
3. **Over-supplying points earns nothing; under-supplying caps the total.** The `N @ M` template counts only the first N points (extras annotated "surplus"), so writing four points for a `2 @ 6` part wastes time while a third weak point never rescues a thin first two. *(Source: scheme pp.3, and `2 @ N` grids throughout, e.g. pp.7, 25, 57, 67.)*
4. **Restating the stimulus or the point sits at the bottom of the band.** "Repetition of statement" is an explicit Weak/zero descriptor; marks come from *development* and *relating to the question*, not from naming. *(Source: scheme p.2 descriptor tables.)*
5. **In the SRP, length is not rewarded and unsupported opinion is not either.** The grid tops out only for *critical analysis, evaluation and judgements supported by data/diagrams*, with an explicit instruction not to reward "unwarranted length"; a data section with no quantitative data or fewer than two sources takes flat deductions. *(Source: scheme pp.71–74.)*
