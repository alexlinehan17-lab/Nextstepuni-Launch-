Q3 Demand and Price Elasticity
(a) State and explain four factors which affect the demand for a good or service. (20 marks)
QA = f (P, POG, Y, T, E, G, U)
1. Price of the good itself.
Generally as the price of a good falls consumers will buy more of that good/they are
getting better value for their money.
2. Price of complementary good.
If the price of a complementary good rises then demand for this good falls - e.g.
an increase in the price of petrol will result in a drop in the demand for large cars.
3. Prices of substitute good.
If the price of a substitute good rises then demand for this good rises, as it has become
relatively cheaper e.g. if the price of butter increases the demand for margarine will
increase.
4. Income of the consumer.
For most goods as income rises the demand for them increases and vice versa
e.g. smaller quantities of goods are bought when a person becomes unemployed.
5. The consumer’s tastes / preference for a commodity.
When a commodity comes into fashion or into season there is an increase in the quantity
demanded at each price. Advertising attempts to influence taste in favour of the good.
6. Expectations concerning future prices / availability of income.
If a consumer expects that future prices are likely to be greater than they are at present,
then there will be an increase in the demand for the good at each price.
7. Government regulations.
If the government initiates a programme to curtail consumption of a particular product
then it may affect the demand for a good e.g. a health education campaign to curtail
cigarette consumption.
8. Unplanned factors.
If there was a sudden heatwave this may result in an increase in the demand for suncream
/ icecream.
4 factors at 5 marks each. State: 2 marks each. Explain: 3 marks each.

A patent/ copyright confers the sole right on a business to a particular manufacturing process for a particular commodity.
• iPad; • Apple iPhone

The government may grant to a company the sole right to supply a good or service so that there is a legal restriction on competition.

Economies of scale
Arise where the largest supplier in an industry has a significant cost advantage over competitors because of its large size. It is unlikely that a potential competitor would be willing to make the capital investment needed to enter the monopolist's market.
• Gas pipeline. • Eircom: telephone lines. • ESB: Grid lines • Iarnrod Eireann: Train tracks

Redistribution
The tax should help the government re-distribute the wealth of the country / achieve other economic objectives.
Evasion
It should be impossible to evade paying the tax.
Incentive to work/save
The tax should not act as a disincentive to work, save or invest.
Brain drain.

Economic effects for the Irish economy
Third level college fees
• Current expenditure by government will decrease as it now would no longer have to pay for the fees. • As families now pay the fees this will reduce their disposable income and so spending falls resulting in a decrease in indirect tax revenue collected. • Some people may not now be able to attend college and have to rely on state benefits which will result in increased government current expenditure. • Less qualified people available to develop the smart economy. • Opportunity costs: government now has this money available for other uses / reduced borrowing. • Equity: those who can afford to do so can now pay for themselves. • With a reduction in skills the country may become a less attractive location for investment.
Increase in the statutory retirement age
• Government finances should improve as people will be paying tax for longer. • Will result in a reduction in the number of people seeking pensions and so reduce government current expenditure. • Less job vacancies for young people may mean that unemployment may increase and so this will increase government current expenditure. • With less job vacancies it may result in an increase in emigration.
Household property tax
• This should lead to an increase in government taxation revenue. • Disposable incomes will drop resulting in a reduction in spending and so less indirect tax revenue collected e.g. VAT / Excise duties. • May result in less people buying houses, so reduced demand leading to lower house prices and unemployment in the construction and related sectors. • Inequitable: may be regressive if ability to pay is not taken in to account. • Purchasing / speculation in the property market may decline resulting in Lower prices.
