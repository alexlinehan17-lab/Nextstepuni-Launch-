## Marking-grammar structure — ORDINARY LEVEL vs HIGHER LEVEL

### 1. Descriptor bands — OL adds a THIRD, smaller band (printed p.2)

Both levels grade developed points on qualitative descriptor tables, not per-fact. The band tables differ:

- **HL** prints **two** tables: a 4-mark table `3 / 2 / 1 / 0` (Excellent / Good / Fair / Weak) and a 5-mark table `4 / 3 / 2 / 1 / 0` (adds "Poor").
- **OL** prints **three** tables (printed p.2): it keeps the `3/2/1/0` and `4/3/2/1/0` tables **and adds a smaller `2 / 1 / 0` table — Excellent / Good / Weak only (no "Fair")**. This is the low-tariff band OL needs for its many small sub-parts and does not exist in the HL scheme.

The descriptor wording is otherwise identical to HL: top band = "In-depth knowledge · Relates to question · Concise · Logical"; bottom band = "No knowledge · Repetition of statement". "Repetition of statement" is an explicit Weak/zero descriptor at OL as at HL.

### 2. Point tariffs — OL uses a FRONT-LOADED "1st @ X / 2nd @ Y" grammar HL never uses

This is the single biggest structural difference.

- **HL is symmetric.** Every HL point-development part is `2 @ M` with both points worth the same: the scheme uses only `2 @ 3`, `2 @ 4`, `2 @ 5`, `2 @ 6`, `2 @ 7` (verified: zero occurrences of "1st @" in the HL scheme).
- **OL is asymmetric / front-loaded.** Alongside symmetric tariffs (`2 @ 2`, `2 @ 4`, `2 @ 6`, `2 @ 7`, `3 @ 4`, `3 @ 8`, `4 @ 3`), OL introduces a **"1st @ X, 2nd @ Y"** grammar where the **first** developed point is worth **more** than the second. Verified instances:
  - `1st @ 8 / 2nd @ 4` (12 marks) — printed pp.22, 25
  - `1st @ 10 / 2nd @ 6` (16 marks) — printed p.26
  - `1st @ 6 / 2nd @ 4` (10 marks) — printed p.30
  - `1st @ 8 / 2nd @ 6` (14 marks)
  - `1st @ 3 / 2nd @ 2` (5 marks)

  Consequence: at OL, one thoroughly developed point earns the majority of a part's marks; a strong first point + a thin second still banks most of the total, whereas a weak first point cannot be rescued by the (lower-tariff) second. **Depth on the first point beats breadth.**

- **OL also uses higher per-point tariffs than HL in places.** HL tops out at `@7` per point; OL runs to `@8` and `@10` on the first point of a front-loaded part — because those marks reward the *development* of a single point, not a second point.
- **OL uses more varied N.** HL is almost uniformly `2 @ M`; OL uses `3 @` and `4 @ 3` (four short points), reflecting more "state/list"-style short parts.

### 3. Calculation rules — step-marked, but NO "% omission" deduction at OL

- Calculations are step-marked at OL as at HL ("Show all your workings" — printed pp.12, 19, 20). Marks are annotated onto the substituted components: e.g. the capital-expenditure calc (printed p.19) is marked `€14.9bn ❻ × 100 ❷ / €105.4bn ❹ = 14.14% ❹` — the correct substitution values carry the bulk of the marks (10 of 16), the ×100 step 2, the final % 4. A right answer with no workings forfeits the substitution ticks.
- **Rounding tolerance** is built in (e.g. the pension calc `243,700 × 100 / 589,500 = 41.3%`, printed p.12).
- **Key difference: the HL "Deduct 1 mark for omission of %" rule does NOT appear anywhere in the OL scheme.** A case-insensitive scan finds only **two** "Deduct" rules in the whole OL scheme, both in the SRP data grid (see §5). The HL unit-omission deduction on calculation parts has no OL counterpart in this year's scheme. (Do not assume it is enforced at OL; verify per year.)

### 4. Diagrams — graded by curve-block + named axis marks; OL softens the labelling demand

- **Graph on graph paper (AC/MC/MR construction, printed p.24): 17 marks, graded by block not by point.** Margin grid: **Axes `2 @ 2` = 4**, **AC = 5**, **MC = 5**, **MR = 3**. The instruction "You must complete/fill in the quantity axis and the costs axis" is enforced by those 4 axis marks. (Contrast HL, whose S&D graph is `17 × 1` graded point-by-point; OL grades each curve as a lump and the two axes as a named `2 @ 2` pair.)
- **Label-in-full part (Perfect Competition long-run diagram, printed p.17):** "Write out **in full** what any **three of the following four** labels represent" — MC → "Marginal Cost", Q → "Quantity", D=AR=MR → "Demand / Average Revenue / Marginal Revenue", AC → "Average Cost". Two OL-specific softenings vs HL: (a) OL offers a built-in choice ("any three of four"); (b) OL requires "write out in full" but **does not** carry HL's explicit "**Do not use abbreviations**" instruction (the phrase does not appear anywhere in the OL scheme).
- **Point-plotting on a diagram:** "Clearly show and label … use label Q1 … use label P1" (printed p.17) — the required labels are the marks.

### 5. Student Research Project (SRP) — OL uses a FOUR-band grid; HL uses five (printed pp.42–44)

- **OL SRP descriptor grid has four bands: Very Good / Good / Fair / Weak** — there is **no "Excellent" column**. Band boundaries for a 10-mark row are `8-10 / 6-7 / 4-5 / 0-3` (printed p.43). **HL uses five bands** (Excellent / Very Good / Good / Fair / Weak) with narrower boundaries `9-10 / 7-8 / 5-6 / 3-4 / 0-2`. OL's top band is therefore wider (8–10) and easier to reach.
- **Shared with HL:** Introduction / Line of Inquiry 10 marks; **Evidence of Data 5 marks with the same two flat deductions — "Deduct 1m if no quantitative data. Deduct 1m if 2 sources not included."** (printed p.43); The Research Process 40 marks; and the general instruction "**Be careful not to penalise skilful brevity, nor to reward unwarranted length**" plus "read the entire … Study … before marking" (printed p.42). The rubric language is simplified at OL ("A good application and analysis…" vs HL's "critical/perceptive analysis").

### 6. Shared conventions (identical wording to HL, printed pp.2, 4)

- Support notes "are neither exclusive nor complete", "not model answers", but "may contain **key phrases which must appear** in the candidate's answer"; "words … must be correctly used in context and not contradicted".
- Annotation grammar identical: numbered ticks ❶–⓫ = valid information worth that many marks; a **surplus** mark (material beyond the required number of points — seen, ignored, not credited); **0** = incorrect answer; a "page seen / information not valid" mark (printed p.4).
- The forward-looking caveat is the same: requirements "may … vary from year to year" (printed p.2). Treat the OL specifics above as this-year, not perennial.

## Candidate-behaviour findings (OL-specific, structural)

1. **At OL, over-develop the FIRST point.** The front-loaded `1st @ 8 / 1st @ 10` tariffs mean a single, fully-developed first point can bank two-thirds of a part's marks; a strong first + thin second still scores well, but a weak first is not recoverable by the lower-tariff second. This is the opposite incentive to HL's symmetric `2 @ M`. *(Source: OL scheme printed pp.22, 25, 26, 30.)*

2. **A % answer with no workings self-caps — but omitting the % sign is NOT a named deduction at OL.** Calculations are step-marked on the substituted values (e.g. printed p.19: numerator 6, denominator 4, ×100 2, answer 4 of 16), so answer-only forfeits the method ticks; however, unlike HL, the OL scheme carries no "Deduct 1 mark for omission of %" rule. *(Source: OL scheme printed pp.12, 19; "Deduct" appears only in the SRP grid, printed p.43.)*

3. **Diagram marks live in the curves and the two axes.** On the AC/MC/MR graph, 4 of 17 marks are the axes (`2 @ 2`) and the rest split AC 5 / MC 5 / MR 3 — a curve drawn without completing the axes loses a named 4 marks. *(Source: OL scheme printed p.24.)*

4. **"Write out in full" still means full terms — but OL gives a 3-of-4 choice and no explicit anti-abbreviation warning.** Candidates must expand labels (MC → "Marginal Cost") for the label part, but only three of four are required and the HL "Do not use abbreviations" sentence is absent. *(Source: OL scheme printed p.17.)*

5. **In the SRP, length is not rewarded and the data section takes flat hits.** The four-band OL grid tops out (8–10) for genuine application/analysis/evaluation, with the explicit "do not reward unwarranted length" instruction; "no quantitative data" or "fewer than two sources" each lose a flat mark. *(Source: OL scheme printed pp.42–43.)*
